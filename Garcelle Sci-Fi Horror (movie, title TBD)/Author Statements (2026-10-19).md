# Garcelle sci-fi horror movie (title TBD): author statements

Staging file: one place for what the author has said about this film, so later files point here. **A movie, not a book**, set in the Tepenian universe. **Not canon until promoted; nothing here goes to the Timeline repo unprompted.** **AUTHOR** = stated in conversation (2026-10-19); **CHECK** = found in the project's files (path given); **OPEN** = needs the author; **PROPOSED** = my suggestion.

## Settled so far (AUTHOR)
- **Universe:** the Tepenian universe.
- **Lead: Garcelle**, a robot. Her name is built from **"G.A.R.: Cell E"**, the detention center where she was held before being **forcibly evicted from Earth**. (CHECK: this matches her character files, below.)
- **Setting:** aboard a **derelict space station in outer space**. The author's immediate idea is **one of the Earth-Moon Lagrange points**; part of them would like it farther away, but they "really envision it as passing across the Moon in one of the early visuals to show that it's in outer space."
- **Time frame:** **around the year 3000**: "quite a bit after the Long Night War", during a time when space infrastructure is fairly well developed.
- **Length:** roughly **85 to 120 minutes**.

## What follows (reasoning, not author statements)
- **The opening's eye imagery rhymes with her history**: the burned black eyes in the extreme close-ups, and her digging into a man's eyes, echo the electric shocks to hers. This may be deliberate or coincidence; the author decides.
- **The opening victim reads as an earlier "salvager"**, consistent with the older note ("occasionally some humans will come"). The second scene's optimistic crew then walk into a trap the audience already knows about.
- **The "derelict station" and the older note's "Von Braun Wheel" may be the same structure** (a Von Braun wheel is a rotating-wheel station, also used in the Timeline for Besifa's possible habitat), but the older note says "her ship"; whether the film's setting is a wheel, a ship or something else is open.
- **Station location:** the opening says "in orbit around the moon", the author's setting says an Earth-Moon Lagrange point, and the author wants the Moon visible. These are three different things: low lunar orbit, a Lagrange point (L1 to L5, each with a different view of Earth and Moon) and a halo orbit around one. They can be reconciled by choosing the view before the location.

## Design implications (PROPOSED; the author decides)
- **Vice-driven deaths.** The author's project already uses Enneagram types for character design; each type has a "passion" (the habitual vice) ⚠️M: 1 anger/resentment, 2 pride, 3 deceit/vanity, 4 envy, 5 avarice, 6 fear, 7 gluttony, 8 lust/excess, 9 sloth. Giving each scavenger a type means the vice that kills them is the one their personality was built around, so the deaths come from character rather than stupidity. (Garcelle herself is an 8w9.) This is a suggestion, not a requirement; the author may prefer their own list of vices.
- **The rip can be the delivery mechanism for the vices.** "Hunting from multiple angles" can be literal: the rip opens onto what each person wants or fears, so each scavenger walks toward their own vice through a door that should not lead there.
- **Structure for the scavengers' track.** Each scavenger gets the same three beats: the vice shown early (so the audience knows it), tested in the middle (it costs them something small), and used against them at the end. The expositional load the author mentioned then does the work of setting up the horror.
- **Revenge needs a target.** Her want is revenge, but the scavengers are strangers, not the Garrison guard who tortured her. So her revenge is **displaced onto humans as a class** (consistent with her loyalties file: "the only good human is a dead human"). The vice-driven killings give her a rule for choosing and a way to feel justified. Whether she sometimes spares someone, and what the film thinks about her judgment, are open (theme is discovered, not designed).
- **Her need** (as distinct from her want) is not yet set. Her file lists weakness, goal, values and internal conflict as TBD.
- **The wheel suits liminal horror.** A Von Braun wheel is a closed loop: you can walk the rim and come back to where you started, and the floor curves up ahead of you in both directions. Both are naturally uncanny, and a tear that connects two points on the loop is a tidy way to break the geometry.
