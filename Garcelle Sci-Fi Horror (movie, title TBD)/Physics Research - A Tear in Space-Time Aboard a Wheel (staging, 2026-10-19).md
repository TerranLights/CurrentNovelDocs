# Physics research: a tear in space-time aboard a rotating wheel (staging, 2026-10-19)

First pass at the author's request for research into theoretical mathematics and physics (the "rip" in space-time inside the station). **I am not a physicist; this is a map of the real ideas closest to the film's, so the film's rules can borrow from them honestly.** Flags: **(search)** = found by web search 2026-10-19 (sources at the end); **⚠️M** = my memory, unverified; **(computed)** = my arithmetic; **PROPOSED** = my suggestion, the author decides. Nothing here is canon.

## 1. The closest real idea: an intra-universe wormhole
- A wormhole joining two points of **the same universe** is a recognized idea: topologically, a region of spacetime whose boundary is simple but whose **interior is not simply connected** (search). It has **two mouths** in one spacetime.
- General relativity **does not fix the topology of space**, so multiply connected space is not forbidden by the equations (search). This is the mathematical hook for "space-time folded onto itself": the relevant mathematics is **topology** (connectedness, loops that cannot be shrunk to a point) and **differential geometry** ⚠️M.
- **What it would look like in the station:** two openings inside the wheel; going into one, you come out of the other. The "hunting from multiple angles" is then literal: she can enter anywhere a mouth is and leave at the other.

## 2. The catch: holding one open
- **Traversable** wormholes need **exotic matter**, with negative energy density, to stay open; the 1988 Morris-Thorne analysis says the supporting matter violates all the energy conditions (search). Visser's later models minimize the exotic matter and show a traveler can cross without passing through it (search). The energies needed are **at the Planck scale** for the simplest models, far beyond any known technology (search).
- **For the film:** a "previous accident" is the author's way around this. A plausible in-story version: a failed experiment, reactor or exotic-matter containment on the station stabilized a throat by accident. The film needs consistent rules, not true physics; PROPOSED: state in-world only that something "holds the tear open" and show that it has a cost.

## 3. Time: the horror lever
- Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever (1988) showed that a traversable wormhole becomes a **time machine** if one mouth is moved fast relative to the other (search): the mouths then sit at **different times**, and going through one can take you to the past of the other.
- **Hawking's chronology protection conjecture** says physics always prevents closed timelike curves; as one is about to form, the quantum stress-energy diverges and the wormhole **blows up** (search). It is a conjecture, not proven (search).
- **PROPOSED, for the film:** this gives a **ticking clock for free**. If the rip joins mouths at different times, the structure becomes **less stable the more its time offset grows**, so the station is slowly tearing itself apart. It also makes "paranormal without ghosts" literal: scavengers see a figure in a corridor who is **Garcelle at another moment**, or **themselves** a few minutes earlier or later.
- **A limit on the wheel itself (computed):** a mouth carried on the rim of a wheel at a comfortable spin (about 2 rpm at a 250 m radius, so the rim moves at about 52 m/s) accumulates a time offset of only about 1.5 parts in 10^14 against a mouth at the hub. Over 100 years that is **under 0.0001 second**. So **the wheel's rotation cannot produce a meaningful time offset by itself**; the accident or the tear's own physics must supply it. (Check: 2 rpm = 0.209 rad/s; v = ωr = 52 m/s; v²/2c² ≈ 1.5×10⁻¹⁴; 100 years ≈ 3.16×10⁹ s; product ≈ 4.8×10⁻⁵ s.)

## 4. The wheel
- Von Braun's 1952 concept was a **250 m diameter** torus spinning at **4.9 rpm** for 1 g (search). Comfort studies suggest **no more than 2 to 3 rpm**, because faster spin makes the Coriolis effect disturb the inner ear (search); at 1 g that implies a radius of roughly **100 to 250 m or more** (a 500 m radius is about 1.3 rpm; a 100 m radius is about 3 rpm) (search).
- **Visual and liminal gifts of a wheel** (PROPOSED): the floor curves up ahead of you in both directions; walking with the spin and against it feels different; **dropped objects drift sideways** (Coriolis); with a small radius, "east" and "west" become as different as "up" and "down" (search). A derelict wheel that has **lost its spin or is spinning slowly** gives half gravity, then none, which is unsettling. The size and spin of the wheel are open.

## 5. The location: a way to have both a Lagrange point and "passing across the Moon"
- The five Earth-Moon points: **L1 (between), L2 (far side of the Moon), L3 (opposite)** are **unstable**, and **L4 and L5 are stable** (search). A derelict with no station-keeping would drift away from L1, L2 or L3 unless it were in a **halo orbit** about one of them, and a **near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO)** around L2 is **stable or nearly stable** (search); it is the orbit chosen for the real Gateway station (search).
- A station in an **L2 NRHO** is in the Lagrange-point family, stays put for a very long time, and **swings past the Moon on every orbit** (close approach near the lunar south pole, far swing out the other way; about 6.5 days per orbit, close approach a few thousand km, far side tens of thousands ⚠️M). That is the **"passing across the Moon" shot, repeated in the film's first minutes and last**, with the Moon big in frame at close approach and a "little dot" in orbit when pulled back.
- **L4 and L5 are stable too** but the Moon would be a fixed disc and the station would not pass across it.
- **PROPOSED: an L2 NRHO** (or the author may want the station orbiting the Moon in low lunar orbit, which the opening's "in orbit around the moon" wording suggests). The author decides; the opening's "orbit around the Moon" and the setting's "Lagrange point" can both be true of an NRHO.
