# The Falkland Treaty (2564-06-21) — Southern Lights' Opening Event

Source: InnerTepeniaGDD `World_History_Reference.md`, `Main-Story/Main_Quest_Reassessment_2026-07-10.md`, `Storyline/DLC_Overview.md`, `Reference/Materials/Treaties/`, `to-be-integrated/treaties/`, `Storyline/Side-Content/District_Internal_Conflict_Quests.md`, `to-be-integrated/miscellaneous/Character_Concept_Bank.md`.

## What's confirmed canon

Signed June 21, 2564 at Stanley, Falkland Islands. Ends the War of Upper Earth — the first anti-robot war. Upper Earth wins; the robot population is too large to exterminate outright, so blanket exile to Antarctica is negotiated instead. **This is explicitly "the terms of their defeat, not a mutual compromise."** Establishes the Federation of Tepenia.

- Exile applies to robots **as a class**, not named individuals — a named robot (Ji-Eun Kim, a nanotech engineer) "was not named in the treaty... she was exiled because she is a robot."
- Human allies aren't stripped of legal personhood by the Treaty directly — a human who elects to go into exile with the robots is instead treated, under Upper Earth's own law, as having *voluntarily and permanently forfeited* their personhood. Same practical outcome (barred from returning), different legal mechanism (individual election vs. blanket revocation).
- The full Treaty text is **intentionally not yet written** in the source GDD — the developer researched real-world treaty language specifically in order to draft it later (see below). This means Southern Lights has room to originate the actual in-world text.
- **In-game presence:** Treaty Archive Vaults (Libra district, Concordia), holographic Treaty seals on display. Two factions care about it directly: the Veilkeepers (obsessed with suppressed information about "why the evacuation happened") and the Treaty Scholars (study its long-term consequences).
- Unresolved even in source canon: who signed for Upper Earth, the full terms beyond the exile provision, what "the evacuation" actually was and what was suppressed about it.

## Real-world treaties used as structural models

Two research libraries exist in the source repo covering the same ground from different angles:

**`Reference/Materials/Treaties/`** — a curated, indexed 38-treaty library (95 files, ~11MB) organized specifically around what a Falkland-Treaty-style document needs, with per-treaty relevance notes:
- *Peace treaties* — Versailles 1919 (esp. the Danzig free-city and disarmament sections), Dayton 1995 (flagged as best model for "terse core treaty + annexes" structure)
- *Population transfer/resettlement* — the Lausanne Convention 1923 (Greek-Turkish population exchange) flagged as **the** closest precedent for compulsory relocation as a peace term; Potsdam 1945 as a "protective transfer" framing, relevant if robot exile gets framed similarly in-world
- *Autonomous territories/exile states* — the **Svalbard Treaty (1920)** and **Antarctic Treaty (1959)** flagged as directly on-point: both are real demilitarized special-sovereignty regimes over polar/near-polar territory
- *Disarmament/demobilization* — Dayton's Annex 1-A flagged as best demobilization template
- *Minority/persecuted-group protection* — Polish Minorities Treaty 1919, Genocide Convention 1948, ILO 169 (indigenous rights), Framework Convention for National Minorities

**`to-be-integrated/treaties/`** — a broader, less-curated research pull (~50 treaties across borders/economics/war-and-ceasefire/other, plus the UN Charter and UDHR standalone). Two were read in full and are worth knowing directly:
- **The Antarctic Treaty (1959)** — almost certainly the Falkland Treaty's direct structural model: peaceful-purposes-only demilitarization, freedom of scientific investigation, **sovereignty claims frozen in place, not renounced** (Article IV), inspection rights for any signatory anywhere in the territory, amendment only by unanimous consent, a 30-year review trigger. Sits in real tension with the game's own framing of the Falkland Treaty as a defeat document, not a mutual-compromise one — worth deciding deliberately whether Southern Lights' Treaty leans into that tension or resolves it.
- **The Hittite–Egypt Treaty (~1259 BC)**, the oldest surviving peace treaty — mutual non-aggression/defensive-alliance clauses, formal renunciation of former hostility, and an extradition clause: fugitives crossing between the two states must be returned, but **explicitly may not be punished or have their families harmed** on return. Thematically close to the Upper Earth Defectors (people who left the winning side voluntarily and can never go back) — worth mining for how a Southern Lights Treaty handles that population specifically.

Taken together: a plausible in-world Falkland Treaty reads as a hybrid of Antarctic-Treaty-style demilitarization language, Lausanne-style compulsory population transfer, Hittite-Egypt-style non-punishment/refugee provisions, and UDHR-style personhood framing — layered under the "terms of defeat" framing the source GDD insists on. This is genuinely open territory: nobody has drafted this text yet, in either project.

## The dramatic core: Maggie Aarden's signature

From `Main-Story/Main_Quest_Reassessment_2026-07-10.md`: **"the actual root [of several Concordia institutions later misattributed to the Long Night War] is Maggie Aarden's coerced signature at the very beginning of Tepenia's history."** Three still-standing Concordia districts (Libra, Gemini, Pisces) unknowingly misattribute their own founding institutions to the *Long Night War* (2812), when the real wound traces back **248 years earlier** to the Treaty's actual signing. Flagged in the source as "the most significant finding about how deep this particular wound actually runs" — and deliberately built as undirected discovery content, never delivered by an NPC, never a tracked quest objective.

Cross-referenced against `Storyline/DLC_Overview.md`: Maggie Aarden is a confirmed robot, DLC2's canonical recruitable/romanceable companion, who settled permanently in **Byrd** — one of only two cities besides Concordia to survive the Long Night War. **Her role as the Treaty's robot signatory is confirmed canon** (locked 2026-07-09, synced across the source repos 2026-07-11) — no longer an open "swappable" placeholder.

Put together: an ageless robot, coerced into signing the document that founded her own nation under duress, who then lived out the following ~250 years in one of the few cities to survive what came after — and nobody in the game's present knows the coercion happened. This is close to a ready-made Southern Lights POV-anchor: she spans the entire 2564–2812 span by construction, and while her identity as signatory is now locked, the source GDD hasn't written her actual story beyond that fact, leaving real room for Southern Lights to originate it (subject to staying reconcilable with the game if the developer wants that).

## The Great Corruption — a live, unsolved mystery sitting right at the opening

From `Storyline/Side-Content/District_Internal_Conflict_Quests.md` (Gemini district's "The Speed of Truth" quest) and `to-be-integrated/miscellaneous/Character_Concept_Bank.md`: **the Great Corruption** is the mass deletion of Janbogo's pre-Falkland-Treaty archives. Specific records were deleted **in a specific order, in the hours before the Treaty was signed** — evidence suggests this was targeted, not accidental, and what survives implies **the Treaty was negotiated under false pretenses**. A faction called **The Long Frequency** (specifically its "Recovery wing") is still investigating this in the game's present, ~250 years later, and hasn't solved it.

This is a live mystery in the shared universe with no answer yet written anywhere. Southern Lights, opening at the moment of the Treaty's signing, is positioned to actually dramatize what was being erased and why — effectively originating the answer to a mystery the game itself has left open.

## Status: tentative vs. confirmed, for this doc

- **Confirmed, safe to build on:** the date (2564-06-21), the exile mechanism (blanket for robots, elective-forfeiture for humans), the "terms of defeat" framing, the Archive Vaults/Veilkeepers/Treaty Scholars as in-world present-day echoes, and (as of 2026-07-09) Maggie Aarden as the robot signatory.
- **Tentative, explicitly open in source canon:** the full Treaty text (not yet drafted anywhere), what the Great Corruption actually erased and why.
