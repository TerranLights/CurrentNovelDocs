# Per-Beat Constraints for Maggie — What Each Beat Must *Be Like*, Not What It Must *Be*

Working session, 2026-09-19. **All proposals, none locked.** This file deliberately does not invent events.
It specifies the *attributes and restrictions* each beat of the fused Save the Cat structure
(`reference/story-structure-definitions.md`) should satisfy for Maggie specifically, so that when actual
events do get invented, there are criteria to check them against.

Derived from three inputs held simultaneously: her Enneagram profile (5w6 Self-Pres, undercurrent 1w9
Self-Pres — see the project's own mined Type 5 material), her robot nature, and her established
world/biography. Companion to `Maggie Aarden - Archetype Translation (Robot, Personality, World).md` and
`Maggie Aarden - Personal Arc vs Grand Timeline Alignment.md`.

---

## Cross-Cutting Constraints (apply to every beat, at every level)

These come first because they invalidate a lot of default beat construction.

1. **She never confronts directly.** Her subtype is the least communicative of the three Fives, with explicit
   "difficulty expressing anger directly — displeasure shows up instead as withdrawal or silence rather than
   confrontation." Any beat whose design assumes escalating verbal conflict will produce *silence* from her
   instead, and fall flat. Beats must escalate by other means (see #4). A beat where she *does* confront
   openly is therefore enormous — reserve those deliberately, don't spend them early.
2. **Feeling arrives late, sideways, or physically.** Her core defense is Isolation — separating the feeling
   from the idea, thinking *about* an emotion instead of having it. Any beat whose power depends on her
   feeling something in the moment needs a workaround: deferred reaction, displacement onto an object or a
   task, or a bodily route that bypasses analysis entirely.
3. **Technical problems are the reliable key to her.** Any beat that needs her active engagement can obtain it
   through a practical/technical hook, and often *only* through one. An appeal framed emotionally trips her
   defenses; an appeal framed as a problem to solve gets her hands moving before her guard is up.
4. **Boundaries are the load-bearing structure of all escalation.** Her subtype builds deliberate walls as
   shelter. Beats therefore escalate by *threatening, breaching, or dismantling boundaries* — not by raising
   volume or stakes in the abstract. Ask of any beat: which wall does this test, and does it hold?
5. **Her coping should keep getting used as evidence against her.** Her analytical detachment is externally
   indistinguishable from the anti-robot claim that robots only simulate feeling. Wherever it can, the
   persecution should be *fed by* her defense mechanism — she hands them their proof simply by coping the way
   her type copes.
6. **Never let her ask for help until it costs everything.** For a Five, asking is the hardest available act.
   Reserve it; when it finally happens it should be structurally load-bearing (see Finale, "Gather the Team").
7. **Scale escalates with the archetype, per Weiland's own symbolic-setting progression** — Home (Maiden) →
   Village (Hero) → Kingdom (Queen) → Empire (King). The *same* beat means something different at each stage
   because the boundary being tested is a different size: her own autonomy → her competence → her people →
   her identity.

## The 1w9 Self-Preservation Undercurrent (15%) — How It Interacts

Added 2026-09-19 at the author's direction. Checked against the project's own Type 1 material
(`Type_1_The_Reformer/Subtypes.md`). **This undercurrent punches far above its 15% weight** — it supplies
several things her dominant type structurally cannot, and it explains parts of her established biography that
a pure 5w6 could not produce.

### The override mechanism, now structurally grounded

In Hornevian terms her dominant Five is **Withdrawn** and her undercurrent One is **Compliant/Dutiful.** They
pull in opposite directions by design. The Five says *retreat*; the One says *this is wrong and someone has to
fix it.* The 15% voice is the one that conscripts her — and the Self-Preservation One's specific engine is
named outright in the source: **resentment at being the one who always has to fix things** — *"why am I always
the one improving reality, when it would benefit everyone?"* That is not merely compatible with her arc; it is
her arc's emotional core, stated in the framework's own words.

### It explains why the resettlement exists at all

A pure Five facing rising persecution withdraws further — she does not organize anything. But the SP One runs
on **chronic worry: compulsive foresight, an ever-present sense that something could go wrong unless vigilance
is maintained, real difficulty relaxing even when things are objectively fine.** That vigilance is the
mechanism that makes her *see it coming* and act **before** the war rather than during it. Her established
biography has the resettlement already underway when the war breaks out — the undercurrent is why. The Five
built her own shelter; the One is the reason she started building shelter for other people too.

### The "third option" is a One's construction

A One refuses morally unacceptable options and builds the correct one. Fighting is wrong; submitting is wrong;
therefore there must be a third thing, and it must be right, and it must be *for everyone.* A pure Five would
simply have left quietly and alone. **Her defining political act is her undercurrent's, not her dominant's.**

### Her anger has three separate locks on it

This is the single most important craft consequence. Stacked:
- **SP Five:** displeasure surfaces as withdrawal and silence, never confrontation.
- **SP One:** anger is *most fully repressed* of the three One subtypes — Reaction Formation converts its heat
  into warmth, making an underlying angry person outwardly gentle, tolerant, supportive.
- **1w9 specifically:** what little escapes surfaces as **stiffness, impatience, or sarcasm**, never direct
  confrontation.

So she is carrying an enormous quantity of unexpressed anger with essentially no channel for it. **Treat her as
a pressure vessel.** Any moment where it genuinely escapes is catastrophic and structurally expensive — ration
those with extreme care, and never spend one casually.

### It solves the leadership problem

A question the dominant type alone can't answer: how does an intensely private, low-communication, withdrawn
Five become someone an entire population rallies behind? **The One's Reaction Formation.** Her outward
gentleness and supportiveness are real on the surface *and* are partly converted anger. People experience her
as safe, steady, and generous — which is true — while the Five behind it hoards everything. She is warm
*because* she is furious and cannot be.

### It makes the ending unforgivable — to her

A pure Five could rationalize the Treaty signature as the correct calculation under impossible constraint: the
math was right, the alternative was extermination. But the One holds a standard and judges the self against it,
and the SP One in particular has **real confidence in her own standards — she rarely doubts the standard
itself, only whether it's being met.** So she cannot take the exit the math offers. **She will never forgive
herself, regardless of whether the decision was correct.** This is the engine of her Elder-stage grief (see the
Archetype Translation doc) and the reason the ending cannot resolve clean.

### Per-beat implications

- **Catalyst / Break into Two:** the undercurrent is the *actual* reason she crosses. The technical hook gets
  her hands moving (Five), but the moral wrongness is what makes retreat impossible (One).
- **Debate:** the two voices should be audibly distinct — the Five budgeting the cost, the One refusing to
  accept that cost as a valid reason. The Debate is an *internal argument between two types*, not a single
  hesitation.
- **Bad Guys Close In:** the SP One's stress behavior is **oscillation between strict self-denial and periods
  of excess — bingeing on comfort, then swinging back to austerity to "undo" it.** This is a concrete,
  observable behavioral tell for her worst stretches (needs robot-appropriate translation; this universe's
  established consumption/vice culture for robots is the natural vehicle).
- **All Is Lost / Dark Night:** the One's inner critic is what makes the low point unendurable. The Five could
  survive loss by withdrawing; the One won't let her stop prosecuting herself.
- **Any beat requiring her to lecture, moralize, or advocate openly:** **forbidden.** 1w9 is "more idealistic
  than activist — happier explaining their ideals than doing the dirty work of persuading or organizing." She
  acts on the wrongness while saying almost nothing about it. Her morality is demonstrated, never argued.

### B-Story option

Per the author's suggestion, the undercurrent can additionally be externalized: **the B-Story is the natural
place to carry the "but was it right?" question consciously**, since her dominant type analytically suppresses
exactly that question while her undercurrent silently screams it. A relational thread (husband, robot friend)
that keeps asking the moral question out loud lets the reader hear what she will not say — and per the fused
structure's own requirement, the B-Story must seem unrelated until it converges at the Midpoint, which works
precisely because she'd never acknowledge the connection herself.

## Beat-by-Beat

### Opening Image (0%)
- Must show her **already inside** a bounded, mastered domain — her boundaries already built and working,
  never their construction. (Activation is never dramatized; established decision.)
- Must read as **sufficiency, not deprivation.** The reader has to see a life that genuinely works, because
  everything downstream is a loss, and a loss requires something worth losing.
- Must contain a **visible boundary** — the composition should have an inside and an outside.
- **Anti-constraint:** must NOT open on her being mistreated. Opening on victimization makes the whole arc
  reactive; opening on contented sufficiency makes it tragic.

### Theme Stated (~5%)
- Must be delivered by someone she **does not consider a competence-equal.** Her Expert role means she filters
  advice by credential — the theme should arrive from someone easy to dismiss, so it can return later with
  force.
- Must be phrased as **practical advice about the work**, never about her emotional life. Anything framed
  emotionally trips Isolation and she'll analyze instead of hear.
- Content axis should touch **scarcity vs. abundance** — the belief that her reserve is finite.

### Set-Up (0–10%)
- Must make her **internal economy visible**: what she spends, what she hoards, what she refuses outright.
- Must establish the **art as private** — nobody has seen it (canonical Five "Preparation Mode").
- Must establish at least one relationship she genuinely values **but underinvests in**, so the later cost is
  legible rather than asserted.

### Care Package (within Set-Up) — *Bell*
- Must be something she **did not ask for and cannot reciprocate.** Accepting unearned giving is precisely what
  the scarcity model forbids; the discomfort of receiving *is* the beat.
- Should be **actional or practical rather than verbal** — she discounts verbal warmth, but can't argue with a
  thing someone did.
- Must be **small enough to accept without triggering full withdrawal.** Too large and she refuses it, which
  kills the Q-Factor payoff later.

### Catalyst (~10%)
- Must be an **intrusion across a boundary, not an invitation.** She declines invitations; she has to be
  breached.
- Must be something **her competence is uniquely suited to address** — otherwise she delegates and withdraws.
  The hook has to be baited with a technical problem.
- **Anti-constraint:** must not require her to feel something immediately. It should require her to *solve*
  something, with the feeling arriving later, uninvited.
- (Per the shared framework, this is the one event permitted to be coincidental — so make it count.)

### Debate (10–20%)
- For a Five this is **not "do I have the courage?" but "what will this cost, and do I have it to spend?"**
  The refusal must be budgeted, not frightened.
- The cost must be **concretely externalized** — time, privacy, the workshop, the relationship — not abstract.
- Her withdrawal here must read as **an active position, not absence.** Silence as argument.

### Break into Two / Doorway of No Return #1 (~20%)
- She must cross **not by choosing engagement but by running out of wall.** A Five doesn't volunteer; the
  walls have already been breached and retreat is simply no longer available.
- **Stronger alternative worth considering:** she crosses to protect *someone else's* boundary — the first
  deliberate expenditure of her reserve on another person. This seeds the integration arc earlier and makes
  the crossing active rather than passive.

### B-Story (early Act 2 → converges at Midpoint)
- Must carry the **relational/emotional material she cannot access directly**, since the framework requires it
  seem genuinely unrelated until convergence.
- Natural home for the husband(s) and the robot friend — feeling can be dramatized *there* without requiring
  her to narrate it.

### Kick in the Shins (early Act 2) — *Bell*
- Must be a **technical failure, not a social one.** Something breaks that she should have been able to fix —
  striking the Expert role where it actually lives.
- Must stay internally consistent and plot-relevant (per Bell), never a random setback.

### Road of Trials / Fun and Games (20–50%)
- The premise being delivered on is **competence deployed at scale** — the pleasure of this stretch is watching
  her be extraordinarily good at something.
- **Critical constraint:** the trials must be *solvable by mastery.* This is where the Lie still works. She has
  to keep winning via the old strategy, or its eventual failure means nothing.

### Pet the Dog (near Midpoint) — *Bell*
- Must involve **spending her own reserve on someone weaker, with no return** — the precise thing avarice
  forbids.
- Must be done **privately**, or she'll rationalize it as strategy and the beat won't land.
- Must carry real plot consequence (per the definition).

### Midpoint / Mirror Moment (50%)
- The "death" that resonates for her is **psychological or professional, never physical.** She is functionally
  unkillable on human timescales and doesn't fear bodily death the way a human protagonist would.
- The Mirror Moment question that actually bites: **not "who am I?" but "the strategy that has always worked
  is now the thing destroying me."**
- **If the False Victory reading is used here** (see the Alignment doc — placement still undecided): the win
  must be *completely genuine*, and must be the exact thing that marks her.

### Self-Revelation (flexible, 2nd half of Act 2) — *Truby*
- Must strike the **scarcity belief** specifically.
- **Cannot be delivered by insight alone.** A Five can think her way around any idea. It has to arrive through
  the body or through an irreversible event — something Isolation cannot process into commentary.

### Bad Guys Close In (50–75%)
- Must run **both axes simultaneously** (external persecution + her own flaws catching up), per the source.
- Her internal axis must specifically be **her withdrawal actively costing other people** — the moment her
  boundary becomes someone else's wound.
- Must **dismantle her retreat options one at a time**, brick by brick, not all at once. Each removal should be
  survivable alone and unbearable cumulatively.

### All Is Lost (~75%)
- Must take **the reserve itself** — not merely a loss, but the specific hoarded thing. The workshop, the
  privacy, the one relationship, the art.
- The traditional "whiff of death" needs **robot reframing**: the strongest available version is something
  **unrepairable**, because repair is her entire identity. The horror isn't death, it's a thing she cannot fix.

### Dark Night of the Soul (75–80%)
- The beat where **Preparation Mode has nothing left to prepare** — she is out of information, and information
  was always the security.
- She must be **genuinely alone** (her home turf) and it must **fail to comfort her** — the first time in her
  existence that solitude doesn't work.

### Moral Decision (within Dark Night) — *Truby*
- Must be built around her **Moral Need** — the cost her withdrawal imposes on others — and must present two
  genuinely equal, genuinely plausible moral options (per the definition).

### Q-Factor (within Dark Night) — *Bell*
- The Care Package returns. Must return **in the register it was given** — a remembered object or action, never
  a speech. Consistency with the setup half is what makes the payoff work.

### Break into Three / Doorway of No Return #2 (~80%)
- The becoming is specifically **integration toward Eight**: decisive action, reclaimed physical presence,
  authority exercised rather than avoided. **This is the beat where she stops observing.**
- Per the framework, A-story and B-story must intertwine — whatever the relational thread taught her has to be
  what makes the solution available.
- Source warning worth honoring: integration toward Eight **surfaces real grief over years of isolation before
  it produces empowerment.** The grief should come first, visibly.

### Finale — the Five-Point Finale (80–100%)
- **Gather the Team:** her version is *admitting she needs people* — the single hardest act in her psychology.
  This should cost more than any other step here.
- **Storm the Castle:** whatever fortification she breaches should **rhyme with the walls she built around
  herself**, deliberately — the beat lands hardest if the thing she forces her way into mirrors the thing she
  spent her whole existence keeping others out of.
- **High-Tower Surprise:** must invalidate **a fact she was relying on**, not merely a plan. Her security is
  informational, so the surprise has to be epistemic.
- **Dig Down Deep:** must be **physical/instinctual**, not cleverer thinking. Thinking harder is the old
  strategy; this beat has to refuse it.
- **Executing the New Plan:** must require **spending the reserve completely** — nothing held back, which is
  the inverse of everything she has ever done.

### Closing Image (final 0–1%)
- Must **mirror the Opening Image in inversion.** If the Opening is her inside a bounded domain she controls,
  the Closing shows her **outside, in the open, with no boundary at all** — which the established ending (the
  exile march to Antarctica) already delivers structurally.
- Should echo the Opening's **physical composition** deliberately, with inside/outside reversed.
