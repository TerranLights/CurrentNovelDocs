## What the author has said (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20)
- **Ricardo Alarcón is from Baja California.**
- **He defected from HyperHedral Industries there.**
- He was **betrayed by HyperHedral**, ultimately **left and fled HyperHedral and Baja California**, and went to **California under a new, assumed name**.
- **"Ricardo Alarcón" is not his real name.** Nobody in the events and setting of *Streetside Harmonies* knows this.
- This book will **detail and depict his personal background**: how he came to be betrayed by HyperHedral, left it, and fled.
- The author wants help with it here, in its own novel folder.
- **Languages:** **all notes in this repo folder are in English** (American English, the repo-wide rule). **The finished, published book will be in Spanish, specifically Mexican Spanish.**

## The story so far (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20; "things I'm able to say for sure"), in rough order
**Before the book opens**
1. **Childhood:** he grew up **very poor in a town in the south of Baja California**, and showed great promise throughout his youth.
2. **Early college-aged years:** **HyperHedral picked him up**, and he was **mentored by a man who was also known to Alonso Laurentino Reyes** (who appears in the *Tales from the New Southwest, Vol. 1* collection).
3. **His career:** most of his working life was spent **in the northern half of Baja California, working for HyperHedral**.
4. **When the book opens, he is already married and has a daughter.**

**In the book**
5. **His daughter grew up unable to talk, and was special-needs.**
6. **In her teenage years**, through his connections at HyperHedral, he **brought home a robot to live as his daughter's sister**, the sister she never got to have.
7. **The gift:** his daughter made her robot sister a gift: **a seashell from the beaches of Baja California, combined with a small metal gear and some computer parts**, put together to **symbolize nature and technology combined**.
8. **Much later in the book, HyperHedral kills his wife, his daughter and his "robot-daughter"** (his daughter's sister). **He discovers it when he comes home one day.** *The reason HyperHedral does it is not yet decided (OPEN).*
9. **He takes the necklace** his daughter gave her robot sister, as a memento of his family. **This is the necklace he eventually gives Valerie in *Streetside Harmonies*** (Ch 37).
10. **He tracks down whoever ordered the hit on his family, confronts him, and kills him.**
11. **With the help of another robot, he acquires a new identity**, so he can pass borders and take transportation without being flagged and tracked down by HyperHedral.
    - **That robot is someone not yet named** (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20). **Ísabel Camila Bóndar is reserved for the CRPG** (InnerTepeniaGDD), so she is not this robot.
    - **What she has to be (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20):** a female robot **with access to government systems and databases**, able to **implant false information about his background** so that "Ricardo Alarcón" looks like **an established person with a real history**, and nobody gets suspicious when his name is pulled up in a **database, a search, or a plain ID scan**.
12. **The ending:** he makes his way into California. Someone asks his name, and he says **"Ricardo Alarcón."** Earlier in the book, the two names **"Ricardo" and "Alarcón" appear separately, in different places, associated with people and things he really enjoys.**

## Checks against *Streetside Harmonies* and the world (CHECK; for the author to confirm)
- **The necklace fits Streetside as written.** Ch 37 stz 5 has Ric lift "t/necklace from above t/door" and put it on Valerie, "careful to link t/chain"; the outline calls it "his daughter's necklace", and in his eyes the robot-daughter was his daughter. The Streetside text could describe it (a shell, a small gear), which would let a reader of both books recognize it. Ch 37 also already has Valerie's own gift to him, a figure made from scrap metal bits, which he sets **over the front door, where the necklace hung** (stz 3, 7-8).
- **His wife is Lucía** (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20): the same Lucia whose birthday Ric keeps in Streetside (Ch 21 stz 10, "it's Lucia's b-day; M: his wife"; the author's appended note: "that Lucia **was** Ricardo's wife"). The Streetside transcription spells it "Lucia" without the accent, as on the handwritten page; the author spells it **Lucía**.
- **"Not his real name" covers only him.** His wife's and daughter's names would be their real ones.
- **"Borders" in the escape.** On the author's post-war map, **Baja California and Baja California Sur are part of Cascadia**, as is California (`worldbuilding/nations/New North America - Territory Definitions (read from map).md`; the Sonora profile also says "Baja California is Cascadian"). So, unless his era's borders differ, the trip from Baja to California crosses no national border. The "borders" could be internal checkpoints, a route through another country, or a different border in his era. OPEN.
- **Why Ric uses a proxy in Streetside (AUTHOR, 2026-10-20).** It isn't that "Ricardo Alarcón" fails a check. **Anyone ordering siligel that consistently, for that long, would draw HyperHedral's attention**, so the orders have to be placed by **someone HyperHedral would see as trivial** and would never bother to investigate. That is the name-proxy of Streetside Ch 18 stz 5-6 ("name-proxy's blown; can't order under his own name"). CHECK: in Streetside Ric supplies more than one robot owner on credit (Ch 1 stz 4-6, "take all you need; pay when you can"), which is exactly the steady, long-running volume that would stand out. OPEN: who the proxy was, and how it got "blown"; Ch 28 stz 8 has an associate later scoring a new supply.
- **Timing.** In Streetside (2149) he is an established Santa Rosa mechanic, a "grizzled viejo", and a long-time friend of Craig, so this book ends some years before 2149. The exact era is OPEN.
- **Robots are legal property in this era**, so the robot-daughter is legally HyperHedral's or his property; how that matters (if at all) is the author's call.
