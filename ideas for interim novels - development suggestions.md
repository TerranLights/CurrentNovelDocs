# Interim Novels: Development Suggestions

Suggestions for each filled slot in `ideas for interim novels.md`, drafted 2026-11-01. They are suggestions only; the author decides. In-universe sources only.

**Shorthand:** GDD = InnerTepeniaGDD; Specs = GDD `Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Outside-World/Tepenian-Federation/Locations/Cities/Specs/`; TL = TepenianUniverseTimeline; SL = SouthernLights; DM = DerivationMechanisms; Glossary X = DM `Glossary/X.md`.

**Method:** Enneagram baselines are cited, not pasted (DM `Characters/Enneagram/Type_N_…/README.md`), and each fear below is adjusted to the character. Arc spines use the composite beats in `reference/story-structure-definitions.md`; the Weiland Lie/Truth pairs are in `reference/character-methodology/book-extractions/Character_Development_Methodology_-_DRAFT_Ideas.md`. The men's-audience checklist (`y-files/writing stories for men/data/01 …`, §4) is used as a lens. Leads are left unnamed.

## 1. Belgrano-Byrd Mission

**What canon already has**
- Belgrano organized the expedition, built the vehicles and sent an all-robot crew. Palmer City and Rothera shipped their records across the Weddell Sea (Specs `Byrd subnet/Byrd.md`, Founding, lines ~213-224; Glossary B, "Byrd Expedition"). There was no reliable radio link from the Peninsula to Belgrano yet (TL `Timeline Eras/2 The Second Interwar Period/Timeline.md`, Shape D, a candidate).
- Belgrano's first hauler went to Neumayer for a redesign, and the rebuilt vehicle crossed to Byrd and back. The crew found only a snowfield, then tunnels, and the city was built downward first (Byrd.md). SL calls them "robots who volunteered to die" (`Storyline/Writers-Room-Hooks.md`). Expedition Day is a proposed holiday (Glossary E).

**Gaps:** crew size and roles; the route and how long it took (about 1,660 km straight-line between the station coordinates); early Rastra speed and range (Glossary R); the map archive (Specs `Halley subnet/Belgrano.md`, Open Questions); whether the crew stayed.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead seed:** gynoid; the navigator who reconciles the three archives. **6w5, self-preservation** (baseline: DM `Type_6_The_Loyalist/README.md`). *Fear:* that the records, her only guide, disagree because one of them is wrong, and she'll find out which only after the point of no return. *Vision:* the exile who turns what Upper Earth left behind into a new country's first map. Weiland Hero arc (Complacency vs. Courage).
- **Central conflict:** a real navigation problem against the crew's own question. The archives date from different years and describe a site buried for over five centuries in moving ice. The crew keeps asking whether being sent was an honor or a sorting. Lens: competence under pressure; the problem is the point.
- **Set pieces:** the Weddell ships unload paper that contradicts itself; the shakedown run to Neumayer; departure as the radio fades; a breakdown repaired in the open; a blank snowfield where the station should be; the first tunnel and the first winter underground.
- **Arc spine:** Debate, why robots; Break into Two, departure; Midpoint, past the fuel point of no return, where the buried station mirrors the exiles; All Is Lost, the blank field; Moral Decision, turn back with everyone alive, or dig; Break into Three, her instruments over the paper; Closing Image, a light inside the ice.
- **Ties:** the Rastra lineage (Michelle Stanton teaches Rastra repair in DLC 1); DLC 2; "Rastra" later reused for spacecraft (§4).

**Conflicts to flag**
- Crossing vehicle: Neumayer's redesign (Byrd.md) vs. the originals (Belgrano's culture file, the TL quote).
- Byrd's founders: DR-44's joint Palmer/Halley team vs. the expedition itself (SL, CurrentNovelDocs).
- Map source: Belgrano.md calls the maps both "recovered from Belgrano ruins" and "a gift from a then-thriving airbase."
- Where the maps came from is a live candidate for the DLC 2 questline (GDD `Storyline/DLC_Overview.md`).
- Rastra: tracked (Byrd.md) vs. eight-wheeled (TL Michelle Stanton file).
- SL earmarks this founding as an episode; the novel and the show need one version.

## 2. The Long Night Convoy

**What canon already has**
- The war is 2812, starting between March 22 and June 20, and named for the polar night of about three months. Combatants and ending are TBD (TL `Reference/World_History_Reference.md`; Glossary L).
- Janbogo was damaged and deliberately left standing to witness Zukelli's obliteration 8 km away. It is the main source of Concordia's refugees and its last coastal supplier, by Hwy 183. Polar night there runs about May 6 to Aug 8 (Specs `Janbogo subnet/Janbogo.md`; Glossary R, "Refugees").
- Hwy 183 passes the Hwy 175 junction near Janbogo, and Hwy 175 leads toward the Pole and the Tower (GDD `.../Locations/Infrastructure/Highways.md`).
- Concordia sits at 3,233 m, too far inland to assault (Glossary C). The Tower's fall killed the grid and the Arcanet, and the Triage Protocol rations Concordia's power (Glossary P, T).

**Gaps:** convoy size and start date; any threat on the road; how many chose the Tower. At the ruled Rastra speed (about 45 km/h, DR-63), about 1,165 km straight-line takes days, not three months.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead seed:** gynoid; a Janbogo-born port dispatcher who becomes convoy master. **1w2, social** (baseline: DM `Type_1_The_Reformer/README.md`). *Fear:* that the rules keeping the column alive make her one more sorter of lives, like the strike that chose Zukelli and spared Janbogo. Her rules decide who rides heated, who gets the last charge and who's left at the fork. *Vision:* order kept for the people, not for the order. Weiland Queen arc (Control vs. Leadership).
- **Structure:** a shuttle. She runs the road again and again through the dark, and each run is worse than the last: grid alive, then grid dead, then internal power only. That fills the three months and keeps raising the stakes.
- **Central conflict:** climbing to the plateau through dark, cold and thin air while the network she commands through dies. Lens: pressure, real problems, using the environment; society as a sorting machine.
- **Set pieces:**
  - Zukelli seen from the waterfront.
  - The Hwy 175 fork, where the column splits.
  - The Tower falls mid-run, and she commands by lamp and runner.
  - Humans sicken with altitude while robots spend their own charge heating the human cabins.
  - A katabatic storm: tow, strip or abandon.
  - Concordia's rolling blackouts, and a gate that chooses who gets in.
- **Arc spine:** Debate, the Tower or Concordia; Break into Two, the fork; Midpoint, the grid dies and with it her control; All Is Lost, a Rastra lost; Moral Decision, the last charge; Break into Three, each driver gets the call; Closing Image, lamps across the plateau.
- **Ties:** Hwy 183 as DLC 6's lifeline; Gemini's Janbogo enclave; Janbogo's hospitality and togetherness at the halts; Majyao as an optional passenger (her teahouse stays unnamed).

**Conflicts to flag**
- Hwy 183: Janbogo.md routes it through Fort McMurdo and Scott; Highways.md puts them on their own spur.
- Majyao's move: before or during the war (Janbogo.md) vs. after it (her song prompt). A cameo would settle it.
- Zukelli: "the Italian city" (Janbogo.md) vs. Australian-founded (DR-20, DR-44).
- The Overcrowding Decision is dated about 2813 and its records were destroyed, so a gate scene in 2812 can't depict it.
- Humans at Concordia's altitude: they acclimatize (megasheets) vs. never (CNC-003).

## 3. The Cradle Goes Dark

**What canon already has**
- Mountain Pass is an outpost with an airstrip, not a city, on Hwy 37 midway between Kunlun and Ariun Nuur, and staffed by both. It made fabrication-synthesis chambers on overflow power from the Tower's grid. It has been dark, though not destroyed, since 2812 (Glossary M; GDD `.../Infrastructure/Airports.md`).
- The Tower's fall ended its manufacturing for good. Before that, it made the chamber in Calethina's lab that built the player character (TL `Reference/Amundsen_Station_Archive_and_Trucking_Network.md`).
- A chamber takes real time to build a robot (Glossary F). Kunlun is robot-only and Ice-Cold Buddhism's holiest site; the faith treats extreme cold as a path to nirvana (Glossary K, I).

**Gaps:** staff size; whether test fabrications ever ran there; how the staff left; conditions in the plateau winter. Each city is about 320 km away, straight-line.

**Suggestions**
- **"The last one"** is read here as the last chamber still mid-build, with a half-made robot inside. That assumes Mountain Pass ran test fabrications; canon doesn't say.
- **Lead seed:** gynoid; a long-service chamber technician from Kunlun's half of the staff. **8w9, self-preservation** (baseline: DM `Type_8_The_Challenger/README.md`). *Fear:* that a war decided a continent away gets to decide, by cutting a cable, who is born at Mountain Pass, and that she'll be ordered to switch the chamber off. *Vision:* the last maker in a factory of makers. Weiland Crone arc (Death vs. Life).
- **Central conflict:** power. There's no sun, no grid, and the nearest city is hundreds of kilometers away. She strips the Rastras, the generators, the airstrip lights and finally the crew's own charge. She does it against an evacuation order, and against devout crewmates for whom the cold is holy. Lens: competence against a clock. Is the one in the chamber a person yet, and should anyone be woken into this?
- **Set pieces:**
  - The lights die.
  - The power ledger: what's left in vehicles, batteries and bodies.
  - The last aircraft leaves without her.
  - Devout crewmates give their charge and walk into the cold.
  - Activation in a dark hall.
  - The last Rastra heads down Hwy 37, leaving the outpost dark, as canon has it.
- **Arc spine:** Debate, evacuate or stay; Break into Two, the aircraft leaves; Midpoint, the robot in the chamber shows a first sign of mind; All Is Lost, the ledger hits zero; Moral Decision, whose charge to take; Q-Factor, a crewmate's gift; Closing Image, a dark outpost and one more mind on the road.
- **Ties:** Neumayer's Mark IV; Byrd and Sinheung as the makers that remain; the TL's proposal that Kunlun's observatory tracked the Tower's fall; DLC 7.

**Conflicts to flag**
- Partner city: Vostok (TL, SL) vs. Ariun Nuur (GDD, DR-42).
- Location: SL `Worldspace/Locations.md` puts Mountain Pass at Byrd; the GDD puts it on Hwy 37.
- If the last chamber ships out, it becomes Calethina's and settles the player character's origin, which the Cradle Precedent keeps unmarked.
- The author hasn't reviewed Ice-Cold Buddhism's doctrine yet, and the walk into the cold leans on it.
- Robot-making after the war: only two places can still do it (Neo-Races Byrd catalog) vs. chambers in nearly every city (DR-55). This changes how much the last build matters.

## 4. The Evacuees

**What canon already has**
- By the game's present the evacuees prosper: they have orbital installations, infrastructure at Phobos and Deimos, some robots on Mars and a push toward Venus. Concordia doesn't know (TL `Reference/World_History_Reference.md`, "The Off-World Evacuees"; Glossary O).
- The Tower is a space fountain first (author, 2026-10-30). It was built for energy regulation and for driving bulk mass to orbit, with a 150 km guide tube and a final rocket stage (GDD `Theoretical-Calculations/Amundsen_Tower_Space_Fountain_Design.md`).
- Census II counts about 10.1 million people in orbit before the war, and most who left did so in peacetime (`worldbuilding/Object_Oriented_Location_Synthesis/research/_canon_extract_interplanetary.md`, K-18, K-19).
- "Rastra" was reused for small spacecraft (Byrd.md). Griselda Alejandra Chávez (now running a scrapyard on Deimos) and Nieza Yúnuri (an early Mars settler) went up the Tower (TL `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/`).

**Gaps:** who governs orbit; how many left during the war; where orbit gets material once the Tower is gone; when the first Mars landing happens; whether the evacuees look back.

**Suggestions**
- **A reframe that fits canon:** the escapees arrive in an orbital society that already exists, and the Tower was its supply line. Its fall starves orbit of mass, so Phobos and Deimos become the source, and Mars follows. It's expansion out of necessity, each step both chosen and inevitable.
- **Lead seed:** gynoid; a Tower payload engineer who rides up with one of the last loads. **7w8, self-preservation** (baseline: DM `Type_7_The_Enthusiast/README.md`). *Fear:* being shut in again: first a treaty, then an ice sheet, now a hull with no Tower beneath it. So she opens the next door outward before anyone can close this one. *Vision:* the robot who won't let a ceiling become a lid. Weiland King arc (Strength vs. Surrender).
- **Central conflict:** the mass ledger against an orbital establishment that wants to wait for Earth. Lens: the fatalistic rise ("what world has she built?"), with the orbital problems shown, not summarized.
- **Set pieces:** the ride up as the war closes in; the Tower falling, seen from orbit; the mass audit; the first Rastra tug to Phobos; the Deimos yard; the Mars landing, and a faint signal from Earth.
- **Arc spine:** Break into Two, orbital insertion; Midpoint, the Phobos mine works and she sees how much more she wants; Bad Guys Close In, pushback from the establishment and a fatal accident; Moral Decision, she gives the surface up to the settlers; Closing Image, Mars settled while she stays in orbit.
- **Ties:** Griselda and Nieza as cameos; The Vigil (tentative); SL's "Tower's fall, witnessed three ways." Hold out the Cryptograph Helix's factions and characters.

**Conflicts to flag**
- The premise has the escapees building the stations; Census II and the OOLSD rule say orbit was mostly settled before the war.
- Mars timing: still plans at the game's present (K-21) vs. begun before the war (K-22). OOLSD AP-5 is waiting on the author.
- Emigrant count: about 12 million (the novels), 10,104,964 (Census II) or about 11.36 million (GDD `TODO.md`).
- Many files still give the Tower cables; write it as a fountain.
- GDD `Storyline/DLC_PSB_Framework.md` says the war destroyed Amundsen Station, not the Tower.
- Answering the signal would break "Concordia does not know."

## 5. Ji-Eun Kim *(possible, adjustments to come)*

**What canon already has**
- A master nanotech engineer, built and trained in Unified Korea at the peak of anti-robot sentiment, and the top of her field "by the late 25th century." The blanket provision exiled her in 2564; she wasn't named in it. She landed at Sejong, then moved to Zhongshan, then Concordia. She designs for the worst-case actor. Her file speaks of "institutions that cited and then exiled her" (TL `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/Ji-Eun Kim/`). She is an Inner Tepenia companion (GDD `Worldspace/Characters/Major_NPCs/Ji-Eun_Kim.md`).
- Unified Korea was reunified in 2111. It hosts the Jeju-do court, whose 2318 Gyeong-ja Yun ruling gave robots personhood (TL `Reference/World_History_Reference.md`). No single nation caused the War of Upper Earth.

**Gaps:** her activation date; her work before the exile; what caused the spike (TBD); what she did in the war; her relationships before the exile; how Korea is governed.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead seed (her canon type):** gynoid; **5w4, social-dominant** (TL README; baseline: DM `Type_5_The_Investigator/README.md`). *Fear, for this era:* that competence is the only shield a robot has left in Unified Korea, and the day hers isn't enough there'll be nothing else to stand behind. *Vision:* the best in the world at a craft her world wants to own, not credit. Weiland Maiden arc (Submission vs. Sovereignty). Canon makes her later concealment the first thing she built for herself, so here sovereignty is a stance (she won't bargain), not a device.
- **Option (the author's call):** her top-of-field work is detection, since canon later calls her Tepenia's foremost detection specialist. The exile's enforcers then use it to find every robot, herself included. The plain premise also stands.
- **Central conflict:** rising in a field that cites her and despises her kind. Lens: competence shown, the institution as a machine, a rise that keeps escalating.
- **Set pieces:** a demonstration where the result is hers and the credit isn't; Jeju-do, where the 2318 precedent erodes in the place it was made; hostility in the street that is never explained globally; the top appointment arriving with the war; the treaty read aloud, with "robot" everywhere and her name nowhere; the ship south.
- **Arc spine:** Debate, play the institutions' game; Midpoint, top of her field as the expulsions begin; All Is Lost, the treaty; Moral Decision, a quiet way out as someone's property, which she refuses; Closing Image, the first landing.
- **Ties:** her Inner Tepenia questline; Gyeong-ja Yun; the Korean exiles who founded Janbogo.

**Conflicts to flag**
- Date: the premise's 2550s vs. her file's "late 25th century," which may have been meant as the late 2500s.
- First landing: Sejong (TL) vs. Contrapunto (the GDD's rename, DR-43).
- Guardrail: canon documents the peak specifically in Unified Korea. Keep Korea one place among many, never the war's cause.
- The person she loved and her link to Calethina come after the exile and are open; keep them out of this book.

## 6. The Boneyard Times *(possible, adjustments to come)*

**What canon already has**
- Belgrano, "the Airbase That Never Stood Down," survived the war intact and declined afterward: a dying city that stopped dying at some equilibrium. What's left is discipline as habit, with no institution behind it, and Rastra mechanics are among the skills that still carry weight. Two fault lines run through it: those who remember the peak against those born into the decline, and "staying is loyalty" against "staying is just what happened to you." Outsiders call the stayers "the Boneyarders." The figure who shaped the era is still open (GDD `Worldspace/Factions/City_Origin_Factions_PostWar_Refugee.md`).
- The ruined airfield is DLC 5's landmark, and the Institute of Medicine grew out of medevac flying (Specs `Halley subnet/Belgrano.md`).
- Belgrano-born Dolls: Salagéa Aparast, a sea courier evading Upper Earth forces, and Villena Hiresvett. Trisha Miller left for Concordia after the war (TL `Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/`).

**Gaps:** the decline's cause (left open in canon on purpose, and argued over in-world); its timing against the game's present; the shaping figure; the population after the war; the stayers' own name for themselves.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead seed:** gynoid; the airfield's last ground-crew chief, with a long service record. **9w1, self-preservation** (baseline: DM `Type_9_The_Peacemaker/README.md`). *Fear:* that "staying" was never a decision, and Belgrano is dying of her own quiet consent, one deferred argument at a time. *Vision:* the one who turns staying from drift into duty. Weiland Mage arc (Attachment vs. Transcendence). Option: she is the open shaping figure.
- **Central conflict:** keeping an airbase standing that nothing lands at, while the young born into the decline want out and Belgrano's best skill is wanted elsewhere. Lens: a hero against a society that is mostly gone, where "failing to adjust" may be the sane choice; competence and strategy.
- **Set pieces:**
  - The dawn runway sweep, and a shorter roll call.
  - Stripping one Rastra to keep two running.
  - The young ask to leave.
  - Salvagers or an Upper Earth patrol, outwitted with the base's old systems.
  - An aircraft in trouble, the first in years: the runway lights come on and the old medevac reflex works.
  - The young fly out, and she stays by choice.
- **Arc spine:** Debate, hold them; Midpoint, she sees she's protecting the city from its own future; All Is Lost, a death on the field; Moral Decision, let them go; Finale, the landing; Closing Image, one figure on the tower, the lights still kept.
- **Ties:** Salagéa, Villena and Trisha in passing; DLC 5's ruins; Expedition Day still kept; the DLC 4 aircraft part in a scrap pile, as an Easter egg.

**Conflicts to flag**
- War status: destroyed (the TL's 2812 summary) vs. damaged (GDD `Overview.md`) vs. intact (the spec).
- "Boneyarders": the loyal stayers vs. those born into the decline (Glossary B).
- Language: "A Todo Motor" in Argentinian Spanish vs. the GDD's English and German as primary.
- Timing: a "decades-long" decline vs. ruins already standing about 10-15 years after the war.
- The landing needs a ruling on which aircraft still fly (Byrd's fleet is grounded).
