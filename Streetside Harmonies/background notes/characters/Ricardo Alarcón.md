## His right hand (AUTHOR, 2026-11-20)

**What the reader sees:** Ric doesn't have full use of his **right hand**. It is mostly healed, but:
- his **far fingers don't bend the way they should**;
- he has **difficulty gripping** things;
- his **fingers have difficulty moving independently** of each other.

**Where it shows:** first in **the opening repair scene**, while he repairs Valerie's arm; then **slightly, in every other scene he is in, wherever it makes sense**; and **most of all, and most urgently, in the Dark Night of the Soul**, when he makes the emergency repairs on Valerie after the Tesla coils' electroshock (Chapter 29, "Valerie under emergency repairs"). The repair scene opens in the **Prologue** (`text/000 Prologue.md`, already written in verse) and continues in Chapter 1, so the Prologue is the reader's first sight of Ric.

**The cause stays in his own book.** In his origin story he breaks the hand killing the HyperHedral executive who ordered the hit on his family, then bolts the bones back together himself while escaping the compound. *Streetside* shows only the effect. If anyone asks, he deflects, and the answer never points to HyperHedral or to his name.

**Notations in the chapters (2026-11-20):** every chapter below with a hand beat now ends with a "**NEW 2026-11-20: Ric's right hand**" note in its `01_adjusted_chapter_layout` file (Ch 1, 16, 18, 20, 29, 34, 35, 37). The stanza text itself is unchanged. The Prologue has no layout file and its verse is finished, so its notation is only here.

## How he works around it (AUTHOR, 2026-11-20)

**The note:** ideally **early on**, and then **throughout the book**, show things Ric would normally do with his right hand alone, or with both hands, that he has to **improvise** some other way because he doesn't have full use of his right hand. Each one has to **fit naturally and realistically in the context of the situation. Nothing forced:** it happens inside an action the scene already has, never a moment staged to display the hand. Nobody needs to comment on it.

**Why it works:** the work-arounds read at first as a mechanic's habits and quirks. Only later does the reader put them together. Years of living with the hand have made them automatic, so he never fumbles for them; he just does them.

### Chapter 29: the Dark Night of the Soul
The layout already has the pieces. The hand turns them into stakes:
- **stz 2:** "Ric frantically opens V; instructs C to fetch cables & wooden/plastic items." Soon Craig isn't only fetching. Ric needs **Craig's hands** for the fine work, and talks him through it: *hold this, there, no, the other one, now turn it.* Craig, who makes things with his hands, becomes Ric's fingers.
- **stz 4:** "Ric is frantically working fast." His hand slows him down: the grip slips, and he has to stop, shake it out, and start again. He's angry at the hand, not at anyone else.
- **stz 5:** "Ric rushes to collect parts & tools together." He fumbles a small part; Craig picks it up.
- **stz 6:** "Ric removes V's scalp and begins attaching cables to her 'insides'." **The single hardest moment for the hand:** small connectors deep inside her head. He steadies his right hand with his left, or straps a tool to it, or hands the connector to Craig.
- **stz 7:** "Ric is writing up a list." The writing is slow and cramped, hard to read.
- **stz 8:** "Using parts, he fixes a fair amount of V." Time passes, and so does the toll on him: he soaks or rubs the hand between jobs.
- **stz 10:** "Ric making finishing fixes." The last delicate work is the slowest.
- ***Save the Cat* (Dark Night):** the mechanic tells her, "Not to worry, EG7-KV42. We'll bring you back online. I promise you." The line isn't in the layout yet; it fits stz 4 or 6, said while his hand is failing him.

**Why it works:** it raises the Dark Night's stakes without a word of backstory. The one man who can fix her is working against his own body, and it lets Craig save her too, with Ric's knowledge in Craig's hands.
