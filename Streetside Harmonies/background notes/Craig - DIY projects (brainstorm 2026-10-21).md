## 1. What his DIY has to show (from his files)

- **Beauty first, function last.** Passions: "beauty, exoticness, stoicism" (Craig bio). Hobbies: pre-war vinyl, antique and second-hand shops, "painting and making other sorts of art".
- **The wound in the work.** His wounding message is "It's not okay to be too functional or too happy" (Craig bio). So in Act 1 his own projects keep avoiding usefulness. The author's Ch 7 stz 3 already says it: "a pretty thing that seems nice but ultimately leads nowhere".
- **The flaw.** "Has difficulty seeing things through to fruition; focuses on what he and the gang don't have." Core weakness: envy. Inciting event: "making negative comparisons" (Craig bio). The unfinished projects can sit next to Martin's finished ones with nothing said.
- **The Sensualist.** "His desire for emotional intensity can have a tendency to interfere with basic life functioning." He can lose the coffee to a project.
- **The skill.** "Very, very good at improvising, reacting to uncertainty when life throws a curveball." In the squat that skill becomes survival.
- **Withdraws** (Hornevian variant, Craig bio). He works at night and alone: Ch 10 stz 12, Ch 26 stz 9.
- **Poor, in 2149 scarcity Cascadia.** He spent his inheritance on Valerie (timeline; Ch 15 stz 5). Materials are salvage, second-hand, pre-war, household scraps.
- **Mostly for Valerie, and never inside her body.** His DIY stays outside her: cups, tins, rolls, cables, beds, lanterns. Her nodes, coolant, siligel, weight, glowing eyes, ocular shutters, overclocking and flight mode are all on the page. Nothing below adds to them.
- **Distinct from Martin.** Martin fixes antique clocks (Martin bio), builds from the "Book of Shapes" (Ch 29 stz 1 and 5) and ends building instruments (Ch 30 stz 2-3, Ch 38 stz 6). Craig never fixes a mechanism or builds an instrument. He decorates, salvages, improvises and composes.
- **The arc in objects.** His moral need: "rededicate some of the time he spends on accumulating collectibles over to actually completing something so the band can succeed; set up positive, constructive routines." End-of-arc redemption: inspiration gets through "if your daily schedule and living space are arranged in ways that support your creativity" (Craig bio). Psychological need: "find a home he can love."
