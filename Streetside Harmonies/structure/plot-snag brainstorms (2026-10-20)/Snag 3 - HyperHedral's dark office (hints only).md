### E1. Nobody needed the lights (a robot-staffed depot whose workers are gone)

**The explanation.** HH ran the site with its own company-owned robots, fitted with the low-light or thermal optics Valerie doesn't have.
- They never needed lights.
- They never came or went: they worked, charged and ate there. That is why the clerk has never seen anyone in or out.

At some point HH removed the units: sold, reassigned or shut down, and the book never says which. Everything else was left running: the charging bays, the job scheduler on the screens, and the workers' siligel.

**Breadcrumb trail.**
1. **Ch 21 stz 5 (already on the page):** "any chance they fitted you w/ thermal vision?" / "just ordinary optics." Alone, it is a joke while hunting drafts.
2. **Ch 22 stz 5:** the clerk adds, "Never seen a light on in there, either." Alone, it is a creepy-building remark.
3. **Ch 22 stz 9-17:** Martin flips the switch by the door and nothing happens. He looks up: the ceiling fixtures have no bulbs, and the sockets are clean. They work by the motorcycle's headlamp shining through the open door.
4. **Same scene, the cradles:**
   - Along the back wall stands a row of cradles, each with a stenciled number and a cable coiled on a hook.
   - Craig lifts one clip. It is the same kind of node he attaches to Valerie every night (Ch 7 stz 12).
   - He hangs it back exactly where it was.
5. **Same scene, the screen:** the one lit screen shows a column of those stenciled numbers, each beside the same blinking word, "AWAITING." The brothers can't do anything with it.
6. **Ch 24 stz 5:** alone at night, Valerie takes "a single box."
   - On its end, someone has written a number in grease pencil, the way people label a lunch.
   - She traces it with a fingertip, then eats "sparingly" (that word is on the page).

**Deduction test.** A careful reader concludes:
- robots worked here in the dark, lived here, and are gone;
- the power is on because nobody switched off the bays and the scheduler;
- the "abandoned" siligel was their food.

Where they went is left open; the reader may fear the worst, given Valerie's terror of being shut down. **No character acts.** The brothers take what fits on the bike, and Valerie never learns where her siligel came from.

**What it shows / sharpens.**
- **Robots as property, without a speech:** a workforce removed, its food left behind.
- **Valerie's arc:** her core fear mirrored in a row of cradles she never sees.
- **Ric's backstory:** a silent motive for an HH man to defect, which the reader can supply.
- Neither of them is mentioned, so it **does not pull focus**.

**Cost or risk.**
- A darker undertone. Keep the units' fate unreadable: "AWAITING", never "RETIRED".
- Low-light optics are a small extension of Ch 21 stz 5, not new technology.
- There is no public reveal, so it fits the lore's timing.
- The cache ends on its own: a crew's rations are finite (Ch 34 stz 5).
- **Answer to (a):** Ric may not know this site, or may know it and avoid it. Either works, and neither is said.

**Cohesion hooks.**

- The buried story here is about robots too, so the robot stays at the center.

## 3b. Flagged: explanations that would force plot (not recommended)

- **A honeypot.** HH leaves the stock and a camera system on to catch gray-market buyers. The reader would expect the trap to spring, which is a consequence, and that means plot.
- **A tracking relay, or a robot registry that locates units.** It puts Valerie on a map, and the reader waits for HH to arrive.
  - A *passive* ownership registry (records only) would be usable.
  - But it leans on PROPOSED lore, the maker-run serial registry.
- **Any sign the theft is noticed:** a new padlock on the second trip, a camera that turns. Either one starts a clock.
- **Linking the cache to the Fair electrocution** (see E9). It assigns blame and demands a reckoning.
- **Tying the office to Ric's late daughter.**
  - The material: "ever since they took..." (Ch 28 stz 3, Martin to Rebecca, not yet proofread) and the "late daughter's necklace" (Ch 37).
  - Making the office part of that loss turns Ric's grief into a mystery to solve.
  - Leave it alone unless the author has already decided that backstory.
- **Wiring HH into the house plot.** For example, HH in the eager clerk's pocket (Ch 6; Ch 17 note: "a few [enemies]; mostly government"), or Mr. Morrow buying HH land. This makes the office part of the spine's antagonism.

## 4. Combinations

- Risk: it is also the heaviest backstory, so every beat must stay wordless.

- The tariff is shown on objects ("HELD", the peso sticker).

- Light tone, and low risk.

## 5. Shortlist (top 3)

1. **E1, nobody needed the lights.** It explains the defining image (dark but powered) more directly than any other option, and it uses a line already on the page (Ch 21 stz 5's thermal vision). It also makes the clerk's "never seen anyone in or out" literally true, and deepens both Valerie's fear and Ric's motive without either being mentioned.
2. **E2, the tap that ran dry.** It closes the loop on every Ch 18 clue: the interrupted shipment, the blown proxy, "can't go himself to HH". It also turns a random clerk's tip into a quiet, satisfying circle, carried by one wordless gesture from Ric.
3. **E3, priced in pesos.** It shows the robot tariff through objects, which the author's Ch 18 note says still needs doing (and Snag 1 needs too), and ties the cache to Valerie's foreign-currency hope. *Runner-up: E4, for the cleanest mirror of the "securing a home" spine.*
