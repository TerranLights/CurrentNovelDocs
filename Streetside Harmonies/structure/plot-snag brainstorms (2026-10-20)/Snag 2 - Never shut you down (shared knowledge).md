## 1. The snag in one paragraph

In Ch 21, Valerie is low on energy after the move into Finley (stz 9). Ric can't charge her because it's Lucia's birthday (stz 10), the building has no power (candles, stz 11), and the siligel supply is in trouble (stz 12-13). She says she "just needs to rest"; Martin asks, "can't just power down for now?"; her eyes flash (stz 13). She clutches Craig so hard he can barely breathe ("...babe..."), loosens, and Craig says, "I'll never shut you down" (stz 14). Martin looks very surprised; Craig asks whether she'll go into flight mode, "like when hitching or under repairs w/ Ric," and she agrees (stz 15). The trouble is that Craig has had her about eight months, and the book has already shown this fear and this promise: Ch 7 stz 11 reads "C reassures her; he'll never shut her down; meant to charge her." When Craig says it again in Ch 21, it plays as news to someone who has heard it before. The stz 15 gloss "like when hitching or under repairs w/ Ric" does the same thing: Craig explains flight mode to the robot who has been through it twice onstage (Ch 1 stz 2-3, Ch 11 stz 3). It's the same kind of slip as the one the author caught in Ch 18 stz 4: after eight months Craig wouldn't ask "are you okay?"

## 2. What the solution has to do

- **Keep the terror real.** Being shut down is death to her. It's her core fear (being trapped in pain and deprivation) made literal, and the book returns to it at Ch 7 stz 11, Ch 21 stz 14 and Ch 28 stz 2. Her knowing Craig won't do it doesn't have to stop the fear. It can be a reflex, or it can be about something else.
- **Only Martin doesn't know.** This is the main lever. Martin was out with Rebecca during the Ch 7 panic (Ch 7 stz 4-7 are M and R; stz 8-13 are C and V at home). His bio says he often stays over at Rebecca's. In Ch 1 stz 8 he "doesn't get flight mode" and would "freak out if he were conscious during surgery." So a line in Ch 21 can only legitimately be news to **Martin**. Either it's aimed at him or it isn't said at all.
- **Martin's suggestion is kindness by human logic.** Someone who'd want anesthesia (Ch 1 stz 8) offers her what he'd want. That isn't callous, and the solution shouldn't make him a villain.
- **The building takes away the usual answers.** There's no outlet, no Ric and not enough siligel. If she runs dry, she shuts down whether anyone chooses it or not. Flight mode is the only conservation left, and Craig's skill is improvising when life throws a curveball (Craig bio).
- **Stz 15 stays her moment of trust** (the author's tentative view, 2026-10-20). Whatever replaces the line, the scene still has to end with her choosing to be immobile and alone while they go, which means depending on them (her wound: "it's not okay to depend on anyone for anything").
- **Respect the three states already on the page:** charging-sleep (she dreams and wakes when unplugged: Ch 4 stz 1-2, Ch 7 stz 12, Ch 31 stz 12); flight mode (code words, aware but still: Ch 1 stz 3, Ch 11 stz 3, Ch 22 stz 1, Ch 23); shutdown (off). Add no new technology, with one small optional exception (Option 7).
- **Fix Ch 7 too, or at least make it the plant.** Ch 7 stz 11 is the first time the promise appears onstage, and it already sits inside the eight months.
