## 1. The snag in one paragraph

The bonsai is in the plan but not in the story. Valerie's arc gives it two beats: Rescue from Without ("Craig teaches her the value and importance of focusing on one thing // somehow related to the bonsai tree") and Sign of Transformation ("Learns to focus on one thing by caring for a bonsai tree, which she learns from Craig"). ## 3A. The bonsai itself

### Two choices to make before picking an option

1. **What the tree says about Craig.** (a) **It is the exception.** It is the one thing he has kept up for years, and that works for a Four because a bonsai is never finished. His bio flaw is "difficulty seeing things through to fruition", and a tree that is only ever refined, never completed, is the one project that flaw cannot kill. (b) **It is the casualty.** He is a Self-Preservation "Sensualist" whose "desire for emotional intensity can have a tendency to interfere with basic life functioning" (Craig bio), so under stress he forgets to water it. A6 uses (b). The others use (a).
2. **Species, which sets what the tree can do.**
   - **Deciduous**, e.g. a vine maple, native to the Pacific Northwest and so at home in Cascadia: red in autumn, bare all winter, buds in late winter. This enables A2 (a bare tree looks dead and isn't) and A7 (a dormant hardwood cutting is a winter technique).
   - **Evergreen**, e.g. a coast redwood. Santa Rosa is redwood country, and a redwood resprouts from a cut stump, which suits Alan's epitaph, "tireless rebuilder" (environ notes). It stays green through the eviction, but there is no bare-winter beat.

   A bud in Ch 38 needs late winter by then. The story's span is unstated (CHECK). If it is still midwinter, the payoff is a swelling bud, not a leaf.

Real bonsai practice used below: wire is left on for weeks to months and must come off before it cuts into the bark. Repotting with root-pruning is done in late winter. Scratching a dormant twig with a thumbnail shows green (alive) or brown (dead).
