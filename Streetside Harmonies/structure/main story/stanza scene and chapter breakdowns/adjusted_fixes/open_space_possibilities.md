# Open space: possibilities (PROPOSED; the author decides)

**Started 2026-11-15, 00:40.** This is outside the 05:00–14:59 worldbuilding window, so everything here uses only world facts **already in the outline**. Where a possibility needs a *new* Cascadian detail, it is written as a **World question** to answer in the next window, not invented here.

## The two goals, and one way to hit both at once

1. **Valerie's flaw on the page (moral need):** through her helpful, random nature she tries to do good for Craig and ends up partly ruining his plans, **and she doesn't realize it.**
2. **More of the world:** new chapters and details that show what life in Cascadia, 2149, is like.

**The idea that joins them: let the world be the mechanism of the backfire.** Her help is innocent by human standards. What turns it into harm is a Cascadian rule she doesn't know or understand: robots aren't legal persons, they travel as cargo and pay tariffs, codes change "every month or two", siligel is scarce, and so on.
- **The detail becomes necessary, not decoration.** The reader learns the rule because it costs Craig something.
- **Her blindness becomes believable.** She isn't careless; she's a robot in a society whose rules about robots she was never taught. Craig, eight months in, absorbs the cost without explaining, so she never finds out.
- **It fits the men's-audience lens** (`writing stories for men/data/01`, §1.2 "man versus society" and §3.1 "the problem is the point"). Society is a real, working machine with its own logic. It isn't a backdrop, and the problem has to be solved, not just felt.

## Possibilities, by slot

### Ch 19 (new chapter, Road of Trials, about 12–14 stanzas)
**What the slot inherits:**
- Ch 18 stz 9: "C says they have no choice but to explore their options here (in S.R.)."
- Ch 20: they're kicked out, and in Ch 21 they end up squatting.

So Ch 19 is Craig's plan for where they'll go next. If Valerie's help is what sinks that plan, **she is the unseen reason they end up in Finley**. That is strong evidence for the moral need, and she never knows it.

- **19-a "The lease" (high):**
  - Craig finds a rental they can just afford.
  - Wanting to help, V fills in the application herself and lists herself as a co-tenant and income earner.
  - **Mechanism:** a robot isn't a legal person, so she can't sign or count as income. Worse, declaring a robot puts the unit under rules for "property".
  - The application dies, and Craig tells her it "just didn't work out".
  - **World question:** what does a Santa Rosa lease, or landlord law, say about robots?
- **19-b "The trade" (medium–high):**
  - Craig plans to sell part of his vinyl collection to a dealer for moving money.
  - V, sparing him the pain of parting with them, trades them herself for something she's sure he'll love more. This echoes her "trade you!" in Ch 4.
  - **Mechanism:** a barter economy and the value of pre-war goods. She trades a fortune for a trinket.
  - **World question:** how do money, barter and pre-war collectibles work in 2149?
- **19-c "The permit" (medium):**
  - Craig applies for a street-performer permit, so they can earn legally (the Ch 2 fine).
  - V performs in the office to show the clerk how good they are.
  - **Mechanism:** a robot performer may need an operator license, a fee or a declaration. Her demo puts her on record.
  - **World question:** how is busking licensed, and how are robot performers classed?
- **Combinable:** 19-a as the spine, with 19-c as one stop on the way. Two stanzas of the city between stops would show post-war Santa Rosa (the 05:00 window).

### 23-B (BGCI, 8 stanzas, "Adjusting to homelessness / squatting")
**What the slot inherits:**
- Ch 23 ends with V reactivated and "perfectionizing": organizing, sorting, arranging.
- The beat sheet has the desk left under the leak (the setup for the H-G in Ch 24).

- **23B-a "Lights on" (medium):**
  - The brothers' system is to keep the squat looking empty: windows dark, front untouched.
  - V cleans the windows, hangs her metal art at the front and makes it homey.
  - **Mechanism:** a building that looks lived-in draws a patrol, a notice or the HyperHedral meter reader (E4). The risk becomes constant for the rest of the act.
  - **World question:** what are the penalties and patrols for squatting, and who checks a written-off building (E7)?
- **23B-b "The perfect order" (low–medium):**
  - Her reorganizing undoes Craig's and Martin's careful layout: the buckets, the rain plan, where things are padded.
  - This quietly sets up the H-G ending up exposed in Ch 24, so her help early on feeds her bigger accident later.
  - World payoff: the physical texture of a quake-damaged, written-off building.

### 25-B (BGCI, 5 stanzas, "C&V playing without M; a goal they try for and fail at")
**What the slot inherits:**
- After Ch 25 she has spent "all of it" on store credit for Martin, so money is gone.
- The H-G is ruined, so the act has lost its drone.

- **25B-a "Filling the drone" (medium):**
  - The goal is to earn back money.
  - V tries to cover Martin's missing part by improvising something new every song. The act loses its anchor, people drift away, and the take is nearly nothing.
  - The world shows through who stops, who doesn't, and what a winter street pays.
- **25B-b "The crowd-pleaser" (medium):**

  - **Mechanism:** the crowd blocks the sidewalk, and the same kind of cop as in Ch 2 fines them. They take a loss.
  - **World question:** street-performance rules.
