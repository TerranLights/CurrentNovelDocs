# Valerie: more possibilities for showing her arc

## What these build on (all already settled)
- **Flaw and needs:**
  - Type 7 (stress → 1 critical/perfectionist, growth → 5 focused), social subtype ("missing out": scatters, half-commits, pencils in backups).
  - **Moral need:** respect other people's time; she fills their schedules because that's caring, and it hurts them. **She doesn't realize how her randomness causes pain** (author, 2026-11-15).
  - **Psychological need:** find joy in the ordinary; focus on one thing.
- **Arc rules (author):**
  - She interrupts others' plans **only until the Tesla Coil shock** (Ch 28 stz 4).
  - After it she's terrified of new things and interrupts no one, **but hasn't learned to focus**. That lesson completes only at the **Closing Image** (Ch 38).
- **Other fixed points:**
  - She can't lie (Ch 18).
  - "Shut down" means Alan's death in her vocabulary.
  - She doesn't know Alan is dead until the grave. Use that irony twice at most; nothing below uses it.
  - Eight months with Craig: his reactions are practiced and never explained.
  - No other squatters. The record exec is cut. The bonsai is a real tree with no wire.
  - Robot facts come only from the files.
  - She's in flight mode on hitchhike rides (beat sheet), so road backfires happen at stops, not in cars.
- **The world as the mechanism of a backfire** (see `../main story/stanza scene and chapter breakdowns/adjusted_fixes/open_space_possibilities.md`). New Cascadian details are written as **World questions** for the 05:00–14:59 window.

## A. The cost she never sees (hidden causes; pre-shock)

1. **"Who's it for?"** · Ch 16 (C&V visit Ric), or Ch 1 · **high**
   - A courier brings Ric's proxy siligel shipment while V is there. Asked who it's for, she can't lie, and she cheerfully explains it's for Ric, who fixes her.
   - Two chapters later: "Name-proxy's blown; can't order under his own name" (Ch 18 stz 5). Nobody tells her.
   - **She is the unseen cause of the shortage** that drives Ch 18 → flight mode (Ch 22–23).
   - *Shows:* honesty plus helpfulness turning into harm. Keeps clear of Ric's own past, which is another book.
   - *World Q:* how proxy siligel orders and couriers work.
2. **"The message she cleared"** · Ch 10 blank stz 13–14 (the night before the trip) · **very high**
   - "So it's fresh when we're back," she resets the {{{Holoputer}}} for her games and clears what it had stored.
   - Mr. Morrow's attempt to reach them about a deal (beat sheet, "Battle / Kick in the Shins") goes nowhere, and they lose the chance to keep the house.
   - *Near:* the first list's #4 "The Notice Nobody Signed For" (check).
   - *World Q:* what the Holoputer is; how messages reach a house in 2149.
3. **"The night before the viewing"** · Ch 19 · **high**
   - She keeps Craig up past 3 a.m. with an idea she can't wait to show him. He arrives at the lease viewing late and frayed, and the place goes to someone else.
   - *Shows:* a robot's time against a human's. Combines with 19-a "The lease".
4. **"The full day"** · Ch 19 · **high**
   - Craig has one appointment that matters. Because she cares, she fills the hours around it with four treats for him, and the treats make him late.
   - *Shows:* the moral need in its purest form, cramming someone else's schedule.
5. **"Free concert"** · Ch 19 (with 19-c "The permit") · medium
   - She plays for the line outside the permit office to cheer everyone up. The office counts it as an unlicensed performance, and the permit is refused.
   - *World Q:* street-performance rules.
6. **"Three projects, one night"** · BGCI before Ch 28 · medium
   - She overclocks to finish three gifts in one night and burns days of siligel. Craig quietly does without something he needed (strings, coffee) to cover it.
   - *Shows:* overclocking as literal cramming.
   - *Near:* "Rationing Day" and "The last cup" (both about rationing, not overclocking).
7. **"The boat fare"** · `15a` (the Bay crossing on the way home; the Golden Gate is in ruins) · low–medium
   - To save them money, she offers to play for their passage. The boatman takes the deal on his terms: they play the whole crossing, then the return run, and miss their connecting ride.
8. **"The souvenir stop"** · `15a` (a stop between rides) · medium
   - She talks them into a ruin for pre-war bits, the Keysight habit. They miss the ride and walk in the rain.
   - *Near:* the first list's #23 "The Ruins of San Jose" (that's on the way south).

## B. Nobody is allowed to be sad (a new thread: the 7 avoids pain, at the cost of other people's grief)

9. **"The trip home"** · the **spine of `15a`** · **high**
   - She has just learned at the grave what "shut down" really means.
   - She copes by filling every stop of the trip home with something to do, so nobody can be sad. Martin wanted silence; Craig wanted to talk about Alan.
   - By Santa Rosa, none of them has grieved, and she thinks the trip went great.
10. **"The walk-through"** · Ch 20 (the eviction) · **high**
    - Craig wants one slow last walk through the house his father built. She turns the move into a game and a race to make it fun, and he never gets his goodbye.
    - *Shows:* a 7's cheer against a 4's need for meaning.
    - *Near:* batch 2 H "Salvage Priorities" (what to keep, not the goodbye).
11. **"She shutters"** · a through-line
    - **Pre-shock:** whenever Craig's face falls, she closes her ocular shutters (the snapshot mechanism, Ch 7 stz 8) and reopens on something new. She literally doesn't look at the hurt. Craig learns the click.
    - **Post-shock:** she can't choose what to look at anymore ("Post-shock" #11).
    - **Ch 38:** eyes open in the rain.
    - *Shows:* "doesn't realize" made physical.
12. **"Playing for the rain"** · Ch 38 (a reading of the Closing Image)
    - She plays *for* the rain instead of to chase it off: the first time she lets a sad thing simply be.
    - *Bookends:* #9 and #10.

## C. Glimpses of focus (the 7 → 5 seed she doesn't yet keep)

13. **"The only time she sits still"** · a through-line
    - She focuses completely only while making something for someone: Alan's statue (Ch 10 stz 2), Rebecca's present (Ch 7 stz 9), Ric's statue (Ch 37), "eyes glowing, intent" (Ch 36, old 36.5). Craig notices; nobody says it.
    - At the end she plays for the rain with the same stillness, the music as a gift.
    - *Shows:* her growth is already inside her generosity.
14. **"One minute, like the drip"** · `23b` · low
    - She tries to get back the calm she found in flight mode by sitting still one minute like the leak. She lasts seconds, laughs, and moves on.
    - *Shows:* the psychological need, and her own suspicion that the calm was good.
15. **"The rain song, unfinished"** · a through-line
    - The melody she built from the leak (Ch 23) she starts again and again, and drifts into new tunes every time (busking, `25b`).
    - Post-shock she won't play it.
    - Ch 38: she plays it start to finish, for the rain, and C&M join.
    - *Builds on* Snag 4 B11.
16. **"The whole side"** · a through-line (the circle of teaching: Craig teaches Valerie focus)
    - Craig's ritual is to listen to a whole album side without skipping (Garmarna, Ch 4/7). Early she can't get past track two. Later she lasts longer.
    - Ch 38: she listens to, or plays, the whole thing.
    - *Complements* batch 3 A #10 "Tracks by dice".

## D. Open doors and backup plans (the social 7's "missing out")

17. **"Both."** · Ch 18 stz 9 / Ch 19 · medium

18. **"The propped door"** · `23b`, or Ch 24 · medium
    - She props every door in the squat open because she can't stand a closed room, and the cold gets in.
    - An alternative cause for the cold that leads to the ruined H-G (batch 3 C #7 "Her cracks" is the other).
19. **"The ring that moves"** · Ch 29 → 38
    - After the ring, she keeps moving it, finger to finger, hand to hand (scatter). At Ch 38 it stays on one finger.
    - *Rides* the ring chain: the part from Ch 10, the ring in Ch 29, worn in Ch 35.

## E. Honesty with an arc (answers the working copy's P7, "honesty has no arc")

20. **"The honest busker"** · pre-shock busking · medium
    - A listener asks if they're any good. She answers too honestly ("Martin flubbed that, but it's okay!"), and Martin's pride and the tips go.
    - *Near:* the first list's #19 "Teaching Out of Turn" (check).
21. **"Is that true?"** · after any hidden setback (#3, #4, 19-a) · low
    - Craig says "it just didn't work out." She asks, "is that true?" He doesn't answer, and she lets it go: she suspects, but doesn't look (ties to #11).
22. **"I don't know"** · post-shock
    - Asked if the show will work, she can't promise, because she can't lie, and says "I don't know." It's the first time her honesty is quiet and kind instead of a blurt or a weapon (Ch 25's "nuclear option").

## I. World texture that also shows her

    - She hears the slur (Mr. Whitley's word, Ch 3 stz 6) and adopts it cheerfully as a game word. Craig flinches.
    - *Shows:* her blindness to pain, pointed at her own.

    - *World:* robots as cargo (canon).
34. **"The price tag"** · pre-shock, at a store
    - She reads a siligel price aloud and cheerfully converts it into how many records Craig could buy, unaware that he has been quietly selling records to feed her. Pairs with 19-b "The trade".
    - *World Q:* siligel prices.

    - She asks permission before every small thing. It looks like the respect she lacked, but it's fear. Craig misses her old interruptions.
36. **"The coil in the doorway"** · Ch 31–34
    - Marcus's younger son, the Jazz cats' Tesla Coil player, sets up a coil. She watches from the doorway and can't go closer: novelty now means danger.
    - *Near:* "Post-shock" #11 and #15.
