## The ending (AUTHOR, 2026-10-25)
- **The film's ending stays ambiguous.** The author's reason: as they understand it, we don't know how the epic actually ends.

**What the research shows** (CHECK, from `research/01 - Original epic play-by-play (George 2020)/`). The reason holds in part, and the decision stands either way:
- **The standard version's ending survives intact** (`SB Tablet XI ...md`, scene XI.17, XI 319-328).
  - Gilgamesh returns to Uruk with Ur-shanabi and tells him to climb the wall and look at the brickwork and the city's acreage.
  - These lines repeat the prologue (I 18-23) almost word for word, and the poem stops there.
- **The ending is still open in four ways:**
  1. **The Old Babylonian ending is lost.** The older version's last surviving scene is the boatman and the punting-poles (`Old Babylonian fragments (chapter 3).md`, OB.40-OB.41). Nothing of Uta-napishti, the Flood, the plant or the homecoming survives from it.
  2. **The epic never narrates Gilgamesh's death.** That comes only in the separate Sumerian poem "The Death of Gilgamesh" (`Sumerian 5 ...md`). Even there, the surviving lines stop before the death itself: he lies down, and a gap follows.
  3. **Tablet XII contradicts the epic.** It was attached to the twelve-tablet series, and in it Enkidu is alive again and goes down to the Netherworld. George says it is not part of the epic, and its purpose is debated (`Sumerian 4 ...md`; George, p. 134).
  4. **The meaning is never stated.** The poem closes on an image, the wall, not a verdict. It never says what Gilgamesh feels or has learned.
