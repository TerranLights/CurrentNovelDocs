# Tablet VIII. The Funeral of Enkidu: play-by-play
Research notes, 2026-10-25. Source: George 2020, chapter 1. A record of the original only; nothing here is adaptation or decision.

Pages 61-68. Preserved: VIII 1-72, 84-188, 208-218. The tablet ends with about thirty more lost lines (George, p. 68).

**Line numbers:** taken from George's margin numbers, read from the EPUB markup (the extracted text shows each margin number only as a bare `>`). Ranges between printed numbers are counted line by line, and "about" marks a count that cannot be pinned to a printed number. Several long or doubled verses in this tablet make the line count and the printed lines disagree (George explains this, p. lv), so some ranges below are approximate.

## George's synopsis (p. 61)
Gilgamesh delivers a great lament for Enkidu. He calls in his craftsmen to make a funerary statue of his friend, and picks from his treasury the grave goods Enkidu will take to the Netherworld to win the favor of the gods who live there. A great banquet is held as part of the wake, and then treasures are offered to the Netherworld gods and displayed in public.

George's introduction adds (p. lii) that Enkidu's funeral and wake in the latter part of this tablet can be read as the ideal model of the mortuary rites held before a Babylonian noble's burial.

## Scenes

### VIII.1 The lament, part 1: Gilgamesh calls on all things to mourn (VIII 1-41, pp. 61-62)
- **Where and when:** "at the very first glimmer of brightening dawn." The place is not named. ⚠️ How long after VII.14 is not stated, because the end of Tablet VII is lost. Enkidu's body is within reach (VIII 57-58).
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks to Enkidu.
- **Play-by-play** (the long list of mourners is condensed into groups, in the order the text gives them):
  1. At first light Gilgamesh begins to mourn his friend.
  2. Gilgamesh → Enkidu, his origins: his mother was a gazelle and his father a wild donkey; wild asses reared him on their milk and the beasts of the wild taught him the pastures.
  3. Wish: may the paths of the Forest of Cedar mourn him day and night without pause.
  4. Wish: may the elders of Uruk and the crowd who blessed the two of them mourn him.
  5. Wish: may the peaks of the hills and mountains mourn (the next line is lost apart from "pure"); may the pastures lament like his mother.
  6. Wish: may the myrtle, cypress and cedar mourn him, the trees the two crept through in their fury.
  7. Wish: may the wild animals mourn him: bear, hyena, panther, cheetah, stag, jackal, lion, wild bull, deer, ibex, all the beasts.
  8. Wish: may the sacred river Ulay, whose banks they walked, and the holy Euphrates, whose water they poured out as libation from skins, mourn him.
  9. Wish: may the young men of Uruk, who saw the two of them fight and kill the Bull of Heaven, mourn him.
  10. Wish: may the working people who fed and praised him mourn him: the ploughman in his furrow, who sings his name; someone in Uruk (lost) who spreads his name; the herdsman, who gave him milk and butter; the shepherd boy, who gave him ghee; the brewer, who gave him ale; the harlot, who anointed his head with sweet oil.
  11. Broken wishes: someone in the wedding house (lost); others (lost) who are to mourn him like brothers and let down their hair like sisters, and weep for him, his mother and father (broken).
  12. Gilgamesh: this very day he himself will mourn him.
- **Specifics:** dawn; "your mother, a gazelle," "your father, a wild donkey"; wild asses' milk; the paths of the Forest of Cedar; "teeming Uruk-the-Sheepfold"; its elders and crowd; hills and mountains; pastures; myrtle (conjectural), cypress, cedar; bear, hyena, panther, cheetah, stag, jackal, lion, wild bull, deer, ibex; the "sacred river Ulay" (glossary, p. 224: the river Karun in Khuzistan, classical Eulaeus); the "holy Euphrates"; libation from skins; the young men of Uruk; the Bull of Heaven; the ploughman and his "sweet yodel"; the herdsman, milk and butter; the shepherd boy and ghee; the brewer and ale; the harlot and sweet-smelling oil; the wedding house; brothers and sisters; loosened tresses.
- **Gaps:** "[began mourning]" (VIII 2) is restored. Conjectural: "did raise," "did teach," "peaks," "myrtle," "his furrow," "in his cow pen" and "myself." "[asses]," "[of the wild]," "[without pause,]," "[mourn you!]," "[when he]," "[forth]," "[who]," "[the shepherd boy]," "[in the house]," "[you,]," "[like]," "[May they weep]" and "[On this]," "[day]" are restored. Italic (uncertain): "cheetah," "jackal," "butter," "lips," "brewer." VIII 12 is lost apart from "pure"; VIII 25, 35-38 and 40 have lost words.
- **Repetitions:** "May ... mourn you" opens nearly every line; many wishes add a "who/whose" clause recalling what the mourner shared with Enkidu. "Teeming Uruk-the-Sheepfold" recurs (VIII 9, 25).

### VIII.2 The lament, part 2: Gilgamesh before the young men and elders (VIII 42-56, pp. 62-63)
- **Where and when:** same occasion; a public address.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks first to the young men and elders of Uruk, then to Enkidu.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh → the young men and the elders of Uruk: listen to him.
  2. Gilgamesh → them: he will weep for his friend Enkidu and wail bitterly, like a hired mourning woman.
  3. Gilgamesh, lamenting: the axe at his side that his arm relied on, the sword at his belt, the shield in front of his face, his festive garment and his "girdle of delight": a wicked wind rose up and robbed him.
  4. Gilgamesh → Enkidu: he calls him by a triple epithet, twice over (see the quotation).
  5. Gilgamesh → Enkidu: together they climbed the mountains, seized and killed the Bull of Heaven, and destroyed Humbaba, who lived in the Forest of Cedar.
  6. Gilgamesh → Enkidu: what is this sleep that has taken him? He has lost consciousness and does not hear.
- **Specifics:** the young men and the elders of "teeming Uruk"; a hired mourner-woman; the axe, the sword at the belt, the shield; his festive garment; his "girdle of delight"; a wicked wind; wild ass, donkey of the uplands, panther of the wild; the mountains; the Bull of Heaven; Humbaba; the Forest of Cedar; "sleep."
- **Short quotation:** "wild ass on the run, donkey of the uplands, panther of the wild" (VIII 50; repeated at VIII 51 with "my friend Enkidu").
- **Gaps:** "[me!]," "[of teeming Uruk,]," "[mountains,]," "[slew]," "[dwelt in the]," "[of Cedar.]," "[you?]" and "[hear me!]" are restorations.
- **Repetitions:** "Hear me" four times in two lines (VIII 42-43). The triple epithet is repeated in back-to-back lines. George's illustration 9 (p. 63) is captioned with VIII 44. The same epithet returns in Tablet X (see that file).

### VIII.3 Death confirmed; Gilgamesh's grief (VIII 57-64, pp. 63-64)
- **Where and when:** at Enkidu's side, directly after the question about sleep.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh and Enkidu's body. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Enkidu does not lift his head.
  2. Gilgamesh feels his heart; it is no longer beating.
  3. He covers his friend's face as one veils a bride.
  4. He circles around him like an eagle.
  5. Like a lioness robbed of her cubs, he paces back and forth.
  6. He tears out his curly hair in clumps.
  7. He rips off his finery and throws it away as if it were taboo.
- **Specifics:** the head not lifted; the heart not beating; the face covered "like a bride"; an eagle circling; a lioness deprived of her cubs; curly hair torn out in clumps; finery thrown away; "a thing taboo."
- **Short quotation:** "He felt his heart, but it beat no longer." (VIII 58).
- **Gaps:** "[his head]" and "[hair]" are restored; "like" (VIII 64) is conjectural.
- **Repetitions:** three similes in a row: bride, eagle, lioness. The tearing-off of finery repeats the gesture at VII 64 (where the subject is unstated).
- ⚠️ **Pronouns:** "he" in VIII 57-58 shifts between Enkidu (who does not lift his head) and Gilgamesh (who feels the heart) without naming either.

### VIII.4 The statue commissioned (VIII 65-72, then VIII 73-83 lost; p. 64)
- **Where and when:** "at the very first glimmer of brightening dawn." ⚠️ This is the second dawn formula in the tablet; the text does not say whether it is the next day.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh calls out to the craftsmen of the land.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. At first light, Gilgamesh sends a summons out across the land.
  2. Gilgamesh → the forgemaster, lapidary, coppersmith, goldsmith and jeweler: make a statue of his friend in his likeness, such as no one has ever made of a friend before.
  3. Gilgamesh, specifying the statue: his friend's limbs are to be of ... (lost). Then, speaking to Enkidu directly: his eyebrows will be of lapis lazuli, his chest of gold, his body of ... (lost).
  4. Eleven lines are lost.
- **Specifics:** dawn; the forgemaster, lapidary (conjectural), coppersmith, goldsmith, jeweler; a statue in his image; eyebrows of lapis lazuli (eyebrows is italic: uncertain); a chest of gold.
- **Gaps:** "[Lapidary!]" is conjectural. "[of]," "[a statue in his image,]" and "[such as no one before]" are restorations. VIII 70 and 72 end in lost words. VIII 73-83 are lost (three asterisks).
- **Repetitions:** fulfills Gilgamesh's promise at VII 82 to make Enkidu a gold statue. The dawn formula repeats (VIII 1, 65, 92, 213).
- ⚠️ **Change of address:** at VIII 71 the speech moves from "my friend" (third person) to "your eyebrows" (second person).

### VIII.5 Gilgamesh's promise to the dead Enkidu (VIII 84-91, p. 64)
- **Where and when:** after the lost lines; same occasion, as far as the text shows.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks to Enkidu.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh → Enkidu: he will lay him out on a magnificent bed, a bed of honor.
  2. He will set him at his left on a seat of repose, and the rulers of the underworld will all kiss his feet.
  3. He will have the people of Uruk mourn and lament him, and fill the fair people with grief.
  4. After Enkidu is gone, he will let his hair become matted in mourning and wander the wild dressed in a lion's skin.
- **Specifics:** a magnificent bed; a bed of honor; on his left; a seat of repose; the rulers of the underworld; the people of Uruk ("so bonny"); matted hair; a lion's skin; the wild.
- **Gaps:** VIII 84 is wholly restored. Most of VIII 85-91 is restored from the parallel at VII 139-147. The surviving words are "on a bed," "I shall place you," "the rulers of the underworld," "The people," "I shall have mourn," "the people so bonny," "After you are gone," "clad in the skin of" and "the wild."
- **Repetitions:** Shamash's prophecy to Enkidu (VII 139-147), repeated here as Gilgamesh's own promise, in the first person.

### VIII.6 The treasury: grave goods for Enkidu (VIII 92-130, pp. 64-65)
- **Where and when:** "at the very first glimmer of brightening dawn" (the third dawn formula in the tablet); Gilgamesh's treasury.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. At first light, Gilgamesh gets up and goes into his treasury (the whole line is a conjectural restoration).
  2. He breaks its seals and looks over the gemstones: obsidian, carnelian, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl and alabaster.
  3. A long list follows, badly broken, in which almost every line ends with the refrain that he "provided for his friend." The surviving pieces, in order:
     - something skillfully worked;
     - items of gold, one of x+10 pounds and several of x pounds (the numbers are lost);
     - something mounted "between them" in thirty pounds of gold;
     - items described by their thickness, by being large, and as belonging to his waist and to his feet;
     - something of x talents of ivory;
     - something whose handle was x pounds of gold;
     - something "mighty" belonging to its arm;
     - something with a quiver and a handle of one talent of gold;
     - a mace of ivory "in his hand";
     - something with a handle of forty pounds of gold;
     - something three cubits long, and its thickness;
     - something of fine gold;
     - something of carnelian, and an iron rod;
     - something whose holder was a wild bull.
  4. The list ends "for his friend" (VIII 130).
- **Specifics:** the treasury's seals; obsidian (italic), carnelian, lapis lazuli (conjectural), mother-of-pearl (conjectural, italic), alabaster; x+10 pounds of gold and x pounds of gold (x is George's mark for a lost number); thirty pounds of gold; x talents of ivory; a handle of x pounds of gold; a quiver; a handle of a talent of gold; an ivory mace; a handle of forty pounds of gold; three cubits; fine gold; carnelian; an iron rod (italic); a wild-bull holder (italic).
- **Gaps:** the opening line (VIII 93) is wholly conjectural. Nearly every line from VIII 96 to 130 has lost its opening, so most objects are unnamed. VIII 103 and 116 are wholly lost. Italic (uncertain): "obsidian," "-pearl," "skilfully worked," "rod," "holder." The handle weight "[was x pounds]" (VIII 120) is restored.
- **Repetitions:** the refrain "he provided for his friend" closes 21 lines of the list.
- ⚠️ **Whose hand and waist:** "the mace in his hand," "of his waist" and "of his feet" do not say whether they mean Enkidu, the statue, or something else.

### VIII.7 The slaughter of oxen and sheep; meat for the rulers of the underworld (VIII 131-133, p. 65)
- **Where and when:** not stated; follows the treasury list.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh; Shamash (addressed); "they" (unnamed) who carry the meat.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh slaughters fattened oxen and sheep and heaps them up for his friend.
  2. Gilgamesh → Shamash: he is displaying these gifts for his friend.
  3. Someone (the subject is lost) carries all the meat to the rulers of the underworld.
- **Specifics:** fattened oxen and sheep, piled high; Shamash; "all the meat"; the rulers of the underworld.
- **Gaps:** "[oxen]" is restored. VIII 132 ("These gifts I display") is a conjectural restoration. VIII 133 opens with lost words.
- **Repetitions:** none. George's synopsis calls this part of the wake "a great banquet."

### VIII.8 Gifts for the gods of the Netherworld, displayed to Shamash (VIII 134-188, then VIII 189-207 lost; pp. 65-67)
- **Where and when:** not stated; follows VIII.7. Each gift is "displayed to the Sun God," so it is shown under the sun (in public, per George's synopsis).
- **Who:** Gilgamesh; Shamash, before whom each gift is displayed. The named deities are the recipients and are not said to be present.
- **Play-by-play:** each entry follows one fixed formula: the object; whom it is for, with the deity's title; Gilgamesh displays it to the Sun God; then his prayer that the deity accept it, welcome his friend and walk at his side. Eleven entries survive:
  1. **Ishtar,** "the great queen" (VIII 134-138): a throwstick of mahogany, "the gleaming wood."
  2. **Belet-ili,** older sister of Enlil (VIII 139-142): a festive gown (rest of the line lost).
  3. **Ereshkigal,** queen of the Netherworld (about VIII 143-147): a flask of lapis lazuli (rest lost). In the prayer she is "queen of the teeming Netherworld" (restored).
  4. **Dumuzi,** "the shepherd loved by Ishtar" (VIII 148-151): a flute of carnelian (rest lost).
  5. **Namtar,** vizier of the Netherworld (VIII 152-156): a chair of lapis lazuli and a staff of lapis lazuli (rest lost).
  6. **Hushbisha,** stewardess of the Netherworld (about VIII 157-161): the object line is lost, and the name, title and prayer are wholly restored.
  7. **Qassu-ṭabat,** the sweeper of Ereshkigal (about VIII 162-167): something he had made (lost), a clasp of silver and copper bangles. The prayer adds a wish that his friend not (lost) or grow sick at heart.
  8. **Ninshuluhhatumma,** "the cleaner of the house" (about VIII 168-174): something of alabaster, inlaid inside with lapis lazuli and carnelian and showing an image of the Cedar Forest, plus something else inlaid with carnelian. The prayer adds that she do something (lost) before his friend, and the same "sick at heart" wish.
  9. **Bibbu,** the butcher of the Netherworld (VIII 175-179): a double-edged dagger with a lapis lazuli haft and a flintstone from the holy Euphrates.
  10. **Dumuzi-abzu,** the scapegoat of the Netherworld (VIII 180-183): a flask of alabaster.
  11. **A deity whose name is lost** (VIII 184-188): something topped with lapis lazuli and something inlaid with carnelian. The dedication and prayer are wholly restored.
- **Specifics:** throwstick; mahogany; festive gown; flask of lapis lazuli; flute of carnelian; chair and staff of lapis lazuli; clasp of silver; bangles of copper; alabaster with lapis lazuli and carnelian inlay depicting the Cedar Forest; double-edged dagger with a lapis lazuli haft; flintstone from the Euphrates; alabaster flask; the Sun God; "the teeming Netherworld." Glossary entries (George): Belet-ili, "Lady of the Gods," the Mother Goddess (p. 221); Ereshkigal, "Mistress of the Great Earth" (p. 222); Dumuzi, lover and husband of Ishtar, punished with yearly death and descent to the Netherworld (p. 221); Namtar, "Doom," minister of Ereshkigal and angel of Death (p. 223); Hushbisha, "Its Fury is Fine," a member of Ereshkigal's court (p. 222); Ninshuluhhatumma, "Lady whose Hands are Fit for Cleansing," a member of Ereshkigal's court (p. 223); Bibbu, Ereshkigal's butcher and cook (p. 221). Qassu-ṭabat and Dumuzi-abzu have no glossary entries.
- **Short quotation:** "may she welcome my friend and walk at his side!" (VIII 138), the formula that closes every entry.
- **Gaps:** most lines end in lost words after the object. Restored titles: "older sister of Enlil," "the queen of the Netherworld," "the vizier of the Netherworld," "the stewardess of the Netherworld," "of Ereshkigal" (the sweeper), "the butcher" (in Bibbu's prayer). The Ishtar of entry 4 ("loved by [Ishtar]") is restored. Italic (uncertain): "throw-"/"stick," "mahogany," "clasp," "Dumuzi" (in Dumuzi-abzu), "scapegoat," "flask" (entry 10), "a flintstone from." "Depicting an image" (entry 8) is conjectural. Hushbisha's entry and entry 11 are almost wholly restored. VIII 189-207 are lost.
- **Repetitions:** the same four-part formula eleven times. The extra wish that his friend not grow "sick at heart" appears in entries 7 and 8 only.
- ⚠️ **Line ranges are approximate** for entries 3, 6, 7 and 8, where the printed lines and the margin count disagree.

### VIII.9 An unidentified speaker (VIII 208-210, after the lost VIII 189-207; p. 68)
- **Where and when:** unknown; after a gap of about nineteen lines.
- **Who:** George's linking note (p. 68): when the text resumes, the speaker is someone other than Gilgamesh. The speaker and the listener are not identified.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. The speaker says something (lost) involving "we."
  2. Something about "their ..." and "their names" (lost).
  3. A mention of a "judge of the Anunnaki" (the rest is lost).
- **Specifics:** names; "judge of the Anunnaki" (glossary, p. 221: in later periods, the gods of the Netherworld).
- **Gaps:** each of the three lines survives only in fragments. George gives no note on the content beyond the change of speaker.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VIII.10 Gilgamesh thinks of damming the river (VIII 211-212, p. 68)
- **Where and when:** directly after VIII.9.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh hears what the unidentified speaker said.
  2. He has the idea of damming the river.
- **Specifics:** "the river" (not named); damming.
- **Gaps:** "[the idea]" is restored, and "damming" is italic (an uncertain rendering). ⚠️ The text does not say what the damming is for or which river is meant.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VIII.11 Dawn: the table displayed to the Sun God; the lost ending (VIII 213-218, then about 30 lines lost; p. 68)
- **Where and when:** "at the very first glimmer of brightening dawn" (the fourth dawn formula in the tablet); at Gilgamesh's gate.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh, before the Sun God. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. At first light, Gilgamesh opens his gate.
  2. He brings out a great table of *elammaku*-wood.
  3. He fills a carnelian bowl with syrup.
  4. He fills a lapis lazuli bowl with ghee.
  5. He decorates something (lost) and displays it to the Sun God.
  6. The rest of the funeral, about thirty lines, is lost. George says these lines are "yet to come to light" (p. 68).
- **Specifics:** dawn; the gate; a great table of *elammaku*-wood; a carnelian bowl filled with syrup; a lapis lazuli bowl filled with ghee; the Sun God.
- **Gaps:** "his gate" (VIII 214) is conjectural. "*Elammaku*" is italic: an untranslated wood name, printed in George's italics. VIII 218 has a lost word. About thirty closing lines are lost.
- **Repetitions:** the dawn formula (VIII 1, 65, 92, 213). Displaying to the Sun God repeats the gift ritual of VIII.8.

## Elements list

### Characters (epithets as given)
- **Gilgamesh:** compares himself to a hired mourner-woman (VIII.2), an eagle and a lioness robbed of her cubs (VIII.3). VIII.1-VIII.11.
- **Enkidu (dead):** the son of a gazelle and a wild donkey, reared by wild asses (VIII.1); "wild ass on the run, donkey of the uplands, panther of the wild" (VIII.2); "my friend" throughout. VIII.1-VIII.8.
- **People of Uruk:** the elders of "teeming Uruk-the-Sheepfold" and the crowd who blessed the two (VIII.1); the young men of Uruk-the-Sheepfold, who witnessed the Bull fight (VIII.1, VIII.2); the people "so bonny" (VIII.5).
- **Working people named as mourners:** the ploughman, the herdsman, the shepherd boy, the brewer, the harlot (VIII.1).
- **Craftsmen:** the forgemaster, lapidary, coppersmith, goldsmith and jeweler (VIII.4).
- **Humbaba,** recalled as the dweller in the Forest of Cedar (VIII.2).
- **An unidentified speaker** (not Gilgamesh), who mentions the "judge of the Anunnaki" (VIII.9).

### Places
- The Forest of Cedar and its paths; its myrtle, cypress and cedar (VIII.1, VIII.2; also shown on the alabaster gift, VIII.8).
- Uruk-the-Sheepfold (VIII.1, VIII.2).
- Hills, mountains and pastures (VIII.1, VIII.2).
- The sacred river Ulay; the holy Euphrates (VIII.1; the Euphrates again in VIII.8).
- The wedding house (VIII.1, broken).
- Gilgamesh's treasury (VIII.6); his gate (VIII.11).
- The underworld, or "teeming Netherworld" (VIII.5, VIII.7, VIII.8).
- "The river" to be dammed (VIII.10, unnamed).

### Objects and materials
- Milk, butter, ghee, ale, sweet-smelling oil; libation water in skins (VIII.1).
- The axe, sword, shield, festive garment and "girdle of delight" (VIII.2).
- Torn hair; discarded finery (VIII.3).
- The statue: lapis lazuli eyebrows, a gold chest (VIII.4).
- A magnificent bed, a bed of honor, a seat of repose (VIII.5).
- The treasury's seals; obsidian, carnelian, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl, alabaster; gold by the pound and the talent; ivory; a quiver; an ivory mace; handles of gold; an iron rod; a wild-bull holder (VIII.6).
- Fattened oxen and sheep, meat (VIII.7).
- The gifts: a mahogany throwstick; a festive gown; a lapis lazuli flask; a carnelian flute; a lapis lazuli chair and staff; a silver clasp, copper bangles; an inlaid alabaster object showing the Cedar Forest; a double-edged dagger with a lapis lazuli haft; a flintstone from the Euphrates; an alabaster flask; a lapis-topped, carnelian-inlaid object (VIII.8).
- A great table of *elammaku*-wood; a carnelian bowl of syrup; a lapis lazuli bowl of ghee (VIII.11).

### Creatures
- Gazelle, wild donkey, wild asses (as Enkidu's parents and nurses, VIII.1).
- Bear, hyena, panther, cheetah, stag, jackal, lion, wild bull, deer, ibex (VIII.1).
- The Bull of Heaven, recalled (VIII.1, VIII.2).
- Wild ass, donkey and panther in the epithet (VIII.2).
- Eagle, lioness and cubs, in similes (VIII.3).
- Oxen and sheep, slaughtered (VIII.7).
- A wild bull as a holder (VIII.6).

### Gods and what they do
- **Shamash, the Sun God:** Gilgamesh addresses him (VIII.7), and every gift is displayed before him (VIII.8, VIII.11).
- **Netherworld recipients of gifts, each asked to welcome Enkidu and walk at his side** (VIII.8): Ishtar ("the great queen"); Belet-ili ("older sister of Enlil," restored); Ereshkigal ("queen of the teeming Netherworld"); Dumuzi ("the shepherd loved by Ishtar"); Namtar ("vizier of the Netherworld"); Hushbisha ("stewardess of the Netherworld," restored); Qassu-ṭabat ("the sweeper of Ereshkigal"); Ninshuluhhatumma ("the cleaner of the house"); Bibbu ("the butcher of the Netherworld"); Dumuzi-abzu ("the scapegoat of the Netherworld"); and one deity whose name is lost.
- **The rulers of the underworld:** will kiss Enkidu's feet (VIII.5) and receive the meat (VIII.7).
- **The Anunnaki:** a "judge of the Anunnaki" is mentioned (VIII.9).

### Numbers and time
- Four dawns, each with the formula "At the very first glimmer of brightening dawn" (VIII.1, VIII.4, VIII.6, VIII.11).
- "By day and by night" (VIII.1); "this very day" (VIII.1).
- x+10 pounds, x pounds, 30 pounds, 40 pounds and a talent of gold; x talents of ivory; three cubits (VIII.6).
- Eleven gift entries (VIII.8).
- Lost blocks: VIII 73-83, 189-207, and about 30 lines at the end.

### Dreams and omens
- None new in this tablet. Shamash's prophecy from VII.8 is fulfilled as Gilgamesh's own promise (VIII.5).

### Customs and rituals
- Dawn lament; calling on nature, animals, rivers and people to mourn; hired mourning women; loosened hair "like sisters" (VIII.1, VIII.2).
- Feeling the heart to confirm death; veiling the face of the dead "like a bride"; tearing out hair; casting off finery as taboo (VIII.3).
- A funerary statue of precious materials (VIII.4).
- Laying out the dead on a bed of honor, on the left, on a seat of repose; public mourning; matted hair and a lion's skin (VIII.5).
- Grave goods taken from the sealed treasury (VIII.6).
- A slaughter and banquet; meat for the rulers of the underworld (VIII.7).
- Gifts displayed to the Sun God for each Netherworld deity, with a fixed prayer for Enkidu's welcome (VIII.8).
- Displaying a table of syrup and ghee to the Sun God at dawn (VIII.11).
- George (p. lii): the whole sequence reads as the ideal model of a Babylonian noble's funerary rites.

## Open questions and uncertainties
- ⚠️ **The gap between VII and VIII:** the time between Enkidu's last words (VII.14) and the first dawn of VIII is unknown; the end of VII is lost.
- ⚠️ **When death is confirmed:** the lament (VIII 1-56) comes before the heart check (VIII 58), so the text does not show whether Gilgamesh had already accepted the death when he began.
- ⚠️ **The four dawns** are not said to be consecutive days.
- ⚠️ **VIII 73-83 are lost:** the rest of the statue's description and whatever led into VIII 84.
- ⚠️ **VIII 84-91 are almost wholly restored** from VII 139-147.
- ⚠️ **The treasury list (VIII 96-130) is badly broken:** most objects are unnamed and the gold weights are partly lost ("x").
- ⚠️ **Hushbisha's entry and the eleventh entry** are almost wholly restored; the eleventh deity's name is lost.
- ⚠️ **Uncertain renderings (italic):** "cheetah," "jackal," "butter," "lips," "brewer" (VIII.1); "eyebrows" (VIII.4); "obsidian," "mother-of-pearl," "rod," "holder" (VIII.6); "throwstick," "mahogany," "clasp," "scapegoat," "flask" (VIII.8); "damming" (VIII.10).
- ⚠️ **VIII 189-207 are lost,** and the speaker of VIII 208-210 is unknown (George, p. 68).
- ⚠️ **"Damming the river" (VIII 212):** uncertain in rendering, purpose and place.
- ⚠️ **Ishtar among the gift recipients:** after Tablet VI, Ishtar ("the great queen") is the first deity offered a gift. The text gives no comment.
- ⚠️ **The burial is not preserved.** About thirty lines at the end of the tablet are lost (George, p. 68).

## Leads (structure only)
- **Lead:** four dawn formulas mark the stages of the funeral: lament, statue, treasury, final display.
- **Lead:** VIII 84-91 repeat Shamash's prophecy from VII 139-147 in the first person, and VIII 65-72 carry out the statue promise of VII 82.
- **Lead:** the mourner list recalls the earlier story in order: Enkidu's animal upbringing, the Forest of Cedar, Uruk's elders, the Bull of Heaven, and the people who fed and anointed him.
- **Lead:** the gift section is built from one formula repeated eleven times; the "sick at heart" wish is added only in the entries for the sweeper (Qassu-ṭabat) and the cleaner of the house (Ninshuluhhatumma), which stand next to each other.
- **Lead:** the alabaster gift for Ninshuluhhatumma is decorated with an image of the Cedar Forest, linking the grave goods to the expedition of Tablets IV-V.
- **Lead:** the triple epithet of VIII 50-51 recurs in Tablet X (see that file).
