# Tablet VI. Ishtar and the Bull of Heaven: play-by-play
Research notes, 2026-10-25. Source: George 2020, chapter 1. A record of the original only; nothing here is adaptation or decision.

Pages 47-53. About 183 lines; the last printed margin number is VI 180.

**Line numbers:** taken from George's margin numbers, read from the EPUB markup (the extracted text shows each margin number only as a bare `>`). Ranges between printed numbers are counted line by line, and "about" marks a count that cannot be pinned to a printed number.

## George's synopsis (p. 47)
Back in Uruk, Ishtar is struck by Gilgamesh's beauty and asks him to marry her. He turns her down with contempt, listing what became of her many earlier lovers. In a rage she goes up to heaven and talks her father Anu into giving her the Bull of Heaven, which George glosses as "fiery" and identifies with the constellation Taurus, so that she can have Gilgamesh killed. The Bull wreaks havoc in Uruk, but the two heroes find its weak spot and kill it. They insult Ishtar again and go back to the palace in triumph to celebrate.

## Scenes

### VI.1 Gilgamesh washes and dresses (VI 1-5, p. 47)
- **Where and when:** The text names no place; "back in Uruk" comes from George's synopsis. No time of day. It follows the close of Tablet V, where the two heroes ride a cedar raft down the Euphrates with Humbaba's head; no interval is stated.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh, alone. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh washes his matted hair and cleans his equipment.
  2. He shakes his hair down loose over his back.
  3. He throws off his dirty gear and dresses in clean clothes.
  4. He wraps cloaks around himself, tied with a sash.
  5. He puts on his crown.
- **Specifics:** matted hair; his equipment; dirty gear, then clean clothing; cloaks; a sash; a crown.
- **Gaps:** none. The passage is fully preserved.
- **Repetitions:** none in this tablet.

### VI.2 Ishtar proposes marriage (VI 6-21, p. 47)
- **Where and when:** not stated; it follows straight on from VI.1.
- **Who:** Ishtar ("Lady Ishtar") speaks to Gilgamesh. He does not answer until VI.3.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Narration: Ishtar looks with desire on Gilgamesh's beauty.
  2. Ishtar → Gilgamesh: the proposal. He should be her bridegroom and give her his "fruits"; he will be her husband and she his wife.
  3. Ishtar → Gilgamesh: the gifts. She will harness him a chariot of lapis lazuli and gold, with wheels of gold and "horns" of amber. He will drive a team of lions and very large mules, and enter "our house" amid the scent of cedar.
  4. Ishtar → Gilgamesh: the homage. When he enters the house, its doorway and footstool will kiss his feet. Kings, courtiers and nobles will kneel to him and bring him the produce of mountain and lowland as tribute.
  5. Ishtar → Gilgamesh: the livestock. His goats will bear triplets and his ewes twins; his donkey, loaded, will outrun any mule; his horse will gallop gloriously at the chariot; no ox will match his at the yoke.
- **Specifics:** chariot of lapis lazuli and gold; gold wheels; amber "horns" (of the chariot); a team of lions; "mules of great size"; the sweet scent of cedar; doorway and footstool; kings, courtiers and nobles; tribute of "produce of mountain and lowland"; goats (triplets), ewes (twins), donkey, mule, horse, ox, yoke.
- **Short quotation:** "Be you my husband and I'll be your wife!" (VI 9).
- **Gaps:** none. The passage is fully preserved.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VI.3 Gilgamesh's reply, part 1: who would keep him? (VI 22-28, then lines 29-31 lost; p. 48)
- **Where and when:** same moment, directly after her speech.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks to Ishtar.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Speech formula: Gilgamesh opens his mouth and speaks to Lady Ishtar.
  2. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: if he did marry her, (broken) something about body and clothing; where would his food and sustenance come from?
  3. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: would she feed him bread fit for a god and pour him ale fit for a king?
  4. Three lines are lost.
- **Specifics:** body and clothing; food and sustenance; bread "fit for a god"; ale "fit for a king."
- **Gaps:** "[Gilgamesh]," "[saying]" and "[And if indeed I]" (VI 22-24) are certain restorations. VI 25 opens with lost words. The key words of VI 26-28 ("whence will come," "Will you feed me," "and pour me ale") are italic restorations, which George marks as conjectural. VI 29-31 are lost (three asterisks; the margin count jumps from 28 to 32). George gives no note on their content.
- **Repetitions:** the pair "bread fit for a god / ale fit for a king" comes back in VII 135-136, where Shamash lists what Shamhat gave Enkidu.

### VI.4 Gilgamesh's reply, part 2: the insults (VI 32-43, p. 48)
- **Where and when:** same speech, after the three lost lines.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks to Ishtar.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: why would he want to marry her? ("Why" is a conjectural restoration.)
  2. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: a string of nine insulting comparisons. She is: a frost that freezes nothing; a slatted door that keeps out neither breeze nor draft; a palace that slaughters its warriors; an elephant that does something (lost) to its "hoods"; pitch that dirties the hands of whoever carries it; a waterskin that cuts its carrier's hands; a boulder that smashes a stone wall; a battering ram that breaks down a wall for the enemy; a shoe that bites its owner's foot.
  3. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: two questions. Which of her bridegrooms lasted forever? Which of her brave warriors went up to the heavens?
- **Specifics:** frost and ice; slatted door; breeze, draft; palace; warriors; elephant; "hoods"; pitch; waterskin; boulder; stone wall; battering ram; the enemy; shoe; bridegroom; brave warrior; the heavens.
- **Gaps:** "[You, *a frost*]": "You" is restored, "a frost" is conjectural. In the elephant line one word is lost and "hoods" is italic (an uncertain rendering). The verbs for the pitch, waterskin and boulder ("stains the hands," "cuts the hands," "smashes"), "a wall for" (battering ram) and "to the heavens" are all conjectural restorations.
- **Repetitions:** the comparisons follow one fixed pattern, an object that fails or harms the person who uses it.

### VI.5 Gilgamesh's reply, part 3: the list of Ishtar's lovers (VI 44-79, pp. 48-49)
- **Where and when:** same speech.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh speaks to Ishtar. Inside his speech he quotes an exchange between Ishtar and Ishullanu (retold, not happening now).
- **Play-by-play** (all retold by Gilgamesh):
  1. Gilgamesh → Ishtar: he will tell her the story of her lovers.
  2. VI 45 is almost entirely lost; only "his arm" survives. ⚠️ It may have held one more lover.
  3. Dumuzi, the husband of her youth: she condemned him to be lamented year after year.
  4. The speckled hoopoe: she loved it, then struck it and broke its wing. It now stands in the woods crying "My wing!"
  5. The lion, perfect in strength: she dug pits for it, "seven pits and seven."
  6. The horse, famed in battle: she made its fate the whip, the spur and the lash, a seven-league gallop and drinking muddy water. She condemned its mother, Silili, to weep forever.
  7. The shepherd (also called grazier and herdsman): he gave her heaps of bread baked in embers and killed young goats for her every day. She struck him and turned him into a wolf. Now his own shepherd boys drive him off and his own dogs bite his haunches.
  8. Ishullanu, her father's gardener: he brought her dates in a basket and kept her table gleaming every day. She eyed him and went to meet him. Retold speech, Ishtar → Ishullanu: a crude sexual invitation (VI 68-69).
  9. Retold speech, Ishullanu → Ishtar: he refuses. What does she want of him? Didn't his mother bake, hasn't he eaten, that he should now eat "the bread of slander and insults"? Should he be covered only by rushes in winter?
  10. When she heard his answer, she struck him and turned him into a *dwarf* (italic: an uncertain rendering). She set him down in the middle of his labors; he can neither go up nor go down (the end of each half-line is lost).
  11. Gilgamesh → Ishtar, closing: must she love him too, and treat him the same way?
- **Specifics:** Dumuzi ("the husband of your youth"); "year upon year"; the speckled hoopoe and its broken wing; the woods; the lion ("perfect in strength"); seven pits and seven; the horse ("so famed in battle"); whip, spur and lash; a seven-league gallop; muddy water; Silili, the horse's mother (George's glossary, p. 224: the mythical mother of all horses, found only in this epic); the shepherd, grazier and herdsman; bread baked in embers; young goats (kids) killed "day after day"; a wolf; shepherd boys; dogs; haunches; Ishullanu, "your father's gardener" (glossary, p. 223: the name means "Shorty," a cultivator of dates); dates; a basket; her table; rushes; winter.
- **Short quotation:** "My wing!" (VI 50), the hoopoe's cry.
- **Gaps:** VI 44 has "[you the tale]" restored. VI 45 is lost apart from "his arm." VI 74 has "[he had to]" restored. VI 78 ends both halves with lost words. "[deal with me]" in VI 79 is a restoration. "Dwarf" (VI 76) is italic.
- **Repetitions:** each lover follows the same pattern: "You loved ..." (what he did for her or what he was), then the punishment. The horse's fate is set out three times with "you made its destiny." Counting Dumuzi, six lovers survive: Dumuzi, the hoopoe, the lion, the horse, the shepherd and Ishullanu.

### VI.6 Ishtar in heaven: Anu gives her the Bull (VI 80-114, pp. 49-50)
- **Where and when:** heaven, immediately after Gilgamesh's speech.
- **Who:** Ishtar; Anu, her father; Antu, her mother (present, silent). Ishtar and Anu speak.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Ishtar hears his words and goes up to heaven in a furious rage.
  2. Weeping, she goes to her father, Anu; her tears flow in front of her mother, Antu.
  3. Ishtar → Anu: Gilgamesh keeps scorning her and telling a story full of foul slander and insults about her.
  4. Anu → Ishtar: wasn't it she who provoked King Gilgamesh into telling that story?
  5. Ishtar → Anu: she asks for the Bull of Heaven so that she can kill Gilgamesh in his own dwelling.
  6. Ishtar → Anu, a threat: if he refuses, she will smash the gates of the Netherworld down to its dwelling, set the world below free, bring the dead up to eat the living, and make the dead outnumber the living.
  7. Anu → Ishtar, a condition: if she wants the Bull, the widow of Uruk must gather seven years' chaff and the farmer of Uruk must grow seven years' hay.
  8. Ishtar → Anu: (the first two lines are lost) she has already stored and grown it. The widow has gathered seven years' chaff and the farmer has grown seven years' hay. With the Bull's fury she will take her revenge.
  9. Anu hears her and places the Bull of Heaven's nose-rope in her hands.
- **Specifics:** heaven; Anu ("her father"); Antu ("her mother"); "King Gilgamesh" (Anu's title for him); the Bull of Heaven; Gilgamesh's "dwelling"; the gates of the Netherworld (conjectural); the dead and the living; the widow of Uruk; the farmer of Uruk; seven years' chaff; seven years' hay; the nose-rope.
- **Short quotation:** "I shall make the dead outnumber the living." (VI 100).
- **Gaps:** "[heard]," "[went up]" and "[Weeping]" (VI 80-82) are restorations. In the threat (VI 97-98), "smash" and "grant" are italic (uncertain renderings), and "the gates of the Netherworld, right down" and "manumission" are conjectural restorations. "Of Uruk" after "the farmer" (VI 105, 111) is conjectural. VI 108-109 are lost at the start. VI 110-111 are largely restored from Anu's own words in VI 104-105. "Have vengeance" (VI 112) is conjectural.
- **Repetitions:**
  - The speech formula "X opened his/her mouth to speak, saying to Y" introduces each speech in the exchange.
  - Ishtar's complaint (VI 84-86) is repeated almost word for word by Anu (VI 89-91), with "me" turned into "you."
  - Ishtar's reply (VI 110-111) repeats Anu's condition (VI 104-105) as already done.

### VI.7 The Bull of Heaven ravages Uruk (VI 115-127, pp. 50-51)
- **Where and when:** from heaven down to "the land of Uruk": its woods, reed-beds and marshes, then the river. It follows directly on VI.6. No time of day.
- **Who:** Ishtar, leading the Bull; the men of Uruk; Enkidu. Gilgamesh is not mentioned until Enkidu speaks to him in VI.8. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Ishtar comes down, leading the Bull.
  2. Once it reaches the land of Uruk, it dries up the woods, the reed-beds and the marshes.
  3. It goes down to the river and lowers the water by seven full cubits.
  4. First snort: a pit opens and a hundred men of Uruk fall into it.
  5. Second snort: a pit opens and two hundred men of Uruk fall into it.
  6. Third snort: a pit opens and Enkidu falls in up to his waist.
  7. Enkidu leaps out and grabs the Bull by the horns.
  8. The Bull spits slaver in his face and flicks its dung at him with the tuft of its tail (an uncertain rendering).
- **Specifics:** the nose-rope (from VI.6); woods, reed-beds, marshes; the river; seven full cubits; three snorts; 100 men, then 200 men; Enkidu in the pit up to his waist; horns; slaver; dung; the tuft of the tail.
- **Gaps:** "[Down came]" (VI 115) is a restoration. "Flicking its dung" (VI 127) is italic (uncertain rendering). Otherwise preserved.
- **Repetitions:** the three snorts share one pattern (it snorts, a pit opens, someone falls in). The numbers climb from 100 to 200, and the third time it is Enkidu.

### VI.8 Enkidu's plan (VI 128-140, p. 51)
- **Where and when:** at the Bull, during the struggle; no break in time.
- **Who:** Enkidu speaks to Gilgamesh ("his friend," restored).
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Enkidu → Gilgamesh: they boasted in their city; how will they answer the crowds of people?
  2. Enkidu → Gilgamesh: he has tested the Bull's might and learned its strength and purpose.
  3. Enkidu → Gilgamesh, the plan: he will test the Bull again, get behind it and seize it by the tuft of its tail.
  4. He will set his foot on the back of its leg; the next line is lost.
  5. Then Gilgamesh, like a brave and skillful butcher, is to drive his knife in between the "yoke" of the horns and the slaughter-spot.
- **Specifics:** "the thronging people"; the Bull's might, strength and purpose; the tuft of the tail; the back of the leg; a butcher; a knife; the "yoke of the horns"; the "slaughter-spot."
- **Short quotation:** "between the yoke of the horns and the slaughter-spot" (VI 140; repeated at VI 146).
- **Gaps:** "[to speak]" and "[his friend]" are restored. "In our" (VI 130), "and knowing its" (VI 133), "shall get me" (VI 135), "back of" and "leg" (VI 137) are conjectural restorations; "city" (VI 130) and "purpose" (VI 133) are italic (uncertain). VI 138 is lost apart from "in ... [it]." "[Butcher, brave and]" (VI 139) is restored from VI 145.
- **Repetitions:** Enkidu's plan (VI 135-140) is carried out in nearly the same words in VI 141-146.

### VI.9 The killing of the Bull (VI 141-146, p. 51)
- **Where and when:** at once, carrying out the plan.
- **Who:** Enkidu and Gilgamesh; the Bull. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Enkidu runs around to the Bull's rear.
  2. He seizes it by the tuft of its tail.
  3. He sets his foot on the back of its leg; the next line is lost.
  4. Gilgamesh, like a brave and skillful butcher, drives his knife in between the yoke of the horns and the slaughter-spot.
- **Specifics:** the rear of the Bull; the tuft of the tail; the back of the leg; the knife; the yoke of the horns; the slaughter-spot.
- **Gaps:** "[tuft]," "[He set]" and "[he thrust in]" are restorations; "the back of" and "leg" are conjectural. VI 144 is lost apart from "[in] ... it."
- **Repetitions:** repeats Enkidu's plan (VI 135-140) almost word for word, as narration. George's illustration 7 (p. 52) is captioned with VI 146.

### VI.10 The heart offered to Shamash (VI 147-150, p. 52)
- **Where and when:** right after the kill; the place is not named.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh and Enkidu, before Shamash (the Sun God). No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. After killing the Bull, they lift up its heart and set it before Shamash.
  2. They step back and throw themselves face down before the Sun God.
  3. Then the two of them sit down together.
- **Specifics:** the Bull's heart; Shamash, "the Sun God"; prostration; sitting down together.
- **Gaps:** none.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VI.11 Ishtar on the city wall; Enkidu throws the shoulder (VI 151-157, p. 52)
- **Where and when:** the wall of Uruk-the-Sheepfold; directly after VI.10.
- **Who:** Ishtar, Enkidu. Ishtar cries out (no listener named); Enkidu answers her.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Ishtar climbs onto the wall of Uruk-the-Sheepfold.
  2. Hopping and stamping, she wails in grief.
  3. Ishtar (a cry, no listener named): Gilgamesh, who mocked her, has killed the Bull of Heaven.
  4. Enkidu hears her, tears off the Bull's shoulder and hurls it toward her.
  5. Enkidu → Ishtar: if he had caught her too, he would have done the same to her and draped her arms in its guts.
- **Specifics:** the wall of Uruk-the-Sheepfold; hopping and stamping; the Bull's shoulder, torn off and thrown; the guts.
- **Gaps:** none.
- **Repetitions:** none. Note: George's illustration 7 is printed here (p. 52), captioned with VI 146.

### VI.12 Ishtar's mourning rites over the shoulder (VI 158-159, p. 52)
- **Where and when:** not stated; directly after VI.11.
- **Who:** Ishtar; the courtesans, prostitutes and harlots she gathers. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Ishtar gathers the courtesans, the prostitutes and the harlots.
  2. Over the Bull's shoulder she starts rites of mourning.
- **Specifics:** three named groups of women (courtesans, prostitutes, harlots); the Bull's shoulder; mourning rites.
- **Gaps:** none.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VI.13 The horns given to Lugalbanda (VI 160-166, pp. 52-53)
- **Where and when:** not stated; it follows VI.12.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh; all the smiths and craftsmen. No speech.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh summons all the smiths and craftsmen.
  2. The craftsmen admire how big the horns are.
  3. Description of the horns: thirty pounds of lapis lazuli in a solid block; their rims two pounds each; together they hold sixty bushels of oil.
  4. Gilgamesh gives them to his god Lugalbanda, to hold oil for anointing.
  5. He takes them in and hangs them in his chamber.
- **Specifics:** smiths, craftsmen; the horns; 30 pounds of lapis lazuli "in a solid block"; rims of 2 pounds each; 60 bushels of oil (the capacity of both horns together); Lugalbanda, "his god" (glossary, p. 223: a mortal king of Uruk later made a god; in one tradition Gilgamesh's father, in another his guardian god); oil for anointing; "his chamber."
- **Gaps:** "rims" (VI 162) is italic (uncertain rendering). Otherwise preserved.
- **Repetitions:** none.

### VI.14 Washing in the Euphrates and the ride through Uruk (VI 167-175, then lines 176-178 lost; p. 53)
- **Where and when:** the river Euphrates, then the streets of Uruk, then the palace. It follows VI.13; no time of day.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh and Enkidu; the people of Uruk (watching); the serving girls of the palace. Gilgamesh asks; the answer has no speaker tag.
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. The two wash their hands in the river Euphrates.
  2. They take each other by the hand and come in.
  3. They drive through the streets of Uruk while the people gather to watch them.
  4. Gilgamesh → the serving girls of his palace: who is the finest of men, the most glorious of fellows?
  5. Answer (speaker not named; by context the serving girls): Gilgamesh is the finest of men and the most glorious of fellows.
  6. Three lines are lost.
- **Specifics:** the Euphrates; hand-washing; holding hands; driving through the streets; the gathered people; the serving girls; "finest among men"; "most glorious of fellows."
- **Short quotation:** "Who is the finest among men?" (VI 172).
- **Gaps:** "[on them]" (VI 170) and "[Gilgamesh the most]" (VI 175) are restorations; "his palace" (VI 171) is conjectural. VI 176-178 are lost (three asterisks); George gives no note on their content.
- **Repetitions:** the answer repeats the question word for word, with Gilgamesh's name put in.

### VI.15 The celebration; night; Enkidu's dream begins (VI 179-183, p. 53)
- **Where and when:** the palace; first the celebration, then night.
- **Who:** Gilgamesh; "the men" (asleep); Enkidu, who starts to speak to "his friend."
- **Play-by-play:**
  1. Gilgamesh celebrates in his palace.
  2. At night the men lie asleep in their beds.
  3. Enkidu has a dream as he sleeps (the dream itself is told in Tablet VII).
  4. Enkidu gets up to tell the dream and addresses his friend. The tablet ends on this speech formula; the speech continues at VII 1.
- **Specifics:** the palace; night; beds; a dream.
- **Gaps:** none in these lines. The tablet ends at about VI 183 (the last printed margin number is VI 180, followed by three lines).
- **Repetitions:** none.

## Elements list

### Characters (epithets as given)
- **Gilgamesh:** "King Gilgamesh" (Anu, VI.6); "who mocked me" (Ishtar, VI.11); compared to a brave and skillful butcher (VI.8, VI.9); "the finest among men," "the most glorious of fellows" (VI.14). VI.1-VI.15.
- **Ishtar:** "Lady Ishtar" (VI.2, VI.3, VI.6); "the goddess Ishtar" (VI.6). VI.2-VI.7, VI.11, VI.12.
- **Enkidu:** Gilgamesh's "friend" (VI.8, VI.15). VI.7-VI.11, VI.14, VI.15.
- **Anu:** Ishtar's father (VI.6).
- **Antu:** Ishtar's mother; present, silent (VI.6).
- **Ishtar's former lovers (retold):** Dumuzi, "the husband of your youth"; the shepherd, grazier and herdsman; Ishullanu, "your father's gardener" (VI.5). The animal lovers are listed under Creatures.
- **Silili:** the horse's mother (VI.5).
- **Groups:** kings, courtiers and nobles (promised, VI.2); the widow of Uruk and the farmer of Uruk (VI.6); the men of Uruk, 100 and 200 (VI.7); "the thronging people" (VI.8); the courtesans, prostitutes and harlots (VI.12); the smiths and craftsmen (VI.13); the people of Uruk in the streets and the serving girls of the palace (VI.14); "the men" asleep (VI.15).

### Places
- Uruk: from George's synopsis only (VI.1); "the land of Uruk" (VI.7); the streets of Uruk (VI.14).
- Ishtar's "our house" (promised, VI.2).
- Heaven (VI.6). The Netherworld, its gates and "the world below" (threatened, VI.6).
- The woods, reed-beds, marshes and the river around Uruk (VI.7).
- The wall of Uruk-the-Sheepfold (VI.11).
- The river Euphrates (VI.14).
- Gilgamesh's palace (VI.14, VI.15); "his chamber" (VI.13).
- The woods where the hoopoe stands (VI.5).

### Objects and materials
- Washing and dress: matted hair, equipment, dirty gear, clean clothes, cloaks, sash, crown (VI.1).
- Bride gifts: chariot of lapis lazuli and gold, gold wheels, amber "horns," the scent of cedar, doorway and footstool, tribute of mountain and lowland produce (VI.2).
- Food: bread fit for a god, ale fit for a king (VI.3); bread baked in embers, slaughtered kids, dates in a basket, "the bread of slander and insults" (VI.5).
- In the insults: frost, ice, a slatted door, a palace, pitch, a waterskin, a boulder, a stone wall, a battering ram, a shoe (VI.4).
- From the lovers list: pits; whip, spur and lash; muddy water; a table; rushes (VI.5).
- The Bull: its nose-rope (VI.6); seven years' chaff and hay (VI.6); the knife (VI.8, VI.9); its heart (VI.10); its shoulder and guts (VI.11, VI.12); its horns, with 30 pounds of lapis lazuli, rims of 2 pounds each and room for 60 bushels of oil for anointing (VI.13).
- Beds (VI.15).

### Creatures
- In the proposal: a team of lions; outsized mules; goats, ewes, a donkey, a horse, an ox (VI.2).
- In the insults: an elephant (VI.4).
- Lovers and the forms they were changed into: the speckled hoopoe; the lion; the horse; the wolf (the shepherd's new form); the shepherd's dogs; kids (young goats) (VI.5).
- The Bull of Heaven (VI.6-VI.13).

### Gods and what they do
- **Ishtar:** proposes marriage (VI.2); complains to Anu, demands the Bull and threatens to raise the dead (VI.6); leads the Bull down to Uruk (VI.7); wails on the city wall (VI.11); holds mourning rites over the shoulder (VI.12). In the retold list she dooms and transforms her lovers (VI.5).
- **Anu:** rebukes Ishtar, sets the seven-years condition, hands over the nose-rope (VI.6).
- **Antu:** present while Ishtar weeps (VI.6).
- **Shamash, the Sun God:** receives the Bull's heart (VI.10).
- **Lugalbanda,** Gilgamesh's god: receives the horns as oil vessels for anointing (VI.13).
- **Dumuzi:** a former husband of Ishtar, doomed to yearly lament (VI.5).

### Numbers and time
- Triplets and twins (VI.2).
- Nine insulting comparisons (VI.4).
- "Seven pits and seven"; a seven-league gallop; "year upon year"; "day after day"; "daily"; winter (VI.5).
- Seven years' chaff, seven years' hay (VI.6).
- Seven full cubits; three snorts; 100 men, 200 men (VI.7).
- 30 pounds, 2 pounds each, 60 bushels (VI.13).
- Night (VI.15). No time of day is given anywhere else in the tablet.

### Dreams and omens
- Enkidu's dream, announced at VI.15 and told in Tablet VII.

### Customs and rituals
- Washing, changing clothes and putting on the crown (VI.1).
- A marriage proposal with promised bride gifts and homage (VI.2).
- Yearly lament for Dumuzi (VI.5).
- Offering the Bull's heart to Shamash and prostrating themselves (VI.10).
- Mourning rites over the Bull's shoulder, performed by Ishtar's gathered women (VI.12).
- Dedicating the horns to Lugalbanda as vessels for anointing oil, hung in the chamber (VI.13).
- Washing hands in the Euphrates; a procession through the streets; the palace celebration (VI.14, VI.15).

## Open questions and uncertainties
- ⚠️ **Where VI.1 happens:** the text never says. "Back in Uruk" is George's synopsis (p. 47). Likewise, "fiery" and the identification with the constellation Taurus come from the synopsis, not from the translated lines.
- ⚠️ **VI 29-31:** lost, between the practical objection and the insults. George gives no note.
- ⚠️ **VI 45:** almost entirely lost. It may have held another lover before Dumuzi.
- ⚠️ **Conjectural wording:** several of the insults (VI 32-40) and the Netherworld threat (VI 97-98) depend on restorations George marks as conjectural.
- ⚠️ **Uncertain renderings (italic):** "hoods" (VI 36); "dwarf" (VI 76); "city" and "purpose" (VI 130, 133); "flicking its dung" (VI 127); "rims" (VI 162).
- ⚠️ **VI 108-109:** the openings of Ishtar's answer to Anu are lost, so it is unclear how she claims to have already met his condition.
- ⚠️ **Gilgamesh during the attack:** he is not mentioned until Enkidu speaks to him (VI 129). The text does not say where he was when the pits opened.
- ⚠️ **"His chamber" (VI 166):** it could be Gilgamesh's chamber or Lugalbanda's; the text does not say.
- ⚠️ **The horns:** the text gives 30 pounds of lapis lazuli in a solid block without saying how that weight relates to the horns themselves.
- ⚠️ **VI 174-175:** the answer to Gilgamesh's question has no speaker tag.
- ⚠️ **VI 176-178:** lost; George gives no note.

## Leads (structure only)
- **Lead:** the lovers list runs on one repeated pattern ("You loved ..." then the punishment), and the insults run on another (an object that fails or harms its user).
- **Lead:** the Bull episode pairs a plan with its execution in near-identical words (VI 135-140, then 141-146).
- **Lead:** the three snorts climb in a series (100, 200, then Enkidu).
- **Lead:** the bread/ale pair from VI 26-28 comes back in Shamash's speech at VII 135-136.
- **Lead:** Ishtar's complaint is repeated back to her by Anu, and Anu's condition is repeated back to him by Ishtar; the exchange is built from mirrored speeches.
- **Lead:** the tablet ends in the middle of a speech introduction; the dream it introduces fills the start of Tablet VII.
- **Lead:** Gilgamesh washes his matted hair and changes his clothes at the opening of this tablet. Matted hair returns in Shamash's prophecy at VII 146 ("matted in mourning") and again at VIII 90. A washing of matted locks also occurs in Tablet XI (see that file).
