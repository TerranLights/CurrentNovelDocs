# Human #1 and Human #2 — Weiland Archetypes, Light Treatment

2026-09-21. **Deliberately shallow.** The robots are the main characters of this story, as they are of every
story in this line; the humans get archetypes so their structural function is defined, not so their inner
lives are mapped. Everything here is **proposed**.

**Structurally, the humans get:**
- **Harmon 8-point arcs** — YOU / NEED / GO / SEARCH / FIND / TAKE / RETURN / CHANGE. Their arc files
  (`huMan-1.md`, `huMan-2.md`) already use this template correctly; nothing needs changing.
- **A Weiland archetype each**, assigned below, at a fraction of the depth the dolls get.

**One shared file rather than two, on purpose** — the two humans' archetypes are defined largely in relation
to each other and to the dolls, and splitting them would mostly duplicate the rationale. Easy to split later
if it turns out to be the wrong call.

---

## The economical option: a resting state, not an arc

Weiland's sequence alternates **transforming arcs** (Maiden, Hero, Queen, King, Crone, Mage) with **resting
Flat states** (Child, Lover, Parent, Ruler, Elder, Mentor). A character sitting in a resting state is a **Flat
archetype** — she already holds a Truth and embodies it, rather than learning one across the book.

That is the natural fit for light treatment: **a Flat archetype requires no beat sheet at all.** It's a
standing identity and a set of relational functions, which is precisely the amount of structure a supporting
character in a sub-300-page book can carry without stealing room from the dolls.

Both assignments below are resting states for that reason. If either human turns out to need real change
across the book, swap that one to the adjacent transforming arc and give it a light beat pass — but the
default should be Flat.

---

## Human #1 — **the Lover** *(resting state between Maiden and Hero)*

Established: "charming type," from Vancouver, travels to Whitehorse on a deal, meets Lucy, leaves, goes
off-course, gets lost, goes radio-silent.

**Why the Lover.** Three things line up:

1. **Its defining function is catalyzing a Maiden.** Your own profile of this archetype says it is *"most
   useful for catalyzing other still-Maiden-stage characters, especially the specific person she loves, whose
   own Maiden Arc her love may be the literal trigger for."* Lucy is on the Maiden Arc, and Human #1 is the
   figure who arrives with her Predator's Proposal. The framework describes his exact job.
2. **It needn't be romantic.** The profile is explicit that the same *intensity-without-full-maturity*
   register applies to any young character's all-consuming devotion to a skill, cause, or craft. So this
   assignment leaves the nature of his bond with Lucy entirely open.
3. **The Lover stays in place while everything changes around her** — the one archetype whose symbolic setting
   (the village) doesn't expand. Human #1 gets lost and goes silent: he becomes the fixed point the entire
   plot converges on. He does not move; the story moves to him. The archetype's static quality is the plot's
   structure.

**Its register:** first, unformed passion — intense but immature, built on only one completed arc. "Charming"
sits comfortably inside that.

**What this constrains, lightly:**
- He is a **catalyst, not a savior.** The Maiden Arc is explicit that the Protector figure is not the Maiden's
  rescuer, and Lucy's own constraints already forbid her being rescued. Two frameworks, same prohibition.
- He should not be *wise.* The Lover holds exactly one Truth, immaturely. Any guidance he offers Lucy should
  be partial at best.
- Live option worth keeping open: the Maiden Arc notes the **Predator and the Protector need not be different
  characters.** Human #1 can be both.

## Human #2 — **the Parent** *(resting state between Hero and Queen)*

Established: "diligent type," from Vancouver, close childhood friend of Human #1 since early childhood,
terribly worried, vows to bring him home, departs with Jennifer.

**Why the Parent.** The register matches — stable, focused inward on the hearth rather than outward on the
wider world, having already proven the capacity for sacrifice. "Vows to bring him home" is a Parent-state
action: responsibility taken for someone specific.

**But the real reason is a structural interlock worth building on.** The Parent's Truth, per your own profile,
is *"a lived, daily version of the Hero's own — 'all my actions affect those I love' — made prosaic rather
than heroic."*

That is, word for word, **the Truth of the Hero Arc Jennifer is on.** Which means Human #2 doesn't have to
teach her anything, explain anything, or deliver a single line about what matters. He simply has to act like
himself in front of her. He is a walking, daily demonstration of the exact Truth her arc requires her to
learn — the prosaic version of the heroic thing she has to become. That satisfies her constraint that *"she
cannot be told her actions matter"* while still giving the lesson a visible source.

**What this constrains, lightly:**
- His utility is **relational, not worldly.** The profile notes a Parent's narrative usefulness is almost
  always in relation to a specific younger character, not to the wider world. Don't give him a plot of his
  own beyond the rescue.
- **Parent/Maiden is the definitional site of first individuation** — so once the group unites, his most
  loaded relationship may be with Lucy rather than with Jennifer.
- **The failure mode is named:** a Parent who represents the Truth badly becomes the Maiden's *antagonist*
  rather than her guide — the "Too-Good Mother / Naïve Father." Worth knowing that's the specific way this
  character could go wrong.

## One hook this surfaced, flagged rather than decided

Lucy's Maiden Arc names a **"Naïve Father"** figure — someone who fails to see through the Predator's
Proposal, or is too eager to accept it. Human #2 has known Human #1 since early childhood and is the person
least able to see him clearly.

That makes Human #2 a natural candidate for Lucy's currently-empty **Fake Ally** slot — and it fits the
framework's definition exactly, which insists a Fake Ally is *not* a traitor but someone acting in genuine
accordance with their own interests, which happen to conflict. A man whose whole motivation is loyalty to his
childhood friend, acting entirely in good faith, could obstruct Lucy's individuation simply by being unable to
see that friend honestly. No villainy required.

Worth considering; not decided here.

---

## Not doing, on purpose

No Enneagram derivation, no Lie/Truth interrogation, no beat-by-beat archetypal pass, no shadow-archetype
work for either human. If one of them later needs it, the methodology is there — but the page budget belongs
to the dolls.
