# Jennifer Lin — Doorway #1, Midpoint/Mirror Moment, Doorway #2: Constraint Specifications

2026-09-21. **Not the beats themselves** — the *attributes* each beat has to have, and the ones it can't, so
that whatever event eventually fills the slot is guaranteed to fit the character and the structure.

Built against `reference/story-structure-definitions.md` (the full composite) and her Stage 1 Derived Sheet at
`../../background notes/characters/Jennifer Lin - Stage 1 Derived Sheet.md`. Everything here is **proposed** —
a constraint that turns out to be wrong for the book should be struck, not worked around.

**On the Midpoint:** it is genuinely open-ended — Snyder allows False Victory *or* False Defeat, Bell's Mirror
Moment allows the three-question form *or* four different flavors of "death," and the Campbell beats attach
wherever they fit. So that section is split in two: **constraints that hold no matter which shape you pick**,
then **a menu of candidate shapes**, each with what it would additionally require. The menu is a set of options
to choose among or combine, not a recommendation.

**Working from:** Social 9w1 (65%) + Social 4w5 undercurrent (35%). Defense: **dissociation** — a dimming of
awareness under threat. Refuge: the **Inner Sanctum**, made literal as her night-sky habit. Flaw: terrified of
bears, great difficulty facing danger. Goal: to apply her passion for astronomy meaningfully. Built for
meteorology and radio engineering. Confirmed ending: maritime/celestial navigation.

**The core structural difficulty, stated up front:** a Nine's characteristic failure is *not choosing* —
attention runs outward toward what others want, and asked what she wants, she struggles to answer. Both her
Doorways are commitment beats. They are therefore the hardest beats in the book for her specifically, and
should be written as such rather than smoothed over.

## Doorway #1 / Crossing the First Threshold (~20% of her own arc — see Open Threads on placement)

*Framework: the hero commits and enters the new world — literally or internally. Should read as a door closing
behind them; the old rules no longer apply.*

Plot slot: Human #2 departs to find Human #1; she leaves with him.

### MUST be true

1. **She chooses actively, and the difficulty shown is specifically the difficulty of locating her own want.**
   The tension in the scene is not "will she be brave" — it's whether she can identify a desire that is hers
   at all. That's her type's actual wound, and it's more interesting than nerve.
2. **She must have a reason of her own, not merely go along with his.** A Nine adopting someone else's agenda
   is the failure mode, not the threshold. **The available own-reason is radio:** Human #1 went *radio-silent*,
   and she is a radio engineer — pulling a weak signal out of noise is literally her profession. Her built
   purpose and the plot's inciting problem are the same problem. She is not accompanying a search; she is the
   person who can conduct one.
3. **The danger is real, known to her, and understood before she commits.** Her flaw is difficulty facing
   danger. If she doesn't know what the route is, the threshold is free. She must know, and go.
4. **If she hesitates, the resolution comes from inside her.** A Nine who is persuaded hasn't crossed
   anything — she's accommodated. Nobody may ask her twice and have the second ask be what works.
5. **Her competence is established before departure.** The reader must know she's a real meteorologist and
   radio engineer, so her usefulness later isn't a surprise and her self-deprecation is visibly inaccurate.
6. **The Inner Sanctum is shown intact first.** The night sky, the solitude, the cup of coolant — the "before"
   state of the defense the journey will pressure.
7. **Departure costs her the conditions her Sanctum depends on:** a known sky, a safe place to sit,
   predictable nights. She's trading a reliable refuge for an unreliable one.
8. **The door closes in a way specific to her legal status.** She's property in a pre-personhood era; leaving
   Vancouver with a man who doesn't own her has a status consequence she can't reverse. This is the version of
   "the old rules no longer apply" that only a non-person protagonist can have.
9. **Write the earlier Cascadia.** This book is set in the **Republic of Cascadia**, before it becomes the
   People's Democratic Republic — see `../../background notes/Vancouver - Lower Mainland Cascadia
   Worldbuilding.md`. Any institutional or official texture in this beat belongs to the earlier state.

### CANNOT be true

1. **She cannot be brought.** No "he needs a radio operator, so he takes her." She goes; she is not taken.
2. **She cannot be fleeing.** Nines acquiesce and stay rather than run, and her seed shows no cruelty to
   escape. A flight reads as someone else's psychology.
3. **She cannot be unafraid.** Courage at the threshold empties the arc. She goes *while* afraid.
4. **Her fear cannot be tested, encountered, or overcome here.** The bear is not at the doorway. She commits in
   the abstract; the bill comes later.
5. **She cannot dissociate through the decision.** Her defense must be conspicuously unavailable in this one
   moment — otherwise she hasn't decided, she's drifted, and a drift is not a Doorway.
6. **Astronomy cannot be the stated reason.** Her Goal is the arc's destination. Leaving *in order to do
   astronomy* delivers her want in Act 1.
7. **She cannot be confident, capable-seeming, or socially expressive here.** The Social Nine countertype's
   outgoingness is end-state motion, not opening condition. At the threshold she presents as the 4w5 surface:
   timid, quiet, self-deprecating.
8. **External validation cannot be what convinces her.** No one telling her she's capable and that being the
   thing that tips it.

## Midpoint / Mirror Moment (50%)

*Framework: Snyder — False Victory or False Defeat. Bell — Who am I? What have I become? What must I become?
Or: "I'm probably going to die," where death may be physical, social, professional, or psychological.*

### Constraints that hold regardless of which shape you choose

**MUST be true**

1. **Her defense is on stage.** The reader must watch her dissociate — see her go absent — and see what it
   costs. Whatever else the beat does, it has to make the dimming visible, because it's invisible to her.
2. **The three questions are answerable in her own terms.** *Who am I* — the one who isn't there when it
   matters. *What have I become.* *What must I become* — present.
3. **She is alone, or functionally alone, at the decisive instant.** A Nine's growth can't occur inside the
   group's agenda, and her Sanctum is a solitary space by definition.
4. **The hazard is one already established** — bears, per her stated weakness and the route worldbuilding. Not
   a newly invented threat.
5. **Her competence is present and insufficient on its own.** She may do real radio or weather work here; it
   must not be enough, because what's missing is presence, not skill.
6. **Her Goal is reframed.** "Apply astronomy meaningfully" must be revealed as too small, or as a way of
   *staying inside the Sanctum* — doing what she loves in a form that keeps her absent from the world. That's
   the psychological death available to her: the refuge mistaken for the destination.
7. **The sky is involved.** Her Sanctum is literally the night sky; the beat should occur under it, and it
   should read differently than it did at the start.

**CANNOT be true**

1. **She cannot conquer her fear here.** That's Act 3.
2. **She cannot be saved purely by another character.** Rescue at the midpoint makes her the object of her own
   arc — the specific risk for a passive-prone type.
3. **She cannot become confident.** Social Nine expressiveness is her ~80% motion, not her 50%.
4. **It cannot be primarily a social death.** She isn't fighting for standing in the group.
5. **The hazard cannot be defeated.** Resolving the threat drains the back half.
6. **She cannot verbalize the realization to the group.** Consistent with a Social Nine hiding pain and not
   burdening others — and it preserves the material for Doorway #2.
7. **It cannot be caused by her own recklessness.** She has none. The situation arrives; she doesn't invite it.
8. **It cannot be coincidental** — same causality rule as everywhere else.

### Candidate shapes — options, not a decision

- **False Defeat — the physical-death flavor, and the cruelest version available to her.** "I'm probably going
  to die," literally, in the wilderness. What makes it hers rather than generic peril: the framework's own
  Type 9 material notes dissociation's advantage is *real* in a genuinely threatening moment — so her mind will
  offer her the dimming, it will feel like relief, and it will nearly kill her. **Her way of surviving is the
  thing that will kill her.** Additional requirement: the reader has to understand the mechanism even though
  she doesn't, or it reads as her simply freezing.
- **False Victory — she saves them and cannot claim it.** Her radio or weather work is what preserves the
  group, and she is structurally incapable of letting it count: Social Nine won't occupy the spotlight, Social
  Four's buried competitiveness stays buried under visible modesty. Additional requirement: someone else must
  be available to absorb the credit, without malice.
- **False Victory — the search succeeds on its own terms and fails on hers.** The thing sought is found, and
  it delivers none of what she needed from finding it. Additional requirement: the find must be real, not a
  false lead, or it's a setback rather than a victory.
- **Psychological death — the Sanctum turns.** The refuge is revealed as the mechanism of her absence. Per the
  source, the more a Nine inhabits the Inner Sanctum, the more they drift into hazy daydreaming and away from
  meaningful action. Additional requirement: the Sanctum must have been shown as genuinely *good* first, or
  this reads as the book punishing her for the thing that gives her joy.
- **Professional death.** Her instruments, her signal work, or her forecasting fails when it matters — the
  built purpose proving inadequate. Additional requirement: distinguish it from "insufficient" above
  (insufficient ≠ failed); this shape needs an actual failure, not a shortfall.
- **Atonement-flavored — the idealized figure turns out to be mortal and flawed.** Her template carries an
  `ATONEMENT WITH THE FATHER` slot at the midpoint, defined as confronting whatever holds ultimate power in
  one's life and frequently realizing that figure is flawed — possibly having to rely on them anyway. This
  shape is unusually well-suited to her type: the source material notes Nines habitually relate to an
  *idealized idea* of someone rather than the person as they actually are, and are drawn toward stronger, more
  assertive figures who supply the vitality they suppress in themselves — an arrangement that works until
  reality intrudes and shatters it. Her midpoint could be exactly that shattering. Additional requirement: she
  must have visibly idealized someone in Act 1 for the illusion to have something to break.
- **Temptation-flavored — the invitation to go back to sleep.** Her template has a `TEMPTATION` slot
  immediately before the midpoint, and for a Nine the temptation is never ambition; it's the offer of peace.
  A way back to the quiet, to the Sanctum, to not having to want anything. Additional requirement: the offer
  must be genuinely available and genuinely comfortable, not a trap she can see through.
- **Belly of the Whale-flavored — total submersion.** Circumstances close over her entirely. Additional
  requirement: it must remove her options without removing her perception, or it becomes another form of the
  dissociation rather than a confrontation with it.

## Doorway #2 / Break into Three / Crossing the Return Threshold (~80%)

*Framework: the moment of clarity — the hero finally understands what they need to do, and more importantly
**who they need to become** to do it. Where the A-story and B-story intertwine, and the B-story's lesson clicks
into place.*

**Who she needs to become: present.** Someone who registers danger and acts anyway, and whose own want counts.

### MUST be true

1. **She stays present under threat, as a deliberate choice.** The exact inverse of the Midpoint. Not
   fearlessness — presence. The beat is her declining to dim.
2. **She states a want, out loud, and it is hers.** For a Nine this is the whole growth axis, and it may be the
   first time in her existence she does it. Hard to overstate how much weight this single action carries for
   her.
3. **Her skills become decisive**, and in the direction of her confirmed ending — radio and weather converting
   toward celestial navigation, intersecting the vessel plot.
4. **It is witnessed.** The Social Nine's growth runs toward participation and, per the source, unusually good
   leadership *because the motivation is unselfish rather than image-driven.* This is where that becomes
   visible to other people.
5. **She visibly declines the Sanctum at least once, on the page** — choosing the real people and the real
   problem over the idealized private space.
6. **Her fear remains, fully intact.** Terrified and functional simultaneously. That combination is the beat.
7. **The B-story's lesson clicks.** (B-story currently undefined — see Open Threads.)

### CANNOT be true

1. **Her fear cannot be cured, explained away, or revealed as unfounded.** The bears stay dangerous; she stays
   afraid.
2. **She cannot be pushed into it.** Same rule as Doorway #1, and it matters more here.
3. **She cannot dissociate at all in this beat.** Not once, not briefly.
4. **She cannot achieve the astronomy goal here.** That's Closing Image material.
5. **No personality transplant.** The change is presence, not temperament — she does not become bold, loud, or
   different in kind. A Nine who suddenly reads as an Eight is a broken arc.
6. **The group cannot decide for her.**
7. **It cannot happen before the stated want exists.** Order is load-bearing: want first, then action. Action
   without the stated want is just accommodation wearing a hero's costume.

## The Weiland Layer — One Archetype Only

### The length constraint, and what it forbids

This book is targeted at **ideally under 300 pages, possibly 300–350**. At that length, with a four-person
main cast, Jennifer can realistically occupy **one single Weiland archetypal arc and no more.** That is a hard
budget, and it rules out several things that would otherwise be tempting:

1. **No arc-to-arc transition.** She does not complete her arc and begin the next one. Her arc's Resolution is
   the end of her material — full stop.
2. **The Weiland arc is a *lens on the existing beats*, not an additional set of beats.** Weiland's skeleton
   (Normal World → Inciting Event → First Plot Point → First Pinch Point → Midpoint → Second Pinch Point →
   Later False Peak → Third Plot Point → Climax → Climactic Moment → Resolution) has to be *the same events*
   as the composite beat sheet, read a second way. A 300-page book with four leads cannot afford two parallel
   beat sheets for one character.
3. **No shadow-archetype detour.** Each arc has a passive and an aggressive shadow counterpart; a doll who
   falls into shadow and is then redeemed needs room this book doesn't have.
4. **She starts at her arc's Beginning state on page one.** No pre-arc runway, no warm-up stage before the
   archetype engages.

### Assigned arc: **the Hero Arc — "Slaying the Dragon."** *Normal World → Adventure World.*

Story Type: Quest. Symbolic setting scope: Village.

**Lie → Truth:** *"My actions are insignificant in the overall scope of the world"* → *"All my actions affect
those I love."*

**Why this one, and not the Maiden Arc — the only other live candidate at her life-stage:** the Hero's Lie is
an exact statement of a Nine's central error. Her whole defense — dimming her own awareness, deferring her own
wants, going absent under pressure — rests on the premise that her presence doesn't change anything. That is
*"my actions are insignificant"* as a way of life. And the Truth, *"all my actions affect those I love,"* is
precisely what her Midpoint has to teach her (her absence has consequences) and what her Doorway #2 has to
prove (her presence does). The arc's named axis is **Complacency vs. Courage**, and her stated flaw is
difficulty facing danger — the arc is aimed directly at the flaw she already has.

**One adaptation required, and it matters.** The Hero Arc's default flavor includes grandiosity and the lure
of unchecked power — an immature grasp of strength that has to be integrated. **Jennifer has no relationship
to power or grandiosity whatsoever**, and importing that flavor would break her. Her version of the same beat
runs in the opposite direction: her temptation is never *too much self*, it's **retreat** — the pull back into
the Inner Sanctum, back to not mattering. Wherever the arc template calls for grandiosity, substitute
withdrawal. The structural function (an immature relationship to her own capacity, requiring integration) is
preserved; the polarity flips.

### How the Hero beats land on the three beats already specified

- **Beginning — "Complacent But Unfulfilled."** Chafing against an unlived life but not yet brave enough to
  leave. This is a textbook description of an average Nine, and it's already what her seed describes.
- **Inciting Event — "Call to Adventure."** A stranger or a strange discovery draws her in against her tribe's
  advice; she initially resists out of complacency. Her Debate beat *is* that resistance, and the framework
  expects it — resistance here is correct, not a flaw in the plotting.
- **First Plot Point — "Crosses the Threshold" = her Doorway #1.** The trouble becomes personal and undeniable,
  and she leaves through a Door of No Return — "excited despite his grief, though his grasp of power is still
  immature." Adapted: her grasp of her *own significance* is still immature.
- **First Pinch Point — "Who Do You Think You Are?"** Pushback forces her to confront that she's been *playing*
  at this rather than actually doing it. This lands perfectly on her established self-deprecation, and gives
  that trait a structural job rather than leaving it as texture.
- **Midpoint — "Remembering Who She Is."** An ambiguous confrontation yielding a real glimpse of her own power
  and identity. Compatible with every Midpoint shape in the menu above; it adds one requirement — whichever
  shape is chosen must include **a genuine glimpse of her own capacity**, not only a glimpse of her failure.
  The beat is ambiguous by design: she sees what she could be *and* it doesn't hold.
- **Second Pinch Point — "It's All Your Fault."** Betrayed by someone trusted and blamed for a real setback.
  Worth flagging how well this pairs with her type: a Nine idealizes people rather than seeing them accurately,
  so she is structurally slow to see a betrayal coming — and the blame will land on someone who genuinely
  struggles to defend her own case.
- **Later False Peak — "Means, Not Ends"** (~62–75%, *not* the Midpoint). A desperate gambit wins, but only by
  compromising everything she's learned — a hollow victory bought the wrong way. For her, the wrong means are
  obvious: winning by going absent, by letting someone else carry it, by not being there.
- **Third Plot Point — "All Is Lost."** A great loss forcing the real choice between the self she was and
  something new.
- **Climax — "Resurrection."** She chooses life and love and rises again, *this time willingly facing even
  death.* This is the single strongest argument for assigning her the Hero Arc: her named flaw is great
  difficulty facing danger, and this arc's climax is defined as willingly facing death. The arc terminates
  exactly on her wound.
- **Climactic Moment — "Dragon Vanquished."** Her inner transformation is what makes the outer victory
  possible. **The Dragon's identity is an open decision** — candidates: the literal bear, the wilderness
  itself, or her own dissociation externalized. The framework's own note supports the third reading: it frames
  the rescued figure as a symbolic externalization of the hero's own psyche rather than merely a separate
  person, which would make the man she's going to find a stand-in for the part of herself that went silent.
- **Resolution — "Kingdom at Peace."** She returns with the healing elixir and takes on real responsibility
  rather than going home unchanged. Her confirmed ending — navigating for the group at sea — is already that.

### Additional MUST / CANNOT from the archetype

**MUST**

1. **Her Midpoint must contain a real glimpse of her own capacity**, however briefly it holds — see above.
2. **Her Climax must involve willingly facing danger**, not surviving it accidentally or being spared it.
3. **Someone she trusts must fail her at the Second Pinch Point**, and it must be the kind of failure she is
   least equipped to see coming or to contest.

**CANNOT**

1. **She cannot end the book having begun a second arc.** No gesture toward what comes next.
2. **No grandiosity, at any point.** The default flavor is wrong for her; substitute withdrawal — see the
   adaptation note above.
3. **The Dragon cannot be vanquished by anyone else.** The outer victory has to be the visible proof of her
   inner one; if another character resolves it, the arc's terminal beat is spent on the wrong person.
4. **She cannot be told her actions matter.** The Truth has to be demonstrated to her by consequence, not
   delivered to her as a line of dialogue.

## Open Threads

1. **Arc placement vs. book placement — a real blocking decision.** The premise has Human #1 going radio-silent
   *some time after* leaving Whitehorse, and only then does Human #2 depart. So her personal arc cannot sit at
   the book's own 20/50/80 marks unless Vancouver is intercut from the opening. Either the book runs parallel
   tracks from page one, or her arc is offset later in book-time and runs on its own clock. This changes pacing
   substantially and should be settled before beats get written. Everything above is placement-independent and
   holds under either choice.
2. **Her departure shares its mechanism with the book's other doll arc** — leaving alongside a departing human
   man. The constraints above deliberately push her *reason* toward the signal problem only she can solve, so
   the two sequences don't read as the same scene twice, but the pair should be checked against each other
   directly.
3. **B-story undefined**, and Doorway #2 can't be fully specified without it, since the framework requires the
   B-story's lesson to click into place there.
4. **Fake Ally and Opposition undefined.** Her template carries slots for both, including `FAKE-ALLY ATTACK` in
   the run-up to the midpoint. The framework's definition is specific: not a traitor, but someone acting in
   genuine accordance with their own interests, which happen to conflict with hers. Worth noting this concept
   is especially live for her, since a Nine idealizes people and would be slow to register the conflict.
5. **Care Package undefined** — the setup half of a pair whose Q-Factor payoff at ~78% feeds directly into
   Doorway #2 above.
6. **Physical description unwritten** despite 8 reference images existing at `../../doll/Jennifer Lin/`.
7. **Her arc template still carries no Weiland slots.** Her archetype is now assigned (see the Weiland Layer
   section above), but `y-template/structure/character arcs/doll.md` — and therefore her own arc file — has no
   rows for the archetypal beats. Since the archetype is meant to be a lens over the same events rather than a
   second beat sheet, this may not need fixing at all; if it does, it's a line-wide change to the canonical
   template.
8. **The Dragon's identity is undecided** — see the Climactic Moment note above.
