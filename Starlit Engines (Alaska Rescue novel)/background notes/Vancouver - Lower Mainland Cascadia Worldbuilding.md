# Vancouver / Lower Mainland Cascadia — Post-War Worldbuilding

Working notes, 2026-09-19. Built by applying Colin Woodard's *American Nations* framework to Vancouver/Lower
Mainland BC specifically, then extending it forward through this universe's own 2083 nuclear war and
subsequent ~150–235 years of reconstruction (Alaska Rescue sits somewhere in the pre-2318 First Interwar
Period, before robots have legal personhood — no more precise date pinned yet). **Two open questions this is
meant to serve at once**: what Jennifer Lin was originally built for, and how the group plausibly acquires a
boat by Act 3. Everything below is proposed worldbuilding, not settled canon.

## Which Cascadia This Book Is Set In — Settled Canon

**This book takes place in the *Republic of Cascadia*.** Author-confirmed 2026-09-21: the Republic of Cascadia
later became the **People's Democratic Republic of Cascadia** — the name that appears on the nation roster and
in the later-era material — with the date and circumstances of that transition not yet determined.

Two consequences for anything written in this file or drawn from it:

1. **Write the earlier state, not the later one.** Institutions, officialdom, how the state describes itself,
   and above all how it treats property (which, before the 2318 Jeju-do ruling, includes both dolls in this
   book's cast) should reflect the Republic. Don't import the later regime's character by assumption.
2. **This book is therefore the *before* picture.** Whatever it establishes about Cascadia's civic life becomes
   the baseline that a later transition has to be consistent with. That's a reason to keep such details
   deliberate and sparse rather than incidental — every one of them constrains work that hasn't been done yet.

## The Real-World Base: Vancouver Under the Left Coast Framework

Per `worldbuilding/extractions/American_Nations_Extraction.md` and this project's own
`American_Nations_to_New_North_America_Cross-Reference.md`, Cascadia corresponds to Woodard's **Left Coast**
(with the interior parts of Washington/Oregon actually belonging to the culturally distinct Far West — a
discrepancy already flagged in that cross-reference file). The Left Coast's own profile: founded by a
19th-century fusion of Yankee missionaries/merchants/intellectuals who controlled the towns and civic
institutions, and Appalachian/Borderlander frontier settlers who filled the surrounding countryside — producing
a genuinely idealistic, reformist civic culture (the historical cradle of the environmental movement, later the
countercultural and tech-utopian movements) layered on top of an individualist, self-exploration-oriented
frontier sensibility Yankeedom itself never fully embraced. Economically: originally maritime trade and
institution-building, later the center of environmentalism and, in the real world's modern era, the global tech
industry. Its closest ally is Yankeedom; its natural opposite is the Far West sitting just inland across the
same mountain range.

**A real-world wrinkle worth stating plainly:** Vancouver/BC's actual historical founding population was
British colonial (Hudson's Bay Company, then wider British settlement), not the Yankee-New-England-missionary
pattern Woodard traces for the American Left Coast further south. Woodard's own book is US-focused and treats
Canada as adjacent but distinct. This project's existing map already folds BC into one unified Cascadia
alongside Washington and Oregon, so this file treats Vancouver as culturally continuous with the Left Coast
pattern **as an authorial extension of the established in-fiction map, not something Woodard's own research
actually supports for BC specifically.** Flagged so it's clear where the book's authority ends and where this
project's own worldbuilding choice begins.

## Extending the Pattern Through 2083 and Reconstruction

The founding-population logic doesn't stop at the 19th century — it has to survive a global nuclear war and
whatever population movement followed it. Proposed reading: Vancouver's harbor and existing infrastructure
(unlike a larger, more obvious strategic target) plausibly survived 2083 relatively intact, making it a natural
rallying point for a wider hinterland's displaced population during reconstruction — not a preserved Left Coast
culture so much as a **reasserted** one, actively rebuilt by whoever took charge of reconstruction rather than
passively inherited. Whether that reconstruction was led by descendants of the original Yankee-reformist civic
class reclaiming their own pattern, or by a genuinely new population that simply re-derived similar
institutions independently, is an open authorial choice — either supports what follows.

## Proposed Post-War Economic Base

Two pillars, chosen specifically because together they can explain both open questions at once:

### 1. Maritime trade and fishing — the backbone

When overland infrastructure (roads, rail, long-distance telecom) is devastated or unreliable across a
shattered continent, coastal shipping becomes *more* essential, not less — the lifeline connecting surviving
port cities to each other (up the coast toward the Republic of Alaska, down toward wherever else survived)
when nothing overland can be trusted. This directly implies Vancouver supports a large, active working fishing
and coastal-trade fleet as a matter of basic post-war survival, not a lifestyle industry — which is exactly the
infrastructure the story needs for a boat to plausibly exist to be acquired at all.

### 2. Reconstruction-era precision/technical industry

The Left Coast's own established later-era reformist and tech-utopian tendency gives a natural throughline: a
devastated world desperately needs rebuilt *basic* scientific infrastructure — remapping land redrawn by war
and new borders, resynchronizing a fractured transport network's timekeeping, rebuilding optical instruments
from scratch, reconnecting isolated communities by radio, assaying salvaged and scavenged pre-war materials,
forecasting weather for a maritime economy that now depends on it more than ever, and — in a fractured, armed,
often dangerous post-war world with real anti-robot political tension already brewing — maintaining genuine
coastal defense capability. **This is, not coincidentally, a direct home for every one of Jennifer's seven
remaining built-purpose candidates** (Surveying, Horology, Optics, Artillery/Ballistics, Radio Engineering,
Spectroscopy, Meteorology) — Vancouver as a surviving reformist civic hub is a plausible regional center for
exactly this kind of "civilization-rebuilding" technical labor, whichever specific field ends up being hers.

## How the Boat Gets Acquired — Concrete Mechanisms

Given pillar #1 above, several grounded routes, not mutually exclusive:

- **A family or employer connection** to a fishing or coastal-trade operation — belonging to Human #1 or #2's
  family, or to whatever institution employed Jennifer or Lucy.
- **A retiring/decommissioned vessel.** A working post-war fleet this old would have aging boats being sold
  off cheap, quietly abandoned, or left salvageable — no elaborate mechanism needed, just ordinary fleet
  turnover.
- **The informal/gray economy.** Neither Jennifer nor Lucy has legal personhood in this era — they're property,
  built for a purpose, in a frontier-reconstruction society that would plausibly run a real black-market/barter
  economy alongside its official one. A favor called in, a theft, an under-the-table purchase — all fit the
  setting's own established power imbalance without requiring new invention.

**The efficient version — one choice answers both open questions at once:** if Jennifer's built purpose puts
her in *direct daily contact* with the people who actually own or operate vessels, the boat's availability
becomes a natural consequence of who she already is, not a separate coincidence the plot has to manufacture.
By that measure, **Meteorology** (a maritime forecasting service) and **Radio Engineering** (coastal
communications) are the two strongest of the seven candidates specifically for this dual purpose — both put
her inside the working fishing/trade fleet's own daily operation. **Horology** is a plausible third (marine
chronometers were historically *the* navigation-critical instrument, so a horologist would have real standing
reason to be embedded in that same maritime world). Surveying, Optics, Spectroscopy, and Artillery/Ballistics
read more as general "civilization-rebuilding institution" work, less directly tied to the boat-owning
community — not incompatible with the ending, just requiring an extra connective step the other three don't.

## Still Open

- Whether Vancouver's post-war reconstruction was led by descendants of the original Left Coast civic class or
  a newly-converged population — either works, not yet decided.
- Which of the seven remaining candidates (if any) actually wins for Jennifer's built purpose — this file
  narrows the field toward Meteorology/Radio Engineering/Horology on efficiency grounds, but doesn't decide it.
- Whether the boat-acquisition mechanism should be the "efficient" version (tied directly to Jennifer's own
  built purpose) or a separate thread entirely (family connection, theft, salvage) — both are supported above.
