# Jennifer Lin — What Was She Actually Built *For*?

Working notes, 2026-09-19. **Not a decision — a set of candidates for the author to choose or react to.**

## The Premise, Stated Precisely

*Alaska Rescue* is set during the First Interwar Period, **before** the Jeju-do ruling (2318). Neither Jennifer
nor Lucy has legal personhood at this point in the timeline — they were each built *for* something, as
property with a function, not brought into being as free agents who later found a purpose. Lucy's answer is
already obvious and already on record: she's a master-level engine mechanic, built for a railyard, and that's
exactly the job she's doing when the story opens. Jennifer's own character sheet currently has this listed as
"a currently-undetermined purpose" — the one open question this file exists to help close.

**The shape the answer needs to have, matching Lucy's own precedent:** a concrete, utilitarian, pre-personhood
commission — something a real employer or institution in Vancouver, BC would have had actual use for — with
her established Passion (astronomy) growing out of the skill set *incidentally*, the way Maggie Aarden's
private Steampunk art grew out of her own industrial-yard repair competency rather than being what she was
built to do. Astronomy should be the thing Jennifer does with the skill once nobody's watching, not the stated
reason she exists.

## Candidate Fields — Full Detail

Eight fields where a skill that's *obligatory* to the profession is also *realistically applicable* to
astronomy, beyond the already-obvious case of maritime celestial navigation. Each entry: what the field
actually requires, why that specific skill transfers to astronomy, and what commissioning Jennifer for that
purpose would plausibly look like in a Vancouver/Cascadia, pre-2318 setting.

### 1. Surveying / Geodesy

**Obligatory skill:** precise angular measurement with a theodolite, and triangulation — fixing exact positions
on the Earth's surface by measuring angles between known points and unknown ones.

**Why it transfers:** this is not an analogy — historically, surveyors establishing reference meridians and
baseline points did so *by observing stars directly*, a discipline literally called geodetic astronomy. The
actual bench skill (reading an angle to fractions of a degree, correcting for atmospheric refraction near the
horizon) is the same skill an observational astronomer uses on a telescope.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for a surveying company or a railway/infrastructure concern doing
land-division or route-planning work around Cascadia — the kind of precision instrument-reading a human
surveyor's assistant would need to perform for hours without fatigue or error. Her astronomy interest would be
a direct, obvious outgrowth: the same instrument, same technique, pointed up instead of at a distant survey
marker.

### 2. Horology (Precision Clockmaking)

**Obligatory skill:** building and maintaining mechanisms accurate to fractions of a second.

**Why it transfers:** astronomy is fundamentally time-dependent — tracking when a star transits, running a
telescope's clockwork drive to counter the Earth's own rotation, correlating observations made at different
times. The two fields share literal common ancestry: the marine chronometer was invented specifically to solve
an astronomical/navigational problem (determining longitude via lunar distance).

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for a clockmaker's workshop or a railway's own timekeeping department
(railroads in this era depend on precise, synchronized time across a whole network) — steady, exacting,
mechanical work. Her astronomy interest follows naturally: someone who spends her existence keeping mechanisms
in perfect time would recognize the sky itself as the original clock.

### 3. Optics / Lens- and Mirror-Grinding (Scientific Instrument Making)

**Obligatory skill:** grinding glass to a precise curvature and polishing out imperfections invisible to the
naked eye.

**Why it transfers:** this is quite literally how telescopes get built. An optician's bench skill and a
telescope-maker's skill are the same craft aimed at a different target — one corrects human eyesight, the other
extends it toward the sky.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for an optical-instrument workshop — grinding lenses for spectacles,
surveying equipment, or ship's instruments. This is the candidate with the most *direct* physical link to
astronomy of all eight: she wouldn't just be skilled in a transferable way, she'd have the literal hands that
built the tool she'd eventually use to look up. **Strong candidate**, worth flagging explicitly.

### 4. Aviation Navigation (Long-Range/Polar Flight) — **ELIMINATED, author 2026-09-19**

**Obligatory skill:** astronavigation with a bubble sextant from a moving, vibrating aircraft — genuinely
harder than shipboard celestial navigation, since there's no stable horizon to reference.

**Why it transfers:** direct star-identification and angular-measurement overlap with astronomy, performed
under worse conditions than almost any other profession that uses the technique.

**Ruled out, definitively:** Jennifer's arc ends with her doing maritime/celestial navigation — see the note on
Candidate 9 below. Building her original purpose as *aviation* navigation puts her one skill-transfer step away
from what she ends up doing, which collapses her arc into "the same kind of navigation, on a different
vehicle" rather than an actual change. The author's own stated reasoning: it doesn't create character change
over time. Kept in this file only as a record of what was considered and why it's out, not as a live option.

### 5. Artillery / Ballistics (Military or Naval Gunnery)

**Obligatory skill:** calculating a projectile's curved trajectory — accounting for gravity, wind, and at long
range even the Earth's own rotation — to hit a target that can't be directly aimed at.

**Why it transfers:** the underlying math is a close cousin of orbital mechanics — both are about predicting a
curved path through a field of forces from a known position and velocity. A gunnery calculator's intuition for
"where will this object actually be" generalizes directly to tracking celestial motion.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for a Cascadia military or coastal-defense application — a less
"dreamer-coded" origin than the others, but a genuinely dark, interesting contrast: a mind built to calculate
where to send violence, that finds peace calculating where distant stars actually are instead. Worth
considering specifically if you want her original purpose to sit in tension with who she is now, rather than
flow naturally into it.

### 6. Radio Engineering / Signal Operation

**Obligatory skill:** building and tuning antennas, and extracting a genuinely weak signal out of background
noise.

**Why it transfers:** this is the entire discipline radio astronomy is built on. A radio telescope is,
mechanically, just a very large and very sensitive radio receiver — the noise-floor problem a radio operator
already knows how to solve is the same problem, at a different scale.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for a communications company or a coastal radio station — Cascadia's
maritime trade would have real, ongoing need for reliable long-range radio operation. Gives her a plausible
reason to already understand signal/noise problems, patience with faint or intermittent data, and comfort
working alone at odd hours — all traits that also describe an astronomer.

### 7. Spectroscopy (Industrial/Analytical Chemistry)

**Obligatory skill:** identifying a substance's exact composition from the specific wavelengths of light it
absorbs or emits.

**Why it transfers:** stellar spectroscopy — determining what a star is made of, and how fast it's moving via
redshift — is the *identical technique*, pointed at the sky instead of a bench sample.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for an industrial or assay laboratory doing material analysis — less
obviously "Cascadia-flavored" than the others, but a clean, direct technical link: she wouldn't be
transferring a skill by analogy, she'd already know exactly how to read a spectrum, just never one from a star
before.

### 8. Meteorology

**Obligatory skill:** reading atmospheric conditions — humidity, turbulence, layered air — and predicting how
they'll change.

**Why it transfers:** astronomers call this exact problem "seeing" — how much the atmosphere will distort or
blur an observation. A meteorologist's trained instinct for when the air will behave and when it won't is
directly useful for knowing when observing conditions will actually be good.

**As a built purpose:** Jennifer built for a weather-observation station or a maritime forecasting service —
again a strong Cascadia-coastal fit, and a nice thematic doubling: someone whose job was reading the sky for
practical, immediate reasons (will the ships be safe today) who fell in love with reading the sky for its own
sake.

### 9. Maritime Celestial Navigation — **ELIMINATED, author 2026-09-19**

**Obligatory skill:** fixing a ship's position using a sextant, star sights, and precise time — the original
skill this whole exercise started from.

**Ruled out, definitively:** Jennifer ends the book doing maritime/celestial navigation — this is now confirmed
canon, not a guess. Building her original purpose as maritime navigation would mean she begins the story
already doing, professionally, the exact thing she ends the story doing. Same flat-arc problem as Aviation
Navigation (#4), and more direct, since this isn't a close cousin of her ending, it *is* her ending. Kept in
this file as a record of what was considered and why it's out, not as a live option.

## Status: Seven Candidates Remain Open

Two eliminated (#4 Aviation, #9 Maritime — both ruled out for the identical reason: too close to Jennifer's
confirmed ending, which would flatten her arc). At that point seven candidates were open — Surveying/Geodesy
(#1), Horology (#2), Optics/Instrument-Making (#3), Artillery/Ballistics (#5), Radio Engineering (#6),
Spectroscopy (#7), and Meteorology (#8) — with the Vancouver/Lower Mainland worldbuilding (see
`Vancouver - Lower Mainland Cascadia Worldbuilding.md`) flagged as the deciding factor, since whichever field
won needed to double as quiet worldbuilding the way Lucy's railyard placement already does for Whitehorse.

## RESOLVED — 2026-09-19: Meteorology + Radio Engineering, Combined

Once that worldbuilding existed (Cascadia's post-war maritime/coastal-trade economy — see the file above), the
author confirmed **#6 (Radio Engineering) and #8 (Meteorology) together**, not chosen between — a single
maritime meteorology-and-communications role for Cascadia's coastal economy. The full skill breakdown, and how
those specific skills produce her established stargazing habit, now lives in its own dedicated file:
**`Jennifer Lin - Built Purpose (Meteorology and Radio Engineering).md`** (same folder). That file supersedes
this one's own #6 and #8 entries above — treat those two short entries here as historical record of the
candidate-comparison stage, not the current, fuller version.

Surveying, Horology, Optics, Artillery/Ballistics, and Spectroscopy are no longer live candidates.
