## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village households (Tatar, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt) against city apartments:** where every hand counts, and where children are kept from work.
- **Ural factory towns and monotowns:** men's usefulness tied to a plant.
- **Pensioners, especially rural grandmothers:** child care, gardens, church and mosque upkeep.
- **Disabled workers in sheltered plants.**
- **Muslim, Orthodox and traditional-faith households:** whether worth is framed as God-given or earned.
- **Bashkir and Kazakh herding families:** children's tasks with animals.
- **Erzya and Moksha villages:** a smaller record to check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnopedagogy is a rich Soviet-era genre (read it critically, as some of it is idealized); present-day survey data mostly national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar; Chuvash; Udmurt; Bashkir.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability terms (*инвалид* is official but contested); elder poverty; layoffs and pensions are politically charged; suicide data, if used, with great care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalogs: "этнопедагогика" with each people's name (чуваш, татар, удмурт, мари, мордва, башкир).
2. Folklore indexes (SUS, the East Slavic tale index, *check*; Tatar and Chuvash tale catalogs): the abandoned-elder tale.
3. RLMS-HSE: grandparent child care and garden plots for the Volga-Ural sample sites.
4. Search Tatar-language web for "файдалы кеше" and "кирәкле кеше".
5. Kazan and Ufa libraries: Fakhreddin's advice books in Arabic-script originals and Cyrillic reprints.
