## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars, Mishars, Kryashens (Orthodox Tatars), Nagaibaks:** one language, different faiths and trading or farming pasts.
- **Bashkirs:** mountain, steppe and Trans-Ural groups, from herding to farming; south Ural wild-hive beekeepers.
- **Chuvash:** upper (Viryal) and lower (Anatri), and villages outside Chuvashia.
- **Meadow, Hill and Eastern Mari; northern and southern Udmurts and Besermyans; Erzya; Moksha; Komi-Permyaks:** some keep their own faith and calendar.
- **Russians of Ural factory towns, Old Believers, Orenburg Cossacks:** different inherited disciplines of labor.
- **Kazakhs of the Orenburg and Chelyabinsk steppe;** settler villages of other origins in Bashkortostan (*check*).
- **Town types:** oil towns (Almetyevsk, Neftekamsk), car towns (Naberezhnye Chelny, Tolyatti), closed nuclear towns, one-company towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian-language labor and health statistics; mixed for Tatar and Bashkir (strong institutes, little in English); thin for Mari, Udmurt, Mordvin and Komi-Permyak outside native-language ethnography. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (its tracker carries "Russia"); these sheets write for the Volga-Ural core and the Ural industrial belt.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Tatar and Bashkir (and Arabic script before 1928); Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Erzya and Moksha; German, Finnish and Hungarian for older Finno-Ugric ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mari and Udmurt sacred groves and rites; penal and camp labor (Mordovian and Perm camps); closed cities; language and autonomy politics; outlets with Russian "foreign agent" labels. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
