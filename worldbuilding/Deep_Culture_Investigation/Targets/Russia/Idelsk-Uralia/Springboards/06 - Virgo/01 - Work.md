# Idelsk-Uralia: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha; Kazakh in the southern steppe. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ural factory towns (Nizhny Tagil, Zlatoust, Magnitogorsk) against farm districts:** industrial and peasant work traditions.
- **Oil districts of southeast Tatarstan and west Bashkortostan:** a post-war boom workforce.
- **Defense plants of Izhevsk and Votkinsk:** Udmurt and Russian workers inside a closed-off economy.
- **Chuvash and Mari villages that send seasonal workers away:** work as departure.
- **Bashkir herders of the Trans-Urals against Bashkir farmers in the west:** different seasonal rounds.
- **Tatar trading families, past and present:** the merchant as a model of work.
- **Old Believers; Orenburg Cossacks; Kazakh herders on the southern steppe.**
- **Migrant workers from Central Asia in Ural cities:** their own view of work, and how locals frame it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and Soviet labor history; mixed for native-language sources; thin in English for Mari, Udmurt and Mordvin work traditions.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (plus Arabic script for the Jadid press); Chuvash; Udmurt; Hungarian, Finnish and German for 19th-century field collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** camp and penal labor in Mordovia, Perm and the northern Urals; migrant-worker politics; anything on the work-shy "parasite" status of Soviet times touches law (Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
