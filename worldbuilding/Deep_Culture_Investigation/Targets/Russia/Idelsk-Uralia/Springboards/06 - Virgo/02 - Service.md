## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs, Kryashens, Orthodox Russians:** religious service in three settings.
- **Mari and Udmurt keepers of their own faith:** communal prayer roles that rotate among households.
- **Bashkirs and Orenburg Cossacks:** inherited ideas of armed service to the state.
- **Chuvash, Erzya and Moksha teachers' and enlighteners' circles:** service to a people through schooling.
- **Large-city volunteers (Kazan, Ufa, Yekaterinburg) against village mutual help.**
- **Workers in closed defense cities:** service to the state as a job.
- **Central Asian migrants in service jobs; Kazakhs on the steppe.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on military and Cossack history and on Soviet trade; mixed on present volunteering; thin on domestic service.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; for 18th- and 19th-century service records, Russian archives and Arabic-script Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mari and Udmurt prayer rites; war service in current and recent wars (heavy censorship and grief); conscription politics; Muslim administrations' disputes; domestic and migrant workers' status.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. elibrary.ru (*check*): "служилые татары", "башкирское войско", "кантонная система".
2. Search Tatar-language sites for "хезмәт күрсәтү" and "сәдака" with "мәчет" (mosque) to see what is called service.
3. *DOBRO.RF* and HSE reports: volunteers by region for Tatarstan, Bashkortostan and the Ural oblasts.
4. Chuvash libraries: Yakovlev's testament and studies of the Simbirsk school.
5. Folklore catalogs: recruits' laments ("рекрутские плачи") in Mari, Udmurt and Mordvin collections.
