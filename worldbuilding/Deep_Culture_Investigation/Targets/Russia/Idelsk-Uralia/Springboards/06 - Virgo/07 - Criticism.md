## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar and Bashkir literary and satirical circles:** a native-language satire tradition.
- **Chuvash and Mari readers of their own satire magazines.**
- **Ural city publics (Yekaterinburg, Perm, Chelyabinsk):** urban protest and comedy.
- **Village households:** in-law criticism and wedding teasing.
- **Muslim and Orthodox believers:** self-criticism in religious form.
- **Bashkir activists around land and mountains:** criticism tied to place.
- **Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha writers:** a thinner record to check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for satire magazines and Soviet forms; mixed for present feedback norms; online reviews plentiful.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (Arabic script before 1928); Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest and press research carries legal risk for people named; some outlets blocked or labeled "foreign agents"; denunciation history is painful; do not identify protesters.
- **Held for review:** none.
