# Idelsk-Uralia: The domain, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar (Cyrillic, with the older Arabic-script and 1920s-30s Latin-script literatures); Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari (meadow and hill); Erzya and Moksha; Udmurt; Komi-Permyak; Kazakh for the southern steppe edge. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming, and sport as self-statement.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars, split:** Kazan Tatars; Mishar; Kryashen (Orthodox, Christian naming); Nagaibaks of the Chelyabinsk steppe; Tatar-speaking communities in northwest Bashkortostan whose census answer has shifted (sensitive).
- **Bashkirs:** mountain, steppe and trans-Ural clans; urban Ufa.
- **Chuvash:** upper (viryal) and lower (anatri); Orthodox and followers of the old faith (*Vattisen yăli*, *check*).
- **Mari:** meadow, hill and eastern Mari of Bashkortostan and the Urals; those who keep the sacred groves and those who do not.
- **Mordvins:** Erzya and Moksha kept apart; Tatar-speaking Karatai Mordvins (*check*).
- **Udmurts:** northern, southern, and the Besermyan.
- **Komi-Permyaks** of the Kudymkar area.
- **Russians:** Volga villages, Ural factory towns, Orenburg Cossack lines, Old Believers; big-city Yekaterinburg and Chelyabinsk.
- **Others:** Kazakhs of the Orenburg steppe; Ukrainians, Germans and Jews of the cities; recent Central Asian arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Tatars and Bashkirs (strong academies and print); mixed for Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha; thin in English for Komi-Permyaks and Besermyan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian throughout; Tatar and Bashkir in three scripts; the Finno-Ugric literary languages; Finnish, Hungarian and Estonian for older Finno-Ugric scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic self-designation, native-language schooling and regional autonomy are politically charged; some national organizations have restricted legal status, so check before contact. Sacred-grove rites may not be open to outsiders. Language-policy law goes to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries "Russia" as pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the Volga-Ural core the name points to (the Idel-Ural republics and the Ural oblasts).
- **Held for review:** none.
