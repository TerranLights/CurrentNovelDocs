## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe peoples**: Bashkirs of the south and trans-Urals, Orenburg Kazakhs, Cossack descendants, where horse-borne daring is a public ideal.
- **Kazan Tatars**, with an epic and Muslim scholarly heritage; **Kryashen** and **Nagaibak** villages with Cossack service history.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha**, whose hero legends center on defense and survival.
- **Komi-Permyak** forest and river communities.
- **Ural mining and steel towns**, where dangerous work defines nerve; the nuclear-closed towns around Mayak.
- **Women**: wartime home-front and medical service; how their courage is named.
- **Veterans' families** across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epics and war memory; mixed for moral courage; thin for everyday bravery norms by group.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir epic language; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya-Moksha folklore texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory and current military service are highly political; repression history and nuclear-accident records are sensitive and partly closed; protect veterans and their families. Some hero legends belong to sacred-grove communities and may be shared selectively.
- **Held for review:** none.
