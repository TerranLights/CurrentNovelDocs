## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar sub-groups:** Kazan Tatars, Mishar, Kryashen, Nagaibaks, and **Teptyar** descendants; Tatar speakers of northwest Bashkortostan whose label is disputed.
- **Bashkir clans and territorial groups:** mountain, steppe, trans-Ural, and the northwest.
- **Chuvash** who stress Bulgar descent against those who do not.
- **Erzya and Moksha**, and anyone who prefers the umbrella "Mordvin".
- **Mari** across three branches; **Udmurt** north and south, **Besermyan**.
- **Komi-Permyaks** around Kudymkar.
- **Russians** who call themselves Uralians first, Volga Russians, Cossack descendants, Old Believers.
- **Mixed families**, the largest group the categories hide.
- **Kazakhs** of Orenburg; Volga German, Jewish and Ukrainian heritage communities; new Central Asian migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census categories and national histories; mixed for lived self-description; thin for small groups (Teptyar, Besermyan, Nagaibak).
- **Languages needed:** all the titular languages; Finnish and Estonian for the kindred-peoples literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census self-identification, the Tatar-Bashkir boundary, origin disputes and regional autonomy are charged subjects, and talk of separation carries legal risk in Russia today; anonymize informants. Nationality-policy law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
