## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars and Mishars:** 1552 martyrdom, Qorban bäyräme, war heroes.
- **Kryashens and Nagaibaks:** Orthodox Tatar-speakers with Cossack service memory.
- **Bashkirs:** epic heroes and Salawat; Trans-Ural and southern villages separately.
- **Ural and Volga Russians:** Orthodox passion-bearers; Old Believers' refusal; factory "labor feat".
- **Mari, Udmurts, Chuvash, Erzya and Moksha:** grove offerings, and wartime losses of small peoples.
- **Orenburg Cossacks:** service and death in war as an estate ideal.
- **Women:** mothers of large families (the Soviet "Mother Heroine" title) and caregivers.
- **Rotational-shift families:** the man away for weeks at a time.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory and martyrs; mixed for caregiving and blood donation by region; thin for village offering rites outside community media.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Mari, Udmurt, Chuvash and Erzya for offerings; Church Slavonic for Orthodox texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the war that began in 2022 drew heavily on the republics, and its death counts and heroization are contested, so use independent and official counts side by side with care; offering ceremonies may limit outsiders; 1552 memory is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
