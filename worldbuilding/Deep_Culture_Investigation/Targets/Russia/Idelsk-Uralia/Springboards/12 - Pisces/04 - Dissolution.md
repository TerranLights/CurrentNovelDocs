## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural against urban Tatars:** language and identity held or loosened.
- **Northwestern Bashkortostan:** Tatar-speaking communities claimed by both peoples.
- **Kryashens and Nagaibaks:** whether a group counts as its own or dissolves into "Tatar".
- **Udmurts, Mari, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks and Chuvash:** different speeds of language shift.
- **Besermyans:** a small group at the edge of the Udmurt identity.
- **Ural factory-town Russians:** whole towns losing their reason to exist.
- **Sufi-minded and reformist Muslims; Orthodox monastics and lay believers:** opposite views of losing the self in God.
- **Mixed Tatar-Russian and other families:** merged or layered identity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census language data and industrial decline; mixed for mystical practice (scattered, partly hidden); thin for dementia care and psychedelic use by region.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic-script Tatar for Sufi texts); Udmurt, Mari, Chuvash, Erzya and Moksha for language-loss debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language loss is tied to ongoing political disputes; the 2019 self-immolation needs great care; census categories are themselves contested; Sufi groups may be wary of outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
