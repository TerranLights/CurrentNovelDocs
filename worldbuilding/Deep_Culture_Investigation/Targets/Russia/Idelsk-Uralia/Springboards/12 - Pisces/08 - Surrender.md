# Idelsk-Uralia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars:** traditionalist Muslims, Jadid-heritage urbanites and secular Tatars on fate and effort.
- **Kryashens:** Orthodox resignation in a Tatar-speaking frame.
- **Bashkirs:** steppe and mountain communities; memories of failed uprisings.
- **Chuvash, Mari and Udmurts:** fate words of their own, and native-faith versus Orthodox views of death.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** separate death and fate lore.
- **Russians:** Orthodox, Old Believers, the secular Soviet-trained; Ural factory towns that "survived the 1990s".
- **Families of the war dead and of former prisoners:** where surrender is shame or mercy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for proverbs, theology and literature; mixed for end-of-life data by region; thin for direct measures of fatalism by people.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic-script Jadid texts); Chuvash, Mari and Udmurt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "fatalistic Russian" or "fatalistic Muslim" label is a stereotype to record and test, never adopt; prisoner-of-war and legion history is painful; end-of-life talk touches grief.
- **Held for review:** none.
