# Idelsk-Uralia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs:** zakat, sadaqa and the mosque community against state welfare.
- **Kryashens, baptized Chuvash and Orthodox Russians:** church alms, sisters of mercy, saints' feasts.
- **Mari, Udmurt and unbaptized Chuvash traditional believers:** feeding the dead and the poor at commemorations; the grove offering as a shared meal.
- **Old Believers of the Urals and Volga:** closed-community mutual aid.
- **Ural factory towns:** plant-run welfare and its collapse after 1991.
- **Orenburg Cossacks and Kazakhs; Erzya and Moksha villages:** steppe hospitality against forest-village help.
- **Labor migrants from Central Asia:** as givers through their own networks and as people whom compassion may not reach.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich history of merchant and religious charity; national giving surveys rarely split by republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (with Arabic-script records before 1928); Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha for village ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some charities and foreign-linked foundations have been closed or labeled, so their archives and sites may vanish; famine memory touches Soviet-era politics; homelessness and drug-use data need care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Bashkortostan historical archive's guide for the Spiritual Assembly fonds and "милостыня" or "сәдака" petitions.
2. Search Hoover Institution's ARA Russian Operations finding aid for "Kazan", "Ufa", "Cheboksary" and "Samara".
3. Search "Если быть точным" for poverty and orphanhood indicators across the six republics and the Ural oblasts.
4. Search the Tatar National Corpus for "шәфкать" and "кызгану" to see how mercy and pity differ in use.
5. Search the Chuvash humanities institute's catalog for "нимӗ" and memorial-feast customs.
