# Idelsk-Uralia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyak. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs**: same-day burial and Islamic memorial reading, varying by town, village and generation.
- **Kryashens and Nagaibaks**: Orthodox Turkic speakers whose memorial customs mix church and older practice.
- **Unbaptized Chuvash, Mari and Udmurts** (many in Bashkortostan and Tatarstan): the dead as kin who are fed and hosted.
- **Erzya and Moksha** apart, and **Komi-Permyaks**.
- **Orthodox Russians** and **Old Believers** of the Urals, who keep their own cemeteries and burial rules.
- **Factory and mining towns** of the Urals: working-age male deaths and secular funerals.
- **Villages along the Techa** and families in the closed cities.
- **Families of soldiers** killed in recent wars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality statistics and Tatar folklore; mixed for Bashkir and Chuvash; thin in English for Mari, Udmurt and Mordvin beliefs.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha; Finnish and Hungarian for older Finno-Ugric work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war-death counts are restricted inside Russia; radiation deaths stayed secret for decades; old-faith memorial rites may be closed to outsiders; suicide in some Finno-Ugric districts is a painful subject.
- **Held for review:** none.
