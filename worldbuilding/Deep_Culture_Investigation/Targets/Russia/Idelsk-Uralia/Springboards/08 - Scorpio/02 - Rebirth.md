## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kryashens** against **Kazan Tatars**: the same language with a history of baptism, return and contested identity.
- **Unbaptized Mari, Chuvash and Udmurts** in Bashkortostan and Tatarstan against baptized kin in their home republics.
- **Erzya** and **Moksha**: separate revival movements and standards.
- **Bashkirs**: revived genealogy and clan identity since 1990.
- **Urban Muslim converts and returners** (young Tatars and Bashkirs who took up prayer as adults) against older Soviet-era Muslims.
- **Orthodox Russians and Old Believers** of the Urals.
- **Factory towns** facing shutdown.
- **People leaving prison or rehab** in the large cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conversion history in English and Russian; mixed on present-day revival; thin and scattered on recovery and redemption outside Yekaterinburg and Kazan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (including Arabic-script sources for Jadid texts); Mari, Udmurt, Chuvash, Erzya.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Muslim revival currents are banned in Russia; coercive rehab involved criminal cases; old-faith revival is tied to ethnic politics and was watched by the state.
- **Held for review:** none.
