# Idelsk-Uralia: Domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia (core lands read here as the Volga-Kama-Ural belt, from Mordovia and Chuvashia to Perm, Sverdlovsk, Chelyabinsk and Orenburg). **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Meadow and Hill Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyak; Old Tatar in Arabic script for the pre-modern record. **Concept in one line:** death and transformation: how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden, and its death ways, sex ways, inheritance and shared resources.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars**, urban and rural, against **Kryashens** (baptized Tatars), **Mishars** of the western Volga, and **Nagaibaks** of the Chelyabinsk steppe: one language family, different funeral and inheritance practice by faith.
- **Bashkirs**: mountain, Trans-Ural and northwestern groups, with tribal lineages that kept genealogies.
- **Chuvash**: upper (Viryal) and lower (Anatri), and the unbaptized Chuvash of Tatarstan, Bashkortostan and Samara.
- **Mari**: meadow, hill and eastern (Bashkortostan) Mari; the old faith's grove congregations.
- **Udmurts** north and south, and the **Besermyans**.
- **Erzya** and **Moksha** separately.
- **Komi-Permyaks**; **Mansi** if the northern Urals are included.
- **Russians**: Ural factory towns, **Old Believers**, **Orenburg Cossacks**, and the closed nuclear cities.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs**; Jewish and German communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian and Tatar; mixed for Bashkir and Chuvash; thinner in English for Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha, but deep in Finnish, Estonian and Hungarian folklore scholarship. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and its sign assume the Volga-Kama-Ural core.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Tatar and Bashkir (Cyrillic, and Arabic script before 1928); Chuvash; the Finno-Ugric languages; Finnish, Estonian, Hungarian and German for older scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** old-faith grove rites that members may not share; Muslim groups banned in Russia; organizations labeled "foreign agent" or "undesirable" (some independent pollsters and media), so sources may be blocked or risky to contact; war deaths by region.
- **Held for review:** none.
