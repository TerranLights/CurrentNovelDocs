# Idelsk-Uralia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar; Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Erzya and Moksha; Komi-Permyak. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs**, whose funeral alms and the handling of a dead person's clothes follow Islamic custom, against **Kryashens**, Orthodox Tatars with their own rites.
- **Chuvash** of the old faith and Orthodox Chuvash, for memorial goods.
- **Udmurts and Mari** who keep family shrines and grove offerings, against those who do not.
- **Erzya and Moksha** villages, separately.
- **Russian Old Believers** of the Urals and Kama: rules on lending dishes and objects to outsiders, to check.
- **Ural industrial cities**: Soviet flats, garages and garden sheds as storage.
- **Komi-Permyaks** of the forest north, for the hunter's gear and the trap-line.
- **Recent migrants and the dispossessed**: Central Asian workers, people deported or resettled.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian for Soviet objects and the 1930s; mixed for Tatar and Bashkir ritual ethnography; thin for Komi-Permyak and Moksha material life.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar; Bashkir; Chuvash; Udmurt; Mari; Erzya, Moksha; Komi-Permyak.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family shrines and their objects are restricted among Udmurt and Mari keepers; dispossession and exile are family trauma, so approach memory projects with care. Property rights go to Libra.
