## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bashkir patrimonial landholders** against the **Tatar, Mishar, Chuvash, Mari and Teptyar settlers** admitted onto their lands: the oldest ownership quarrel in the region.
- **Russian peasant communes** of the Volga against **possessional factory workers** of the Ural works.
- **Orenburg Cossacks**, whose land came as service allotments.
- **Kazakh pastoral groups** on the Orenburg edge, with seasonal rather than fixed claims.
- **Mari and Udmurt grove keepers**, for land owned by no one or by the gods; check how they describe it.
- **Muslim communities** with waqf property, and **Old Believers** with community-held chapels and cemeteries.
- **Post-Soviet owners:** oil-town elites (Tatneft, Bashneft), agroholdings, dacha and garden-plot holders, holders of unused land shares.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 18th-19th-century Bashkir land question and for Ural industry; rich for current statistics; thin for Finno-Ugric customary claims after 1930.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (Arabic script for waqf and khanate documents); Bashkir; Chuvash; the Finno-Ugric languages; Arabic, Persian and Chagatai for charters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Bashkir land history and Tatar-Bashkir census disputes are politically charged; sacred groves' status is contested. The law of property goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
