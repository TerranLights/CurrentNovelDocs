## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar merchant and town tradition** (Kazan's Old Tatar Sloboda, Kargala) against Tatar farming villages of the Zakamye.
- **Bashkir pastoral and honey-hunting groups** of the southern Urals (Burzyan, Abzelil) against settled northwestern Bashkir villages.
- **Finno-Ugric forest and field peoples:** Mari, Udmurt, Besermyan, Komi-Permyak, Erzya, Moksha, each with its own household and offering customs.
- **Chuvash** upper and lower, plus Chuvash settlers in Bashkortostan, Samara and Ulyanovsk.
- **Russian Ural factory towns** shaped by the Demidov and Stroganov estates; **oil towns** (Almetyevsk, Oktyabrsky, Ishimbay); **auto towns** (Tolyatti, Naberezhnye Chelny).
- **Old Believers** of the Urals and the Kama, long noted for merchant capital.
- **Orenburg Cossack** settlements and **Kazakh** steppe villages.
- **Smaller groups:** Kryashens, Nagaibaks, Mishars, Teptyar descendants, Bashkortostan's German and Latvian villages, newer Central Asian traders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian for household statistics and Ural industry, and for Tatar merchant history; mixed for Bashkir and Chuvash; thin and older for the Finno-Ugric peoples outside the classic ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar in three scripts; Bashkir; Chuvash; the Finno-Ugric literary languages; Arabic for pre-1917 Muslim texts; German and Finnish for early fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** offerings at Mari and Udmurt sacred groves and family shrines may be closed to outsiders; Soviet dispossession (dekulakization) and the 1921-22 famine remain painful family memory. Property law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries "Russia" as pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the Volga-Ural core (the Idel-Ural republics and neighboring Ural and Volga oblasts).
- **Held for review:** none.
