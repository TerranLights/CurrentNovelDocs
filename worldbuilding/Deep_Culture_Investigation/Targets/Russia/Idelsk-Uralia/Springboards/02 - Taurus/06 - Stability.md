# Idelsk-Uralia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar; Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Tatar and Chuvash villages** with homesteads held by one family, against **new Soviet cities** (Naberezhnye Chelny, Tolyatti, Neftekamsk) built by newcomers.
- **Bashkirs of the Trans-Urals**, with a pastoral past of seasonal moves, against long-settled northwestern Bashkir villages.
- **Eastern Mari** of Bashkortostan and the Urals, descended from earlier migrants, against Mari of Mari El.
- **Old Believers** of the Upper Kama and the Urals.
- **Orenburg Cossack** stanitsas and **Kazakh** steppe villages.
- **Komi-Permyak** forest villages under depopulation.
- **German and Latvian** villages of Bashkortostan, settled colonists with their own continuity.
- **Monotown workers** and **oil-field shift workers** who live between two places.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration statistics and Soviet policy; rich local-history material, scattered and offline; thin in English.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (Arabic script for epitaphs and older village chronicles); Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Kazakh; Arabic for gravestones.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "stability" is a political slogan, so separate official usage from lived meaning; the resettlement of villages and Soviet deportations are painful; Old Believer communities may limit access to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
