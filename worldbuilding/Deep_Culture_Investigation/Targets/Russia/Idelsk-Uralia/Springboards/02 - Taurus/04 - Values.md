*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Practicing and secular Muslims** among Tatars and Bashkirs, and the differences between Kazan, village and steppe.
- **Kryashens**, Orthodox in faith and Tatar in speech.
- **Orthodox Chuvash** against **followers of the old Chuvash faith**.
- **Mari, Udmurt and Erzya native-faith communities**, each stating its own values.
- **Russian Orthodox, Old Believers and the Soviet-secular urban majority** of the Ural cities.
- **Moksha villages**, apart from Erzya.
- **Youth subcultures** of Yekaterinburg, Perm and Kazan against their parents.
- **Central Asian Muslim migrants**, whose Islam may differ from local Tatar practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in survey and religious material in Russian and Tatar; mixed for Bashkir and Chuvash; thin and activist-led for the native faiths.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (Arabic script for the 18th-19th-century theologians); Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Erzya; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** native-faith rites and sacred groves may be closed to outsiders; "traditional values" is a political term today, so separate official statements from what people say; the federal decree that lists values belongs to Capricorn. Islamic groups deemed extremist by the state are a legal minefield to avoid.
- **Held for review:** none.
