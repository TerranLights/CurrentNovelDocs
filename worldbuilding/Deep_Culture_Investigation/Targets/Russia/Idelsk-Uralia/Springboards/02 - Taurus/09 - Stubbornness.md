## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believers** of the Urals and Upper Kama, by concord.
- **Kryashens** and the families who **returned to Islam**, against those who stayed Orthodox.
- **Mari and Udmurt native-faith keepers**, against converted neighbors.
- **Bashkir civic and land movements** of the southern Urals (Kushtau, Baymak).
- **Yekaterinburg and Perm civic activists**, urban Russian holdouts.
- **Orenburg Cossacks**, with their own story of loyalty and revolt.
- **Industrial unions** of the auto and steel towns.
- **Chuvash, Erzya and Moksha** villages, on endurance and compromise.
- **Elders** across all groups, on refusing change in old age.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious resistance in the 18th-19th centuries; rich for recent civic protests in press; mixed for everyday stubbornness in family life; thin for Finno-Ugric minority voices outside activism.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Church Slavonic; Tatar (Arabic script for 19th-century Muslim sources); Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current protest movements and their leaders face prosecution, so treat living people with care; Tatarstan's sovereignty history is politically charged; the old self-immolations are delicate for Old Believer communities. Legal disputes go to Libra and Capricorn.
