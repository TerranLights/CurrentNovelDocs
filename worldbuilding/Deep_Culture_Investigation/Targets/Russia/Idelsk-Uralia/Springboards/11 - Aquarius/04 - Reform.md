## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar and Bashkir Muslims:** new-method and old-method camps, and their present heirs (reform-minded and conservative imams).
- **Kryashens and Orthodox Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts:** reformed by missionary schools, and reformers of their own (Yakovlev's Chuvash circle).
- **Mari and Udmurt traditional-faith communities:** inner renewal against outside pressure.
- **Ural industrial cities:** perestroika and 1990s reform up close.
- **Old Believers:** a group defined by refusing the 17th-century church reform.
- **Village vs. city Russians** on Stolypin, collectivization and post-Soviet farm reform.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Jadidism and imperial reform; mixed for the 1990s; thin for Finno-Ugric reform currents in English.
- **Languages needed:** Tatar in Arabic script (pre-1927) is essential; Russian; German and Turkish scholarship helps.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Muslim reform currents can be labeled "extremist" in present Russia; coercive rehab involves victims. Legal reform goes to Libra (text) or Capricorn (legal culture).
- **Held for review:** none.
