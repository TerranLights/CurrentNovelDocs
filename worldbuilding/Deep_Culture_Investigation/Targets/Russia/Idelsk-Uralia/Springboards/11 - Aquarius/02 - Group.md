# Idelsk-Uralia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan, Naberezhnye Chelny and Ural factory districts:** territorial youth groups by neighborhood.
- **Bashkir clans** in the Trans-Urals against Ufa's urban Bashkirs.
- **Kryashens, Nagaibaks and Muslim Tatars:** group lines drawn by faith.
- **Cossack descendants** (Orenburg, Ural) and their revived hosts.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt and Mordvin movements**, compared one by one, not as a "Finno-Ugric" bloc.
- **Plant collectives** in Togliatti (AvtoVAZ), Chelny (KAMAZ), Magnitogorsk.
- **Labor migrants** from Central Asia and the Caucasus: group lines drawn around them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on street gangs, Soviet collectives and history; mixed on present protest groups; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, plus Kazan street slang; Tatar and Bashkir for movement press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Bashkir and Tatar activists face prosecution; some groups are banned as "extremist"; names of protesters must be protected. Gang material involves victims. Law questions go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
