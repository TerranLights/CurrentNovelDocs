## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believers** (chasovennye and others) in the Urals and Volga: dissent as heritage.
- **Mari and Udmurt traditional-faith followers;** Eastern Mari of Bashkortostan.
- **Kryashens and Muslim Tatars:** each other's "nonconformists" depending on the teller.
- **Ural city youth:** rock, punk and street art in Yekaterinburg, Perm, Chelyabinsk.
- **Tatar and Bashkir writers and activists** at odds with both Moscow and their own republics.
- **Pious Muslim converts and newly religious youth in secular families.**
- **Rural eccentrics and hermits:** village oddness, as folklore and local press tell it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet dissent and Ural music; mixed for faith minorities; thin for rural eccentrics.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir for minority writers; Mari and Udmurt for faith communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** in present Russia the "LGBT movement" is ruled extremist and several dissident and human-rights bodies are banned or labeled; gender and sexual nonconformity must be researched with care for informants and researchers, mostly through exile and international sources. Traditional-faith rites may be closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
