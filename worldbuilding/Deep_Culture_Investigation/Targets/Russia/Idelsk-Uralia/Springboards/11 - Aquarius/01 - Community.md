# Idelsk-Uralia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar villages vs. Kazan and Naberezhnye Chelny:** mahalla against high-rise districts.
- **Kryashen villages and Mishar villages** (Penza, Ulyanovsk, Nizhny Novgorod edges): different faiths, possibly different community forms.
- **Bashkir mountain and Trans-Ural villages vs. Ufa**, and **Bashkortostan's Tatars, Chuvash and Eastern Mari**.
- **Ural monotowns** (Nizhny Tagil, Magnitogorsk, Asbest): one plant as the community's center.
- **Old Believer settlements and Orenburg Cossack stanitsas.**
- **Udmurt, Besermyan, Mari and Mordvin villages**, each with its own festival and prayer cycle.
- **Mansi and Komi-Permyak communities:** small, remote, possibly very thin in print.
- **Central Asian migrants in the big cities:** community made through compatriot networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and the Soviet planned towns; mixed for present village life; thin for small Finno-Ugric and Indigenous groups.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Udmurt, Mari, Chuvash, Erzya, Moksha for village press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disaster victims and nuclear-exposed villages; special settlers' descendants; sacred-grove gatherings closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
