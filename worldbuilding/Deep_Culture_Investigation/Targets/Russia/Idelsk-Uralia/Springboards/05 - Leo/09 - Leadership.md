## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars:** Kazan urban elites, Mishar villages, Kryashen parishes; mahalla imams against state-appointed heads.
- **Bashkirs:** clan memory (biys) in the mountain and steppe districts.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts:** traditional-religion priests and village elders against Orthodox clergy and Soviet-style chairmen.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** revival-era leaders and literary heroes.
- **Russians:** Old Believer lay leaders, Orenburg and Ural Cossacks, factory directors in monotowns, big-city entrepreneurs.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs:** elder and kin-based leadership.
- **Women leaders:** village councils, teachers, collective-farm heads, business owners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical and political leadership; mixed for everyday and informal leaders; leadership surveys are national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for older documents); Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya; Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national-movement leaders of 1917-20 and later activists can be politically charged; the muftiates' rivalries are delicate; traditional-religion priests may not wish to be quoted. Formal authority and office go to Capricorn; election law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
