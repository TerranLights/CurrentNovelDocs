## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars:** Kazan townspeople, Mishar and Kryashen villages; *namus* in religious and secular families.
- **Bashkirs:** clan and epic honor in the mountain and steppe districts.
- **Chuvash, Udmurts, Mari, Erzya, Moksha:** kin-group honor and village reputation.
- **Russians:** Orenburg Cossacks, Old Believers, Ural factory workers, officers' and cadets' milieus, 1990s street and business codes.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs:** *ar-namys* in kin life.
- **Men and women:** test whether honor splits by gender; ask women directly.
- **Veterans and their families,** including those of recent wars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epics, literature and war memory; mixed for everyday honor codes; very thin for measured honor ideology.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for older texts); Chuvash and Udmurt; Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chastity customs and violence justified by honor must be studied in the words of those who lived them, with consent; crime codes and military memory are politically charged; recent war service is a risky topic. Law on honor and defamation goes to Libra; duty and military law's spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
