## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian villages** of the Middle Volga and Urals: old shaming customs; Old Believers' strict norms.
- **Tatars** (Kazan, Mishar, Kryashen) and **Bashkirs:** memory of forced conversion and of slurs; *oyat* in family life.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha:** imperial names, festival mockery, and urban ridicule of village speech.
- **Conscripts and their families;** men in or after penal colonies.
- **Schoolchildren:** rural children and minority-language speakers in city schools.
- **Central Asian migrants** in the cities: public humiliation in raids and media.
- **Women:** reputation shaming, then and now.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Historical and ethnographic records are good; hazing and prison material comes through NGOs, journalism and a few scholars; minority-language mockery songs need archive work.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha for festival folklore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison caste and hazing topics can expose informants to danger; never name victims without consent; some advocacy groups face pressure. Punishment law goes to Libra; the spirit of discipline to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
