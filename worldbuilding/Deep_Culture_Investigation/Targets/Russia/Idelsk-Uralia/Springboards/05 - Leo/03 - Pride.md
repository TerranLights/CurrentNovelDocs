## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars** against **Mishar**, **Kryashen** and **Nagaibak**: different heroes and calendars of pride.
- **Bashkirs** of the mountain, steppe and northwest; clan-based pride against republic-level pride.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** smaller peoples whose pride claims are often made against assimilation.
- **Russians:** Ural factory towns, Old Believers, Orenburg Cossacks, Middle Volga villages; regional patriotism of Yekaterinburg against Perm or Chelyabinsk.
- **Muslim, Orthodox, traditional-religion and secular** groups: pride as sin in some teaching, as dignity in others.
- **Men and women; older and younger:** pride in work, in children, in the people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective pride (monuments, holidays, ethnic surveys); mixed for personal pride and bragging norms, where folklore and proverbs lead.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic-script sources before 1927); Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya and Moksha.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and national pride claims touch separatism charges; Bashkir-Tatar and Kryashen identity disputes are heated; open LGBTQ Pride activity is criminalized, so use published scholarship only and expose no one. Laws on symbols and identity go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
