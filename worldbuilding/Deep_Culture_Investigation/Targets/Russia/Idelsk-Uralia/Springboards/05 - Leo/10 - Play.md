## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars** (Kazan, Mishar, Kryashen) and **Bashkirs:** horse games, wrestling and children's chain games; Muslim views of music and gambling.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** round dances, youth evening games, festival games.
- **Mansi** of the northern Urals: festival play with sacred limits.
- **Russians:** Ural factory-town courtyards, Old Believer families (strict on games), Cossack villages (horse and martial play).
- **City and village children;** children of Central Asian migrants in city schools.
- **Adults:** fishermen, hunters, card and domino players, gamers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk games and Soviet childhood memoirs; mixed for today's play; time-use and sport data exist by region, while free play is unmeasured.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Komi-Permyak for game names and rhymes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some ritual games (bear festival, prayer-feast games) belong to closed ceremonies; children's data needs care; gambling law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
