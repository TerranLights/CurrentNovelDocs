## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars:** Kazan Tatars, Mishar, Kryashen (Orthodox) and Nagaibak; Muslim, Orthodox and secular festive calendars differ.
- **Bashkirs:** mountain, steppe and northwestern groups; the Bashkir-Tatar language border in the northwest.
- **Chuvash:** upper (Viryal) and lower (Anatri); Christian and unbaptized villages.
- **Finno-Ugric peoples:** meadow, hill and eastern Mari; northern and southern Udmurts, Besermyan; Erzya and Moksha; Komi-Permyaks. Traditional-religion communities apart from Orthodox ones.
- **Russians:** Ural factory and mining towns, Old Believers, Orenburg Cossacks, the Middle Volga villages; the big cities (Yekaterinburg, Perm, Samara, Chelyabinsk) as their own units.
- **Kazakhs of the Orenburg steppe;** smaller Ugric (Mansi) presence in the far north of Sverdlovsk region.
- **Urban and rural,** and generations: Soviet-era club culture against post-Soviet festival revival.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for Russian and Tatar, good for Bashkir and Chuvash; thinner and older for Mari, Udmurt, Mordvin and Komi-Permyak, where Soviet folklore volumes and Finnish collections dominate. Statistics are by federal subject and do not match the post-war nation's lands; **the nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (the tracker carries "Russia"), so this sheet writes for the Volga-Ural core: the six republics, the Ural regions and the Middle Volga.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Tatar and Bashkir (Cyrillic, with Tatar Latin script in some publications); Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha; Finnish and German for older field collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some pollsters carry "foreign agent" labels; minority-language schooling and national-movement history are contested; traditional-religion rites (Mari, Udmurt, Erzya) may include closed prayers, so follow what communities share. Gambling, public-order and festival regulation go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
