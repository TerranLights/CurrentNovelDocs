## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Factory towns** of the Urals and the Kama (Magnitogorsk, Nizhny Tagil, Naberezhnye Chelny): Soviet labor-hero heritage.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** tarkhan and clan memory; religious titles; republic prize culture.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** recognition through the titular-language press and small honors systems, and claims to be recognized at all.
- **Russians** of the Middle Volga villages, the Ural cities and the Orenburg Cossack settlements.
- **Muslims, Orthodox, Old Believers, traditional-religion believers:** different honored titles and seats of honor.
- **Families of the repressed** against families of war heroes: two memory cultures.
- **Teachers, doctors and village clerks:** the main holders of honorary titles outside the arts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal honors (decrees, prize lists, press); mixed for everyday thanks and credit, where fieldwork and etiquette guides are needed.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (older sources in Arabic script); Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya for the press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression memory work faces closures and labels (the national memory society was dissolved in 2021); keep informants' family histories private. The legal side of honors and rehabilitation goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
