## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars, Mishar, Kryashen:** religious and secular norms on women's voice and dress differ.
- **Bashkirs:** sesen culture and the long-song tradition; eastern and southern districts.
- **Chuvash:** Christian and unbaptized villages; song as the open channel.
- **Mari** (meadow, hill, eastern), **Udmurts**, **Erzya and Moksha:** personal-song traditions and the effect of Russian-language schooling.
- **Russian** Ural workers' towns and Old Believer villages (strict norms of restraint) against Yekaterinburg and Perm bohemia.
- **Generations:** late-Soviet *neformaly*, 1990s street-gang youth, today's online creators.
- **Gender:** men's and women's channels (laments, munajat, chastushki, takmak); LGBTQ people, under heavy pressure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich song and dress records; modern survey data mostly national or by federal district; minority-language online scenes need fresh fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir for songs and media; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya for song collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political speech, protest and LGBTQ expression carry real risks for informants; some sources are blocked or labeled; ask nothing that could identify a speaker. Speech and dress law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
