## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** Muslim teaching and national memory (1552, 18th-c. revolts) as two sources of dignity talk.
- **Kryashen and Nagaibak:** baptized Tatars, whose history includes coerced conversion.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha:** funerary and elder-respect customs; traditional-religion believers.
- **Volga Germans and their descendants;** labor-army families in the Urals.
- **Russians** of factory towns (work and wage dignity) and of the Middle Volga villages; Old Believers (dignity kept under persecution).
- **Central Asian migrant workers** in Yekaterinburg, Kazan, Samara; disabled and elderly people in institutions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Church, Muslim and literary sources are rich; measured data on respect is national or thin; camp and deportation memoirs exist but are scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script before 1927); German for Volga German memoirs; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt for funerary ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression memory, migrants' status and conditions in institutions are risky topics for informants; some monitoring groups carry labels. Constitutional and legal dignity goes to Libra; the spirit of law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
