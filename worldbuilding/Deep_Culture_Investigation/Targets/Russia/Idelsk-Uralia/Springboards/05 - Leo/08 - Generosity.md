## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars** (urban Kazan, rural Mishar villages, Kryashen) and **Bashkirs:** Islamic alms, hospitality and merchant waqf traditions.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurts, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** collective help work, communal prayer feasts, guest customs.
- **Russians:** Old Believer mutual aid in the Urals (strong internal giving networks), factory-town paternalism, Orenburg Cossack hospitality.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs:** steppe hospitality customs.
- **Wealthy and poor:** oil and metals elites against monotown households.
- **Religious and secular givers;** urban online donors and village work-bee neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history (merchants, waqfs, church charity) and for Islamic customs; mixed for today's giving, where HSE and CAF data exist only at broad levels.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (waqf deeds in Arabic script and Arabic); Chuvash and Udmurt for work-bee terms; Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** traditional-religion rites may be closed to outsiders; charities with foreign links face labels; corporate giving can be political. Tax and nonprofit law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
