*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars** at home against the **diaspora** (Finland, Turkey, Kazakhstan, Moscow), and Mishars, whose emigrant communities differ.
- **Bashkirs** with herding and summer-pasture memory against settled farmers.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha** village men who work away seasonally, and the families who stay.
- **Eastern Mari** and **Udmurts** east of the Kama, descendants of migrants.
- **Russians** of Ural factory towns (evacuees' descendants) and **Old Believers** who fled east.
- **Volga Germans** (deported, partly returned or emigrated); **Orenburg Kazakhs** across a new border; **Central Asian labor migrants** arriving.
- Students who leave for Moscow, Kazan or abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on historical travel accounts, hajj and diaspora; mixed on present tourism (official data, promotional); thin on smaller peoples' own migration stories.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar; Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Erzya; Kazakh; Arabic for Ibn Fadlan; German for academy journals; Turkish and Finnish for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportations and labor-army memory are painful; migrant labor is politically charged since 2024; foreign travel and emigration after 2022 are sensitive for those who left. The Dyatlov case draws conspiracy writing; use archive-based sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
