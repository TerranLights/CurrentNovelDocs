## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs:** nikah beside civil registration; Kazan, Mishar and village practice may differ.
- **Kryashens, Orthodox Chuvash, Mari and Udmurts:** church marriage and godparent ties against older rites.
- **Traditional-faith Mari and Udmurt villages:** marrying within or across faith lines.
- **Erzya and Moksha**, compared separately.
- **Bashkir clans:** rules on marrying outside one's descent group.
- **Old Believers; Cossack descendants; factory towns.**
- **Mixed Russian-Tatar and other couples** in Kazan, Ufa and Yekaterinburg.
- **Labor migrants** and transnational spouses; **merchant and business families**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, registry statistics and wedding folklore; mixed for business partnership in practice; thin for unregistered couples, who official data barely see.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha for wedding songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pressure to marry within the faith or ethnic group is a live family issue; Idel-Ural is politically charged in present-day Russia; bride-capture customs in older ethnography need careful reading.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for «межнациональные браки Татарстан» and «смешанные браки Башкортостан».
2. Pull marriages and divorces by region from the latest Rosstat demographic yearbook for the Volga-Ural republics and Ural provinces.
3. Search the Orenburg archive guide for «Хусаинов торговый дом».
4. Search library catalogs for «свадебные причитания мордва» and «чувашская свадьба песни».
5. Read the Family Code's marriage-contract chapter, then search sudact.ru for Kazan and Ufa rulings on it.
