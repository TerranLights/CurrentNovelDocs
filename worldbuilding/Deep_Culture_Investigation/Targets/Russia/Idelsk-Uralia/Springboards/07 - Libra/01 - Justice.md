# Idelsk-Uralia: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha, Kazakh; Arabic for religious rulings. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars and Bashkirs** with a qadi and muftiate tradition, against **Kryashens and Orthodox Chuvash** under church discipline.
- **Mari and Udmurt traditional-faith villages:** divine redress through grove rites beside state courts.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** rural Mordovia as a penal region and as a home population.
- **Orenburg and Chelyabinsk Kazakhs:** *bi* custom.
- **Russians:** Old Believer communities with their own councils; Cossack descendants and the *krug*; factory towns.
- **Labor migrants from Central Asia:** how they meet police and courts.
- **Prisoners and their families** in the penal districts; **urban against rural**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on courts and imperial cases; mixed on customary judgment; thin on Kazakh and Mansi practice.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Tatar and Bashkir for proverbs and religious rulings; Udmurt and Mari for grove rites; Arabic and Old Tatar for pre-1928 rulings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political trials and protest cases are live and dangerous to the people named; the Memorial databases were hit by court-ordered liquidation, so reach is uncertain; prison records are closed; grove rites belong to their communities.
- **Held for review:** none.
