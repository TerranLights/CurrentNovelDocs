## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars of Kazan** (long experience as go-betweens and negotiators with Moscow) against **village Tatars and Mishars**.
- **Bashkirs:** kurultai politics and clan elders.
- **Chuvash, Mari and Udmurts:** republics with smaller bargaining power.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** naming and representation between two peoples.
- **Komi-Permyaks:** a merged autonomy.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs and Russians:** cross-border kin and trade.
- **Ural Russians:** industrial elites and unions.
- **Diaspora bodies and migrant associations** (Uzbek, Tajik, Azerbaijani) in the cities; **women as family peacekeepers** in all groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial and Muscovite diplomacy and 1990s federal bargaining; mixed for everyday tact; thin for the Finno-Ugric peoples' own accounts in their languages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Erzya, Moksha, Mari, Udmurt and Komi-Permyak for congress materials; Kazakh for steppe sources; Old Russian for envoys' books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** autonomy, sovereignty and Finno-Ugric activism are politically charged and some activists are prosecuted; diaspora congresses have been scrutinized by security services.
- **Held for review:** none.
