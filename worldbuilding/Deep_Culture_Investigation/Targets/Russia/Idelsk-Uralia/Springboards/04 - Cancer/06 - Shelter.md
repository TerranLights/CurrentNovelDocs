*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar and Bashkir villagers**, whose ways of lodging the traveler come partly through Islam.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt and Mordvin villages**, each with guest customs of their own.
- **Russian Orthodox communities** around monasteries and pilgrim routes.
- **Old Believers**, who sheltered fugitives from the state in the past.
- **Factory-city incomers**: special settlers' descendants, evacuees, dormitory workers.
- **Central Asian labor migrants** today, often in crowded rented rooms.
- **The roofless** in Yekaterinburg, Samara, Kazan and Ufa.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the famine, evacuation and special settlements; thin and politically shaped for present-day homelessness.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; English for the relief records; Tatar and Bashkir for famine songs and waqf documents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression archives are harder to reach since the closure of Memorial (2021-2022); refugees from the war in Ukraine are a political topic; women's shelters protect residents' identities. Housing law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
