## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars**, **Mishars**, **Kryashens** and **Nagaibaks**: who belongs to "the Tatars" is itself the question.
- **Bashkirs** and **Tatars of northwestern Bashkortostan**, where self-naming has shifted between censuses.
- **Teptyars**, an old estate group now absorbed into larger peoples.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyak**, each with a homeland republic and a scattered population outside it.
- **Russians**: long-settled Volga villages, Ural factory towns, Old Believers, Orenburg Cossacks.
- **Volga German descendants**, **Kazakhs** of the Orenburg border, and **Jews** of Kazan and Yekaterinburg.
- **Newcomers**: Central Asian labor migrants in Kazan, Yekaterinburg and Samara.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich, and politically charged. Much writing on ethnic belonging has a side to it.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir above all; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt and Erzya for their own bodies' publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census disputes (Kryashen, Bashkir-Tatar); activists in national movements may face pressure; Mari traditional-faith gatherings.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the 2010 and 2021 census tables on ethnic self-identification for Tatarstan and Bashkortostan, including subgroups.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "идентичность кряшен" and "северо-запад Башкортостана идентичность".
3. Browse the World Congress of Tatars site for its diaspora programs.
4. Search a Tatar library catalog for "авыл тарихы" and note the districts covered.
5. Search "Изге Болгар җыены" for coverage of the yearly gathering.
