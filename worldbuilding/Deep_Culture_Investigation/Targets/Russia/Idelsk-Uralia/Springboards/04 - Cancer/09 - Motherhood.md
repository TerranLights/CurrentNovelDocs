*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar; Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari; Udmurt; Erzya and Moksha; Komi-Permyak. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar Muslim mothers** and **Kryashen mothers** (Orthodox, with Marian devotion).
- **Bashkir mothers** of the south and the northeast.
- **Chuvash, Mari and Udmurt mothers**, whose peoples keep mother-goddess lore.
- **Erzya and Moksha mothers.**
- **Russian mothers** in villages, Ural factory towns, Old Believer and Cossack communities.
- **Mothers of many children** in rural Bashkortostan and Tatarstan against one-child city families.
- **Mothers of soldiers.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth rites and state programs; mixed for mothers' own voices by ethnic group.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Mari, Udmurt and Erzya for goddess lore; Arabic-script Tatar for reformist manuals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** soldiers' mothers' groups face state pressure; the Mari traditional faith's prayers to mother deities; abortion and birth policy are political. Maternity leave as law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

3. Locate *Тәрбияле ана* in the KFU library or a digitized Tatar book collection.
4. Pull regional maternity-capital supplements for Tatarstan, Bashkortostan and Chuvashia.
5. Search Tatar folklore collections for "килен җырлары".
