# Idelsk-Uralia: Domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Turkic, Cyrillic, older Arabic-script texts); Chuvash; Mari (meadow and hill), Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha and Komi-Permyak (Finno-Ugric). **Concept in one line:** communication and learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home, with neighbors, siblings and short journeys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars, Mishar Tatars, Kryashens (Orthodox Tatars) and Nağaybäks** (Chelyabinsk): one written language, different dialects, religions and schooling histories.
- **Bashkirs by region** (mountain-forest east, steppe south, the northwest with its Tatar-Bashkir overlap).
- **Chuvash:** Viryal and Anatri dialect areas, plus diaspora villages in neighboring republics and oblasts.
- **Mari:** meadow, hill and eastern (Urals) Mari; two written standards.
- **Udmurts and Besermyans;** **Erzya and Moksha** as two peoples, not one "Mordvin" voice; **Komi-Permyaks** in Perm Krai.
- **Ural Russians:** mining-town workers, Old Believers, Orenburg Cossacks, and the big cities (Yekaterinburg, Perm, Chelyabinsk, Samara).
- **Kazakhs in Orenburg and Chelyabinsk; Volga Germans and Mennonites; Jews; Central Asian labor migrants;** the **Deaf community** (Russian Sign Language).
- **Village against city:** a district village and Kazan or Ufa may differ for the same people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Tatar, Bashkir and Russian (academies, censuses, imperial archives); mixed for Chuvash, Mari and Udmurt; thinner for Besermyans, Nağaybäks, eastern Mari and Komi-Permyaks.
- **Languages needed:** Russian throughout; Tatar and Bashkir (Cyrillic, plus Arabic script before 1928 and the Latin Yañalif of 1928-39); Chuvash; the Finno-Ugric languages for folklore. Older scholarship also in German and Hungarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language-in-school disputes and census identity disputes (Kryashens, northwest Bashkortostan) are politically charged; treat as contested. Some sacred Mari and Udmurt prayer speech is restricted to its keepers. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the Volga-Ural core the name implies** (the republics on the Volga and Kama and the Ural oblasts from Perm and Sverdlovsk to Orenburg).
- **Held for review:** none.
