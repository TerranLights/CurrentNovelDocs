*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kryashens and Nağaybäks:** Turkic speech and Orthodox faith; the clearest case of two belongings.
- **Muslim Tatars** of Kazan against those of the Urals and Orenburg; secular and practicing families.
- **Bashkirs and Tatars of northwest Bashkortostan:** a shared zone between two peoples.
- **Mari and Udmurt traditional believers** and their Orthodox neighbors; **Besermyans** between Udmurt and Turkic traits.
- **Chuvash:** Orthodox majority with an older faith revived by some.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** often counted as one "Mordvin" people though they speak two languages.
- **Ural Russians:** Old Believers and official Orthodoxy; Europe-side and Asia-side towns.
- **Mixed families and urban bilinguals** in Kazan, Ufa, Cheboksary, Izhevsk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religion between faiths and for identity surveys; mixed for everyday two-ness; thin for minority-language words.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census categories and "separate people" debates are political; double faith may be described as a slur by some believers; avoid using it as the community's own word unless they do.
- **Held for review:** none.
