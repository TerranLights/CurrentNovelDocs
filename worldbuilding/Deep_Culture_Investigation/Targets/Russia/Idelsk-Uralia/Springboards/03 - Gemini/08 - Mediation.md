## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim Tatars and Bashkirs:** imam, ishan and elder as go-betweens; matchmaking customs.
- **Kryashens and Nağaybäks:** priests and godparents in the same role.
- **Bashkir clans and districts:** elders' assemblies may differ between steppe and forest.
- **Chuvash, Mari and Udmurts:** priests of the old faith against Orthodox clergy; who is sent for.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** wedding go-betweens and village elders.
- **Ural Russians:** village *skhod*, Old Believer elders and preceptors (наставники), factory-town unions.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs:** elders and the steppe's own customs of settlement.
- **City professionals:** family mediators and school programs against village elders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial go-betweens and interpreters; mixed for village elders and matchmakers; thin for present-day informal mediation.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic-script records for imperial Muslim cases); Chuvash; Mari and Udmurt for ritual roles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2010 federal mediation statute and courts' referral are law (Libra; the spirit of settling outside court to Capricorn). Prayer leaders' roles may be described only as their communities allow. Interethnic tension monitoring is politically delicate.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Crews and Frank for archival series on imams settling family quarrels; list fonds and districts.
2. Search the Orenburg regional archive's guide for the Border Commission and its interpreters.

4. Find Perm Krai's reconciliation-service reports and look for similar programs in Tatarstan and Bashkortostan.
5. Query the muftiates' sites for family-reconciliation services or imams' counseling.
