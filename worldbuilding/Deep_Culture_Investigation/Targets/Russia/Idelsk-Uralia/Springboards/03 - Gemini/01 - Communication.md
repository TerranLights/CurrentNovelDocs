# Idelsk-Uralia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyak. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars against Mishar Tatars** (Nizhny Novgorod, Penza, Ulyanovsk, Mordovia villages): different dialects and greeting habits to check.
- **Kryashens and Nağaybäks:** Orthodox Turkic speakers; church speech and address forms may differ from Muslim Tatars.
- **Bashkirs** of the southeast steppe, the mountain-forest zone and the Tatar-speaking northwest.
- **Chuvash** of Chuvashia against Chuvash villages in Tatarstan, Bashkortostan and Samara.
- **Hill Mari and meadow Mari;** **eastern Mari** of Bashkortostan and Sverdlovsk.
- **Udmurts, Besermyans, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** each with its own shift toward Russian to measure.
- **Ural Russians:** factory towns, Old Believer villages, Orenburg Cossack stanitsas, and the million-cities.
- **Kazakhs of Orenburg,** **Central Asian migrants,** **the Deaf community.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian and Tatar; mixed for Bashkir and Chuvash; thin on everyday talk (as opposed to language policy) for the Finno-Ugric peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Cyrillic, and Arabic script for older letters and books); Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha for folklore and prayer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mari and Udmurt prayer speech by priests (Mari *kart*) may be restricted to the community; ask before using. Language choice in public is politically loaded in the republics; free-speech limits fall to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search dissercat.com and eLibrary.ru for «речевой этикет» with each of «татарский», «башкирский», «чувашский», «марийский», «удмуртский».
2. Find Musina's and later Kazan ethnosociological surveys on language use by domain; note which settings they ask about.
3. Query the Ufa institute and IYaLI catalogs for säsän and äyteş recordings and the Bashkir folk-creativity volumes.
4. Search the National Library of Tatarstan and republic museums for published front-letter collections in Tatar and Bashkir.
5. Contact or search VOG branch sites in Kazan and Yekaterinburg for Deaf club histories and sign-language interpreting services.
