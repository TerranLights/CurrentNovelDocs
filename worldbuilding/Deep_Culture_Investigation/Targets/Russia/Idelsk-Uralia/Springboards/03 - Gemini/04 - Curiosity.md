## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** Muslim teaching on proper and improper inquiry; secular science pride in Kazan and Ufa.
- **Kryashens and Nağaybäks:** church-centered communities with their own heritage curiosity.
- **Mari and Udmurt traditional believers:** sacred groves and prayers outsiders often want to see; rules on who may watch.
- **Chuvash:** strong local-lore and ethnographic museum tradition to compare.
- **Ural Russians:** works-town tinkerers, rock-hounds, Old Believers wary of the outside.
- **Orenburg Cossacks and steppe Kazakhs:** frontier knowledge.
- **Big-city youth** (Kazan, Yekaterinburg, Perm) against village youth.
- **Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks, Besermyans:** less studied; their own revival interest in their past.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for learned societies and science history; mixed for folk attitudes; thin for minority-language words on curiosity.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash; Finno-Ugric dictionaries for the missing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mari and Udmurt prayer gatherings in sacred groves are not open to every onlooker; outsiders' curiosity about them is itself a question to handle with care. Dyatlov Pass touches the dead and their families.
- **Held for review:** none.
