## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan and Mishar Tatars:** madrasa and Jadid traditions; urban against village *mäktäp* memory.
- **Kryashens:** church-school and Ilminsky tradition; Kryashen-language primers.
- **Bashkirs:** steppe herding families against forest beekeepers; the Galiya school's influence.
- **Chuvash:** the Yakovlev tradition; upper against lower Chuvash schooling.
- **Mari, Udmurts, Besermyans, Erzya, Moksha, Komi-Permyaks:** missionary schooling history and current revival projects differ.
- **Ural Russians:** works-town apprenticeship, Old Believer home literacy, Cossack schools of the Orenburg host.
- **Volga Germans and Mennonites** (Samara, Orenburg): church and community schools.
- **Migrant children** in Kazan, Yekaterinburg and Chelyabinsk schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the imperial school wars and Jadidism; mixed for folk learning; thin for present-day home learning in small languages.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** the place of republic languages in school is a live dispute (its legal side goes to Libra); missionary schooling is remembered differently by different communities; Mari and Udmurt sacred knowledge passed only to some.
