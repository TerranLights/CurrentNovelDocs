# Idelsk-Uralia: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) is researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar and Bashkir villages:** fear of "what people will say" in tight mahallas to check, beside city anonymity in Kazan and Ufa.
- **Kryashens and Nağaybäks:** small communities where reputation travels fast.
- **Udmurts and Mari:** the Multan memory; rumor and suspicion of sorcery in village life, as communities describe it.
- **Chuvash:** village talk and youth gatherings, upper against lower Chuvash.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** scattered villages with their own ditty traditions.
- **Ural Russians:** closed nuclear towns (Ozyorsk, Snezhinsk) where secrecy breeds rumor; factory towns; Old Believer villages.
- **Orenburg Kazakhs and migrants:** reputation within diaspora networks.
- **Teenagers online against elders on benches.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical rumor cases and proverb collections; mixed for village gossip; thin for minority-language words and for present-day gossip among small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for pre-1928 satire); Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha for ditties and terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Multan case and sorcery accusations touch a people's dignity; handle as the community and historians tell it. Slander and defamation as law go to Libra. Closed-town secrecy and anonymous channels may be risky to research.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Field on Bezdna and Korolenko on Multan, then search the National Archive of Tatarstan and Udmurt archives for case files.
2. Look up "сплетня" and "слух" in the Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha bilingual dictionaries to complete the search terms.
3. Search Isänbät's and Dal's collections for sayings on the tongue and on talk, and log them as leads.
4. Find ЦДООСО and Tatarstan party-archive guides for popular-mood reports, 1930s-1980s.
5. Survey *Podslushano* pages for Kazan, Ufa, Izhevsk and Yekaterinburg to see what kinds of posts appear.
