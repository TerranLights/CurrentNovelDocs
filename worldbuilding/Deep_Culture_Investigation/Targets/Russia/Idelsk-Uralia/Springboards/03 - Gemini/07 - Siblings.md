# Idelsk-Uralia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars:** Kazan, Mishar and Kryashen families; Muslim and Orthodox inheritance customs.
- **Bashkirs:** steppe and forest households; the youngest son's role to compare across districts.
- **Chuvash:** upper and lower Chuvash; village households in Tatarstan and Bashkortostan.
- **Mari and Udmurts:** large rural households; Besermyans as a separate case.
- **Erzya and Moksha:** compare their sibling terms instead of treating "Mordvin" as one system.
- **Ural Russians:** Old Believer families (often large to check), Cossack stanitsas, factory towns with smaller families.
- **Kazakhs of Orenburg and Chelyabinsk;** **Central Asian migrant families,** where older brothers may migrate first.
- **City and village:** one-child urban flats against multi-child rural homes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on kin terms and fertility; mixed on adult sibling relations; thin on twins and sibling conflict in small-language communities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir; Chuvash; Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha (kin terms differ, and the forms here need checking).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inheritance quarrels touch law (Libra; Capricorn for its spirit). Family genealogies can be private. War losses are close to living memory.
- **Held for review:** none.
