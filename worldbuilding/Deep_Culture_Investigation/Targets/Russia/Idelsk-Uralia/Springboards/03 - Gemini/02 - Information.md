## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar-language and Russian-language audiences** in the same city: different outlets and possibly different trust.
- **Kryashen communities:** church-school literacy tradition and Kryashen-language publications, separate from Muslim print.
- **Bashkirs** in Ufa against village districts; **northwest Bashkortostan** where Tatar media are read.
- **Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha and Komi-Permyak readers:** small-language press with older, rural readers to check.
- **Ural industrial cities** (Yekaterinburg, Chelyabinsk, Perm, Magnitogorsk, Nizhny Tagil): Russian-language local media ecosystems.
- **Orenburg borderland:** Kazakh speakers and cross-border media.
- **Old Believers:** a manuscript and handwritten-book tradition in the Urals; distrust of some official sources to check, not assume.
- **Central Asian migrants:** information through community chats and diaspora networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the imperial and early Soviet press and archives; mixed for present-day local media; thin for small-language online use.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir in Arabic script (pre-1928), Latin Yañalif (1928-39) and Cyrillic; Chuvash; Finno-Ugric languages for their press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some foreign and independent outlets are blocked or labeled in Russia; access may need archives such as the Wayback Machine. Censorship as law goes to Libra; its spirit to Capricorn. Kin genealogies (*şäcärä*) may be family property.
- **Held for review:** none.
