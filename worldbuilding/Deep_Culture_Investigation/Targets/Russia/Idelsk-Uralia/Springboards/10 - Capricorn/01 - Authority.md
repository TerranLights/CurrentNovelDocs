# Idelsk-Uralia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar Muslims** under different muftiates, and **Kryashens**, whose priests and church ties set another source of authority.
- **Bashkirs**: rural elders and clan memory against Ufa officialdom; Trans-Ural against northwest districts.
- **Mari traditional-faith villages** (Meadow and eastern Mari) against Orthodox Hill Mari villages.
- **Chuvash**: Orthodox, traditional-faith and the small Muslim-adjacent communities in Tatarstan and Bashkortostan.
- **Udmurts and Besermyan**; **Erzya and Moksha**, each with its own national congress claiming to speak for the people.
- **Russians**: Old Believers (priestly and priestless), whose rejection of church authority is itself a source; Ural factory towns; **Orenburg Cossacks**, with their elected atamans.
- **Kazakhs** of Orenburg; Central Asian migrants, who face police checks as a daily form of authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state and religious authority in the imperial period; mixed for household and elder authority; thin for the Finnic peoples outside regional series.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for pre-1927 periodicals); Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya and Moksha for folklore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest and sovereignty coverage; religious groups that Russian courts have banned; Mari and Udmurt prayer-ground authority shared only as communities allow; the Multan case touches an old ritual-murder charge, handled with care.
- **Held for review:** none.
