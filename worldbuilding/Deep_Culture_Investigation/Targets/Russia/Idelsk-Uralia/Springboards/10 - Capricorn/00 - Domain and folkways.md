# Idelsk-Uralia: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Cyrillic, with older Arabic-script and 1920s-30s Latin-script material); Chuvash; Meadow and Hill Mari; Erzya and Moksha; Udmurt; Komi-Permyak where Perm lands are carried. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed (career, status, reputation, age ways, time ways, rank, order and power).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazan Tatars** (city against the rural Zakazanye), **Mishar Tatars**, **Kryashens** (Orthodox Tatars) and **Nagaybaks** (Cossack-service Christians): different routes to authority (mosque, church, service).
- **Bashkirs** by region: Trans-Ural, mountain and northwest Bashkortostan, where clan memory and Tatar influence differ.
- **Chuvash**: upper (Viryal) and lower (Anatri), with Orthodox, traditional-faith and Muslim-adjacent villages.
- **Mari**: Meadow, Hill and eastern (Ural) Mari, separated by their link to the traditional faith and its priests.
- **Erzya** and **Moksha** (and the small Shoksha group), each with its own movement and language.
- **Udmurts** (north and south) and **Besermyan**; **Komi-Permyaks** if Perm lands are carried.
- **Russians**: Ural mining-town workers, Old Believer communities, **Orenburg and Ural Cossacks**; also **Kazakhs** of the Orenburg steppe, descendants of **Volga Germans**, and Central Asian labor migrants in Kazan and Yekaterinburg.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Tatars, Bashkirs and Russian imperial administration; mixed for Chuvash and Udmurts; thinner for Mari, Erzya, Moksha and the small groups, where much sits in regional institutes' Russian-language series.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Tatar and Bashkir, including Arabic-script sources before 1927; Chuvash, Mari, Erzya, Moksha, Udmurt for folklore and the press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the pre-war lands listed are all of Russia, so this sheet writes for the Volga-Ural core. Sovereignty, language-education and protest topics are politically sensitive in Russia; some outlets are designated foreign agents or blocked. Mari and Udmurt prayer-ground rites are shared only as communities allow.
- **Held for review:** none.
