## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars of Kazan** and **rural Tatars**, and the Tatar diaspora in Moscow and abroad, whose ambitions may point outward.
- **Bashkirs** of Ufa against the Trans-Ural districts, where opportunity and leaving home differ.
- **Chuvash**: a people with a long history of seasonal migration; compare villages that send rotation workers with Cheboksary.
- **Mari**, **Udmurts**, **Erzya** and **Moksha**: rural-to-city moves and assimilation as a route up; whether rising means leaving one's language.
- **Russians**: Ural metal and defense towns (Magnitogorsk, Nizhny Tagil, Izhevsk), Volga auto towns (Togliatti, Naberezhnye Chelny), and university cities.
- **Women** in each group, whose ambitions may be judged by different measures.
- **Central Asian migrants**, seeking a foothold in the region's cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for merchants, Soviet industry and education data; mixed for current aspirations by people; thin for Finnic and small-group ambition outside sociology dissertations.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar (Old Tatar and Chagatai-influenced texts for pre-modern sources); Bashkir, Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya, Moksha for local press and folklore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** business elites and corruption cases; assimilation as a route up is sensitive in national movements.
- **Held for review:** none.
