*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Meadow Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha; Arabic for Islamic legal texts. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Capricorn takes the spirit of the law; the letter is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars**: urban secular, devout, and the reform-minded (jadid) heritage, each with different weight on sharia, custom and state law.
- **Bashkirs**, whose land rights and customary law long set them apart; Trans-Ural against northwest districts.
- **Kryashens**, **Nagaybaks** and **Orenburg Cossacks**: church law and Cossack self-rule.
- **Chuvash**, **Mari**, **Udmurts**, **Erzya**, **Moksha**: custom (*yüla*, *yylol*, *koy*) against imperial and Soviet law.
- **Russians**: old commune villages, Ural factory towns, closed cities, and **Old Believers**, long at odds with state law.
- **Prison-town populations** around camps in Perm and Mordovian lands, and **migrants** who meet the law mostly through document checks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial peasant justice and post-Soviet courts; mixed for Islamic legal culture (needs Arabic and Old Tatar); thin for Finnic customary law outside regional series.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir in Arabic script; Arabic; Chuvash, Mari, Udmurt, Erzya, Moksha for village custom.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "extremism" and "foreign agent" laws shape what can be said about the courts; activists' cases touch living people; prison and criminal codes involve stigma.
- **Held for review:** none.
