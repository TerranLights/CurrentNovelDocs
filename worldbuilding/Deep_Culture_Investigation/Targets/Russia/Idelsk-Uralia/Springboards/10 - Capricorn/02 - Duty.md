*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar Muslims**, with religious duties (prayer, fasting, alms, memorial rites), against **Kryashens**, whose duties follow the Orthodox calendar.
- **Bashkirs**, whose service history differs from the Tatars', and **Nagaybaks**, whose identity grew from Cossack service.
- **Chuvash**: traditional-faith villages, which keep their own rites for the dead, against Orthodox ones.
- **Mari** prayer communities, where shared offerings are an obligation, against urban Mari in Yoshkar-Ola.
- **Udmurts** and **Besermyan**; **Erzya** and **Moksha** villages, for family duties and commemoration.
- **Russians**: Ural factory towns, rural Volga villages, Old Believers, **Orenburg Cossacks**.
- **Central Asian migrant families** in Kazan and Ufa, whose duties stretch to kin abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military service and religious duty; mixed for communal labor (scattered across regional series); thin for present-day filial duty outside national surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for pre-1927 sources); Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya, Moksha for village ethnography and folklore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current military duty and mobilization are politically charged and legally risky to discuss in Russia; Jalil's legacy is contested between Soviet and national readings; prayer-ground duties shared only as communities allow.
- **Held for review:** none.
