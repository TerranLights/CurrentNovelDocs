## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatar descendants of murza families** against peasant-descended Tatars; **Mishars**, whose service history differs; **Kryashens**, ranked by church rather than mosque.
- **Bashkir clans** and the Trans-Ural against northwest Bashkortostan, where land-rights history set different ranks.
- **Chuvash**, **Mari**, **Udmurts**, **Erzya**, **Moksha**: once mostly state peasants; compare titular-republic elites with rural speakers and with members living outside their republics.
- **Russians**: old service gentry, Ural factory hierarchy, closed-city staff, **Cossacks** with their own ranks.
- **Kazakhs** in Orenburg; **Central Asian migrants**, often at the bottom of urban job hierarchies.
- **Women** in each group, for rank by gender.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial estates and Soviet nationality ranking; mixed for present-day class and address norms; thin for Finnic peoples' internal rank outside regional series.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script, and Old Tatar for khanate-era documents); Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya, Moksha for address forms and wedding ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the ranking of languages and peoples is politically charged; prison and gang hierarchies carry stigma and touch living people; prayer-ground roles shared only as communities allow.
- **Held for review:** none.
