# Idelsk-Uralia: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Idelsk-Uralia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Tatar, Bashkir, Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya, Moksha. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tatars** schooled in the old-method and reformed madrasa traditions, and today's secular Kazan families.
- **Bashkir** rural families, and the Trans-Ural steppe districts with their own sport and horse culture.
- **Kryashens**, **Nagaybaks** and **Orenburg Cossacks**: church calendar and military upbringing.
- **Chuvash**, **Mari**, **Udmurts**, **Erzya** and **Moksha**: folk child-rearing studied by their own ethnopedagogy schools.
- **Russians** of Ural factory towns and closed cities, with industrial and military traditions; **Old Believers** with strict rules of life.
- **Youth subcultures** of Kazan and Naberezhnye Chelny, for the gang-discipline thread.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for schools, the army and camps; mixed for home discipline (polls national, not regional); thin for Finnic folk correction outside dissertations.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Tatar and Bashkir (Arabic script for madrasa sources); Arabic and Persian for Sufi texts; Chuvash, Udmurt, Mari, Erzya, Moksha for folk pedagogy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family violence; camp and prison topics; Levada's foreign-agent status; gang history touches living people and victims.
- **Held for review:** none.
