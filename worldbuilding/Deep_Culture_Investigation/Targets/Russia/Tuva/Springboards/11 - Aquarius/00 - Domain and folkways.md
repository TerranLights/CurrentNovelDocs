## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Tuva** (Khemchik basin: Barun-Khemchik, Dzun-Khemchik, Bai-Taiga, Mongun-Taiga, Ovyur): steppe and mountain herders, strong lineage ties.
- **Central Tuva and Kyzyl** (Ulug-Khem, Tandy, Piy-Khem): urban and mixed life, the capital's associations.
- **Southern Tuva** (Tes-Khem, Erzin, Tere-Khol): border pastures facing Mongolia.
- **Tozhu Tuvans** (Todzha): reindeer herders and hunters with their own dialect.
- **Russians**, split into Old Believers of the Upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem) and Soviet-era settlers in Kyzyl and Turan.
- **Smaller groups:** Khakas, Mongols, Kyrgyz and other newcomers in Kyzyl.
- **Diasporas:** Tuvans of Tsengel (Bayan-Ölgii) and the Dukha of Khövsgöl in Mongolia; Tuvans of the Kanas area in Xinjiang; students in Siberian cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Russian-language ethnography and history are rich; Tuvan-language sources are local; English is thin outside music and shamanism.
- **Lands:** the header carries "Russia" as pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands of the Republic of Tyva.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Tibetan in support; Qing-era Manchu and Mongolian archives for the oldest records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic knowledge some practitioners keep closed; the interethnic tensions of 1990 and later outmigration; the purges of lamas and herders in the 1930s.
- **Held for review:** none.
