# Tuva: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Classical Mongolian for the pre-modern layer. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the culture weighs the group against the person, draws "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lineage-strong western districts** (Barun-Khemchik, Bai-Taiga, Mongun-Taiga) against **urban Kyzyl**, where lineage may matter less.
- **Tozhu** groups in the taiga, with their own lineages and hunting bands.
- **Russians:** Old Believer congregations as closed groups; Soviet-era settlers and their children; recent arrivals in state jobs.
- **Faith groups:** monastics and lay Buddhists; shamanic societies; Orthodox and Protestant congregations.
- **Generations:** those raised in party and youth organizations against those raised after 1991.
- **Border districts** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Ovyur) with ties across the line to Mongolia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Soviet ethnography of lineages and banners is rich; present-day group life rests on press, elections and online sources.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; Classical Mongolian and Manchu for the Qing layer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990 conflict and any talk of ethnic blocs; "клановость" is used as an accusation in politics and should be read as such; reports of youth gangs in Kyzyl (query "молодёжные группировки Кызыл") need care and many voices.
- **Held for review:** none.
