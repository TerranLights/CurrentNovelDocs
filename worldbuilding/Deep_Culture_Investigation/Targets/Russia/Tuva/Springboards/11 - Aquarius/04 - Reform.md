*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families** who lived through collectivization and its undoing, by district (Khemchik, Tes-Khem, Mongun-Taiga).
- **Kyzyl's educated class**: teachers, officials, scholars who carried reforms.
- **Buddhist revivalists, shamanic revivalists and Christian converts**: three models of renewal, often rivals.
- **Old Believers**, whose identity rests on refusing a reform.
- **Tozhu**, where settlement and school policy fell on a small, scattered people.
- **Russian Soviet-era settlers**, often the agents of reform, and their view since 1991.
- **The young**: rural youth in development programs; urban youth and their own projects.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet campaigns (Russian archives and histories); mixed for present-day reform, mostly official sources and press.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Tuvan for local voices; Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts; Mongolian and Tibetan for Buddhist history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the purges that came with the 1929 turn; alcohol and its toll, which outsiders use as a stereotype (name it as a contested public issue with many voices); the Old Believers' wish to be left alone.
- **Held for review:** none. (The text of laws on alcohol, land and language goes to Libra; their spirit to Capricorn.)
