# Tuva: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; German and Mongolian for some folklore and border material. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former noble and monastery families** against **arat** families, in memory and in standing today.
- **Women and men**, by generation: Soviet-era professionals, herding families, young urban women.
- **Elder and younger** within families and workplaces.
- **Tuvans and Russians** in towns, state jobs and land use; Old Believers apart from both.
- **Herders and town dwellers**: income, schooling and services.
- **Western, central, eastern (Tozhu) and southern districts**, which may differ in wealth and access.
- **Monastics and laity** inside the Buddhist community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Federal statistics give regional figures; the old social order is well covered in history; present-day attitudes to equality in Tyva are thin, since national surveys (World Values Survey, ESS) rarely break out one republic.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; German for Taube's editions; Mongolian for the Dukha and Tsengel.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** poverty figures can be read as a stereotype of the republic; present them as variation inside it. Families of those dispossessed in the 1930s may still feel the stigma. Gender and family violence data are delicate and need local voices.
- **Held for review:** none. (The letter and spirit of laws on equal rights go to Libra and Capricorn.)
