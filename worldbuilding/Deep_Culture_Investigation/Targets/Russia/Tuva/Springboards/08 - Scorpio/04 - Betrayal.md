# Tuva: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Manchu for the early-twentieth-century shifts of allegiance; classical Mongolian for one medieval source. **Concept in one line:** breaking the trust of those who relied on one: turning against, deserting, deceiving or exposing people or a cause to which one owed loyalty.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of princely and lama families** against descendants of the revolutionary youth who denounced them.
- **Western Tuva and the old Khemchik princely seats** against the center: different sides in 1911-1921.
- **Russian settlers and Old Believers**, who came under the Qing, the Tsar, the Whites and the Reds in turn.
- **Kyzyl's party and state families** against herding families in the kozhuuns.
- **Women and men**: infidelity and its judgment, without assuming one standard.
- **Clans (söök)**: whether duty to clan or to state wins when they clash.
- **Soldiers' families** and **refusers' families** in recent wars.
- **Tuvans of Mongolia and China**: kin across a border that once marked a choice of loyalty.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political betrayal in archives and histories; mixed for marriage and friendship; thin for informing in daily life today.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Mongolian, Manchu, classical Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1929-1938 denunciations name living families; the 1944 entry is a political subject in present-day Russia; refusers' cases can put people at risk, so cite only what they made public.
- **Held for review:** none.
