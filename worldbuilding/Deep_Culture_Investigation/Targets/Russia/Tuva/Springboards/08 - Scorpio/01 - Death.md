# Tuva: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for the lamas' death texts; Mongolian for kin across the border. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist families and shamanist families, and those who call both**: whose rites the dying receive and how the soul's path is described.
- **Herding camps of Mongun-Taiga, Ovyur and Erzin**: death far from hospitals; the plague focus in the southwest.
- **Todzha (Tozhu) reindeer herders**: taiga burial and soul beliefs, recorded separately.
- **Kyzyl and the mining town of Ak-Dovurak**: hospital deaths, industrial accidents, city cemeteries.
- **Russians and Old Believers of the upper Yenisei**: Orthodox and priestless teaching on the soul.
- **Families of soldiers**, from 1943 to now: how war death is honored.
- **Young men and the bereaved by suicide or violence**: a group to separate in the statistics and with care.
- **Tuvans in Novosibirsk, Krasnoyarsk and Moscow**: bodies sent home, mourning from afar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in mortality statistics and Soviet ethnography of soul beliefs; mixed for grief as people live it now; end-of-life care data thin.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Tibetan (liturgies), Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and alcohol deaths; war deaths; shamans' soul-guiding knowledge as they choose to share it; naming the recently dead. Approach the bereaved only through their own channels.
- **Held for review:** none.
