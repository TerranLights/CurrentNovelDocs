# Tuva: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for restricted Buddhist teaching; Mongolian for cross-border kin; German for one nineteenth-century source. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden, with privacy as one facet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shamans and lamas**: two traditions of restricted knowledge with different keepers.
- **Todzha and other hunters**: avoidance names in the taiga.
- **Women married into a clan**: what they may not say, and what they hold back.
- **Families of the repressed**: secrets kept under the Soviet state, and the hidden graves of their dead.
- **Old Believers**: privacy as a rule of life.
- **Kyzyl youth online** against **elders in camps**: anonymous talk against face-to-face reserve.
- **LGBT Tuvans**, where they speak for themselves: hiding and disclosure, handled with care.
- **Tuvans of China and Mongolia**, whose identity may be kept quiet in official records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Taboo vocabulary and folklore are rich in Tuvan philology; family and personal secrecy are thin; state secrecy is in archives.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Tibetan, Mongolian, German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and Buddhist restricted knowledge is recorded only as its keepers allow and never retold; LGBT lives are legally and socially risky in present-day Russia, so cite only what people chose to publish; looting sites are not to be mapped.
- **Held for review:** none.
