# Tuva: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Tibetan for shared cleansing rites; Latin for one medieval account. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shamanist and Buddhist households**: who cleanses, with what (smoke, water, texts), and whether the two are kept apart.
- **Herders in the high west (Mongun-Taiga, Bai-Taiga)** against valley farmers and townspeople: death, camp moves and cleansing.
- **Mining towns, Ak-Dovurak and Khovu-Aksy**, and coal-burning Kyzyl: purging as cleanup of land and air.
- **Descendants of the purged** (lamas' families, former princes' families, purged officials' kin), and families of the purgers.
- **Old Believers of the upper Yenisei**: purity through separation.
- **Russians who stayed and those who left after 1990**: memory of being pushed out, from both sides.
- **Todzha Tuvans**: taiga cleansing customs recorded apart from the steppe.
- **Pilgrims to arzhaans from across the republic**, set against resort visitors from outside it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1930s repressions in Russian archives and databases; mixed for cleansing rites; thin for current institutional "clean-ups" beyond the press.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Mongolian, Tibetan, Latin (one source).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the repressions touch living families; the 1990 interethnic events are disputed and painful; any measures on alcohol are law and go to Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
