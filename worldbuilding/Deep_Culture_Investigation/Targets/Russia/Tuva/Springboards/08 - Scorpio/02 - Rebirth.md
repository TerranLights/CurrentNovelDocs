# Tuva: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan and Mongolian for reincarnation and lineage terms. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the dead who return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lamas, shamans and their followers**: rebirth as a teacher's return (*khubilgan*), against rebirth as the shaman's calling.
- **Protestant converts in Kyzyl and the kozhuuns**: rebirth as a break from family custom.
- **Old Believers of Kaa-Khem**: renewal through strict practice rather than conversion.
- **Herders of the south and west** who rebuilt flocks after hard winters, against townspeople who rebuilt after earthquakes.
- **Todzha Tuvans**: a small group whose language and reindeer herding are the subject of revival efforts.
- **Generations**: those raised under the Soviet ban, against those raised in the revival.
- **People in recovery from alcohol**, and those who quit through a vow made at a temple or with a shaman.
- **Tuvans of Mongolia and China**, where rebirth of identity runs against other national languages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for post-Soviet revival, in English and Russian; mixed for recovery; thin for personal conversion stories outside church publications.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Tibetan, Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamans' accounts of their calling are personal; recovery and relapse are private; avoid framing any faith's converts or any revival as one voice.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-origin canonical texts on rebirth, as a category only.
