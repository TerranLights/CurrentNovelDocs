# Tuva: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families** in the kozhuuns, often with many children, against **Kyzyl families** with fewer.
- **Generations**: Soviet-era large families against today's.
- **Todzha** reindeer-herding families, where camp size and work may set different sibling roles.
- **Western districts** (Mongun-Taiga, Bai-Taiga) against the **central valleys**: inheritance customs may differ by district.
- **Russian families**, with Orthodox godparents and their own birth-order expectations.
- **Old Believer families**, another large-family tradition.
- **Families with a parent working away**, where grandparents or elder siblings raise the young.
- **Mixed Tuvan-Russian families**, whose sibling terms may be split by language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Kin terms and folklore are documented; adult sibling relations, care of parents and estrangement are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family loss, guardianship and alcohol-related harm are delicate; inheritance quarrels are private; avoid the stereotype of the "large rural family".
- **Held for review:** none.
