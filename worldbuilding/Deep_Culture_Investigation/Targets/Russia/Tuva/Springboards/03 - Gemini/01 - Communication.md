# Tuva: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; classical Mongolian script and Tibetan for older papers and prayer. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families in the kozhuuns** against **Kyzyl's apartment dwellers**: the camp and the city may keep different paces and registers.
- **Generations**: elders schooled in Tuvan, Soviet-schooled adults often more at ease in Russian, and young people writing Tuvan online.
- **Women and men**, especially brides in a husband's family, where avoidance speech may apply.
- **Mongun-Taiga and the west**, close to Mongolia, against **Todzha** in the taiga, with its own dialect and hunting talk.
- **Russian and Old Believer villages**, with separate speech norms, and the mixed families between them.
- **Shamans, lamas and Orthodox clergy**, each with a different speech to the sacred.
- **Deaf Tuvans**, a language community of their own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore genres and archives are well kept; conversation itself (pauses, turn-taking, interruption) is barely studied and will need fieldwork or recorded media.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; classical Mongolian script and some Manchu context for Qing-era papers; Tibetan for prayer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic invocations and curses may be guarded; name taboos touch on family privacy; social media is personal speech, so quote nothing.
- **Held for review:** none.
