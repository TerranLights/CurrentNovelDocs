# Tuva: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian for cross-border kin ties. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected (or not).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Clan-dense western and central kozhuuns** against **Kyzyl's mixed neighborhoods**, where ties may be chosen more than inherited.
- **Todzha**, a small, spread-out population: ties held across great distance.
- **Border districts** (Erzin, Mongun-Taiga, Tes-Khem) with kin and trade across the Mongolian line.
- **Russians and Old Believers**, whose networks run through church, village and work, not clan.
- **Women's networks** of kin and neighbors against men's networks of herding, wrestling and work.
- **Elders alone in town apartments** against elders in a camp of kin.
- **Tuvans living elsewhere in Russia**, students and workers, and those who return.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Kinship, clan and festivals are well described; loneliness, online ties and favors are thin and may need interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; Mongolian for cross-border kin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan politics is contentious and easily stereotyped; mourning rites and cairn ceremonies are communal, not for display; private chats are off limits.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up kin and in-law words in Tatarintsev's dictionary and the Tuvan-Russian dictionary.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for «Шагаа обряд посещения родственников».
3. Pull the Rosstat living-conditions survey tables for social contacts in the Republic of Tyva.
4. Read Ledeneva, then run «клановость Тува» to see how local writers frame ties.
5. Search for Tuvan student associations in Novosibirsk and Krasnoyarsk.
