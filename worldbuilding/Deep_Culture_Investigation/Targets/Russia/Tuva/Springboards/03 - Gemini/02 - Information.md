## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kyzyl's internet users** against **high-pasture herders** in Mongun-Taiga or Bai-Taiga, where signal and newspapers may be scarce.
- **Todzha**, reached seasonally by river, winter road or air: isolation shapes where news comes from.
- **Border districts** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Mongun-Taiga), which may hear Mongolian broadcasts.
- **Tuvan-language and Russian-language audiences**, who may follow different outlets.
- **Generations**: television-first elders against phone-first youth.
- **Knowledge holders**: lamas, shamans, clan elders who recite genealogies (сөөк), and schoolteachers, each trusted for different kinds of knowing.
- **Russian and Old Believer villages**: whether their media use differs is a question, not an assumption.
- **Tuvans studying and working away** (Krasnoyarsk, Novosibirsk, Moscow), relaying news home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official figures and press archives exist; local trust in sources, rumor and private group chats are barely documented.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; Tibetan for monastic holdings; Mongolian for border media and pre-1944 records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Russian media and polling operate under heavy state pressure (some outlets and pollsters carry official labels); divination and genealogy may be private; censorship and information law belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Open Тывастат's site and list its yearly compilations and survey releases.
2. Search tuva.asia and «Тувинская правда» archives for «слухи» and «фейк» to see how rumor is reported locally.
3. Ask the humanities institute's archive for its finding aid of field recordings.
4. Query the National Museum's holdings of Tibetan books.
5. Run «устранение цифрового неравенства Тыва» for the list of connected villages.
