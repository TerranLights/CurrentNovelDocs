# Tuva: Domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan (тыва дыл; Cyrillic since the 1940s, Latin before, classical Mongolian script earlier), Russian; Mongolian and Tibetan for older material. **Concept in one line:** the domain of communication and learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home (speech ways, learning ways, neighbors, siblings, short journeys).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Tuvans** (Ulug-Khem, Khemchik valleys, Kyzyl): base of the standard language; town against village.
- **Western Tuvans** (Barun-Khemchik, Bai-Taiga, Mongun-Taiga, Ovyur): high pasture, close Mongolian and Altai contact, centers of throat singing.
- **Tozhu (Todzha) Tuvans** in the northeast taiga: reindeer herders and hunters with their own dialect and camp customs.
- **Southeastern Tuvans** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): steppe and border districts, Mongolian contact.
- **Russians**: late-tsarist settlers and Soviet incomers, mostly Kyzyl and the valleys; separate speech and schools.
- **Old Believers** of the upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem district): closed villages, their own reading and teaching.
- **Buddhist, shamanic and Orthodox circles**: different teachers of the young.
- **Diaspora kin** (Tsengel Tuvans and the Dukha in Mongolia; Tuvans of the Kanas area in China): comparison only, outside the nation's lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for language, folklore and Soviet-era ethnography; thin for everyday pragmatics (pauses, interruption, gesture) and for post-Soviet neighbor life. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the tracker carries only "Russia"); these sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Tyva.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; classical Mongolian script and Tibetan for pre-1930 monastic and official papers; German and English for outsiders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic invocations and clan (сөөк) knowledge may be held by families or practitioners; the 1930s repression of lamas and shamans; early-1990s Tuvan-Russian tension.
- **Held for review:** none.
