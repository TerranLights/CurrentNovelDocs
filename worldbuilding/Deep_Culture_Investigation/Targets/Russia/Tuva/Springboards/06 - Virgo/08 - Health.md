# Tuva: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan (Cyrillic), Russian; Tibetan for lama medicine; Mongolian for cross-border healers. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding camps far from clinics** (Mongun-Taiga, Erzin, Todzha) against **Kyzyl**: access shapes the mix of healers.
- **Todzha reindeer herders:** taiga healing knowledge and remote care.
- **Buddhist households** (lama-doctors), **shamanist households** and those who use both.
- **Russian and Old Believer villages:** the bathhouse (*баня*), Orthodox prayer for healing, and attitudes to state medicine.
- **Men and women; elders and the young:** who seeks care, who nurses, who speaks of mental strain.
- **Tsengel and Kanas Tuvans and the Dukha:** healers outside Russian health care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; official statistics, Soviet medical history and shamanic texts are all available; practice inside families needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for lama medicine; Mongolian for cross-border healers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' methods and chants may be restricted to initiates; ask what may be shared. Tuberculosis, alcohol and suicide figures are stigmatizing; present them with context, never as a trait. Alcohol-sale rules and health law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-origin medical texts behind Tibetan lama medicine, as a category only.

## 5. First queries

5. Search "советская медицинская экспедиция Тувинская Народная Республика" for history-of-medicine work.
