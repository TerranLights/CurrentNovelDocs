# Tuva: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for Buddhist teaching; Mongolian for Qing-era administration. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small district centers** (for example Mugur-Aksy, Toora-Khem, Erzin) where nearly everyone is kin or neighbor, against **Kyzyl**: the room for impartial office differs.
- **Districts with different clan mixes**: clan surnames cluster by district; test whether that shapes views of office, without assuming it.
- **Russian residents** of Kyzyl and Turan: how they see impartiality in republic offices.
- **Buddhist clergy and shamanic societies**: their own appointments and their teaching on favor.
- **Young graduates returning from Novosibirsk, Krasnoyarsk or Moscow**: expectations formed elsewhere.
- **Public-sector workers and herders**: who depends most on official decisions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Law and court records are open; surveys of favoritism by region are few; Tuvan-language talk of it is mostly oral or online.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan; Mongolian for Qing-era papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "clan" favoritism is a common outside stereotype of the republic; frame it as a question and seek Tuvan voices on both sides. Election-forensics work and groups such as Golos carry legal risk in Russia. Named officials in corruption cases need care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Law 273-FZ's articles on conflict of interest and close relatives in service.
2. Search sudrf.ru for Tuvan judgments with "отвод судьи" to sample recusal claims.
3. Find the Tyva anticorruption commission's annual reports on the government site.
4. Search INDEM's publications for regional tables naming Tuva.
5. Ask a Kyzyl temple or the Kamby-Lama's office which teaching texts on equanimity they use.
