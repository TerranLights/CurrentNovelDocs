# Tuva: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Manchu for the Uriankhai question; Chinese for Qing frontier records. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders and the young** in Kyzyl: the polite forms of address and the Shagaa greeting in decline or revival.
- **Herding households** where the guest-host frame governs every visit, against **town offices**.
- **Western districts near Altai and Mongolia** (Mongun-Taiga, Bai-Taiga): kin and trade across two borders.
- **Southeastern border districts** (Erzin, Tes-Khem): daily dealings with Mongolian neighbors.
- **Todzha**: relations with neighboring taiga peoples across the Sayan.
- **Tuvan-Russian bilingual go-betweens**: teachers, officials and interpreters who carry talk between communities.
- **Buddhist clergy**: ties with Tibetan and Mongolian monasteries.
- **Tuvans in federal posts**: brokers between the republic and Moscow (contested figures).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Uriankhai question in Russian, Mongolian and Chinese; thin for everyday tact in print.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Mongolian, Chinese; Manchu for Qing archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 incorporation and Tuvan sovereignty are charged in Russian public life; federal figures from Tuva are politically sensitive; Tibetan Buddhist contacts touch China-Russia relations.
- **Held for review:** none.
