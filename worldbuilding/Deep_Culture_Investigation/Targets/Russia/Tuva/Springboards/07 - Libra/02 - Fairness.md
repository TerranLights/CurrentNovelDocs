# Tuva: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; the Todzha dialect for hunting shares; Mongolian for tribute papers. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders and townspeople**: wages and public-sector pay set one idea of a fair share; herd and pasture set another.
- **Todzha hunters and reindeer herders**: distinct rules for dividing the catch.
- **Birth order and gender**: the youngest son, elder sons and daughters in inheritance.
- **Generations**: those who worked on Soviet collective farms against those who came of age after 1991.
- **Districts and their wrestlers**: each district's following at Naadym; compare views of judges' calls.
- **Russians and Tuvans in Kyzyl and Turan**: fairness in hiring and housing as each side describes it.
- **Kyzyl's newer traders** (Chinese and Mongolian merchants *check*): fair price across lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin. Sports and statistics are easy to reach; customary sharing rules sit in scattered ethnography; Qing-era tribute papers are rich but hard to reach.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan (including Todzha forms), Russian; Mongolian for tribute and trade papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kin and clan favoritism in hiring is a charged topic; name it as a question, not a trait. Poverty data invite pity framing. Exam-cheating stories risk a stereotype.
- **Held for review:** none.
