# Tuva: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Chinese for the Tuvan communities across the borders. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tuvans by kozhuun:** whether district ties (west, center, south, east) sort belonging below the people level.
- **Tuvan-speaking and Russian-speaking Tuvans** in Kyzyl: language as a line inside one people.
- **Todzhu:** listed separately as a small-numbered Indigenous people *check*; formal and felt belonging may diverge.
- **Russians:** old settler families (from the late 19th century *check*) and Soviet-era arrivals; their 1990s departures are a separate thread.
- **Old Believers** of the upper Yenisei; **mixed-heritage** families; **Khakas, Altai, Mongols** and others in Kyzyl.
- **Outside comparison:** Tuvans in Mongolia and in China's Altai.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: good sociology on identity and interethnic relations; felt belonging in daily life is thin and needs fieldwork and Tuvan-language media.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian, Chinese, Manchu for the older records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990 Tuvan-Russian conflicts and Russian outmigration; separatism is a politically sensitive topic under current Russian law; Indigenous status and its benefits are contested.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for "этническая идентичность тувинцев" and "Анайбан".

5. Search *Новые исследования Тувы* for "оваа" and "Наадым".
