# Tuva: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Todzhu dialect forms for the taiga northeast. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe herders** of the west and south (Ovyur, Mongun-Taiga, Erzin): yurt and seasonal-camp homes.
- **Todzhu** reindeer families: taiga tents and village houses in Toora-Khem.
- **Kyzyl residents:** Soviet flats against the self-built private sector.
- **Village Tuvans** in collective-farm settlements (Shagonar, Chadan, Turan *check*): the log house as the settled home.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** the Siberian log house and the taiga hamlet.
- **Outside comparison:** Mongolian and Xinjiang Tuvans, to see how the yurt home fares outside Russia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: dwelling ethnography and census housing tables are good; home as a feeling has to come from literature, song and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; German for Tschinag.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced settlement and collectivization; house cleansing rites may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
