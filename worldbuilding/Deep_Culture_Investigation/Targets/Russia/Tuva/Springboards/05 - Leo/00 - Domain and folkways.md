# Tuva: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan (тыва дыл, *tyva dyl*), Russian; Mongolian for the southern borderlands and the cross-border Tuvan communities. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Tuvans** (Barun-Khemchik, Dzun-Khemchik, Mongun-Taiga, Bai-Taiga, Ovyur, Sut-Khol): mountain pastoralism, distinct dialect area; check whether music and stone carving cluster here.
- **Central Tuvans** (Kyzyl, Tandy, Ulug-Khem, Piy-Khem): urban and town leisure, institutions, theater and stage.
- **Southern steppe** (Erzin, Tes-Khem): closest to Mongolia; check borrowed festival forms.
- **Tozhu (Todzhu) reindeer herders** of the northeast taiga: a small-numbered Indigenous people with their own dialect and economy; check separate games and songs.
- **Tere-Khol and the Kungurtug basin**: remote, with its own settlement history.
- **Russians**: Old Believer villages of the upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem) and the town Russians of Kyzyl, Turan and Ak-Dovurak.
- **Buddhist, shamanist and Christian households**, which may frame pleasure and festivity differently.
- **Cross-border Tuvans** in western Mongolia and the Altai of China, and the Dukha reindeer people of Khövsgöl: comparison, not target lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music and festivals (much English and Russian work); mixed for games and courtship; thin for measured leisure. Exact lands still to be settled: the tracker carries only "Russia" as pre-war lands, so this sheet and the other Leo sheets are written for the Republic of Tuva as core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan (Cyrillic with Ң, Ө, Ү), Russian, Mongolian; Tibetan for Buddhist festival texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic rites and some songs may be restricted by practitioners; alcohol at celebrations is a stereotype risk, so frame through data only; khöömei has been heavily marketed abroad, so separate staged from local practice.
- **Held for review:** none.
