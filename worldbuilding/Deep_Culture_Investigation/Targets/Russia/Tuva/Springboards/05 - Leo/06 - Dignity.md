# Tuva: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for Buddhist moral texts. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders and the young**: respect owed upward and its decline or change.
- **Herders and town workers**: dignity of work, and how each looks at the other.
- **Families of repression victims**: memory of indignity.
- **Women and men**: dignity at home and in public.
- **Tozhu**: dignity of an Indigenous way of life.
- **Buddhists, shamanists, Christians and the nonreligious**: different grounds for human worth.
- **Russians and Old Believers**: Orthodox and Old Believer teaching on the person.
- **The poor and the comfortable**: decency and appearance in hardship.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnography of respect and funeral rites is good; measured data on treatment and respect are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for monastic teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression testimony, domestic violence and prejudice need careful handling and informant protection; poverty must not become a stereotype of the people.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Request Potapov's *Очерки народного быта тувинцев* and note its sections on elders and guests.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for «тувинская этнопедагогика уважение».
3. Search the Russian State Library for Dyakonova's funeral-rite study.
4. Pull the KOUZh tables for the Republic of Tuva on housing and living conditions.
5. Search the republic's social-protection ministry site for care-home listings.
