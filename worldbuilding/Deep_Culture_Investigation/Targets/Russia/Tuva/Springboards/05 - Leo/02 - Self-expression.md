# Tuva: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and German for diaspora voices. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural herding families and Kyzyl's urban young**: different audiences and different stakes for open expression.
- **Men and women**, especially in khöömei, laments and dress.
- **Elders and the young**, including in-law avoidance and who may speak first.
- **Western, central and southern Tuvans**: dialect areas with possibly different song forms.
- **Tozhu reindeer herders**: a small taiga community where display rules may differ.
- **Buddhists, shamanists and the growing Protestant congregations**: each with teaching on the guarded tongue or on testimony.
- **Russians of Kyzyl and the Old Believer villages**: Old Believer reserve and dress rules against town habits.
- **Tuvans abroad and in Russian cities** (students, migrants), who express themselves in other settings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; music and dress are well documented, everyday speaking-out and emotion display are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; German and English for Sainkho Namtchylak's interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression and interethnic relations (the events of 1990) are sensitive; LGBTQ expression in present-day Russia is legally and socially risky, so any research here must protect informants; shamanic song may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for «речевой этикет тувинцев» and «табу» to map the rules on holding back.
2. Search YouTube and VKontakte for «тыва рэп» and «Тыва кыс» to sample young and women's voices.
3. Find Kyrgys's monograph in the Russian State Library catalog and read its chapters on individual style.
4. Search the WVS Russia documentation for the region variable and the Tuva code.
5. Search tuva.asia for «самовыражение» and «национальный костюм» to collect public debate.
