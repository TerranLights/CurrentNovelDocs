# Tuva: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for monastic festival texts; Mongolian for comparison. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families and Kyzyl residents**: joy in festivals and seasons against city leisure.
- **Generations**: elders' songs and the young's concerts and clubs.
- **Buddhists, shamanists, Christians and Old Believers**: spiritual joy in four forms.
- **Western, central, southern and Tozhu communities**: local festivals and their own calendars.
- **Men and women**: who sings, dances and laughs in public at a gathering.
- **Russians of the settler towns**: Soviet and Orthodox holidays.
- **Tuvans abroad and in other Russian regions**: joy at reunions and festivals far from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and music; mixed for life satisfaction; thin for everyday gladness.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Tibetan for Tsam texts; Mongolian for comparison.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and depression data need careful handling; alcohol at celebrations is a stereotype risk and should be studied only through data and people's own accounts; some ritual dances may limit filming.
- **Held for review:** none.
