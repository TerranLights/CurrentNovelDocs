# Tuva: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Chinese for the cross-border Tuvan groups; Old Turkic for early identity terms. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Clans (söök):** whether Mongush, Oorzhak, Kuular, Saaya and others still carry meaning, and where.
- **Kozhuun identities:** western high-pasture districts, central valleys, the southeastern Mongol border.
- **Tozhu Tuvans:** separate legal and self-identification as taiga people.
- **Urban Russian-dominant Tuvans** against Tuvan-dominant villages; the language divide inside families.
- **Russians:** Old Believers on the upper Yenisei; settler-origin towns (Turan); Soviet-era arrivals in Kyzyl and Ak-Dovurak.
- **Faith:** Buddhist, shamanist, Orthodox and Protestant Tuvans, and the nonreligious.
- **For contrast only:** Tsengel and Dukha Tuvans in Mongolia; Tuvans in Xinjiang; Tofalars and Soyots.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Strong Russian-language history and ethnology; survey data on identity exists but is scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian, Manchu, Chinese; Old Turkic in transliteration.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990 interethnic tensions and Russian out-migration; the 1944 incorporation and how it is told; Uriankhai is a contested name with more than one referent.
- **Held for review:** none.
