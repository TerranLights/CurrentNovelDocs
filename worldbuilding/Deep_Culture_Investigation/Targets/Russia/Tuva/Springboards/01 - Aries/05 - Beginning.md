# Tuva: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian for the shared lunar new year; Tibetan for Buddhist rite texts. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist and shamanist households:** whether Shagaa rites follow a lama's or a shaman's sequence.
- **Southeastern Tuva** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): closeness to Mongolian Tsagaan Sar practice.
- **Western Tuva** and **central valleys:** herding calendar and kozhuun-level festival customs.
- **Tozhu Tuvans:** a taiga year set by reindeer and hunting seasons.
- **Russians:** Old Believer calendars; Orthodox and secular New Year in Kyzyl; settler founding stories.
- **City and village:** January 1 against Shagaa as the "real" beginning.
- **Rural migrants in Kyzyl:** fresh starts and what is left behind.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and archaeology; thin for personal fresh starts after failure, which need interviews and social media.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian; Tibetan for rite texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some birth and consecration rites belong to families or clergy; the native animal-year calendar is a classifier only, never a pointer; archaeological "first" claims can serve nationalist uses: weigh them.
- **Held for review:** none.
