*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Tuva** (Kyzyl, Ulug-Khem, Chaa-Khol): urban, bilingual, the most Russian-language exposure.
- **Western Tuva** (Barun-Khemchik, Dzun-Khemchik, Bai-Taiga, Mongun-Taiga): high pasture, western dialect, the Altai border.
- **Southeastern Tuva** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): Mongol border, Mongolic loans, steppe herding.
- **Tozhu Tuvans** (Todzha kozhuun): taiga reindeer herders and hunters with their own Indigenous status.
- **Russians**: Old Believer villages on the upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem) and settler-origin towns such as Turan; separate them from Kyzyl's Russians.
- **Faith lines:** Buddhist, shamanist, Orthodox, Old Believer, the nonreligious; Soviet-educated against post-1991 generations; women and men.
- **For contrast only** (outside the core lands): Tsengel and Dukha Tuvans in Mongolia, Tuvans in Xinjiang, Tofalars, Soyots.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries only "Russia" as pre-war land, so the post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this Aries set is written for the core Tuvan lands of the upper Yenisei basin.
- **Data:** mixed. Rich Russian-language ethnography and folklore; thin survey data on self-construal; little in English.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian; Mongolian; Old Turkic (runic, in transliteration); Tibetan for Buddhist manuscripts; German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamans' ritual knowledge and names given in rites; the 1990 interethnic tensions; native animal-year almanacs are classifiers only, never pointers.
- **Held for review:** none.

3. Read Vainshtein on clans and the herding camp, then Kenin-Lopsan on the soul, side by side.
4. Search "енисейские эпитафии перевод" for translated stelae and note the self-description formulas.
5. Search Tuva's ЗАГС for its yearly lists of popular names.
