# Tuva: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan (тыва дыл, Cyrillic with ң, ө, ү), Russian; Tozhu (Todzhu) speech of the northeast; Mongolian for the western border districts and the diaspora; Classical Tibetan for Buddhist texts. **Concept in one line:** how the culture meets suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape (the domain, with Fischer's "magic ways" as its folkway).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Tuvans** (Kyzyl, Ulug-Khem, Dzun-Khemchik): the main urban and steppe stream.
- **Western Tuvans** (Bai-Taiga, Barun-Khemchik, Mongun-Taiga): stronger Buddhist and Mongolian ties, high-mountain herding.
- **Tozhu Tuvans** of the northeast taiga: reindeer herding and hunting, with their own speech and spirit lore.
- **Southern border districts** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): lake and desert-steppe life, Mongolian neighbors.
- **Russians**, including the Old Believer villages of the upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem): Orthodox and priestless faith.
- **Kyzyl's urban mixed population** against the rural herding families.
- **Tuvans abroad:** in western Mongolia (Tsengel) and in China's Altai, as contrast cases.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for shamanism and Buddhism (a heavily studied field); mixed for mental health and private belief; thin for surveys at the republic level.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Tuvan lands (the former Republic of Tyva).
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan and Russian first; Mongolian and Classical Tibetan for religious texts; German for some diaspora writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some shamanic knowledge is held within lineages; Soviet-era repression of lamas and shamans; suicide and alcohol data touch grieving families; ask before naming living healers.
- **Held for review:** none.
