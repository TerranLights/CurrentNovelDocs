# Tuva: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Tozhu speech; Classical Tibetan for Buddhist no-self teaching; Mongolian and Chinese for the Qing-era record. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families with living clan memory against fully urban Kyzyl families.**
- **Tozhu taiga people:** small numbers and language pressure.
- **Western and southern districts:** stronger Tuvan-language use.
- **Russian residents:** many left after 1990; those who stayed, and Old Believers who keep apart.
- **Mixed Tuvan-Russian families:** identity merging or split.
- **Former mining and dam towns:** Khovu-Aksy, Ak-Dovurak, Shagonar.
- **Tuvans in Mongolia and China:** clearest cases of language and identity loss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and language; mixed for trance and Buddhist practice; thin for dementia and private self-loss.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian, Mongolian; Chinese and Manchu for Qing-era files; Tibetan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1944 is a political subject, with divided views; flooded graves; shamanic trance may be lineage-held; "sect" coverage can stigmatize believers.
- **Held for review:** none.
