# Tuva: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Manchu for Qing ranks; Chinese for the Tuvans of Xinjiang; Hungarian and German for older ethnography. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of noble and official lines** against **commoner lineages**, by kozhuun: whether old rank is still spoken of.
- **Western and central kozhuuns** against the **Erzin-Tere-Khol borderlands**.
- **Monastic and lay Buddhists; shamans of different lineages.**
- **Tozhu** camps: leadership in small herding groups.
- **Tuvans and Russians** in Kyzyl, Turan and Ak-Dovurak: rank by language and job.
- **Old Believers:** a community that keeps its own ordering apart.
- **Newcomers in Kyzyl's suburbs** against long-settled townspeople.
- **Tuvans of Tsengel and Kanas** under Mongolian and Chinese classifications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on historical ranks and material culture; thin on present-day measured inequality inside the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Manchu for Qing material; Chinese for Xinjiang; Hungarian for Diószegi's untranslated notes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic standing is politically delicate; clan rank claims can be personal; burial archaeology and the return of remains.
- **Held for review:** none.
