# Tuva: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian (borderlands, Qing records); Old Turkic (inscriptions). **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families in western and southern kozhuuns** and **Kyzyl's office workers**: household authority against the bureaucratic kind.
- **Buddhist, shamanist and Orthodox** circles: three rival religious authorities, with converts to Protestant churches as a fourth.
- **Tozhu Tuvans:** authority over taiga and reindeer, with Indigenous status.
- **Old Believers** of Kaa-Khem: authority from elders and books.
- **Russians** of the towns, often with ties to federal institutions.
- **Mongolian-speaking families** near Erzin, and Tuvans of Tsengel under Mongolia's state: one people under different states.
- **Generations:** the Soviet-trained against those raised after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich historical writing on princes and the people's republic; thin opinion data on trust in authority for Tuva alone.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Tuvan; Mongolian and Manchu for the Qing; Old Turkic for titles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** criticizing officials carries risk in present-day Russia, so respondents and commenters may self-censor; the 1930s executions; 1990s ethnic tension; shamans' restricted rites.
- **Law:** the permission share is scored by reading law in force (Libra's method); this sheet points to the spirit only.
- **Held for review:** none.
