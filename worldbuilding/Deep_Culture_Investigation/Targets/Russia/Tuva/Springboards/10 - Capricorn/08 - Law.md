# Tuva: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian (and Oirat sources) for older codes. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law only; its letter is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders in the south and west** (Erzin, Mongun-Taiga, Ovyur, Bai-Taiga): distance from courts; custom near borders.
- **Kyzyl residents:** courts, lawyers and police close at hand.
- **Tozhu hunters:** custom over game against federal hunting rules.
- **Old Believers:** a higher law first.
- **Russian townspeople** and **Tuvan townspeople:** to test for different trust in police and courts.
- **Former prisoners and their families.**
- **Buddhist, shamanist and Christian** ideas of wrongdoing and its punishment.
- **Tuvans in Mongolia and Xinjiang:** one custom under three legal systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Legal-culture writing exists in Russian dissertations; Tuva-level trust data are scarce; prison codes are documented mostly in journalism.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Tuvan; Mongolian for codes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** self-help justice and livestock theft can implicate named people; prison material; corruption cases under way; violence statistics risk stereotype, so pair them with Tuvan voices.
- **Law:** spirit only. The permission share and the codes in force are read by Libra's method.
- **Held for review:** none.
