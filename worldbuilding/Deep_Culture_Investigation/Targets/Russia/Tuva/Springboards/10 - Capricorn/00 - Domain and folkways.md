# Tuva: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan (тыва дыл, Cyrillic since 1943; Latin script 1930-1943; Mongolian script for older official papers), Russian; Mongolian for the Erzin and Tere-Khol borderlands and the diaspora; Tozhu speech forms for the northeast. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed: authority, duty, career, status and reputation, with the age, time, rank, order and power ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western kozhuuns** (Dzun-Khemchik, Barun-Khemchik, Bai-Taiga, Ovyur, Mongun-Taiga): old princely banner seats and the Ustuu-Khüree monastery; older lines of rank may be remembered differently.
- **The center** (Kyzyl, Ulug-Khem, Chaa-Khol, Piy-Khem, Tandy): the capital and the state's offices.
- **The south and southeast** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): the Mongolian border and Mongolian speakers.
- **Tozhu Tuvans** of the northeastern taiga: reindeer herders and hunters, a small-numbered Indigenous people; other camp and leadership forms.
- **Russians** of Kyzyl and the towns (Turan, Ak-Dovurak, Shagonar), and the **Old Believers** of the upper Yenisei (Kaa-Khem): separate religious and civic hierarchies.
- **Religious lines and generations:** Buddhists, shamanists, Orthodox, Protestant converts; those formed in the people's republic, in Soviet Tuva and after 1991.
- **Tuvans abroad and away:** Tsengel in western Mongolia, the Altai of Xinjiang, students and workers in Novosibirsk, Krasnoyarsk and Moscow.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries only "Russia" as pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sheet and the sign's other sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Tuva.
- **Data:** mixed. Russian-language ethnography and history are rich; Tuvan-language writing is less indexed; Tuva-specific survey data are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Tuvan first; Mongolian for Qing-era papers and the borderlands; Manchu and Classical Mongolian for the oldest archives; Old Turkic for the inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1930s purges of lamas and shamans; the 1990 interethnic violence; war and mobilization reporting under Russian media restrictions; closed shamanic rites; clan names as personal data.
- **Held for review:** none.
