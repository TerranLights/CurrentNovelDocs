# Tuva: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan and Russian; Mongolian and Manchu for the Qing-era record and the diaspora; Tozhu dialect for the taiga. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe herders with inherited winter camps** against newcomers given camps by programs.
- **Tozhu Tuvans:** collective clan lands and hunting grounds.
- **Russian farming villages** of the central basin, where plots and houses were owned on a Russian pattern.
- **Kyzyl** owners, renters and dacha settlers.
- **Former collective-farm workers** against those who left the land.
- **Buddhist monasteries** as owners before the 1930s and again after restoration.
- **Mining-district residents** near deposits.
- **Diaspora:** Tsengel and Dukha (Mongolia), Kanas (China), each under another state's property system.
- **Women and men,** since ownership of herds and houses may pass differently *check*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; strong historical record of land and class, weak on how people today speak about ownership.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Tuvan; Mongolian and Manchu for the Qing archive; Chinese for Kanas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1930s confiscations and the families labeled bai; Indigenous land claims; mining disputes; any legal reading goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
