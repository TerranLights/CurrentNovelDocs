# Tuva: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan and Russian; Tozhu dialect, Tofa and Soyot for the taiga peoples; Mongolian for the diaspora in Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe herders of the west and center:** a yurt household that moves several times a year.
- **Tozhu reindeer herders and hunters:** belongings carried on reindeer, forest caches.
- **Kyzyl apartment dwellers:** urban consumer goods, storage, secondhand trade.
- **Buddhist lamas and devout households** against shamanic families: renunciation against ritual objects owned and passed down.
- **Old Believers:** separate dishes and goods, a distinct sense of worldly things.
- **Russian villagers of the central basin.**
- **Tofalars and Soyots**, if in the lands.
- **Diaspora** in Mongolia (Tsengel, Dukha) and Xinjiang (Kanas): what migrants and border peoples keep.
- **Generations:** those who lived through collectivization against those raised after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; strong on objects and funeral customs in Soviet ethnography, thin on present-day feelings about belongings.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Tuvan; Mongolian and German for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamans' gear and the handling of a dead person's things; museum objects taken in the Soviet period; theft (its legal side goes to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
