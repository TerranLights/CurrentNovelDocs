# Tuva: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Tuva, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian for kin across the border. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families** on seasonal camps against **Kyzyl's** apartment dwellers: whether offerings and blessings travel with the move to town.
- **People who visit both lama and shaman** against those who keep to one: test how often this occurs, not assume it.
- **Generations:** those schooled under Soviet atheism, those raised in the 1990s revival, and the young.
- **Todzhu Tuvans** in the taiga: reindeer herders' faith in spirit-masters of place, to compare with the steppe.
- **Western kozhuuns** (Barun-Khemchik, Mongun-Taiga) against the **east and south** (Kaa-Khem, Erzin): Buddhist density to check.
- **Tuvan Christians** (Orthodox, Protestant) and the families around them.
- **Russians** and **Old Believers**: secular, Orthodox and priestless lines.
- **Tuvans of Mongolia's Tsengel district:** the same practices under Mongolian Buddhism.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Ethnographic and folklore sources are good in Russian; measured faith (attendance, prayer) is thin and patchy.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian; Classical Tibetan for Buddhist liturgy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic healing involves private troubles; some invocations may not be for outsiders. Conversion is a family-level conflict in some homes; handle with care.
- **Held for review:** none.
