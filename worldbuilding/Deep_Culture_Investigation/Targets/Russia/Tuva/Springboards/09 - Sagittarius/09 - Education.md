## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families** who send children to boarding schools, against **Kyzyl** families with schools nearby.
- **Remote kozhuuns** (Todzhu, Mongun-Taiga, Tere-Khol) against the central valley: distance, teacher supply and language.
- **Tuvan-medium and Russian-medium** schooling: two routes to the same diploma.
- **Monastic and secular** learning: lamas trained in Buryatia, Mongolia and beyond, and university graduates.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** Russian-language schools, and Old Believer families who may limit state schooling; check.
- **Women and men:** who goes on to higher study, measured, not assumed.
- **Graduates who leave** for Siberian and Russian universities, and those who return.
- **Tuvans in Mongolia and China.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Soviet school story and official statistics; thinner for how families value degrees today.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan (Latin and Cyrillic eras), Russian; Classical Mongolian and Tibetan; Mongolian and Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school memories may be painful; language-of-schooling questions are politically live. Law on schooling belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** monastic colleges in South Asia that train Tuvan monks (category only).
