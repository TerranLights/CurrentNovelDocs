## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Tuvans** (Barun-Khemchik, Dzun-Khemchik, Mongun-Taiga, Bai-Taiga, Ovyur): monastery country before the purges; check how Buddhist memory differs from the center.
- **Kyzyl and central Tuva:** temples, churches and shamans' clinics side by side; urban unbelief.
- **The Todzhu (Tozhu) Tuvans** of the northeastern taiga: reindeer herders with their own dialect; check whether Buddhism reached them as it did the steppe.
- **Southern steppe** (Erzin, Tes-Khem, Tere-Khol): border ties to Mongolia; pilgrim routes and kin across the line.
- **Russians** of Kyzyl and the Turan-Uyuk basin: Orthodox and secular layers.
- **Old Believers of the upper Yenisei** (Kaa-Khem villages): a separate, older Christian tradition in the taiga.
- **Protestant converts** among Tuvans: a newer layer to check.
- **Tuvans of Mongolia** (Tsengel, Khovd) and of Xinjiang (Kanas): how the same peoples' faith runs under other states.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker gives the pre-war lands only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands of the Tuva Republic.
- **Data:** mixed. Russian-language ethnography is rich; Tuvan-language sources fewer online; faith surveys thin, as the census does not ask.
- **Languages needed:** Tuvan, Russian; Mongolian and Chinese for the diasporas; Classical Tibetan for Buddhist texts; Old Turkic for the stelae.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic rites and some sacred sites may carry restricted knowledge; ask practitioners' consent. Purge memory is touchy.
- **Held for review:** monastic colleges in South Asia where Tuvan clergy train (category only).
