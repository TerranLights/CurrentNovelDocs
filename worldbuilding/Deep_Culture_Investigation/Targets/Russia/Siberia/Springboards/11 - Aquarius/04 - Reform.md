# Siberia: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Altai, Sakha, Nenets, Evenki. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Stolypin migrants** against old settlers, who saw the resettlement from opposite sides.
- **Former nomads settled by Soviet reform:** Evenki, Evens, Nenets, Khanty.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist reformers and their critics.
- **Altaians:** Ak Jang followers, Orthodox converts and shamanist families.
- **Sakha:** revivers of the aiyy teaching against Orthodox and secular Sakha.
- **Irkutsk and the Baikal shore:** activists, against mill workers in Baikalsk who lost their jobs.
- **Akademgorodok** reform economists and their students.
- **Kuzbass** mining towns after the restructuring of the coal industry.
- **Old Believers**, defined by their refusal of the 17th-century church reform (compare with Nonconformity).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state reforms (archives, Soviet and Western histories); mixed for movements from below; thin for Native peoples' own judgments of reforms, outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat and Mongolian for Buddhist reform; Altai for Ak Jang; Sakha.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Baikal Environmental Wave and other groups carry state designations or were closed; protest data is risky to gather in Russia today; collectivization and forced settlement are painful memories; legal texts of reforms go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
