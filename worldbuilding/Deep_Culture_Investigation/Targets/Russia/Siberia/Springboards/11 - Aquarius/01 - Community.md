# Siberia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Sakha, Altai, Siberian Tatar; Nenets, Khanty, Evenki; German. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believer communities:** the Semeiskie, Altai and Yenisei hermitage settlements (community kept by withdrawal).
- **Russian villages** of old settlers and of Stolypin-era migrants; villages moved for dams on the Angara and Yenisei.
- **Industrial and closed towns:** Norilsk, the Kuzbass mining towns, the closed nuclear towns (Seversk, Zheleznogorsk), the oil cities of Surgut and Novy Urengoy.
- **Akademgorodok** and other science towns.
- **Sakha alaas villages** against Arctic hunting and herding settlements.
- **Buryat ulus villages:** datsan-centered (Aga, Khori) against shamanist western communities.
- **Herding and fishing camps:** Nenets, Khanty, Evenki, Dolgan, Nganasan.
- **Siberian Tatar villages and mosque communities;** **Altai and Khakas villages** organized by clan.
- **German and Mennonite villages** (Halbstadt and Azovo national districts).
- **Intentional communities:** Vissarion's settlements in the Kuragino district.
- **Migrant communities** in the cities (Central Asian, Chinese).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian villages and Soviet planned towns; mixed for Sakha and Buryat (good regional scholarship, much of it in Russian); thin and scattered for herding camps and rotational workers.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for local press; Nenets and Khanty for camp oral material; German for Mennonite records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** closed towns restrict access; disaster blame is politically touchy; Old Believer and Vissarionite communities may refuse outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
