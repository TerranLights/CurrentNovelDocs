## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian settler layers:** old settlers (chaldony), Stolypin-era migrants, Soviet construction and oil arrivals, rotational workers in Khanty-Mansi and Yamal.
- **Old Believers:** the Semeiskie of Transbaikalia and Buryatia, the Kerzhaks and remote communities of Altai and the Yenisei (closed communities by choice).
- **Buryats:** western (Irkutsk, shamanist) against eastern (Khori, Aga, Buddhist); the Shenekhen Buryats in China as diaspora.
- **Sakha:** central uluses against Arctic ones; Dolgans of Taimyr beside them.
- **Altai Republic:** southern Altai-Kizhi and Telengits against northern Tubalars, Chelkans and Kumandins.
- **South Siberian Turkic peoples:** Khakas, Shors, Teleuts, Chulyms; **Siberian Tatars** of the Tobol-Irtysh, Baraba and Tomsk groups.
- **Northern peoples:** Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Selkups, Kets, Evenki, Evens, Nganasans, Enets, Tofalars, Soyots (herding, hunting and fishing camps differ).
- **Settler minorities:** Germans and Mennonites (Altai Krai, Omsk), Ukrainians, Poles (Vershina), Kazakhs, Central Asian and Chinese migrants.
- **Scientific towns:** Akademgorodok (Novosibirsk), a community built around an ideal.

## 4. Flags
- **Scope:** the tracker carries "Russia" as the pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sign's sheets are written for the core West and East Siberian lands (Tyumen north to Yamal, Omsk, Novosibirsk, Tomsk, Kemerovo, Altai, Krasnoyarsk, Irkutsk, Buryatia, Transbaikalia), with Sakha as a candidate.
- **Data:** rich in Russian ethnography and Soviet statistics; mixed in Buryat, Sakha and Altai; thin in the Northern peoples' own writing (oral sources dominate).
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat, Sakha, Altai, Khakas, Tatar; Northern languages for oral material; German and Polish for settler and exile records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regionalist and "federalization" talk is prosecuted as separatism in present-day Russia, so informants need care; Memorial and Levada carry state designations; shamanic and sacred-site knowledge may be restricted by its keepers.
- **Held for review:** none.
