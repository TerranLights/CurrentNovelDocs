# Siberia: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Siberian Tatar, Kazakh; Even and Evenki for youth fieldwork. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young people in Northern villages** (Even, Evenki, Nenets, Dolgan) against those in regional capitals.
- **Sakha and Buryat national intellectuals,** whose hopes for their peoples' future are written down.
- **Buryat Buddhists and Orthodox believers:** religious hope in different forms.
- **Russian villagers in emptying districts** against residents of growing cities (Novosibirsk, Tyumen, Krasnoyarsk).
- **Descendants of Stolypin migrants and of Soviet arrivals** who came on a hope.
- **Oil-district families** who plan to leave "for the mainland."
- **Central Asian migrants** who come to Siberian cities for work.
- **Kazakhs and Siberian Tatars** in mixed villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics on migration and well-being; mixed for religious hope (good shrine and festival material); thin for Northern youth outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha for Kulakovsky and cinema; Buryat; Even and Evenki for village fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and alcohol data about Indigenous districts must be handled without stereotype; hopes for a people's future can be read as separatism under current Russian law, so quote public texts only; searches for the missing involve grieving families.
- **Held for review:** none.
