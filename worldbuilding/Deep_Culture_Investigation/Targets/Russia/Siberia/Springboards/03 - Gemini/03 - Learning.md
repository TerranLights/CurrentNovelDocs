## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Reindeer-herding families** (Nenets, Dolgans, Evenks) against settled fishing and hunting families: how far children follow adults' work.
- **Khanty and Mansi** of the oil districts against those on remote rivers.
- **Kets and Selkups**: very small communities where the last fluent speakers are elders.
- **Altai, Khakas, Shor and Teleut** children: republic schools against Russian-medium schools in Kemerovo and Altai krai.
- **Siberian Tatars**: mosque schooling past and present.
- **Old Believers**: learning at home from church books.
- **Russian old settlers, Stolypin-era migrants and Soviet-era newcomers**.
- **Science and university towns** (Akademgorodok, Tomsk) against one-industry towns.
- **Siberian Germans, Kazakhs and Central Asian migrants**.
- **Western Buryats**, if inside, and Buddhist learning in datsans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet and post-Soviet education history and for ethnopedagogy theses (uneven quality); mixed for learning by watching and pitching in; thin for each small people's own account in its own words.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the minority languages for primers and teaching stories; German for the German villages; English for Western ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school memories are painful and personal; initiation and craft knowledge may be held back by its owners; school policy as law goes to Libra and Capricorn. Formal schooling and higher learning as an institution lean toward Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** none.
