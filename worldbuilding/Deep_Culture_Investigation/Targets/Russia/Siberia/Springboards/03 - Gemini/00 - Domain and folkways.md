## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian old settlers against later migrants** (Stolypin-era peasants, Soviet builders, oil and gas shift workers on *вахта*): speech and neighbor customs differ by arrival.
- **Old Believers** (Kerzhaks of the Altai, Semeiskie of Transbaikalia if inside): closed learning and archaic speech.
- **Siberian Tatars** (Tobol-Irtysh, Baraba, Tomsk groups): long-settled Turkic and Muslim, distinct from Volga Tatar migrants.
- **Ob-Ugric peoples** (Khanty, Mansi) and **Samoyedic peoples** (tundra and forest Nenets, Selkup, Enets, Nganasan): herding, fishing and taiga lives make different talk.
- **Ket**: a Yeniseian language isolate with very few speakers.
- **Evenks and Dolgans** of the Yenisei and Taymyr.
- **Southern Siberian Turkic peoples**: southern and northern Altaians (Telengit, Tubalar, Chelkan, Kumandin), Teleuts, Shors, Khakas, Chulym Turks, Tofalars.
- **Western Buryats** of Irkutsk oblast; Buryatia proper and Sakha only if their lands are inside.
- **Siberian Germans, Poles, Ukrainians, Kazakhs**; newer Central Asian and Caucasus migrants in cities.
- **Akademgorodok and Tomsk** against villages and one-industry towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian dialects, folklore and Soviet ethnography; mixed for minority-language use today; thin for everyday rural talk. The post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where it meets Idelsk-Uralia, Tuva and Künnarantaiga); this sheet is written for the core of Western and Eastern Siberia.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the minority languages (Cyrillic with extra letters); German and Finnish for early outsiders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bear-feast songs and some shamanic texts are restricted knowledge (Khanty, Mansi and others); boarding schools and the exile and camp past are painful. Language policy as law: Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
