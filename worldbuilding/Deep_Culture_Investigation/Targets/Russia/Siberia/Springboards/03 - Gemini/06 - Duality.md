# Siberia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Mansi, Khanty, Nenets, Altai, Khakas, Buryat, Siberian Tatar; Hungarian for Ob-Ugric folklore collections. **Concept in one line:** how a culture lives with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how it treats people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian old settlers** with Native ancestry against later Russian migrants: mixed descent remembered or hidden.
- **Khanty and Mansi**: the moiety system as lived today.
- **Nenets, Selkups, Kets, Evenks**: paired worlds and spirits, by people.
- **Altai** (Burkhanist, Orthodox, shamanist families), **Khakas, Shors, Teleuts**: Christian and older layers side by side.
- **Siberian Tatars**: Muslim and older layers.
- **Western Buryats**, if inside: white and black shamanic lines, Buddhism beside them.
- **Mixed families** in cities, and **children of mixed marriages**.
- **Closed-town residents**.
- **Siberian Germans, Poles, Kazakhs** and **Central Asian migrants**: two homelands.
- **Peoples split by the post-war borders** (Evenks, Buryats, Kazakhs, Tuvans): to check where new lines fall.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dual organization in ethnography and for Siberian regionalism; mixed for bilingualism; thin for present-day feelings about holding two identities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Hungarian and Finnish for Ob-Ugric collections; the minority languages for paired terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic categories and moiety rites belong to their peoples; "Siberian" separatism is politically sensitive; mixed-descent and passport history touch private family matters.
- **Held for review:** none.
