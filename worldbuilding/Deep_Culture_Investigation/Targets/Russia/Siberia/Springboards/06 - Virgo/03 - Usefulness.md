## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village and small-town households with plots** against city apartment households: self-provisioning and usefulness.
- **Old-settler Russians and Old Believers** of the taiga, where self-sufficiency was a religious and practical matter.
- **Northern herders and fishers** (Nenets, Khanty, Mansi, Evenks, Selkups): use of every part of an animal, and the roles of elders in camps.
- **Southern Turkic peoples** (Khakas, Altaians, Shors, Teleuts): taiga gathering against herding; mining-town Shors.
- **Buryats:** herding families against city families in Ulan-Ude; elders' roles.
- **Kuzbass and Norilsk mining towns:** workers and towns shaped by closures and layoffs.
- **German, Kazakh and Ukrainian villages** of the steppe south; **pensioners** in shrinking villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for economic and Soviet framings; mixed for elders and disability; Indigenous knowledge good in regional institutes, mostly in Russian.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Khanty and Mansi terms through the Ob-Ugric Institute's bilingual publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** do not frame any people as "useful" or "useless"; Soviet labels for disabled people carry stigma; the old anti-parasite measures are law (Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
