# Siberia: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Altai, Buryat, Sakha; Evenk for sharing norms. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Altaians, Kazakhs of the Altai and Omsk borderlands, Buryats:** bride customs and their present-day debates, each separately.
- **Evenks, Evens, Nenets:** sharing duties against guarding herds and grounds.
- **Hunters and fishers** on licensed grounds, Russian and Indigenous.
- **Old Believers:** guarding the household from the world.
- **Lake Baikal communities** (Olkhon Buryats, Listvyanka Russians) facing tourism.
- **City dwellers** in Soviet-built blocks: turf in shared space.
- **Generations:** whether jealousy is read as love or control.
- **Muslim Siberian Tatars:** family honor and marriage customs, as they describe them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Proverbs, epics and ethnography are rich; measured data on control in couples is thin and often from NGOs under pressure.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Altai and Buryat for bride customs and epic; Evenk for sharing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence and bride seizure; NGOs labeled "foreign agents"; law on family violence goes to Libra; attitudes to migrants can feed stereotypes, so record variation only.
- **Held for review:** none.
