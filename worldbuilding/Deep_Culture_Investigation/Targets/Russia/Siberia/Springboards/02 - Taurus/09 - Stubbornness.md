## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believers** (Semeiskie, kerzhaks, taiga hermits): firmness in faith as a duty.
- **Khanty, Mansi and Nenets** of the oil and gas north: holding sacred places.
- **Sakha and Buryats:** revived rites, political autonomy and its loss.
- **Siberian Tatars:** memory of the khanate.
- **Mining towns** (Kuzbass, Norilsk): long strikes and blockades.
- **Exiles and their descendants** (Decembrists, Poles of 1863, political prisoners).
- **Cossack descendants** of Transbaikalia and the Omsk line.
- **Altai Krai Germans and Mennonites:** keeping faith and language through deportation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history, memoir and fiction; mixed for measured traits (few regional studies); press on recent disputes is rich but partisan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, including 17th-century texts; Khanty and Nenets for oral accounts of uprisings; Sakha for Manchary lore; Buryat for the okrug debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** uprisings and their suppression; self-burnings; current disputes with energy firms; protest history can expose living people; avoid treating "Siberian stubbornness" as a national trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
