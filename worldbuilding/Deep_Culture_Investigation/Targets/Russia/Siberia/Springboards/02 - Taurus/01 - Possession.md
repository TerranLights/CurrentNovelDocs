# Siberia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Sakha, Altai, Khakas; Evenk, Nenets, Khanty, Mansi, Selkup; German for Altai Krai's German villages. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mobile households** (Nenets and Evenk herders, Khanty fishing camps) against **settled** villages: what fits on a sledge against what fills a house.
- **Old Believers** (Semeiskie, Altai kerzhaks, taiga hermits): rules on outsiders' goods, and what is kept from the past.
- **Buryat Buddhists** against **Buryat shamanists**: teaching on attachment against offerings to spirits.
- **Sakha** families: heirloom silver and horse gear.
- **Deported and exiled groups** (Germans, Balts, Poles, Kalmyks once settled here): goods lost and carried.
- **Soviet-built cities** (Akademgorodok, Bratsk, Norilsk) against **old trading towns** (Tobolsk, Irkutsk, Kyakhta).
- **Kets, Tofalars and Selkups:** tiny peoples where a single field study may be the only source.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for herders' belongings in Western ethnography and Soviet museum work; mixed for urban households; thin for everyday possession among the smallest peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Nenets and Evenk for possessive grammar; Buryat, Classical Mongolian and Tibetan for datsan texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred objects (Khanty and Mansi spirit images, Nenets sledge objects) are not for outsiders' eyes; burial goods and their display; property seized in dekulakization and deportation.
- **Held for review:** none.
