*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian old settlers** (*starozhily*) against Stolypin-era settlers, Soviet migrants and shift workers in Yugra and Yamal: different depths of belonging.
- **Old Believers:** the *Semeiskie* of Transbaikalia and the *Kerzhaki* of Altai: a self defined by faith and separation.
- **Cossack hosts** (Siberian, Transbaikal): a self tied to estate and service.
- **Siberian Tatars** (Tobolsk, Tyumen, Tara, Baraba) and the *Bukhartsy*: the pre-Russian Muslim lineage.
- **Ob-Ugrians** (Khanty, Mansi) and **Samoyedic peoples** (Nenets, Enets, Nganasan, Selkup); **Kets**; **Evenki and Dolgans**: northern selves of reindeer, river and taiga.
- **Southern Turkic peoples:** Altaians (northern Tubalars, Chelkans, Kumandins against southern Altai-kizhi, Telengits; Teleuts), Shors, Khakas, Tuvans (and Todzhu), Tofalars, Chulyms.
- **Buryats:** western (Cisbaikal) against Transbaikal (Khori); shamanist against Buddhist lineages.
- **Deported and diaspora groups:** Germans (Altai, Omsk), Poles, Ukrainians, Kazakhs, Central Asian migrants; and science towns such as Akademgorodok.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian-language ethnography of the larger peoples; thin for the smallest (Kets, Enets, Chulyms, Tofalars) and for quantitative self-construal work.
- **Lands:** the header lists only "Russia" as pre-war land; the nation's exact lands (the Urals edge, Yamal and Yugra, the line with the Far East) are still to be settled. These sheets are written for the core lands from the Urals to Transbaikalia.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat, Tuvan, Altai, Khakas; German and Finnish for early fieldwork (Castrén, Kai Donner).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamans' and clans' restricted knowledge; sacred sites and human remains in museums; Siberian regionalism is politically sensitive, and some activists and archives (Memorial) carry official labels.
- **Held for review:** none.
