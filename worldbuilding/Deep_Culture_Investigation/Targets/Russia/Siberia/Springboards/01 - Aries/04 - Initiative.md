## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Merchant heirs and old-settler towns** (Irkutsk, Tomsk, Tobolsk) against Soviet-built industrial cities (Bratsk, Norilsk, Novokuznetsk).
- **Akademgorodok and university cities** against villages.
- **Prospectors and gold towns** of the Lena and Yenisei basins.
- **Buryats** in Ulan-Ude markets and herding villages; **Tuvans** and **Altaians** in tourism and herding.
- **Reindeer herders** (Nenets, Evenki, Dolgans) running cooperatives.
- **Siberian Tatars and Bukhartsy** with their trading history.
- **Trading migrants** from Central Asia, the Caucasus and China in city markets, often in a weak position.
- **Old Believers,** with a reputation for self-reliant farming, to check.
- **Women founders** against men, and the self-employed against the salaried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for merchant history and modern business statistics; mixed for everyday initiative; thin for Indigenous enterprise outside reports and a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat and Tuvan for village and market speech; Chinese for cross-border trade sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** business founding can touch corruption and racketeering in the 1990s; some environmental volunteer groups are restricted. Business law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
