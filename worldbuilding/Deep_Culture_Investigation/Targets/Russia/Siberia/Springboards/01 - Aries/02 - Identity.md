# Siberia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Tuvan, Khakas, Altai, Shor, Siberian Tatar; Khanty, Mansi, Evenki, Nenets; German and Polish for settler minorities. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian Siberians:** *korennye sibiryaki* against people who call themselves simply Russian; the cities (Novosibirsk, Omsk, Tomsk, Irkutsk, Krasnoyarsk) against the villages.
- **Siberian Tatars and Bukhartsy** against Volga Tatar migrants: one name, two histories.
- **Buryat sub-groups:** Khori, Ekhirit, Bulagat, Khongodor; Russian-speaking urban Buryats.
- **Altai:** Telengits, Teleuts, Tubalars, Chelkans, Kumandins against "Altai-kizhi"; Shors and Khakas next door.
- **Tuvans** and the Todzhu and Tofalar reindeer peoples.
- **Northern peoples:** Khanty and Mansi river and taiga groups; Nenets of Yamal and Gydan; Selkups, Kets, Enets, Nganasans, Dolgans, Evenki.
- **Cossack descendants** and Cossack revival societies.
- **Settler and deported minorities:** Germans, Poles, Ukrainians, Latvians and Estonians in old villages, Kazakhs in the steppe borderland.
- **Descendants of exiles and Gulag prisoners:** an identity with no simple place of origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the larger peoples and for Soviet-era ethnic statistics; mixed for regional Russian identity; thin for the smallest peoples and for sub-groups merged in the census.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat, Tuvan, Khakas, Altai; German for the settler districts; Polish for Vershina.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Siberian regionalism and separatism are politically sensitive; ethnic categories and census merges are contested; imperial terms such as *inorodtsy* appear in old sources only. How the state legally assigns status goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Demoscope Weekly archive (demoscope.ru) for the 1897 and 1926 census tables on Siberian provinces.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "сибирская идентичность" and "региональная идентичность сибиряков".
3. Search *Новые исследования Тувы* for "этническая идентичность".
4. Search for "сибирские татары перепись идентичность" to open the Siberian Tatar debate.
5. Search library catalogs for Müller's *История Сибири* in its modern edition.
