## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler Russians (*starozhily*) against later migrants:** different family memories of settlement, exile and Soviet construction.
- **Cities:** Novosibirsk, Omsk, Tomsk, Irkutsk, Krasnoyarsk and the Kuzbass mining towns: different histories as seats of power, learning and labor.
- **Turkic peoples:** Siberian Tatars (with the memory of the Siberian Khanate), Altai and its northern groups (Tubalar, Chelkan, Kumandin), Telengit, Teleut, Shor, Khakas, Chulym, Dolgan.
- **Mongolic:** Buryats, divided by Cisbaikal and Transbaikal, clan (Khori, Ekhirit, Bulagat) and Buddhist or shamanist ties; Soyots.
- **Ob-Ugric and Samoyedic:** Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Selkup, Nganasan; reindeer herders against oil towns.
- **Tungusic and others:** Evenki, Ket, Tofalar; Sakha, if in Siberia's lands.
- **Religious and settler groups:** Old Believers (*kerzhaki*, *semeiskie*), Cossack societies, Germans, Poles, Estonians, Kazakh communities, recent Central Asian migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian archives, ethnography and polling; mixed for Turkic and Mongolic peoples; thin for the smallest peoples (Ket, Tofalar, Chulym, Soyot).
- **Lands:** the tracker carries "Russia" as its pre-war lands, so the nation's exact borders (against Idelsk-Uralia, Tuva and Künnarantaiga) are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core Siberian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat, Sakha, Altai, Khakas for folklore and press; Old Turkic; German and English for scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** talk of Siberian autonomy can fall under Russian separatism laws; some shamanic and clan knowledge is for insiders only; exile and Gulag records touch living families.
- **Held for review:** none.
