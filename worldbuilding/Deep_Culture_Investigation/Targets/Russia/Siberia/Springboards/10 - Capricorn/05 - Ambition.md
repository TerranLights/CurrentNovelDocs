## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old merchant towns** (Irkutsk, Tomsk, Tobolsk, Kyakhta) against Soviet new towns (Bratsk, Angarsk, Norilsk, Surgut, Novy Urengoy).
- **Novosibirsk and Akademgorodok:** science and tech ambition.
- **Oil and gas districts:** migrants from across the former Soviet Union who came for the "long ruble".
- **Buryats and Altai:** histories of national ambition, and young people moving to Ulan-Ude, Moscow or Mongolia.
- **Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Evenki:** ambition as education, office or keeping a herd; views may split by generation.
- **Sakha,** if in the lands: the diamond economy and Yakutsk's growth.
- **Rural districts** losing young people, and Old Believer villages that hold to contentment.
- **Migrants from Central Asia and the Caucasus** working in Siberian cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for economic history and migration; mixed for attitudes; thin for Indigenous views beyond writers and activists.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat and Mongolian for the 1917 movement; Altai for Choros-Gurkin's circle.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national movements of 1917 and their leaders' later repression are political topics; both men are linked to the 1930s repressions (*check*), and their legacies are argued over.
- **Held for review:** none.
