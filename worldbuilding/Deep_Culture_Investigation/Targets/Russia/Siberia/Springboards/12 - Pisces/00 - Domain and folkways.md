## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian old settlers** (*starozhily*, *chaldony*) against Stolypin-era and Soviet migrants: different depth of local belief and healing lore.
- **Old Believers:** Altai *kerzhaki*, Transbaikal *semeiskie*, taiga hermits: suffering, flight and seclusion as religious choice.
- **Deportee descendants:** Volga Germans (Azovo, Halbstadt districts), Poles, Balts: confinement as family memory.
- **South Siberian Turkic peoples:** southern Altaians (Altai-kizhi, Telengits, Teleuts) against northern ones (Tubalars, Chelkans, Kumandins); Khakas subgroups; Shors; Chulym Tatars.
- **Siberian Tatars** (Tobol-Irtysh, Baraba, Tomsk): Muslim forms of the unseen.
- **Buryats:** western, shamanist and Orthodox, against eastern, Buddhist.
- **Northern peoples:** Evenks, Kets, Selkups, Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Dolgans, Nganasans, Tofalars, Soyots.
- **City against village;** science towns (Akademgorodok) against mining and camp-founded towns (Norilsk).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian ethnography and Gulag history; thin and scattered for the smallest peoples; Soviet-era texts treat belief as "survival" and need critical reading. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheets of this sign are written for the core West and East Siberian lands, with Tyumen's north, Sakha and Transbaikal named where they may be carried.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Buryat, Altai, Khakas, Sakha; German for early academic travelers (Gmelin, Müller, Pallas, Radloff *check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic knowledge some families keep closed; sacred sites (Ukok plateau, Olkhon, Altai mountains) and the Pazyryk remains dispute; Soviet boarding schools for northern children; Indigenous suicide; repression archives under pressure; "foreign agent" labels on some researchers and groups.
- **Held for review:** none.
