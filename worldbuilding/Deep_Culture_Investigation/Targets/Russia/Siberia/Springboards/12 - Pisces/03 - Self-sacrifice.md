# Siberia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (and Church Slavonic for devotional texts); Buryat, Altai, Khakas, Khanty, German. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believer communities** (Altai, Tomsk taiga, Transbaikal *semeiskie*): martyrdom and fasting as inherited norms.
- **Exile and Decembrist descendants and the Irkutsk intelligentsia:** sacrifice as a civic story.
- **Mining towns** (Kuzbass, Norilsk) and **rotation workers** in the north: industrial sacrifice.
- **Siberian Germans:** the wartime labor army (*Trudarmiya*) and how families speak of it.
- **Altaians, Khakas, Buryats, Khanty:** heroes executed in the 1930s; offering rites kept separate from self-giving.
- **Women in villages** against professional women in cities: the self-denying mother ideal tested by class and place.
- **Families of soldiers** in recent wars: sensitive and politically charged.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory, Decembrists and Orthodox saints; Indigenous ideas of giving oneself are thinly covered and often folded into Soviet hero stories.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Church Slavonic; Altai, Buryat, Khanty for local memory; German for *Trudarmiya* memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory is state-controlled and current-war sacrifice is a political minefield; mine-disaster families; Kazym and 1930s repression; self-immolation history must not be framed as praise.
- **Held for review:** none.
