# Siberia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Siberian Tatar; Buryat; Sakha; Evenki; Japanese (for prisoner-of-war memoirs). **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher; release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Siberian Tatars** and **Buryats:** remembered submission to Moscow against remembered refusal.
- **Evenks, Sakha (if carried), Buryats:** the shaman's call as surrender to spirits.
- **Orthodox villagers and Old Believers:** *smirenie* against refusal of the "Antichrist" state.
- **Buddhist Buryats:** impermanence and letting go.
- **City recovery and therapy circles** against village vows of sobriety.
- **Communes and new religious movements** (Kuragino, the Altai).
- **Descendants of deportees and of the 1920s defeated:** how defeat and endurance are told in families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich archival and ethnographic material for historic submission and shamanic calls; thin, scattered data on hospice, recovery and ordinary acceptance in Siberia specifically.
- **Languages needed:** Russian (including 17th-c. chancery Russian); Buryat, Sakha, Evenki; Japanese for POW memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide framed as giving up; current-war language on surrender; members of high-control groups and their families; "fatalism" as a label must not be adopted for any people.
- **Held for review:** none.
