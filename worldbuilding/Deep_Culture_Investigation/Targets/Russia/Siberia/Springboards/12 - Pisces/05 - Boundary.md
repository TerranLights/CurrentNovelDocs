# Siberia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (with Siberian dialect words); Altai, Khakas, Buryat, Evenki; Kazakh and Mongolian for border areas. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Closed-town residents** against open-city neighbors (Tomsk against Seversk).
- **Old Believers** (Lykov-type hermits, *kerzhaki*, *semeiskie*): firm purity lines.
- **Altaians and Khakas:** in-law avoidance customs and sacred-site limits, compared by district.
- **Buryats:** threshold and hearth lines in the dwelling; urban flats against rural houses.
- **Evenks, Kets, Selkups:** clan lands and their edges, against state maps.
- **Frontier districts** on the Mongolian, Kazakh and Chinese borders: Kosh-Agach, Kyakhta.
- **Siberian Germans and Kazakhs** in mixed villages: ethnic lines in marriage and neighborliness.
- **Northern industrial towns** (Norilsk) and their "mainland" talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Closed towns, Old Believers and sacred sites are well written about; personal-space and family-boundary data for Siberia specifically is likely thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and dialect dictionaries; Altai, Khakas, Buryat; Kazakh and Mongolian for border areas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** closed towns and border zones need permits; sacred-site knowledge is partly restricted; the Ukok remains are a live grievance; migrant attitudes are charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
