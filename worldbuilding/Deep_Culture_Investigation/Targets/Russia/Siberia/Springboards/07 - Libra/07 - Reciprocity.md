*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler villages** (work bees, borrowed tools) against **Soviet and post-Soviet cities** (favor networks, workplace funds).
- **Buryats**, **Altaians**, **Khakas**: offerings to place spirits and gift exchange between families.
- **Khanty, Mansi**: exchange with the bear and the forest; **Evenks, Nenets, Dolgans, Selkups**: sharing within hunting and herding camps.
- **Siberian Tatars**: Islamic obligations of alms and sacrifice.
- **Germans and Mennonites**: church-based mutual aid; **Old Believers**: closed exchange inside the community *check*.
- **Central Asian migrants**: remittances, rotating help and obligations to kin abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography of offerings and in 1990s barter studies; mixed in household-transfer data (national samples, small regional cells); thin on present-day gift accounting in Siberian families.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat; Mansi and Khanty for the bear texts (Finnish and German for the early editions); Evenki; Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bear-ceremony texts and parts of shamanic offering practice are restricted knowledge; migrants' remittance channels can be policed.
- **Held for review:** none.
