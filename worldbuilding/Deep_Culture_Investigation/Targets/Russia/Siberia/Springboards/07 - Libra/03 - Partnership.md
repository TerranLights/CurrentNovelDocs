# Siberia: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat; Altai; Siberian Tatar; Evenki; Nenets; Khanty and Mansi; German (Mennonite). **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian city couples** (Novosibirsk, Krasnoyarsk, Omsk) against **village couples**, and against **shift-work families** of Yugra and Yamal.
- **Buryats**: sworn brotherhood, clan exogamy, Buddhist and shamanist wedding forms.
- **Altaians, Shors, Khakas, Teleuts**: alliances between clans through marriage.
- **Evenks, Nenets, Khanty, Mansi, Selkups, Dolgans**: hunting and herding partnerships and the family camp as a working unit.
- **Siberian Tatars** (nikah and civil registration); **Germans and Mennonites**; **Old Believers**; **Central Asian migrants** (marriages that span two countries).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registered marriage and cohabitation figures; mixed for ritual kinship and business partnership; thin and dated for Indigenous partnership forms.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat (Mongolic), Altai and Tatar (Turkic); Evenki, Nenets and Khanty for camp and herding terms; German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990s aluminum and coal "wars" over business partners in Krasnoyarsk and the Kuzbass involve criminal cases; domestic violence sits behind many partnership statistics.
- **Held for review:** none.
