# Siberia: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (including old-settler dialects); Buryat; Evenki; Nenets; Khanty; Ket; Khakas and Shor; Altai. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler Russians** (*chaldony*, *starozhily*) against Stolypin-era migrants: distinct village belief traditions.
- **Urban unbelievers** of Novosibirsk, Tomsk and Krasnoyarsk: belief and unbelief after Soviet atheism.
- **Buryats** of Ust-Orda, Aga and the Selenga valley.
- **Altai-Kizhi and Telengits** against the northern Altai peoples (Tubalars, Chelkans, Kumandins).
- **Khakas and Shors** of the Sayan-Altai.
- **Evenks** of Evenkia, the Baikal north and Transbaikalia.
- **Nenets, Enets and Nganasans** of the tundra; **Khanty, Mansi and Selkups** of the Ob.
- **Kets** of the Yenisei, a small people with their own language family.
- **Siberian Tatars:** Islamic and pre-Islamic layers of belief.
- **Newcomers to the Altai** drawn by its spiritual reputation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era ethnography of belief; mixed for present-day folk belief (polls are national); thin for small northern peoples today.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and its Siberian dialects; Buryat; Evenki; Nenets; Khanty; Ket; Khakas and Shor; Altai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spirit figures and sacred sites are not for outside display without consent; older ethnography often calls beliefs "survivals" and must be read against the peoples' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Diószegi's volume first, to list which peoples have the fullest belief records.
2. Search eLIBRARY.ru for "мифологические рассказы" with "Сибирь", "Ангара", "Алтай".
3. Pull the Pew 2017 data and check whether Russian respondents can be split by federal district.
4. Search VTsIOM's site for "приметы", "сглаз", "гадание" and note settlement-size cuts.
5. Search regional archive guides for "Союз воинствующих безбожников" in Novosibirsk, Irkutsk and Tomsk.
