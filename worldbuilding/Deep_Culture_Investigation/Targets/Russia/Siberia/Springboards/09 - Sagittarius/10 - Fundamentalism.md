# Siberia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (with Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts); Buryat and Tibetan; Altai; Siberian Tatar and Arabic for Islamic sources; Uzbek, Tajik and Kyrgyz for migrant communities. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Priestless and priestly Old Believers:** different degrees of separation from the world.
- **Orthodox conservatives** against **mainstream parishes:** the anti-INN current and monastery-centered piety.
- **Vissarion's followers** and their non-member neighbors in the Kuragino district.
- **Slavic neo-pagans** in Omsk, Novosibirsk and the Altai.
- **Altai and Buryat revivalists** (Ak Jang, Tengrists, shamanic purists) against Buddhist and Orthodox members of the same peoples.
- **Siberian Tatars** against **Central Asian migrants:** different Islamic traditions in the same mosques.
- **Pentecostals and unregistered Baptists:** literal reading and separation.
- **Secular critics and anti-cult activists:** those who apply the label.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Old Believer history; mixed for new religious movements; thin, politicized and often one-sided for Islam and banned groups.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Church Slavonic; Buryat and Tibetan; Altai; Arabic and Siberian Tatar; Central Asian languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "extremist" and "sect" are legal and political labels in Russia, so every source must be read for who applies them; members of banned groups (Jehovah's Witnesses among them) and of prosecuted communities may be at risk; violence is the rare edge, never the frame.
