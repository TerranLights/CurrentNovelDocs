*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler Russians** against **Stolypin-era and Soviet migrants**: families who came by cart against those who came by train or Komsomol ticket.
- **Northern herders:** Nenets of Yamal, Evenks and Dolgans of Taimyr; seasonal rounds as they describe them.
- **Buryats:** pilgrims and traders between Baikal, Mongolia and Tibet.
- **Altaians and Khakas** in tourist regions, as hosts.
- **Rotational workers** in Khanty-Mansi, Yamal and Norilsk.
- **Descendants of exiles and deportees** (Volga Germans, Balts, Poles, Kalmyks): journeys they did not choose.
- **Central Asian and Chinese migrants** and Siberian shuttle traders.
- **Young people moving west** to Moscow and St. Petersburg.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exploration and the railway; mixed for migration and tourism data; thin for Indigenous accounts of movement in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; German for eighteenth-century sources; Nenets; Evenki; Dolgan; Buryat; Mongolian and Chinese for the border.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation routes and burial places are painful history; some border and closed-city zones (Norilsk) restrict access; migration law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
