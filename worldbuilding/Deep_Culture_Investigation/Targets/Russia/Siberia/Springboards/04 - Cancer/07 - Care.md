# Siberia: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Altai, Siberian Tatar; Khanty and Evenki for village and camp care. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Angara, Lena and Ob settler villages:** aging villages whose children live in cities.
- **Sakha villages** with healers and large kin; **Buryat families** combining datsan, shaman and polyclinic.
- **Altai** Burkhanist and shamanic healing next to Orthodox and secular care.
- **Evenki, Ket, Selkup, Dolgan and Nganasan** settlements far from hospitals.
- **Big-city families** in Novosibirsk, Omsk and Krasnoyarsk using paid sitters and private care homes.
- **Oil-north families:** pensioners leaving the north to be cared for elsewhere.
- **Siberian Tatar and Kazakh families:** Muslim duties toward parents in practice.
- **Russian German and Old Believer communities** with their own church charities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and literature; mixed for family care in villages; thin for Indigenous care practice and for paid care markets.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat and Altai for healers' terms and media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing knowledge may be closed or held only by certain people; care-home conditions and dementia are touchy for families; medical records are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
