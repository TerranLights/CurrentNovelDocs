# Siberia: The domain, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Altai, Khakas, Siberian Tatar; Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Selkup, Ket, Evenki, Dolgan, Shor; German and Kazakh for settler and border communities. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family: household forms, kin, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old settlers (*старожилы*, *чалдоны*) against later migrants** (Stolypin-era peasants, Soviet construction and oil workers): different household depth and roots.
- **Old Believers:** *семейские* in Transbaikalia, *кержаки* in the Altai; separate family discipline.
- **Sakha, Buryat, Altai and Khakas:** Turkic and Mongolic pastoral kin systems, each with its own clan and settlement pattern.
- **Reindeer-herding peoples** (Nenets, Evenki, Dolgan, Khanty herders): the camp as household, families split between tundra and settlement.
- **Small hunting and fishing peoples** (Ket, Selkup, Shor, Tofalar, Chulym): small numbers, sharp language loss.
- **Siberian Tatars, Russian Germans (Altai, Omsk), Kazakhs (Omsk, Altai Krai) and Ukrainian-descended villages.**
- **City against village and shift camp:** Novosibirsk, Omsk, Krasnoyarsk and Irkutsk flats against villages and the *вахта* (rotational shift work) of the oil north.
- **Descendants of the exiled and deported** (Poles, Lithuanians, Volga Germans, Kalmyks, dekulakized peasants) and newer Central Asian labor migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian settlers and for statistics by region; mixed for the larger Indigenous peoples; thin and older for the smallest peoples, where much is outsiders' ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Sakha, Buryat and Altai for their own scholarship and press; German, Polish, Finnish and Hungarian for older expedition ethnography of Ob-Ugric and Samoyedic peoples.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan knowledge, sacred family objects and some genealogies are not for outsiders; boarding-school and deportation histories are painful and politically charged. **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (above all whether Sakha, Buryatia and Transbaikalia and the Arctic okrugs belong); these sheets are written for the Western and Eastern Siberian core with those lands included provisionally.
- **Held for review:** none.
