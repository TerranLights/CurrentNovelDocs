# Siberia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Altai, Khakas; Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Evenki, Selkup, Siberian Tatar. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old settler villages** of the Angara, Lena and Ob: the log house and yard, deep local roots.
- **Flooded-zone families** (Bratsk, Ust-Ilimsk, Boguchany reservoirs): home as a lost place.
- **Oil and gas north:** shift workers, newcomers in prefab towns, families who never meant to stay.
- **Reindeer herders** (Nenets, Khanty, Evenki, Dolgan): tent, settlement house and town flat in one family.
- **Buryat and Altai pastoral villages:** winter and summer places, the yurt kept beside the house.
- **Siberian Tatar villages** (the *юрты* of the Tobolsk and Tyumen area) and **Russian German villages** in the Altai and Omsk.
- **Big-city flat dwellers** (Novosibirsk, Omsk, Krasnoyarsk) and their dachas.
- **Closed and industrial towns** (Norilsk; nuclear towns such as Seversk and Zheleznogorsk): home under restriction.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler houses, literature and statistics; mixed for Indigenous home ideas outside the larger peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat and Nenets for terms of home and land; Finnish and German for older expedition sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced moves (dams, collectivization, resettlement of small villages); closed towns limit access and archives.
- **Held for review:** none.
