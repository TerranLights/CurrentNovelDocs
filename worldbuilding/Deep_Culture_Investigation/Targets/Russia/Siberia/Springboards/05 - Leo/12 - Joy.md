# Siberia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Siberian Tatar, Altai, Khanty, Mansi; Sakha where carried. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler villages** and **Old Believers** (feasts within strict rules) against **city youth**.
- **Buryats**: Buddhist festivals and circle dances.
- **Khanty and Mansi**: spring and bear-feast gladness, with restricted parts.
- **Nenets, Evenks, Dolgans**: herding-year celebrations.
- **Altai, Khakas, Shors, Teleuts**: summer festivals and song.
- **Siberian Tatars and Kazakhs**: Sabantuy and Muslim feasts.
- **Germans and Poles** of old settlements: church feasts and songs.
- **Shift workers**, whose joys fall in rotation, not by the calendar.
- **Sakha**, with its summer festival, where carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, ensembles and national surveys; mixed for regional well-being; thin for private joy and its words in minority languages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; minority languages for joy terms and song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous suicide is a sensitive topic; ask communities how they wish it framed; sacred feasts have closed parts; joy is shaped now by war losses and mobilization in some districts.
- **Held for review:** none.
