# Siberia: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Altai, Khakas, Siberian Tatar, Khanty, Nenets, Ket. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khanty, Forest Nenets, Evenks, Kets and Selkups:** memories of conquest, purges and boarding schools.
- **Buryats, Altai, Khakas and Shors:** shamans and lamas persecuted in the 1930s.
- **Deported peoples' descendants:** Germans, Kalmyks, Balts, Poles, and their own villages.
- **Conscripts and their families** across the regions; **prisoners** and **prison-town residents**.
- **Siberian Tatars and Kazakhs**, Muslim and Turkic norms of shame.
- **Women** in homes and workplaces; **LGBT people** under current law.
- **Central Asian migrants**, facing raids and checks.
- **Village against city:** mocking customs that survive in one and not the other.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for deportation and repression history; mixed for hazing and prisons; thin for everyday teasing norms, which need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; German, Polish and Lithuanian for deportee memoirs; minority languages for shame vocabularies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison-caste words are slurs; survivors of hazing, prison and boarding schools need consent; some NGOs carry "foreign agent" labels; current-war conscription is charged.
- **Law:** criminal rules on hazing or abuse as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries

5. Search "частушки Сибири сборник" in the Russian State Library catalog.
