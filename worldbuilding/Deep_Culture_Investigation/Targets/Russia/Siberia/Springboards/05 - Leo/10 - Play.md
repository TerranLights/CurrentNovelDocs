## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-settler villages** (winter games, fistfights, *posidelki*) against **Soviet new towns** (courtyards, pioneer camps).
- **Cossack descendants** of Omsk and Krasnoyarsk.
- **Nenets, Khanty, Mansi, Selkup** herding children; **Evenk, Dolgan, Ket** hunting families.
- **Altai, Khakas, Shor, Teleut** festival games; **Buryat** games of skill.
- **Siberian Tatars** and **Kazakhs**: Sabantuy and horse games.
- **Germans** of Omsk and Altai Krai; **Poles** of Vershina.
- **Children in boarding schools** against those raised in camps.
- **Gamers and esports players** in the cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk and Indigenous games in folklore and ethnography; mixed for current children's play; thin for adult play beyond surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; minority languages for game names and rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parts of the bear feast are closed to outsiders and to women or men by turn; ask first. Fistfights and dangerous dares appear in youth play and online.
- **Held for review:** none.
