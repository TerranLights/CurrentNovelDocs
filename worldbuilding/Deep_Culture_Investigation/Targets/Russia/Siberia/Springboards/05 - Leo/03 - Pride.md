# Siberia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Buryat, Siberian Tatar, Altai, Khakas, Nenets, Khanty. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City rivals:** Novosibirsk, Omsk, Tomsk, Krasnoyarsk, Irkutsk, Tyumen.
- **Old settlers** against Soviet-era incomers and oil-boom arrivals.
- **Descendants of exiles** (Decembrists, Poles, deported Germans and Balts): pride in exile ancestry.
- **Buryats** of Ust-Orda and the Republic; **Altai** (with Telengits and other subgroups), **Khakas**, **Shors**, **Teleuts**.
- **Siberian Tatars** and **Bukharans**, against Volga Tatar settlers.
- **Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Evenks, Dolgans** and the smaller peoples: pride in herding, hunting and language.
- **Kazakhs**, **Germans**, **Ukrainians** of the steppe borderlands.
- **Generations:** Soviet builders against their grandchildren.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for regionalist writing and Soviet construction pride; mixed for surveys, rarely split by Siberian region; thin for Indigenous views told in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Buryat, Tatar and Altai sources for the national movements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Siberian regionalism and "federalization" marches (Novosibirsk, 2014) are treated as separatism by the state; war memory and current-war pride are charged; LGBT Pride is banned.
- **Held for review:** none.
