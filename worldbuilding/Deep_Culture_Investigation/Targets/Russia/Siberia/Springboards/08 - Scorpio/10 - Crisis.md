# Siberia: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian village and taiga households**: stores, gardens and kin help.
- **Mining and industrial towns** (Kuzbass, Norilsk, Bratsk, Bodaybo): disasters at work, closures, strikes.
- **Regional capitals**: crisis services, protests, media.
- **Sakha villages along the Lena and in the north**: floods, fires, thaw.
- **Buryats**: Baikal hazards; Buddhist and shamanist responses to disaster.
- **Nenets, Khanty, Selkup herders and fishers**: herd loss, oil spills.
- **Evenki and Evens**: remote camps far from rescue.
- **Altai and Khakas**: earthquakes (the 2003 Chuya earthquake) and dam fears.
- **Old Believer settlements**: how far they rely on themselves or on outside help.
- **Labor migrants**: crises without a safety net.
- **Generations**: those who lived through the 1990s against the young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, epidemics and the 1990s; mixed for personal crisis; thin for how small peoples interpret catastrophe beyond Tunguska and herding studies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Nenets and Evenki through Russian sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official casualty counts may differ from local ones; mental-health crises and suicide; blame of officials after disasters.
- **Held for review:** none.
