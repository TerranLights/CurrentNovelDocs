# Siberia: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Siberia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Altai, Khanty, Mansi, Nenets. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox Russians**: Lent, confession, deliverance prayers, the bathhouse.
- **Old Believers**: purity rules toward the outside world.
- **Russian village healers' clients**: fright, curse and evil eye.
- **Sakha**: fire, smoke and blessing speakers.
- **Buryats**: juniper smoke and Buddhist cleansing rites, against shamanist ones.
- **Altai and Khakas**: juniper and blessing rites.
- **Khanty, Mansi, Nenets, Selkup**: purity rules tied to gender and sacred places.
- **Evenki and Evens**: cleansing after a death or a bad hunt, to check.
- **Families of the purged** (dekulakized, repressed clergy and writers) against families of the purgers.
- **Labor migrants and minorities** treated as outsiders now.
- **Polluted towns** (Norilsk, Usolye-Sibirskoye, Baikalsk, Seversk).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Soviet purges and on pollution; mixed on folk cleansing; Indigenous purity rules are documented mostly by outsiders.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Khanty and Nenets terms through Russian sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gendered purity rules and sacred places (ask the peoples how they want them treated); shaman rites; repression files; raids on migrants. Corruption law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Find the Book of Memory for each Siberian region and note which waves of purge it lists.
2. Library search: «очищение» with «якуты», «буряты», «ханты» and «обряд».
3. Read Slezkine's chapter on the 1920s-30s campaigns, then follow his Russian archival citations.
4. Search Sakha media for «аластааһын» and «алгысчыт».
5. News archives: «Норильск разлив дизтоплива» and «Усольехимпром ликвидация» for how blame and cleanup were told.
