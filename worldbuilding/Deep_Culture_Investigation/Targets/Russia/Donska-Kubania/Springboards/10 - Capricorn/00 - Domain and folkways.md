# Donska-Kubania: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; the Kuban dialect (*balachka*) and Ukrainian; Adyghe; Armenian (Nor Nakhichevan and Hamshen varieties); Greek (Pontic too); Kalmyk; Nogai *check*. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty, with career, status, reputation and the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don against Kuban Cossacks,** and within Kuban the Black Sea (Zaporozhian, *balachka*) stanitsas against the Line stanitsas: different founding bargains of service.
- **Upper Don against Lower Don;** Cossacks against the *inogorodnie* who had no share in the Host's self-rule.
- **Adyghe** of Adygea (Temirgoi, Bzhedug, Abadzekh lineages), and the **Shapsugs** of the Sochi-Tuapse coast: princely against assembly-based traditions.
- **Armenians** of Nor Nakhichevan (now in Rostov) with their old magistrate, against **Hamshen** Armenians of the Sochi coast.
- **Pontic Greeks; Meskhetian Turks; Don Kalmyks' heritage; Nogais; Ukrainians; Tatars; Koreans; Chechens and Dagestanis** in Rostov Oblast; Old Believers and returned Nekrasovites; Molokans.
- **Industrial and port towns** (Shakhty, Taganrog, Novorossiysk) against farm stanitsas and the resort coast.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Cossack history and Soviet-era sources; mixed for Adyghe material; thin for regional survey cuts. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the Capricorn concept sheets are written for the core lands (Rostov Oblast, Krasnodar Krai, Adygea).
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian for Black Sea Host papers; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Kalmyk; Turkish for diaspora Circassian work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** decossackization and famine (1932-1933 "black boards" in Kuban stanitsas); the 1864 Circassian expulsion; Cossacks under German command and the 1945 Lienz handovers; the Kushchevskaya killings (2010); Levada's "foreign agent" label; current war and dissent cases.
- **Held for review:** pointers on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
