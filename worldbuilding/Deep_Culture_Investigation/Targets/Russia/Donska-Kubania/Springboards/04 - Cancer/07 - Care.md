# Donska-Kubania: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Turkish; Kurmanji; Pontic Greek. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stanitsa families** where kin live nearby, against **city families** in Rostov-on-Don and Krasnodar who hire a *сиделка*.
- **Retirees who moved south** to Krasnodar and the coast, far from their children: care at a distance.
- **Adyghe** families: which child's household is expected to keep the parents is a question; compare villages with Maykop.
- **Muslim** families (Adyghe, Meskhetian Turks, Kurds) and **Orthodox** and **Old Believer** families: religious duties of care.
- **Armenian** and **Pontic Greek** households; **Yezidis**; **Koreans**.
- **Mining towns** of the eastern Rostov steppe: whether work injuries shape family care is a question.
- **Men as carers**: a group to separate in every community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state programs and charity reports; mixed for folk care (older ethnography, little recent); thin for minority families' care arrangements.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Turkish; Kurmanji; Armenian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** illness and disability are private; use published and consented accounts. Care-for-house arrangements can hide exploitation; the contract law belongs to Libra. Healing rites may have parts their keepers do not share. Care of wounded soldiers from the current war is a live subject.
- **Held for review:** none.
