## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe khutors** of the eastern Don, emptied as the young leave, against growing **Krasnodar suburbs**.
- **Coal towns** of the Rostov steppe against farming stanitsas.
- **Kuban stanitsas** blacklisted in 1932-1933, against those that were not: family memory may differ.
- **Don Kalmyks** and **Germans** deported in the 1940s; **Cossack émigré families** split in 1920.
- **Adyghe** families of the 1864 exile, and of men who left for work in the north.
- **Meskhetian Turks**, **Yezidis** and **Armenians** of the coast: kin left behind elsewhere.
- **Children in care** and **elders in state homes** across all groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for famine, war and deportation documents; mixed for present child-welfare data; thin for how families now speak of a parent who left.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Kalmyk; German; Armenian; Turkish; Kurmanji.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1932-1933 famine is politically charged between Russian and Ukrainian readings; use both and the documents. Children moved from Ukraine during the current war are a live and contested subject. Deportation memory belongs to the deported peoples first. Adoption and child-support rules belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
