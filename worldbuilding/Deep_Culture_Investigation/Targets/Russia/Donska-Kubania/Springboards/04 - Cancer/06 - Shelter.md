# Donska-Kubania: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Kalmyk for the Don steppe; Turkish; Kurmanji; German and Yiddish for wartime records. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stanitsa households** that took in kin and evacuees against **city** residents relying on official points.
- **Resort families** of Sochi, Anapa and Gelendzhik who let rooms each summer.
- **Adyghe** and **Shapsug** villages: guest duty against new commercial lodging.
- **Don Kalmyks**: a memory of mobile dwelling and of the 1943 deportation.
- **Old Believers**; **Meskhetian Turks** and **Yezidis**, who themselves sought shelter in the 1990s.
- **Central Asian labor migrants** in Krasnodar and Rostov.
- **Arrivals from the Donbas and Ukraine's border regions** since 2014.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime and 1864 history; mixed for present homelessness (small NGOs, press); thin for migrant housing and for animal shelters beyond the news.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Kalmyk; Turkish; Kurmanji; German, Yiddish and Hebrew for Holocaust records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** displacement from Ukraine and wartime evacuation are political now; prefer UN and testimony sources and note who published what. The 1864 removal, the Holocaust in Rostov and the 1943 Kalmyk deportation need survivors' and descendants' voices first. Asylum and residence rules belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
