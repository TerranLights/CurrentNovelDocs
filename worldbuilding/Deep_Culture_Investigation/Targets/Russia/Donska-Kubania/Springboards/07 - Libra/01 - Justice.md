# Donska-Kubania: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ukrainian and Kuban balachka; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish (Meskhetian). **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don and Kuban Cossack descendants**, split by whether families were deported or dispossessed in 1919-1933: their relation to historical justice may differ from that of resettled newcomers.
- **Nekrasovite and other Old Believer communities**, with codes of their own.
- **Adyghe of Adygea against the Shapsugs of the coast**: the 1864 memory and the diaspora's demands weigh differently.
- **Meskhetian Turks of Krasnodar Krai**, whose dealings with local registration law are a test of equal treatment.
- **Armenians of Rostov and the Sochi coast; Pontic Greeks; Kurds and Yazidis**: each has its own elders' channels before the courts.
- **Industrial towns** (Novocherkassk, Shakhty, Novorossiysk) against farming stanitsas, where land disputes dominate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet and post-Soviet court history and for Cossack customary material; mixed for Adyghe customary justice; thin for minorities' own dispute channels.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Turkish; Armenian; Ukrainian for older Black Sea Host papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current political and war-related prosecutions; the Kushchevskaya victims' families; blood-feud cases; Meskhetian Turks' statelessness history; *Новая газета* and some sources face legal restrictions in Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.
