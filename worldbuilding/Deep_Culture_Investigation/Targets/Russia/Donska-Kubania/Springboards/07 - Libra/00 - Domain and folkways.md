## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossacks against Kuban Cossacks**, and inside Kuban the Black Sea (Zaporozhian-descended, balachka-speaking) stanitsas against the Line stanitsas of Russian origin: different founding bargains with the crown.
- **Upper Don against Lower Don** stanitsas, and Cossacks against the *inogorodnie* (non-Cossack settlers): who had a share and who did not.
- **Adyghe (Circassians)** of Adygea, and the **Shapsugs** of the Black Sea coast (Tuapse and Sochi districts), whose kin ties run to a large diaspora abroad.
- **Armenians:** the Nakhichevan-on-Don community (resettled from Crimea in 1779) against the Hamshen Armenians of the Sochi coast.
- **Pontic Greeks** (Anapa, Gelendzhik, Krymsk area), **Meskhetian Turks** of Krasnodar Krai, Kurds and Yazidis, Ukrainians, Tatars, Koreans (Koryo-saram), Assyrians, Kalmyks of the old Don lands, Chechens and Dagestanis in Rostov Oblast.
- **Faith lines:** Orthodox; Old Believers (Nekrasovite descendants too); Molokans; Sunni Adyghe; Armenian Apostolic; Protestants.
- **Cities against stanitsas:** Rostov-on-Don, Krasnodar, Sochi, Maikop against farming and fishing villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian-language Cossack ethnography, statistics and law; mixed for Adyghe material; thin for smaller minorities. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the Libra concept sheets are written for the core lands (Rostov Oblast, Krasnodar Krai, Adygea).
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Adyghe; Ukrainian for balachka sources; Armenian; Greek; Turkish for Meskhetian and diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Circassian expulsion of 1864 and its naming; decossackization (1919 onward) and later rehabilitation; Cossack collaboration in 1941-1945 and the 1945 Lienz handovers; Meskhetian Turks' treatment in Krasnodar Krai; current war- and dissent-related cases; "foreign agent" labels on some bodies.
- **Held for review:** pointers on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
