## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossack descendants**, heirs to a tradition of bargaining with Moscow as a semi-separate Host.
- **Kuban Black Sea and Line Cossack descendants**, and their long frontier dealings with Circassian neighbors, from war to guest-friendship.
- **Adyghe and Shapsugs**, with a large diaspora in Turkey, Jordan and Syria that shapes their outward diplomacy.
- **Kalmyks of the former Don lands** and their memory of steppe alliances and the 1943 deportation.
- **Armenians and Greeks**, with kin-states and consulates to turn to; **Meskhetian Turks**, many of whom left for the United States in the 2000s.
- **Port cities** (Novorossiysk, Taganrog, Azov) against inland stanitsas: foreign contact is daily in one, rare in the other.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial-era diplomacy and the Circassian question; mixed for everyday tact in southern speech; thin for minority communities' own protocols.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources; Armenian; Greek; Italian and Latin for Tana; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Circassian question and "genocide" language are politically charged in Russia; the 2023 mutiny and wartime foreign relations; consular data may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
