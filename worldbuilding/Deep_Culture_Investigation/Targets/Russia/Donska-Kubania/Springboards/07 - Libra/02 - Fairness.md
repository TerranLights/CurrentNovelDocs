# Donska-Kubania: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ukrainian and balachka; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cossack descendants and the descendants of non-Cossack settlers (inogorodnie)**: the old split over shares may still color talk of fair land.
- **Kuban grain farmers against agroholdings and their workers**: share-owners, leaseholders and laborers see a fair rent differently.
- **Coal towns of eastern Rostov Oblast** (Shakhty, Gukovo, Novoshakhtinsk), where wage fairness dominates.
- **Black Sea resort workers** (Sochi, Anapa, Gelendzhik), whose seasonal pay and prices for visitors raise other questions.
- **Adyghe and Shapsug communities**, with seniority-based sharing at feasts.
- **Armenian and Greek traders and farmers**; **Meskhetian Turks**, who long lacked registration and so equal access.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: strong for land history and national surveys; thin for regionally split experimental data and for minorities' sharing rules.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Ukrainian for older Black Sea Host material; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land raiding cases can involve officials; labor protest coverage may be limited; registration of Meskhetian Turks.
- **Held for review:** none.
