## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City audiences in Rostov-on-Don and Krasnodar** against **stanitsa and khutor households**, where broadband and newspapers differ.
- **Black Sea resort towns** (Sochi, Anapa, Gelendzhik): seasonal populations, tourist information economies.
- **Eastern Donbas mining towns**: a long history of rumor and official silence around mine disasters, *check*.
- **Adyghe communities**: Adyghe-language broadcasting and print as an information world of their own.
- **Armenian communities**: diaspora media from Armenia and Moscow as alternative sources.
- **Kalmyk, Pontic Greek, Meskhetian Turkish families**: deportation records and family archives as contested information.
- **Older viewers of state television and younger users of messengers**, inside every group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for archives and press history; current trust data is constrained, since national pollsters work under legal pressure and regional cuts are rare.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian for some Kuban press; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek for Bosporan material; Kalmyk (and Oirat script) for older records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "foreign agent" labels on sources; famine and 1962 records; wartime censorship in a border region; personal data in genealogy work. Any law on information goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
