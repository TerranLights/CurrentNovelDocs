# Donska-Kubania: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian and its Don and Kuban dialects, Ukrainian-based Kuban speech, Adyghe, Armenian, Pontic and standard Greek. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Adyghe villages and Maykop**: respect register and in-law avoidance against urban mixed-language talk.
- **Shapsugs of the Black Sea coast**: a small Adyghe community among Armenians and Russians.
- **Don stanitsas, upper and lower Don**: the assembly tradition and dialect talk.
- **Kuban stanitsas of the old Black Sea and Line districts**: who uses balachka with whom.
- **Rostov-on-Don's street and port speech**: the city's own urban slang tradition, *check*.
- **Don Armenians and Hamshen Armenians**: different Armenian dialects, different bilingual habits.
- **Pontic Greeks, Meskhetian Turks, Kalmyks**: families where children may broker between languages.
- **Deaf communities; older rural speakers against younger city speakers**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian speech etiquette in general, mixed for the south in particular; the Adyghe literature is solid but leans on Kabardian data; thin for the smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Adyghe, Armenian, Greek; Kabardian for comparative Circassian work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public speech and online talk under current Russian restrictions (speech law goes to Libra or Capricorn); balachka's Ukrainian roots as a political subject; in-law avoidance customs are family matters, so approach through published ethnography first.
- **Held for review:** none.
