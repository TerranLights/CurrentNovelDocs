## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Don and lower Don Cossacks** (верховые, низовые): distinct speech and settlement histories.
- **Kuban Black Sea Cossacks and Line Cossacks**: Zaporozhian-descended and Don or Russian-descended; the root of the balachka split.
- **Inogorodnie** (non-Cossack settlers) and their descendants: a different relation to stanitsa institutions.
- **Adyghe subgroups** (Temirgoy, Abadzekh, Bzhedug, Shapsug), with the Black Sea Shapsugs near Sochi apart.
- **Armenians**: Don Armenians of Nakhichevan-on-Don and Chaltyr against Hamshen Armenians of the Sochi-Tuapse coast.
- **Pontic Greeks** (Gelendzhik, Krymsk, Anapa areas), **Kalmyks** of the Salsk steppe, **Meskhetian Turks**, **Koreans**, **Ukrainians** of the western Rostov lands, **Assyrians**, **Roma**.
- **Eastern Donbas mining towns** (Shakhty, Novoshakhtinsk, Gukovo), **port cities** (Novorossiysk, Taganrog), **resort coast** (Sochi, Anapa), **regional capitals** (Rostov-on-Don, Krasnodar, Maykop) against farm districts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian for the Cossack lands; moderate for Adyghe; thin for Hamshen, Pontic Greek, Kalmyk and Korean communities here.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian for balachka sources; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Kalmyk; some Turkish for Circassian and Meskhetian diaspora material.
- **Lands:** the header carries "Russia" broadly; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sign's sheets are written for the core Don and Kuban lands and Adygea.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1864 Circassian expulsion and its naming; de-Cossackization (расказачивание); the 1932-33 Kuban famine and blacklisted stanitsas; wartime deportations of Kalmyks, Greeks and Meskhetians; Ukrainian identity in the Kuban as a political question; border-region media under wartime rules.
- **Held for review:** none.
