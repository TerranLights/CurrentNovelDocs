# Donska-Kubania: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (with dialect kin terms), Ukrainian-based Kuban speech, Adyghe, Armenian, Greek, Kalmyk, Korean. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cossack farm families** where land and service were shared among brothers, against **inogorodnie** families.
- **Adyghe** families, with their customs between brothers, sisters and the mother's kin; the **Shapsugs** apart.
- **Don Armenians** and **Hamshen Armenians**; **Pontic Greeks**; **Meskhetian Turks**, whose households may hold several married brothers, *check*.
- **Kalmyks** and **Koreans**, with age-ranked sibling terms.
- **Rural large families** against **urban one-child families** in Rostov and Krasnodar.
- **Children in state care**, siblings kept together or apart.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for family-size statistics; mixed for ethnography of sibling roles; thin on adult sibling contact at the regional level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Adyghe, Armenian, Greek, Kalmyk, Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor customs that touch a sister's conduct; children in state care; inheritance quarrels. Inheritance law and family law belong to Libra or Capricorn; keep this sheet on custom and feeling.
- **Held for review:** none.
