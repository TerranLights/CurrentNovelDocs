## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban Rostov and Krasnodar**, with universities, museums and science clubs, against **remote stanitsas and khutors**.
- **Taman peninsula and lower Don**: communities living on top of ancient sites, where digs are part of local life.
- **Black Sea coast** (Gelendzhik, Sochi, Tuapse): dolmen country, tourists and seekers beside long-settled Adyghe, Armenian and Greek villagers.
- **Adyghe communities**: guest customs that may set limits on questions; compare Adygea's villages with the Shapsugs.
- **Cossack revival groups** and **non-Cossack** residents: whose past people are curious about.
- **Children and the old**: who is allowed to ask whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for local-lore and museum history; mixed for present-day hobbies; thin for measures of curiosity as a trait in these lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Ottoman Turkish, Italian and German for early travelers (use translations first).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** looting of archaeological sites and dolmens; DNA ancestry claims feeding ethnic quarrels; Adyghe sacred places and graves, which should not be treated as attractions; questions about Circassian deportation history put to families.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Felitsyn museum's site and eLibrary.ru for «Фелицын краеведение» to trace the local-lore tradition.

3. Find a translation of Interiano on the Zichs and of Evliya Çelebi's Circassian chapters.
4. Search the Russian Geographical Society's regional branch pages for expeditions and youth programs.
5. Search for «почемучки» studies in southern Russian kindergartens, or fall back to Chukovsky as the frame.
