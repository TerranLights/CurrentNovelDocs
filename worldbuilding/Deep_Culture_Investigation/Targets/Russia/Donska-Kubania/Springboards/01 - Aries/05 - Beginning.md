## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Sea Cossack descendants** (who date themselves from the 1790s landing) against **Don Cossacks** (whose origins are older and debated).
- **Adyghe** communities, for whom the 1860s are an ending and a survival rather than a founding; the **Shapsugs** of the coast in particular.
- **Don Armenians**, **Hamshen Armenians** and **Pontic Greeks**: each with its own arrival story.
- **Don Kalmyk** descendants after the 1943 deportation and return.
- **Recent newcomers**: Northern and Siberian Russians moving south for climate, and people displaced by the war in neighboring Ukraine, now in Rostov Oblast and the Kuban.
- **Orthodox, Muslim and Protestant** converts and returnees: religious new beginnings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for founding myths, calendar rites and migration statistics; mixed for birth rites outside the Adyghe and Cossacks; thin for personal fresh-start narratives except online.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Ukrainian for Kuban calendar songs; Armenian; Greek; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** founding stories here overlay the Adyghe expulsion; the war-displaced in Rostov Oblast are a vulnerable group; Kalmyk deportation memory.
- **Held for review:** none.
