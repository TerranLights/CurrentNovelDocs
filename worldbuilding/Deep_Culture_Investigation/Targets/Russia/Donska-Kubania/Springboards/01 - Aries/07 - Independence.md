*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stanitsa and khutor households** with plots against **apartment dwellers** in Rostov-on-Don, Krasnodar and Novorossiysk.
- **Adyghe auls** in Adygea and **Shapsug villages** on the coast: kin networks and household crafts.
- **Armenian family businesses** in Rostov and on the coast; **Hamshen farmers** in the Sochi hills (tea, citrus and hazelnut growing *check*).
- **Pontic Greek** villages.
- **Mining towns of eastern Rostov** (Shakhty, Novoshakhtinsk, Gukovo) after closures: dependence on transfers.
- **Pensioners**, **rotation workers' families** and **people displaced by the war in Ukraine**, who depend on aid.
- **Men and women**: who is expected to provide and who to depend, in each group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for agricultural and household statistics; mixed for kin-help ethnography; thin for attitudes to asking for help, where national survey items would need local samples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Ukrainian for Kuban famine memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1932-33 famine and its framing (Holodomor debate) is politically charged in Russia and Ukraine; regional independence movements are legally risky (point them to the autonomy sheet and Libra). The war-displaced are a vulnerable group.
- **Held for review:** none.
