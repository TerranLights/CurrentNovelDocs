## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossacks** (upper, middle and lower Don; Old Believer and Orthodox), against **non-Cossack Don Russians** and the old *inogorodnie* (outsider settlers): status-based versus civic selves.
- **Kuban Black Sea Cossacks** (Zaporozhian roots, balachka speech) against **Line Cossacks** (Russian-speaking, eastern Kuban): two founding stocks.
- **Adyghe**: the Temirgoy, Bzhedug and Abadzekh communities of Adygea, and the **Shapsugs** of the Black Sea coast.
- **Don Armenians** of Nor Nakhichevan (Rostov) and **Hamshen Armenians** of the Sochi coast: different dialects, faiths and histories.
- **Pontic Greeks** of the Kuban and coast; **Don Kalmyks** (Buzava), as historically present; **Nekrasovite** returnees; **Ukrainians** of the Taganrog and Azov coast; **Meskhetian Turks** remaining after the 2000s emigration; **Koreans** of Rostov Oblast; Chechens and Dargins of eastern Rostov.
- **Cities against stanitsas**: Rostov-on-Don, Krasnodar, Sochi against farm settlements.
- **Diasporas**: Cossack emigré communities abroad; the Circassian diaspora in Turkey, Jordan and Syria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Cossacks and Adyghe (revival literature, museums, folklore archives); mixed for Armenians and Greeks; thin for Kalmyks, Koreans and Meskhetian Turks. Surveys are mostly at Russia or federal-district level.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core Don and Kuban lands named above.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Adyghe; Kuban balachka and Ukrainian; Armenian; Greek.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1860s Circassian expulsion and its naming as genocide; de-Cossackization (1919-20) and the 1932-33 famine in the Kuban; the 1962 Novocherkassk shootings; official versus grassroots Cossack revival; Ukrainian identity in the Kuban. Treat all of these as contested ground, not settled frames.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
