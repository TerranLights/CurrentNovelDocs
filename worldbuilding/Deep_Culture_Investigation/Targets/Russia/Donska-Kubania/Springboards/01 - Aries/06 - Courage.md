## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossacks** and **Kuban Cossacks**: different war songs, regiments and hero lists.
- **Adyghe** communities, including **Shapsugs** on the coast, with their own warrior and lament traditions.
- **Don Kalmyk** descendants, who served in the Don Host (*check*).
- **Pontic Greeks** and **Hamshen Armenians**: refugee histories and endurance as courage.
- **Women** in each group: Cossack wives and widows, Adyghe women's roles in heroic songs, wartime nurses and partisans.
- **Miners of eastern Rostov** and **seamen of Novorossiysk and Taganrog**: dangerous trades.
- **Urban professionals** for moral courage: journalists, environmental activists and lawyers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on military and folk courage; mixed on civilian bravery; thin on moral courage except in national-level studies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Ukrainian for Kuban song texts; Greek; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cossack collaboration with Germany and the 1945 repatriations; current war recruitment from these lands; activists and journalists may face prosecution and should not be named without care. The 1860s Caucasus War is told very differently by Adyghe and Cossack sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
