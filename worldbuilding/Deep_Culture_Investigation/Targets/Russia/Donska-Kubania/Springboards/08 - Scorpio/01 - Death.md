# Donska-Kubania: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Kuban *balachka*; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish (Meskhetian); Kalmyk. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, including grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox Cossacks against Old Believers and Molokans:** different burial rites, different readings of the good death.
- **Adyghe and Meskhetian Turkish Muslims:** fast burial, different mourning times.
- **Armenian Apostolic families (Don and Hamshen):** their own requiem cycle and graves.
- **Pontic Greeks:** Orthodox, but with their own memorial food and laments.
- **Rostov's Jewish community:** the Holocaust dead and Jewish burial.
- **Kalmyk and Roma families:** small, distinct death customs.
- **Coal-mining towns:** mine deaths and how communities mourn them.
- **Soldiers' families** across all groups; **urban secular** families in Rostov and Krasnodar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, war dead and church texts; mixed for minorities; thin for open regional data on dying and suicide.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Church Slavonic, Ukrainian, Adyghe, Armenian, Greek, Turkish, Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine and de-Cossackization dead; the 1864 Circassian dead; the Kalmyk deportation; suicide figures; families' grief after recent wars.
- **Held for review:** none.
