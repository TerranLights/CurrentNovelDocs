# Donska-Kubania: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Adyghe, Armenian, Pontic Greek, Turkish (Meskhetian), Kalmyk. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brides and young wives** in Adyghe and Cossack families, against men: rules of being seen differ sharply.
- **Infants and children:** customs of hiding and showing them.
- **Elders on emptying *хутора*** against city dwellers.
- **Shapsugs, Hamshen Armenians and Pontic Greeks of the Sochi-Tuapse-Anapa coast:** small coastal peoples behind a resort façade.
- **Kalmyks, Meskhetian Turks, Kurds and Yazidis, Roma, Koryo-saram, Ukrainians.**
- **Uniformed Cossack patrols:** a hypervisible group, against the people they watch.
- **Labor migrants** on farms and building sites; **resort service workers.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief and ritual; mixed for media representation; thin for the invisible groups, whose own voices are mostly online or in NGO reports.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Pontic Greek; Turkish; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBT visibility carried legal risk before the war; surveillance data is not public; Roma and migrant communities face hostile coverage, so sources need careful reading.
- **Held for review:** none.
