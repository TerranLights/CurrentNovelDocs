## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boys and girls,** in each group: Cossack rough play and skill games are coded male, *check* girls' games.
- **Stanitsa children** (river, steppe, orchard) against **city courtyard** children.
- **Adyghe, Shapsug, Kalmyk, Armenian, Pontic Greek and Meskhetian** families: each has its own table games, festive games and children's lore.
- **Generations:** Soviet yard and camp childhood against today's scheduled and screen play.
- **Resort-coast families,** whose summer is others' play season.
- **Roma and labor-migrant children,** often outside organized leisure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and Soviet-era camp material; mixed for contemporary play; Adyghe and Kalmyk sources exist but are scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian; Adyghe; Kalmyk; Armenian; Pontic Greek.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pre-war "patriotic" youth programs reshaped camps and games; fist-fighting revivals can be read as violence; gambling sits in the domain sheet.
- **Held for review:** none.
