## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossacks** (circle tradition) against **Kuban Cossacks** (a host founded by a grant, with Zaporozhian roots).
- **Adyghe and Shapsugs:** eldership, feast leadership and the old gentry.
- **Kalmyks:** former noble lines and Buddhist clergy.
- **Armenians** (church-centered), **Pontic Greeks**, **Meskhetian Turks** (elders' councils, *check*), **Kurds and Yazidis** (inherited religious roles), **Roma** (family heads), **Old Believers and Nekrasovites** (lay leaders).
- **Women's leadership:** in families, schools, kolkhozes, church sisterhoods and Adyghe and Cossack women's councils.
- **Rural (stanitsa, *хутор*)** against **urban and corporate** leadership.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Cossack history and Soviet-era regional politics; mixed for informal leadership; thin for minority communities' own leaders, often seen only through outsiders' terms.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Kalmyk; Armenian; Turkish; Kurmanji.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pre-war political leaders and the June 2023 mutiny events in Rostov are politically charged; terms like "цыганский барон" are outsiders' labels; authority and the right to command belong to Capricorn, office as law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
