# Donska-Kubania: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (with Don and Kuban dialect forms), Adyghe, Armenian, Ukrainian; Turkish for the Meskhetian community. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men,** separately in each group: laments, song and dress codes differ by gender.
- **Cossack revival activists** against unaffiliated descendants of Cossacks; **stanitsa** against **Rostov and Krasnodar** youth.
- **Adyghe** in Adygea and the coastal **Shapsugs:** etiquette of restraint before elders and in-laws.
- **Don Armenians** and **Hamshen Armenians**; **Pontic Greeks**; **Kalmyks** of the Salsk steppe.
- **Meskhetian Turks, Kurds and Yazidis:** stricter community codes on dress and speech, *check*.
- **Old Believers, Molokans and evangelical Protestants:** faith rules on dress, music and modesty.
- **Eastern Donbas mining towns:** working-class styles of speech and display.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and dialect sources; mixed in survey data; contemporary online data rich but noisy.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and its regional dialects; Adyghe; Armenian; Ukrainian; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pre-war limits on political speech and on LGBT expression mean silence in public sources is not evidence of absence; Cossack patrols that policed public behavior; foreign-agent labels on independent media and pollsters.
- **Held for review:** none.
