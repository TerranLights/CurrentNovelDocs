# Donska-Kubania: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Adyghe and Shapsug** hosting codes against **Cossack** *кунак* and feast customs.
- **Don Armenians and Hamshen Armenians;** **Pontic Greeks;** **Meskhetian Turks and Kurds** (Muslim alms and hospitality).
- **Kalmyks** (Buddhist merit-making through giving, *check*).
- **Old Believers and Molokans:** close-knit mutual aid.
- **Wealthy business donors** in Krasnodar and Rostov against everyday givers in stanitsas.
- **Labor migrants,** whose remittances flow outward, against settled families.
- **Resort towns,** where hospitality is also a trade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hospitality customs and Circassian travel accounts; mixed for survey data; thin for informal giving, which needs ethnography and online sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Pontic Greek; Turkish; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pre-war laws on "foreign agents" and "undesirable organizations" cut many charities off from foreign funds; wartime collections for soldiers dominate pre-war giving news and need separating from ordinary charity; travelers' accounts of Circassia served British anti-Russian aims.
- **Held for review:** none.
