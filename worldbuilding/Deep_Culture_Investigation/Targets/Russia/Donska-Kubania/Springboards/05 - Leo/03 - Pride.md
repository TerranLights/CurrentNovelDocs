# Donska-Kubania: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian (Kuban usage), Adyghe, Armenian, Kalmyk. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cossacks against *иногородние*** and their descendants: a historical divide in who could claim pride of origin.
- **Don against Kuban:** two hosts with separate anthems, symbols and founding stories.
- **Black Sea against Line Cossacks** inside the Kuban.
- **Adyghe and Shapsugs:** pride entangled with the memory of 1864.
- **Don Armenians, Hamshen Armenians, Pontic Greeks, Kalmyks, Ukrainians, Meskhetian Turks, Koryo-saram:** minority pride in a Cossack-coded region.
- **Occupations:** miners of the eastern Donbas, Kuban grain farmers, Rostov and Taganrog industrial workers, Black Sea fishers and resort workers.
- **Rostov's city self-image** against Krasnodar's and Sochi's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official and Cossack symbolism; mixed for surveys; thin for minority pride outside organizations.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Adyghe; Armenian; Ukrainian; Kalmyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** competing claims over Cossack identity (ethnic group or estate); Circassian memory of the 1864 expulsion; nationalist and wartime patriotism in pre-war sources; Pride as a movement was banned before the war, so public sources on LGBT pride are scarce or come from exile.
- **Held for review:** none.
