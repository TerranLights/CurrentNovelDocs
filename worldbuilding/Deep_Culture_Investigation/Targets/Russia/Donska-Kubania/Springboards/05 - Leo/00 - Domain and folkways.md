## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Don Cossacks, upper against lower Don;** **Kuban Cossacks, Black Sea (Zaporozhian-descended) against Line:** different origins and festive repertoires.
- **Adyghe (Circassians),** including the coastal Shapsugs: a separate grammar of display (*dzhegu*, *khabze*).
- **Don Armenians of Nakhichevan-on-Don and Hamshen Armenians of the Sochi and Tuapse coast:** different migrations and dialects.
- **Kalmyks of the Salsk steppe**, **Pontic Greeks** of the coast, **Ukrainians**, **Meskhetian Turks**, **Kurds and Yazidis**, **Roma**, **Koryo-saram**, **Old Believers** (including the returned Nekrasovites) and **Molokans**.
- **City against stanitsa:** Rostov-on-Don, Krasnodar and the Sochi resort strip against farm villages; the eastern Donbas mining towns (Shakhty, Novoshakhtinsk).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Cossack song lore and Adyghe epic and etiquette; mixed for surveys (thin regional cells); thin for small minorities. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets write for the core Don and Kuban lands (Rostov Oblast, Krasnodar Krai, Adygea), noting the Kalmyk, Stavropol and Volgograd edges where they matter.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian for Kuban sources; Adyghe; Armenian (Western and Hamshen dialects); Kalmyk; Pontic Greek; Turkish and Kurmanji for the smaller communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Circassian expulsion of 1864 and its commemoration; the 1919 decossackization; the 1943 Kalmyk deportation and the Meskhetian displacements; the revived Cossack hosts' paramilitary role; pre-war media controls, foreign-agent labels on pollsters, and the ban on LGBT organizations, all of which shape the public record.
- **Held for review:** none.
