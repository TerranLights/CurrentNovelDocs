# Donska-Kubania: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Kuban *balachka* and Ukrainian; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek and Pontic Greek; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Border and coastal cities** (Taganrog, Rostov, Novorossiysk, Yeysk, Sochi) against **inland stanitsas.**
- **Volgodonsk** (a 1999 bombing and a nuclear power plant) and the **mining towns.**
- **Adyghe; Don and Hamshen Armenians; Pontic Greeks; Meskhetian Turks; Yazidis; Roma:** fears drawn from each group's history.
- **Labor migrants,** who may fear police and officials.
- **Women and men** (night safety; hiding fear); **children and the old.**
- **Orthodox, Old Believer, Protestant, Muslim** frames of the fear of God and of evil.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and attacks in the press; mixed for polls; thin for folk fears outside folklore archives.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek and Pontic Greek; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime alarms, drone strikes and the 2023 mutiny are current and controlled topics; respondents need anonymity, and outlets may be blocked. Victims of 1999 and 2017 have living kin. Fear of outsiders must be studied as prejudice, not adopted.
- **Held for review:** none.
