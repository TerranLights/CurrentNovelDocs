## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Registered Cossack societies** and **non-Cossack residents:** service as a group identity against a private duty.
- **Adyghe** families: valor and kin duty within *хабзэ*; returnees from Syria.
- **Don and Hamshen Armenians:** church martyrdom and genocide memory.
- **Pontic Greeks:** memory of the 1949 deportation and of Pontic losses.
- **Meskhetian Turks:** sacrifice and displacement, and the families split by resettlement abroad.
- **Old Believers** and returned Nekrasovites.
- **Women and men:** the self-denying mother and the soldier son; Cossack widows.
- **Military families** now, against families without anyone serving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory and official commemoration; thinner for caregiving, everyday sacrifice and non-Russian groups.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Church Slavonic; Adyghe; Armenian (Classical and modern); Greek; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacrifice in present wars is state-promoted and risky to question; respondents need anonymity. Memorial lists name the dead: handle family data with care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the *Азовское осадное сидение* tale in a scholarly edition (search NEB) and list its words for sacrifice.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "жертвенность казачество", "лIыгъэ адыгэ хабзэ" and "Вардананц Ростов армяне".
3. Pull the regional *Книга памяти* index for three districts (one Don, one Kuban, one Adyghe).
4. Search regional news for "Почётный донор Ростовская область" and "Краснодарский край" counts.
5. Search the Soldiers' Mothers committees' sites for Rostov and Krasnodar branch statements.
