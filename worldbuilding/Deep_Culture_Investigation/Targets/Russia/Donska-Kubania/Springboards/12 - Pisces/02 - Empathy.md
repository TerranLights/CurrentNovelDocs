# Donska-Kubania: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Kuban *balachka* and Ukrainian; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women** in Cossack stanitsas: who may weep, keen or show feeling.
- **Older and younger generations,** before and after the 1990s.
- **Russians, Adyghe, Armenians, Pontic Greeks and Meskhetian Turks:** empathy within and across groups.
- **Labor migrants** from Central Asia and the Caucasus in Krasnodar and Rostov: how they are read and how they read locals.
- **Displaced people** from neighboring war zones, received in Rostov Oblast.
- **Cities against stanitsas and auls;** the resort coast's seasonal strangers.
- **Professionals** (nurses, teachers, police, clergy) against lay people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Many small Russian-language psychology studies with local samples, rarely comparable; ethnic comparison work exists but is politically framed.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ukrainian for Kuban; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** empathy toward the other side of current wars is a dangerous topic for respondents; keep sources public and anonymous. Adyghe grief over 1864 is contested in Russian media.
- **Held for review:** none.
