# Donska-Kubania: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Donska-Kubania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Kuban *balachka* and Ukrainian; Adyghe; Armenian; Greek; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kuban Ukrainian-origin families:** identity shift over a century, and those who keep or drop the *balachka*.
- **Adyghe:** urban Maikop and Krasnodar against auls; returnees from Syria and Kosovo who re-enter the language.
- **Don and Hamshen Armenians;** **Pontic Greeks**, many of whom emigrated to Greece in the 1990s; **Meskhetian Turks**, split by resettlement abroad.
- **Old Believers** against Orthodox; **Molokans and Pentecostals** against restrained worship.
- **Mining towns** of the Eastern Donbass against the grain stanitsas and the resort coast.
- **Drowned-village families** around the Tsimlyansk and Krasnodar reservoirs.
- **Young people** in club and festival culture on the coast against their elders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Census and divorce data exist by region; ecstatic experience and self-loss are thinly studied and mostly in church or sect sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for census and dialect work; Ukrainian for Kuban speech; Adyghe; Armenian (the Nakhichevan dialect included); Greek; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kuban Ukrainian identity and its counting in censuses is politically charged; Adyghe language loss is tied to 1864 and contested. Psychedelic and drug use belong to other concepts and to Libra or Capricorn for law. Sect labels in Russian media can be hostile: read groups' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** none.
