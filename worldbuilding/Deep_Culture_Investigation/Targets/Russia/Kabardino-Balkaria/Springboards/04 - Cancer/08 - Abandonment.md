## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians, against Balkars**: the 1860s emigration and the 1944 deportation are different kinds of being left; separate their memory.
- **Upland Balkar villages** emptied after 1944 or in later resettlement, and families still tied to them.
- **Families of labor migrants** working in other regions: who is said to have left, and who is said to provide.
- **Divorced mothers and their children**: where children go after a marriage ends, and the words used for a parent who leaves (a question).
- **Russian and Cossack families** affected by out-migration of the young: elders left in plains towns.
- **Old people living alone** in Nalchik against those in village households.
- **Children in state care**, and those taken in by kin without papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: orphan and child-support data exist; abandoned villages and emigration are well documented in histories; the feeling of being left lives in laments, poetry and talk.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Ottoman Turkish for emigration records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shame around relinquished children and old people in homes; deportation and 1860s memory; the genocide question. Divorce, custody and support law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
