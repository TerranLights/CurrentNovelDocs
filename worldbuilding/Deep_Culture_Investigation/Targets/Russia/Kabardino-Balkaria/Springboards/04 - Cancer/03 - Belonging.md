## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians who name themselves Circassian** and those who keep "Kabardian": the ethnonym as a sign of wider or narrower belonging.
- **Balkars**, whose self-names place them by gorge and by mountain origin, and whose ties to Karachays across the border may be a second circle.
- **Russians and Cossacks** of the plains towns: belonging to the republic against belonging to Russia.
- **Ossetians and other small groups** (Meskhetian Turks, Koreans, Armenians, *check*): minorities among minorities.
- **Syrian Circassian repatriates** and returnees from Turkey: kin by origin, newcomers by experience.
- **Nalchik youth** and village youth: city belonging against the home village.
- **Devout Muslims, secular families** and Orthodox Christians: faith communities as circles of their own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: identity surveys and census data exist in Russian; felt belonging is mostly in press, song and social media; English is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Turkish and Arabic for repatriates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kabardian-Balkar tensions over land and representation; the Circassian genocide recognition question; the 2005 armed attack on Nalchik and the policing of Muslim communities that followed; informants may face risk. Citizenship and nationality law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
