# Kabardino-Balkaria: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian (Kabardino-Circassian, Cyrillic script), Karachay-Balkar (Cyrillic), Russian; Ossetian for the republic's Ossetian villages. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain, home and family: what home is and who is family, with the folkways of building, family and child-rearing.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of the plains and foothills** (Baksan, Zolsky, Chegem, Urvan, Tersky districts): Baksan-valley and Terek-plain villages differ in settlement history.
- **Balkars by gorge society** (Cherek or Malkar, Bezengi, Khulam, Chegem, Baksan or Urusbi), and those resettled lower after 1957: mountain house forms against later ones.
- **Russians of Nalchik and Prokhladny** and the **Terek Cossack stanitsas** of Prokhladnensky and Maysky districts (*check*): a Slavic house tradition on the same land.
- **Ossetian villages** of Leskensky and Urvansky districts (*check*).
- **Meskhetian Turks** and other small communities (*check* presence and numbers); **Nalchik's Mountain Jews (Juhuro)**, mostly emigrated (*check*).
- **Circassian repatriates from Syria** and families with kin in Turkey, Jordan or Israel: homeland seen from both sides.
- **Historical estates** among Kabardians (princely, noble, commoner): fosterage and guest customs differed across them.
- **City and village**, and degrees of religious practice, within each people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian-language ethnography and census tables; mixed in the native-language press; thin in English, thinnest for Balkars and small communities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Ossetian; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation and the 1864 expulsion of Circassians are living grief; land and district-boundary disputes between Kabardian and Balkar communities; bride abduction and violence in the home; family law goes to Libra and Capricorn, not here. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
