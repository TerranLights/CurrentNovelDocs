## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian families with grandparents at home**: how much of a child's raising passes to the grandmother and grandfather.
- **Balkar families in mountain villages and in herding**: learning by work at pastures and hay meadows.
- **Nalchik families with one or two children**: tutoring, sports and the city's newer parenting talk.
- **Russian and Cossack families**: an Orthodox and Soviet line of upbringing on the plains.
- **Families of historic noble lines**, the group the fosterage sources mostly describe: what memory of it remains.
- **Devout Muslim families** sending children to Quran classes, against secular ones.
- **Repatriate families from Syria**: raising children between Arabic, Kabardian and Russian.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk pedagogy and 19th-century ethnography in Russian; mixed for today's practices; thin in English.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; some Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnopedagogy writing often presents one ideal voice for a people, so read it as a source to examine, not as description; corporal punishment and child neglect; the contested place of religious schooling. Custody and child law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
