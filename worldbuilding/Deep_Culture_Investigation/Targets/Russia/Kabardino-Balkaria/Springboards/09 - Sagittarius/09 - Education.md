## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kabardian and Balkar families of noble descent, for whom fosterage and later Russian schooling marked rank, against families of commoner descent who rose through Soviet schools.
- Balkars schooled in exile, against Balkars schooled after the return.
- Villages that keep native-language classes strong, against Nalchik schools where Russian dominates.
- Graduates who stay in the republic, against those who study and settle in Moscow, Rostov or Saint Petersburg.
- Young people trained in Islamic schools, at home or abroad, against university graduates.
- Russians and Cossacks of the northern districts, and the Russian-language schools they attend.
- Diaspora returnees whose children enter the republic's schools without Russian.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Soviet and imperial school history in Russian; mixed on present-day attitudes to schooling; thin in English.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for most school and university history; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for native-language teaching; Arabic for madrasa sources; Turkish for diaspora students.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** study at foreign Islamic universities is monitored by the security services; the native-language question is political. Early schooling and curiosity are Gemini; the law on schools and languages goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
