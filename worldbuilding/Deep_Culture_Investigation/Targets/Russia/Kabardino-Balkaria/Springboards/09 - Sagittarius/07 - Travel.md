*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Balkar families with a deportation journey in their past, against Kabardian families with an 1860s emigration branch abroad: two different family maps of the far away.
- Mountain Balkars of the Elbrus district, who host climbers and skiers, against lowland villages that see few outsiders.
- Kabardians with kin in Turkey, Jordan or Syria who visit both ways, against families with no contact abroad.
- Returnees from Syria now living in Nalchik and the villages.
- Young people who leave for study or work in Moscow, Saint Petersburg and the northern oil regions.
- Terek Cossacks, whose own history includes service journeys and the 1920 deportations *check*.
- Meskhetian Turks, who came after their own deportation and many of whom later moved on to the United States *check*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on exile, deportation and mountaineering; mixed on everyday travel and hospitality now.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Turkish, Arabic; German for Güldenstädt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportation and the 1860s emigration are grief and grievance for families; repatriation is politically charged; border-zone rules near Elbrus may limit fieldwork. Short journeys and commuting are Gemini; homesickness and belonging are Cancer.
- **Held for review:** none.
