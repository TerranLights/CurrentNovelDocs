## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kabardian grandmothers who keep amulets and protective rites, against grandsons who learned from preachers that such things are forbidden.
- Balkars of the Upper Balkaria and Bezengi villages, where older mountain beliefs may have lasted longest, against lowland Balkar settlements.
- Followers of the Khabze revival, who restore the old gods by choice and on a different warrant.
- Soviet-educated secular urbanites in Nalchik, for whom belief may be a cultural label more than a claim.
- Russians and Cossacks of the northern districts, with Orthodox and Slavic folk beliefs of their own.
- Mountain Jews and Meskhetian Turks *check*, each with its own household beliefs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on recorded folk belief, mostly from the nineteenth century and Soviet ethnography; thin on present-day belief, which needs fieldwork and online sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Arabic; German for Klaproth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet ethnography calls older beliefs "survivals" and needs a critical reading; asking about amulets or healers can draw a person into the dispute between Muslim currents. Magic ways as practice are Pisces; acting on belief is concept 6.
- **Held for review:** none.
