## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of princely, noble and commoner descent**: whether the old records set different revenge rules by estate, and whether lineage memory still shapes views, are open questions (*check*).
- **Balkars of the five mountain societies**: feud and reconciliation customs may have differed between gorges.
- **Ossetians**: their own feud vocabulary and reconciliation customs.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: frontier reprisals remembered from the other side.
- **Families touched by the 2000s violence**, on both sides: whether revenge talk persists, and who discourages it.
- **Urban young men in Nalchik and village young men**: personal retaliation, fights and the pull of honor, as questions.
- **Women**: their part in urging, preventing or ending revenge.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century custom and folklore; mixed for current practice; thin and sensitive for revenge tied to the insurgency.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Ossetian for one unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feud talk can endanger informants and inflame relations between families and peoples; avoid any framing that makes revenge a fixed trait of a people; legal sides of revenge and compensation go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
