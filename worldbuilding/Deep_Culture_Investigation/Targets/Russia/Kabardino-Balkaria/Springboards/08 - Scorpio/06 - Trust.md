## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians and Balkars**: trust across the two titular peoples after the land disputes and the Kendelen incident.
- **Village kin networks and Nalchik residents**: the radius of trust may differ between close village lineages and the city.
- **Balkars of Elbrus district** against **Balkars of Cherek district**: distinct relations with republic authorities over land and tourism.
- **Russians and Cossacks of the northern districts**: trust in republic bodies headed largely by the titular peoples, as a question.
- **Older people schooled in Soviet institutions** and **young people online**: whom each relies on for news and help.
- **Returnees from Syria, Turkey and Jordan**: trust between newcomers and hosts in villages that took them in.
- **Meskhetian Turks and Ossetians**: small groups' trust in neighbors and local officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fosterage, kunak and hospitality ethnography; mixed for surveys, since national polls seldom sample the KBR well; thin for trust inside families.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kabardian-Balkar tensions are easily inflamed by research framing; trust in police and courts belongs to Capricorn; avoid romanticizing hospitality as a fixed trait of either people.
- **Held for review:** none.
