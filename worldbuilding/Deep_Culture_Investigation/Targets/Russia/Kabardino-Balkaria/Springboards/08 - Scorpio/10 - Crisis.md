## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain Balkar villages** against **plains Kabardian villages**: different hazards, from avalanches and mudflows to river floods.
- **Kabardian and Balkar political memories of 1991-96**: the same crisis seen from opposite sides.
- **Nalchik residents** and **village households**: how the 1990s collapse and unemployment were weathered.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks of Prokhladny and Maysky**: whether 1990s out-migration is remembered as their own crisis.
- **Ossetians and Meskhetian Turks**: small communities' reliance on kin and neighbors in emergencies.
- **Labor migrants and their families**: crisis as a long absence.
- **Old people who lived through the war and deportation**, and young people: what each calls a crisis.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for natural hazards and official reports; mixed for the 1990s political crises; thin for personal and mental-health crises.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1991-96 Balkar-Kabardian disputes remain politically delicate; excess-mortality figures are contested; mental-health crisis carries stigma; keep informants anonymous.
- **Held for review:** none.
