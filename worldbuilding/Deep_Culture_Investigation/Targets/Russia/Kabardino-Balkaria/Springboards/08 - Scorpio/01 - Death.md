## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian villages of the plain**: the Muslim rite meets older Circassian memorial customs; worth testing village by village.
- **Balkars of Upper Balkaria and the Chegem and Baksan gorges**: crypt burial grounds, exile deaths and mountain accidents shape their dead differently.
- **Balkars born in exile**: their parents' graves lie in Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** of Prokhladny and Maysky: Orthodox commemoration days (Радоница) and Soviet secular burial.
- **Ossetians**: Orthodox and Ossetian folk commemorations of the dead, to be told apart.
- **Mountain Jews** of Nalchik: who tends their graves after emigration (*check*).
- **Families of those killed in the 2000s insurgency**, and of security-force dead: grief that is politically marked.
- **Syrian Circassian returnees**: war dead left in Syria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and 19th-century ethnography; mixed for statistics; thin and guarded for insurgency deaths.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Arabic for gravestones; Ossetian for one unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in deportation and in counterinsurgency are politically charged; families may not speak openly; withheld bodies are a live grievance. Keep informants anonymous.
- **Held for review:** none.
