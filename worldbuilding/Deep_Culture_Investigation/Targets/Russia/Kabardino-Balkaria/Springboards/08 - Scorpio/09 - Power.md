## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian families of princely, noble and commoner descent**: whether old rank still carries weight in marriage and politics.
- **Balkar taubiy and özden descendants** against others: the same question in the mountains.
- **Kabardians and Balkars** in the republic's offices: the share of posts between the two titular peoples is a standing political question.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: their place in republic power and in the northern districts' local government.
- **Elbrus district Balkars**: power over land and tourism money.
- **Young men** in sport, security services and religion: three distinct routes to standing.
- **Women**: public office against power inside the household.
- **Diaspora Circassians**: influence of Turkish and Jordanian Circassian associations on homeland affairs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the old estates and Soviet history; mixed for post-Soviet elite networks; thin and guarded for informal power.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Arabic and Turkish for the diaspora and Mamluk history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** writing about the republic's ruling families and the share of posts between peoples is politically sensitive; avoid naming living private individuals beyond public officeholders; legitimacy and rank as authority belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
