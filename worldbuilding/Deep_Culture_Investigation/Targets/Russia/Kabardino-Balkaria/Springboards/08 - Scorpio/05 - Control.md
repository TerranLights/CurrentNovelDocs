## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian and Balkar households**: compare who keeps the stores and money; the mistress of the house against the head of the family.
- **Young married women and their mothers-in-law**: whether household control runs along this line is a question to test.
- **Mountain Balkar herders and plains Kabardian farmers**: control of pasture, flock and water takes different forms.
- **Nalchik youth and village youth**: parents' and elders' control over marriage choice and movement.
- **Muslim congregations under the official Spiritual Board and independent groups**: who controls mosque life and preaching.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Prokhladny and Maysky**: Cossack societies' claims to keep order.
- **Syrian Circassian returnees**: control of their status and movement by officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for feast and family etiquette; mixed for hazard and weather control; thin and risky for coercive control in families and for state control.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence and bride abduction are private and shameful matters; election and detention data are politically charged and some monitors are labeled "foreign agents" in Russia, so cite with care; keep informants anonymous.
- **Held for review:** none.
