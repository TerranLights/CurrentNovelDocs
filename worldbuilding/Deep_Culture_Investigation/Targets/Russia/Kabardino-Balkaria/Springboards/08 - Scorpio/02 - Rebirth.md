# Kabardino-Balkaria: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Turkish and Arabic for returnees. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balkars of the upper Cherek and Chegem gorges** against **Balkars of lowland settlements**: whether families got back to their own villages after 1957 is a question to test gorge by gorge (*check*).
- **Kabardians of Baksan and Zolsky districts** against **Nalchik Kabardians**: language revival and Islamic return may run differently in town and village.
- **Syrian, Turkish and Jordanian Circassian returnees**: three diasporas starting over, each with its own Kabardian dialect and habits.
- **Russian Orthodox and Cossack communities**: Orthodox revival after 1991 and the Cossack revival movement.
- **Ossetians and Meskhetian Turks**: the latter with their own history of deportation and resettlement.
- **Young Muslims** against elders brought up in Soviet secular life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public revival discourse; mixed for return memoirs; thin for personal conversion and recovery stories.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Turkish and Arabic for returnees.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion away from Islam, banned religious groups and militant reintegration are risky topics; Islamic revival is policed; keep informants anonymous.
- **Held for review:** none.
