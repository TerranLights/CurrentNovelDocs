## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian families of former noble and of peasant descent**: whether service between ranks left traces in today's manners.
- **Balkar families of former taubiy and of free or dependent descent**, the same question in the highlands.
- **Terek Cossack stanitsas**, whose ancestors held land in return for state military service.
- **Daughters-in-law and the young** in each group, as the ones expected to serve at home and at table.
- **The Juhuro community of Nalchik** and its own religious service.
- **Circassian repatriates from Syria and Jordan**, bringing diaspora traditions of state service.
- **Public-sector workers in Nalchik** against private service workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on hospitality and military service in history; thin on present-day service work and volunteering beyond federal counts.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for etiquette texts; Arabic and Turkish for the Mamluk and diaspora threads.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service after 2022 and casualties from the republic; old estate ranks and named lineages; the Mamluk memory used in present-day nationalism. The state's civil service as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
