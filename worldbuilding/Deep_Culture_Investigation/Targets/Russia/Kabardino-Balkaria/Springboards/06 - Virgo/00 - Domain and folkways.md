*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of the plains** (Baksan, Zolsky, Chegem, Urvan districts): farm and horse lineages, under the Adyghe code.
- **Balkars of the five highland societies** (Malkar on the Cherek, Bezengi, Khulam, Chegem, Urusbiev on the Baksan): herding rounds and the deportation break differ by gorge.
- **Russians and Terek Cossack stanitsas** (Prokhladny and Maysky districts): a separate farm, service and church tradition.
- **Nalchik's urban mix**, with its Mountain Jewish (Juhuro) remnant: city trades.
- **Tyrnyauz and the Elbrus villages**: mining, then guiding and tourism.
- **Ossetian, Meskhetian Turk and Korean communities** (*check* locations) and **Circassian repatriates from Syria**.
- **Diasporas** in Turkey, Jordan and Syria; Balkars left in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Soviet and Russian ethnography and folklore; mixed for statistics (many federal surveys lack a republic breakdown); thin in English.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore, press and terms; Turkish and Arabic for the diaspora.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries only "Russia" as pre-war lands, so the post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core republic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation, the 1860s Circassian expulsion, Kabardian-Balkar land disputes, the 2005 Nalchik events; current limits on speech and archives. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
