## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Balkar households**, where herd work needs every hand, against **lowland Kabardian** farm households.
- **Urban Nalchik families** with salaried work, against village families with home plots.
- **Elders across groups**, including those who returned from exile in 1957 and their children.
- **Disabled people and their families**, in each language community.
- **Young men without work** who stay, against those who leave for work elsewhere in Russia.
- **Terek Cossack stanitsas** and Russian towns.
- **Circassian repatriates**, whose diplomas and trades may not be recognized locally.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore and ethnopedagogy are rich; measured data on felt usefulness is thin and mostly national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for tales and idioms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability and stigma; suicide and despair among older or unemployed men if met (handle with care); Soviet longevity claims are contested and must be read critically. Pensions as law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Kabardian and Balkar folklore collections for the old-father tale ("старика спрятал", "обычай убивать стариков нарты").
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "этнопедагогика адыгов трудовое воспитание" and "народная педагогика балкарцев".
3. Pull КОУЖ regional tables for Kabardino-Balkaria on help to relatives and care of grandchildren.
4. Look up disabled employment for the republic in the Social Fund's open data.
5. Find the phraseological dictionary in the National Library of the republic and list entries for "use" and "burden".
