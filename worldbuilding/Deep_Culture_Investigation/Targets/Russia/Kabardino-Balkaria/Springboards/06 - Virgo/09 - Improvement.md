## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian and Balkar reformers** of the 19th and early 20th centuries, against keepers of older custom.
- **Balkar families moved to the plains** after 1957, against those who returned to the gorges.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**, whose stanitsas met Soviet improvement campaigns in their own way.
- **Religious revivalists and secular modernizers**, each with a program for betterment.
- **Young urban Nalchik residents** in the self-help market, against village elders.
- **Language activists**, against families who chose Russian for their children's prospects.
- **Repatriates and the diaspora**, who bring outside ideas of what the homeland should become.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on enlighteners and Soviet campaigns; mixed on self-help; thin on measured mobility for the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for enlighteners' texts and language projects; Arabic for religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** imperial and Soviet "improvement" imposed on peoples, told first in the affected peoples' voices; the 1944 to 1957 exile and resettlement; religious revival watched by the state; native-language schooling rules (as law, Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
