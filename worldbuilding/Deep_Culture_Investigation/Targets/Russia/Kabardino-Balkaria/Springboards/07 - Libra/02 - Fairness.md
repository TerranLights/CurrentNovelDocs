## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland Kabardian farming villages and Balkar mountain villages:** different stakes in how land and pasture are shared.
- **Nalchik public-sector families and rural households:** whether jobs and places are won by merit or connections may be judged differently.
- **Northern Russian-majority districts** (Prokhladny, Maysky): former collective farms and their rules for dividing land shares.
- **Women and men as heirs:** state law and Islamic rules give them different portions.
- **Older and younger generations:** Soviet leveling against market rewards.
- **Circassian repatriates from Syria and Turkey:** newcomers' claims to housing and land against those of long residents.
- **Small communities** (Ossetian, Meskhetian Turkish, Korean) (*check*): fair treatment as minorities in a two-titular republic.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: feast etiquette and proverbs are well recorded; present-day fairness perceptions in KBR are thin and mostly national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for proverbs, children's lore and table etiquette.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land and pasture between the titular peoples is a live, heated question; exam and hiring claims touch named officials. Treat both carefully.
- **Held for review:** none.
