*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Arabic for religious healing terms. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians and Balkars:** each group's image of the other, especially in land quarrels.
- **Russian residents and the titular peoples:** the mirror images between local Russians and their neighbors.
- **Young observant Muslims:** a group named in official anti-extremism talk; their own account of being labeled.
- **Circassian activists and state media:** opposite framings of the same history.
- **Meskhetian Turks and other small groups** (*check*): blamed or not in local quarrels.
- **Village and Nalchik households:** whether misfortune is put down to envy, witchcraft or chance.
- **Natives of KBR living in other Russian cities:** how they meet the outsider label.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: literary and media images and the 1944 case are rich; local evil-eye belief and mutual stereotypes have thinner, uneven data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore terms; Arabic for healing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Wahhabi" and "extremist" labels carry criminal risk; the genocide question is contested; ethnic jokes and slurs are to be cited as claims, never repeated as fact. Keep named private persons out.
- **Held for review:** none.
