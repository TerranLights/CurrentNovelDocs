# Kabardino-Balkaria: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish for the Kabardian diaspora. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balkar high villages:** test whether haymaking and herding help ran by turns between families.
- **Lowland Kabardian villages:** the scale of wedding and funeral giving, and who keeps track of it.
- **Nalchik apartment neighbors:** exchange among people without shared lineage.
- **Russian and Cossack families:** their own work-help and wake customs.
- **Meskhetian Turkish families** (*check*): wedding exchange by their own customs.
- **Women who keep a family's gift accounts:** the unseen side of exchange.
- **Repatriates:** what local kin owe newcomers, and what is owed back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** moderate: wedding and funeral exchange are well described; present-day money sums and private ledgers are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for tale collections and gift terms; Turkish for Uzunyayla.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bridewealth and wedding costs touch Islamic and state rules; funeral contributions are private; no figures for named families.
- **Held for review:** none.
