*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Kabardian princes and Balkar nobility (таубий):** marriage alliances once ran between the two noble orders.
- **Mixed couples** (Kabardian-Balkar, Kabardian-Russian, Balkar-Russian) in Nalchik: partnership across language and faith.
- **Orthodox Russian couples:** church weddings and Cossack customs of courtship.
- **Couples married only by nikah:** their standing outside the registry.
- **Elbrus and Chegem district households:** partners or bystanders in the tourism economy.
- **Kabardian Christians and Muslims:** small Orthodox Kabardian groups may form couples differently (*check*).
- **Repatriate couples from Syria:** marriages made in the diaspora meeting homeland customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for noble alliances and old fosterage; moderate for couples (registry and census); thin for business partnerships.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for courting songs and kin terms; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "voluntary entry" framing of 1557 is contested; nikah-only and second marriages touch private lives; land for tourism is disputed.
- **Held for review:** none.
