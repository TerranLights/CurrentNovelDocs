## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians by district** (Baksan, Zolsky, Chegem, Urvan, Leskensky, Tersky): plain and foothill marriage and council customs may differ.
- **Balkar mountain societies** (Malkar, Bezengi, Holam, Chegem, Urusbiy in the Baksan gorge), and Balkars resettled to lowland villages after 1957: separate the old society courts from post-return practice.
- **Russians**, including Terek Cossack families in Prokhladny and Maysky districts: their own stanitsa justice and marriage ways.
- **Smaller groups:** Ossetians, Meskhetian Turks, Koreans, Armenians, Ukrainians (*check* each): separate marriage and quarrel rules.
- **Nalchik against the villages**, and by generation: where state, religious and customary forms compete.
- **Repatriates:** Circassians from Syria, Turkey and Jordan settled since the 1990s, who carry diaspora marriage and dispute practice back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for 19th-c. adat and Circassian etiquette; thinner for Balkar sources abroad and for private practice now.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore, press and terms; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride abduction, blood feud, 1944, 1864, land disputes between the titular peoples, the 2005 Nalchik attack and religious policing are live; keep private parties unnamed.
- **Lands:** the tracker's pre-war lands are broad ("Russia"); the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. These sheets are written for the core lands of the Kabardino-Balkarian Republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
