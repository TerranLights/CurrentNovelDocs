## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boys in combat-sports schools and boys outside them**: the sports school may be the main place discipline is taught, which would make it a unit of its own.
- **Kabardian horse-breeding villages of the Zolsky and Baksan districts**: training of horse and rider as a family discipline.
- **Balkar highland families with guiding and rescue work**: mountain craft as discipline handed between generations.
- **Girls and young women in dance ensembles and in households**: posture, restraint and bearing taught to them, described in their own words.
- **Russian and Cossack-descended families**: cadet schooling and Orthodox fasts as a separate route.
- **Observant and less observant Muslims**: fasting and prayer as self-discipline, by generation.
- **Repatriates from Syria and Turkey**: they may bring other school and family norms of correction.
- **Urban Nalchik teenagers online**: whether online self-discipline trends reach them, and in which language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport, dance and ethnography; thin for measured attitudes or habits at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folktales, school texts and literary journals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment and abuse in families, schools and sports; interviews with minors need consent and care.
- **Held for review:** none.
