# Kabardino-Balkaria: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Ossetian for the Ossetian minority. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian lowland villages and Nalchik Kabardians**: test whether a father's or an elder's word carries the same weight in town as in the village.
- **Balkar highland families** of the Cherek, Chegem and Baksan gorges: compare the authority of the senior kinsman with that of the old leading families.
- **Practicing Muslims who follow the official board and those who follow independent teachers**: religious authority is split, and loyalty to each may differ.
- **Russians and Cossack-descended villages**: Orthodox parish, ataman and state office as rival or stacked authorities.
- **Ossetian households**: the senior at table as a figure of authority, to set beside the Kabardian and Balkar forms.
- **Officials and security personnel versus the people they police**: authority as seen from each side.
- **Young adults with Moscow or abroad experience**: whether outside careers loosen deference to home authorities.
- **Repatriated Circassians** from Syria and Jordan: they bring other expectations of state and elder authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and ethnography in Russian; thin for measured attitudes, since federal polls rarely publish republic-level samples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for proverbs, folklore and village speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the counterinsurgency years, religious persecution claims, and ethnic contests over top posts; interviewees may fear speaking about officials; keep sources anonymous where needed.
- **Held for review:** none.
