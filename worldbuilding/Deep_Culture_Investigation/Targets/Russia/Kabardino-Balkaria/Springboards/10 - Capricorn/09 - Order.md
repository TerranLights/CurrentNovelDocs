## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian plain villages**: dance gatherings, weddings and village quarters as their own system of order.
- **Balkar highland villages**: pasture borders and village boundaries make land order the sharpest issue.
- **Nalchik residents by district**: perceived safety and noise may differ between old center and new blocks.
- **Russian and Cossack towns of the north**: a different street order and Cossack self-policing.
- **Young men**: the group most often stopped, policed and blamed, in their own words.
- **Women**: what safety in public means for them, from their own accounts.
- **Observant and secular families**: what counts as public disorder (music, dance, dress) may differ.
- **Elbrus district residents and tourists**: order under a border regime and a resort economy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; crime and mortality data are open, perceived-safety data for the republic is thin, events are well covered by the press.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for festive and household terms and for local accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2005 attack and counterterrorism; ethnic clashes such as Kendelen; land protests; avoid naming private participants.
- **Held for review:** none.
