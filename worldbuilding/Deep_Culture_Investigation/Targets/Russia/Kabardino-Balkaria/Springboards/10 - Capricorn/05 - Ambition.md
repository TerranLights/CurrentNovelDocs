## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village youth and Nalchik youth**: the routes on offer (sport, army, police, university) differ sharply, to test.
- **Young women of both peoples**: education and careers allowed or encouraged, and how marriage plans interact, in their own words.
- **Kabardian and Balkar officials**: whether the ethnic balance of posts shapes how far an ambitious person can rise.
- **Balkar highlanders of the Elbrus and Chereksky districts**: tourism and land as the stakes of ambition.
- **Russians of Prokhladny and Maysky**: many families have moved away since the 1990s (*check*); ambition may mean leaving the republic altogether.
- **The republic's people in Moscow**: a working elite outside, with its own view of home.
- **Repatriates from Syria**: rebuilding careers from scratch, as a fresh case.
- **Former athletes**: a group whose ambition was channeled early and narrowly.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; history and biographies are rich, measured aspiration data for the republic is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for literature and village talk; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption allegations around careers and posts; the ethnic balance of offices; avoid naming private persons from press reports.
- **Held for review:** none.
