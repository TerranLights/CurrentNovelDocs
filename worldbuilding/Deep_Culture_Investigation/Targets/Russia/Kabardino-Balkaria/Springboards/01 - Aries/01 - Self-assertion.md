## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men and elders at Kabardian village feasts:** the speaking order may let a young man assert himself only in set forms.
- **Daughters-in-law in Kabardian and Balkar homes:** avoidance customs may shape whether and how a wife states her wishes.
- **Balkar gorge villages (Chegem, Cherek, upper Baksan) against plain villages:** separate household and assembly practice in each.
- **Nalchik office workers:** test whether workplace Russian allows a bluntness that home language does not.
- **Russian and Terek Cossack towns in the north:** a different scale of directness to set beside the others.
- **Returnees from Syria:** they may meet local speech rules from outside and press claims to officials.
- **Circassian and Balkar activist groups:** public claims and the limits put on them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; etiquette ethnography rich for Kabardians and fair for Balkars; measured assertiveness data for the republic likely absent.
