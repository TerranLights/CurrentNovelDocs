## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian noble-descended families against commoner lines:** fosterage and marriage rules were tied to rank, so the room to choose may differ.
- **Balkar herding families in the upper gorges:** time on the summer pastures may give the young a separate domain.
- **Nalchik students and graduates against village youth:** city study and work may move decisions from parents to the person.
- **Labor migrants and their families at home:** choices made far away and judged at home.
- **Women of each people:** marriage, study and dress are the main fields where personal and family say may collide.
- **Followers of the official Muslim body against independent religious circles:** two authorities over a Muslim's daily conduct.
- **Russian and Cossack families in the north:** a separate family model for comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; marriage and fosterage ethnography rich; measured autonomy data at republic level thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore and oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride abduction, women's choices, and the security policing of religion; protect informants. Self-rule claims of the 1990s are politically live. The legal side goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
