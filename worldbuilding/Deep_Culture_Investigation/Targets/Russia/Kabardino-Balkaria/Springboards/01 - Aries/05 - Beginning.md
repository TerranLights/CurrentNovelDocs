## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian grain and orchard villages:** the plowing start of the year belongs to farming life and may differ from herding valleys.
- **Balkar highland communities:** the herding year and spring pastures may set a different start.
- **Balkars by generation since 1957:** separate those born in exile, those born after return, and their children.
- **Repatriates from Syria, Turkey and Jordan:** starting over in a homeland known from stories.
- **Russian and Terek Cossack villages:** Orthodox and Soviet calendars of beginnings.
- **Muslim families, secular families and Khabze revivalists:** three ways to mark a birth and a new year.
- **Nalchik city families:** the Soviet "first of September" and New Year's Eve as rival beginnings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich; birth and calendar ethnography good for both peoples; revived festivals need care, since revival and survival can blur.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for rite names; Russian for most studies; Arabic for some returnees.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** firsts (Elbrus, settlement of gorges) are claimed by both peoples; deportation and return; old-faith revival is contested by some Muslim authorities. Omens and almanac customs at beginnings are folklore pointers only.
- **Held for review:** none.
