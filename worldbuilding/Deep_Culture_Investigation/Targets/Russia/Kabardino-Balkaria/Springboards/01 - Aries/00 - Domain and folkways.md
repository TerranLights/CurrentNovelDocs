## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of the Baksan, Chegem and Urvan valleys against the Lesser Kabarda lands along the Terek (*check* which districts):** the historic split of Greater and Lesser Kabarda may show in dialect and lineage memory.
- **Balkars of the five mountain societies (Malkar, Chegem, Bezengi, Kholam, Baksan-Urusbiev) and Tyrnyauz:** each gorge has its own lineages and dialect forms.
- **Balkar families by deportation memory (1944 to 1957):** separate by generation to see whether exile in Central Asia enters self-description.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks of Prokhladny, Maysky and the north:** a different naming system and frontier identity.
- **Returnee Circassians from Syria, Turkey and Jordan in Nalchik:** a self formed in diaspora, possibly with Arabic or Turkish as a first language.
- **Smaller groups (Ossetians, Meskhetian Turks, Koreans, Armenians, Germans *check*):** own naming and dress.
- **Practicing Muslims, secular families and followers of the Khabze revival:** three sources for what a person is.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian and Kabardian ethnography; Balkar material thinner but solid; Soviet framing and older Orientalist portraits need critical reading. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish, Arabic and Ottoman Turkish for diaspora and archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 Balkar deportation; recognition of the Circassian genocide; Kabardian and Balkar land disputes; the 2005 Nalchik attack and security policing of religion. Blood feud and customary law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
