## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian and Balkar villages with old feud memories:** test whether grievance between families is still carried and how it is settled.
- **Young men in Nalchik sports and street life:** public fights may be read as anger, honor or play; separate them.
- **Women in each people:** whether a woman's open anger is allowed, and toward whom.
- **Activists of the Circassian and Balkar movements:** collective anger turned into marches, letters and fasts.
- **Young people in independent religious circles:** grievance at policing, and preachers' answers.
- **Repatriates from Syria:** anger and trauma from war, possibly handled with other norms.
- **Russian and Cossack towns:** a further baseline for public temper.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; linguistics and feud ethnography rich; measured anger data at republic level likely absent; domestic violence counts likely incomplete.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Arabic for sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic clashes, insurgency and counter-insurgency, domestic violence; use published reports only and name no private persons. Feud law and reconciliation courts belong to Capricorn and Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
