## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian plain villages (Baksan, Zolsky, Urvan districts) with orchards and family farms:** farming offers a field for initiative distinct from the city.
- **Balkars of the Elbrus district:** tourism and guiding shape a different kind of venture.
- **Nalchik traders and service businesses:** city enterprise with kin capital.
- **The young in each people:** acting unasked toward elders may be praised in service but checked in decisions.
- **Women who start businesses (crafts, catering, beauty):** separate their routes and the support they get.
- **Returnees from the diaspora:** they may bring business habits from Turkey, Syria or Jordan.
- **Russian farming towns of the north (Prokhladny, Maysky):** a contrast in how farms began.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; business statistics good; ethnography of communal work fair; studies of personal initiative thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore, enlighteners and village life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** raiding traditions are open to stereotype; read them as history of an estate institution. Business data may touch on clan networks and corruption claims; stay with published sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
