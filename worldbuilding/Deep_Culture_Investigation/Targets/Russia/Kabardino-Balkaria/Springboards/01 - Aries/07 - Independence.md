## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Balkar villages before and after 1944:** a household economy cut off and rebuilt on new terms.
- **Kabardian plain villages with plots and orchards:** self-provisioning may stay central to a household's standing.
- **Nalchik salaried families:** wages and transfers rather than land.
- **Families with men in seasonal work far away:** independence bought by absence.
- **Elders and the disabled:** who cares for them shows where dependence is accepted.
- **Terek Cossack settlements:** a frontier tradition of armed self-reliance to compare.
- **Those who back or reject the "voluntary union" story:** political independence as a live question, not a settled one.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and fiscal statistics; household-level studies mixed.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for sage lore and village life; Ottoman Turkish for the Crimean and Ottoman sides.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence and secession talk is politically risky in the present republic; land disputes; abreks are claimed both as rebels and as criminals. Law on land and status belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
