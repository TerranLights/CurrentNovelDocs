## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian lineages of noble descent and commoner families:** the knightly code was an estate code; check what courage meant outside it.
- **Balkar highland families:** heroic songs tied to gorges and houses may give local exemplars.
- **Balkar women in exile and return:** endurance may be named as courage differently from men's deeds.
- **Soldiers and veterans of the 20th and 21st centuries:** Soviet, Russian and volunteer service give rival models.
- **Mountain guides and rescuers in the Elbrus district:** courage as a trade.
- **Terek Cossacks:** a separate warrior tradition with its own memory of the frontier.
- **Young Muslim men in religious circles:** a different vocabulary of moral steadfastness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epic and heroic song; mixed for modern moral courage.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for songs and epic; Russian for military history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory (the Caucasian War, WWII and deportation, Abkhazia) is contested; the "brave mountaineer" image is a stereotype to study, not to repeat; insurgency and security operations must not be framed as courage either way without both sides' sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
