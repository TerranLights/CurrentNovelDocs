## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian women embroiderers and Balkar women felt-makers**: two textile crafts, two ornament stocks, possibly different ideas of who owns a pattern.
- **Kabardian smiths, saddlers and armorers of the plain** against **Balkar stone and woodworkers of the gorges**: making shaped by the materials each place offers.
- **Bards and folk poets** against **Soviet-trained writers and composers**: improvisation inside a tradition against authorship in print.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: Cossack song and craft, with a separate line of transmission.
- **Mountain Jews of Nalchik**: carpet and metal crafts *check*, and music with its own sources.
- **Circassian repatriates from Syria, Turkey and Jordan**: ornament and music reshaped abroad and brought back.
- **Young designers in Nalchik**: ornament remade as fashion and digital design.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Craft and folklore scholarship is rich in Russian; data on today's makers and incomes is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for verse and the journals; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora makers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** quarrels among North Caucasus peoples over who first made a dance, tune or ornament; the Soviet "folk creation" frame, which blurred named makers; objects held outside the republic since the nineteenth century.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up гъуазджэ, ІэпщІэлъапщІэ, уста and жырчы in the Kabardian-Russian and Karachay-Balkar-Russian dictionaries.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "джегуако" OR "джэгуакІуэ" and for "балкарский войлок" OR "кийиз".
3. Search the National Museum of the KBR and the Russian Museum of Ethnography catalogs for "кабардинцы" and "балкарцы".
4. Request Form 7-NK figures for amateur arts from the Ministry of Culture of the KBR.
5. Read the contents of *Іуащхьэмахуэ* and *Минги-Тау* for articles on tradition and innovation.
