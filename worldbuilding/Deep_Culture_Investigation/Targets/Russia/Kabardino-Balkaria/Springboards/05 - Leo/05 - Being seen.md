# Kabardino-Balkaria: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Juhuri for Mountain Jewish sources. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young brides in Kabardian and Balkar households**: visibility ruled by in-law avoidance.
- **Infants and young children**: shown at feasts but guarded from the harmful gaze.
- **Balkars of the gorges and of Nalchik**: a people once removed from the map, now seen again.
- **Kabardians who record as "Circassian" against those who keep "Kabardian"**: visibility under which name.
- **Mountain Jews**: a small community counted under shifting census labels.
- **Meskhetian Turks and Koreans**: minorities rarely shown in republic media.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: a majority in Russia, a minority in the republic's ethnic image.
- **Migrant workers and the poor in Nalchik**: the unseen workforce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ritual and belief are well described, while media and census debates are scattered in press and activism.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for ritual terms; Ottoman Turkish via translation; Juhuri for Mountain Jewish voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** security cameras and counter-terror regimes make being watched a political matter; do not identify private people from social media; the Memorial society was dissolved in 2021 and its archives may be hard to reach.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up нэрылъагъу, кёз тийиу, бет ачар and бешик той in the dictionaries and in eLibrary.ru.
2. Search *Кавказский узел* and *Адыгэ псалъэ* for "перепись" AND "черкесы" (2010, 2021).
3. Find Ermakov's photographs of Kabarda in the Georgian National Museum or the Russian Museum of Ethnography catalogs.
4. Read Kosven's essays on avoidance and Karaketov's on amulets.
5. Compare article counts and active editors on the kbd and krc Wikipedias.
