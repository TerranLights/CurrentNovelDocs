# Kabardino-Balkaria: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian village households with a guest house**: hospitality as a built, inherited duty.
- **Balkar gorge households**: hosting where winters and distance shape what can be offered.
- **Families of former princely and noble lines**: feast-giving that once made standing.
- **Urban Nalchik families in apartments**: whether hosting shrinks or moves to restaurants.
- **Observant Muslims and secular families**: zakat and sadaqa against Soviet-era collective help.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: work bees and Orthodox almsgiving.
- **Mountain Jews**: community charity and links with emigrants in Israel.
- **Syrian Circassian repatriates**: on the receiving side, and later as givers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hospitality customs; thin for measured giving, and informal giving is largely uncounted.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for customary terms; Arabic for diaspora charities; German for Güldenstädt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** charities linked to religious groups have faced security checks; wedding and bride gifts belong to Libra; household wealth to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** none.
