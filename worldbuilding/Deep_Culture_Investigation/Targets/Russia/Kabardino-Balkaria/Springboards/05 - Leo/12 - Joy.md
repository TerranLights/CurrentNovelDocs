# Kabardino-Balkaria: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Juhuri and Hebrew for Mountain Jewish holidays. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian women and men**: who sings, plays the accordion and dances when, and who watches.
- **Balkar gorge villages**: seasonal joys tied to pastures and to the spring.
- **Mourning households**: how long joy is set aside, and how strictly.
- **Observant Muslims**: the two bayrams as the high points of the year, against secular calendars.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: Maslenitsa, Easter and the state holidays.
- **Mountain Jews**: Purim and Passover in Nalchik, and links with emigrants.
- **Meskhetian Turks and Koreans**: their own festive cycles.
- **Older and younger generations**: Soviet-era holidays against new ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festive song and calendar custom, thin for measured joy at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for songs and festival names; Juhuri and Hebrew for Mountain Jewish sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious disputes over music and dance at celebrations; avoid presenting any group as cheerful or dour by nature.
- **Held for review:** none.
