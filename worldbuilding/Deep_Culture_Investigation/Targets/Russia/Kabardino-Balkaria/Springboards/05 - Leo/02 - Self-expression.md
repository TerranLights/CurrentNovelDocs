## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men**: laments, love songs and dress may be open to one and closed to the other.
- **Young brides and in-laws in Kabardian and Balkar households**: avoidance customs set when one may speak at all.
- **Elders against young people in Nalchik**: the generation gap in style and in speaking one's mind.
- **Observant Muslims, Sufi circles and secular families**: dress and music as statements of faith or of its absence.
- **Balkar gorge villages against lowland Kabardian villages**: expression may vary with village size and closeness.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: a separate set of holiday licenses (e.g. Maslenitsa) for loosening restraint.
- **Mountain Jews, Meskhetian Turks, Koreans**: minority dress and speech in a mixed city.
- **Syrian and Turkish Circassian returnees**: styles of expression learned elsewhere, and how they are received.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Etiquette and folklore studies are rich; survey data on self-expression at republic level is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for songs, poems and speeches; Turkish and Arabic for returnees.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** headscarf and dress disputes; prosecutions for online speech; political expression around May 21 and the Balkar deportation; avoid identifying private people from social media.
- **Held for review:** none.
