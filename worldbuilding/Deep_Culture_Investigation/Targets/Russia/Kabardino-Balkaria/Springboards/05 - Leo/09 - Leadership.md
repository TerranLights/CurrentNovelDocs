## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian villages where princely or noble families are remembered**: inherited standing against earned leadership.
- **Balkar gorge societies (Baksan, Chegem, Khulam-Bezengi, Cherek)**: each had leading families; their memory may differ.
- **Elders and young men**: elders lead the table; young men may follow coaches, imams or online figures.
- **Women**: leadership in the household, in schools and in offices, against the public face of the feast.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks**: atamans, circles and Soviet-style directors.
- **Mountain Jews, Meskhetian Turks and Koreans**: leaders as go-betweens for a minority.
- **Circassian repatriates**: leaders of returnee groups who speak for newcomers.
- **Urban professionals in Nalchik**: managers and business owners as a newer type of leader.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; historical and ethnographic sources are rich, current measures are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for feast speech and movement texts; Turkish for Meskhetian bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious leaders and youth groups are watched by security services; do not profile individuals. The formal right to command and hierarchy belong to Capricorn; elections and office law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
