*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian (East Circassian, Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ, often typed as I or 1), Karachay-Balkar (Turkic, Cyrillic), Russian; for minorities, Juhuri (Judeo-Tat), Ossetian and Turkish. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure (creativity, play, pleasure, romance, children, celebration, sport).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of the plain and foothills** (Baksan, Zolsky, Urvan, Chegem, Leskensky and Tersky districts): the jegu tradition and its stage forms come mainly from here.
- **Balkars, by gorge society** (Baksan valley, Chegem, Khulam-Bezengi, Cherek or Malkar): mountain festivals, games and songs may differ between valleys.
- **Nalchik and Tyrnyauz against the villages**: clubs and nightlife sit in towns.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** (Prokhladny and Maysky districts): Cossack song, sport and holiday cycles.
- **Mountain Jews of Nalchik**: a community with its own festive calendar and music.
- **Meskhetian Turks, Ossetians, Koreans**: smaller groups with their own celebrations.
- **Circassian repatriates from Syria and other diaspora returnees**: festive forms shaped abroad.
- **Religious and generational lines**: observant Muslims, secular Soviet-trained families and young people may treat music, dance and celebration differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: ethnography and folklore are rich, in three languages; republic-level surveys are thin, and breakdowns by ethnicity rare.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheets of this sign are written for the core lands of the Kabardino-Balkar Republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore, press and song; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora work; Juhuri for Mountain Jewish material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation of the Balkars and their return; the nineteenth-century war and the expulsion of Circassians; Kabardian-Balkar land disputes; the security situation after the 2005 Nalchik attack. ## 5. First queries
1. Look up джэгу, гуфІэгъуэ, оюн, той and тепсеу in B. M. Kardanov's *Кабардинско-русский словарь* and the *Карачаево-балкарско-русский словарь* (1989) to map how each language splits the domain.
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "черкесское игрище" OR "джэгу" and for "балкарские народные игры".
3. Check the Rosstat time-use survey documentation for whether the Kabardino-Balkar Republic is in the sample.
4. Ask the Institute for Humanitarian Research in Nalchik for its folklore archive catalog.
5. Search VKontakte for "адыгэ джэгу" and "малкъар той" to see current festive display.
