# Kabardino-Balkaria: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Arabic for Islamic terms. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian families with branches in Turkey, Syria and Jordan:** stories of those who left rather than yield, told by those who stayed.
- **Balkar families of the deportation:** endurance in exile and return, against acceptance of loss.
- **Devout Muslims and the Soviet-raised secular:** trust in God set against personal control.
- **Young athletes in combat sports:** a code of never yielding inside a faith that teaches submission to God.
- **The very old and the dying, with their carers:** the deathbed as each family arranges it.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** Orthodox humility and Cossack honor against yielding.
- **Families of former insurgents:** surrender to the state, its risks and its stigma.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Archival and historical framing are rich; data on letting go in daily life and recovery are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian (including 16th-17th c. chancery Russian), Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Arabic; Ottoman Turkish for emigration records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "voluntary accession" is official doctrine and challenging it can draw pressure; insurgent surrender is a security subject; the 1860s and 1944 are painful memories. Amnesty law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
