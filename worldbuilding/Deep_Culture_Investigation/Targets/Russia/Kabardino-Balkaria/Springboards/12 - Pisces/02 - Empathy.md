*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women, elders and the young:** the etiquette of restraint may set different rules for showing and reading feeling; variation to measure, not assume.
- **Kabardians and Balkars across the group line:** the main test of empathy for the other side in land and memory disputes.
- **Nalchik's mixed schools and courtyards against single-ethnic villages:** daily contact against distance.
- **Families marked by the 1944 deportation against families not deported:** whose suffering is recognized by whom.
- **Repatriates from Syria and Turkey:** newcomers reading local cues in a shared ethnicity but a foreign upbringing.
- **Russians in Prokhladny and Maysky:** Slavic everyday codes of sympathy beside Muslim condolence customs.
- **Ossetian and Kabardian neighbors along the eastern edge:** a long-mixed border zone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin. Etiquette ethnography and literature are rich; measured empathy data for the republic are likely sparse and student-based.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic surveys touch Kabardian-Balkar tensions; the insurgency years shape who may be pitied in public. Grief visits are private; fieldwork needs consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
