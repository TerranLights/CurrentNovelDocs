*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Activists who say "Circassian" against those who keep "Kabardian":** two answers to merging into a larger whole.
- **Balkars who stress one people with the Karachay against those who stress Balkaria:** the same question on the Turkic side.
- **Urban Russian-speaking youth against village native speakers:** where language loss is fastest.
- **Highland villages emptied in 1944 and never fully resettled:** places that ended, and how descendants speak of them.
- **The Mountain Jewish remnant and the Ossetian minority:** small groups at the edge of absorption.
- **Repatriates from Syria and Kosovo:** a test case of merging with a homeland they had not lived in.
- **Mixed Kabardian-Balkar-Russian families:** identity held, blended or chosen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Census language data and administrative history are rich; data on ecstatic or mystical self-loss are thin and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Arabic for Sufi terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the republic's partition debates, census identity campaigns and language protests are politically live; Sufi and Salafi currents are a security topic; deportation memory is painful. Language law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
