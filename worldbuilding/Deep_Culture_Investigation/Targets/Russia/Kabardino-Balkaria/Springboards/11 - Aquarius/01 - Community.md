# Kabardino-Balkaria: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **High Balkar villages** (Upper Chegem, Upper Balkaria, Bezengi) against **lowland Balkar settlements** built after 1957: a community rooted in a gorge against one assembled on new ground.
- **Large Kabardian plains villages** (around Baksan, Nartkala, Zalukokoazhe) where quarters and lineages map onto streets, against **Nalchik apartment blocks**, where neighbors are often strangers.
- **Cossack stanitsas** in Prokhladny and Maysky districts, with their own society and church.
- **Mosque congregations**: long-standing village mosques against newer town congregations whose members chose them.
- **Repatriate households** from Syria and Turkey, and whether they form their own quarter.
- **The Mountain Jewish remnant** in Nalchik and its emigrant networks.
- **Workers' towns**: Tyrnyauz and its former mining community, against farming villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography of village custom; thin in quantitative data on neighboring or belonging below the federal level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for local press, funeral notices and village groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "джамаат" was the name of armed groups in the 2000s; searches mixing community and Islam will surface security material, which is not a picture of ordinary congregations. Deportation memories are painful and family-held.
- **Held for review:** none.
