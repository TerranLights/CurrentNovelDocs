## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian and Balkar enlighteners** as two traditions with different presses and patrons.
- **Official clergy, reformist young believers and Khabze revivalists**: three rival programs of religious renewal.
- **Soviet-era reformers and their heirs** (party and Komsomol cadres, teachers) against those who remember the costs.
- **High Balkar villages moved to the lowlands** against those left in place.
- **Baksan valley residents** facing resort development against Nalchik planners.
- **Terek Cossacks**, whose 1990s revival was framed as restoration.
- **Repatriates** bringing ideas of reform from Syria, Turkey and Jordan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th- and early 20th-century enlighteners; mixed for Soviet campaigns (archive-dependent); the 2000s religious reform debate is well documented but mostly through conflict.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for journals and essays; Arabic for manuscripts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2000s religious reform movement led into armed conflict; labels such as "Wahhabi" are often used to stigmatize. Use scholarly and believers' sources beside official ones, and avoid treating any program as the people's voice. Legal reforms belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
