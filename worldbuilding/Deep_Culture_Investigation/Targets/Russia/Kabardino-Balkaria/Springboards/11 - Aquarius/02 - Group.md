# Kabardino-Balkaria: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the group is weighed against the person, where the line between "us" and "them" falls, and how far members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lineage-centered families** in the villages, where a lineage council still meets, against **urban professionals** who rarely see their *фамилия*.
- **Descendants of princely and noble Kabardian houses** against commoner lineages: whether old estate groups still act as groups.
- **Balkar gorge societies** as rival "we"s inside the Balkar people.
- **Terek Cossacks**, whose group is a military host with ranks.
- **Young men's chosen groups**: sports clubs, fan groups, prayer circles, street groups.
- **Veterans** of the Abkhazia war and of Soviet and Russian armies.
- **Repatriates** whose "we" was formed in Syria or Turkey.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1990s national movements and for kin groups in ethnography; mixed for present-day group behavior; regional survey samples are small.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for songs and movement papers; Turkish for diaspora movements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** armed jamaats of the 2000s, interethnic tension between the two titular peoples, and blood feud (a law topic: Libra and Capricorn) are delicate; avoid framing either people as a single bloc. Security-service and activist sources both need critical reading.
- **Held for review:** none.
