## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of Greater Kabarda** (Baksan, Chegem, Zolsky, Urvan districts) against **Lesser Kabarda** villages in the Terek district: different histories of princely rule and contact.
- **Balkars by gorge society**: Malkar (Cherek), Bezengi, Khulam, Chegem, Urusbiy (Baksan and Elbrus); the *ч*- and *ц*-dialect split; Balkars resettled to lowland villages after the 1957 return.
- **Russians of Nalchik** against **Terek Cossack stanitsas** (Prokhladny, Maysky): urban newcomers against an old frontier community with its own host organization.
- **Smaller groups**: Ossetians, Meskhetian Turks, Armenians, Mountain Jews (once a Nalchik community), Ukrainians.
- **Religious lines**: traditional Sunni village Islam, younger reformist believers, Khabze revivalists, Orthodox Christians.
- **Repatriates** from Syria, Turkey and Jordan; the **diaspora** abroad.
- **Generations**: those schooled in Soviet internationalism against the post-1991 cohort.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kabardian ethnography and etiquette; mixed for Balkars (strong folklore, thinner modern sociology); thin for the Cossack and smaller minorities as separate subjects.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for nearly all scholarship; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore, press and terms; Turkish and Arabic for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1864 expulsion and its recognition, the 1944 Balkar deportation, Kabardian-Balkar land and pasture disputes, and armed Islamist violence (the 2005 Nalchik attack) are live political topics; treat activists' and security services' sources alike with care. Customary law (*адат*) belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
