*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Ottoman Turkish for the oldest outside account. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men's circles** (age-mates from one village, wrestling partners, the groom's party) against **women's friendships**, often formed at school and kept through marriage moves.
- **Cross-people friendships in Nalchik** (mixed schools, the university, workplaces) against single-people villages.
- **Families with remembered *кунак* ties** to Ossetian, Cossack or Balkar houses.
- **Fostered and milk-kin bonds**, if still practiced, against ordinary friendship.
- **Conscripts and veterans**, whose friendships formed far from home.
- **Returnees** and their local friends.
- **Emigrants** (Mountain Jews, Russians who left, young people in Moscow) keeping friends at a distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal bonds (kunak, fosterage) in historical ethnography; thin for present-day friendship patterns, which no regional survey covers well.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for proverbs, songs and wedding terms; Russian for scholarship; Turkish for Evliya Çelebi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendships between unrelated young men and women are bound by custom and family honor; ask about them with care. Betrayal of a friend belongs to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none.
