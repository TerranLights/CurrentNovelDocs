## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balkars of the exile generation** and their grandchildren: hope as memory against hope as plan.
- **Kabardians at home** against **diaspora Circassians**: the same homeland hoped for from opposite sides.
- **Young people planning to leave** (Moscow, abroad) against those set on staying.
- **Devout families**, for whom hope is framed by God's will, against secular ones.
- **Families of the missing**.
- **Meskhetian Turks**, with a homeland hope of their own.
- **Russian and Cossack families**, many of whose kin have left the republic.
- **Farmers on the plains and herders in the mountains**, whose yearly hopes ride on weather and markets.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for blessings, rites and the deportation and return; thin for measured optimism in the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for blessings and rites; Russian; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared and the deportations are raw subjects for families; recognition of the 1864 events is politically charged. Faith belongs to Sagittarius and fear to Pisces.
