# Kabardino-Balkaria: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar, Russian; Arabic for religious speech. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian village elders and Nalchik youth**: whether the table-elder's order of speech still holds in town gatherings and group chats.
- **Balkar families of the upper Cherek and Chegem gorges against those resettled lower down after the return of 1957**: avoidance speech and greeting length may differ by place and family history.
- **Former noble and commoner lineages in Kabarda**: whether speech manners marked rank, and whether any trace remains.
- **Russian and Cossack residents in Prokhladny**: their norms of directness against those of Kabardian and Balkar neighbors.
- **Women and men in each people**: whether avoidance and address rules differ by sex.
- **Muslim-observant families and secular families**: religious formulas in everyday greetings.
- **Deaf signers**: a separate channel, with its own etiquette.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore and etiquette studies are plentiful, recorded conversation and pragmatics data are thin.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** exile letters and family silences about 1944; religious speech after the security campaigns of the 2000s, so interviews need care.
