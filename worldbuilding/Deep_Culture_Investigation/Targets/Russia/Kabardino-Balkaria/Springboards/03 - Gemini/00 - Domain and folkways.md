## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians of the Baksan, Chegem, Urvan and Zolka valleys and plains**: the Baksan speech behind the literary standard against other village speech and Nalchik talk.
- **Balkars of the five mountain societies (Malkar/Cherek, Bezengi, Kholam, Chegem, Baksan-Urusbi)**: dialect split, and the schooling gap of the 1944 to 1957 exile.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** of the Prokhladny and Maysky districts: whether households run in Russian only.
- **Ossetians, Meskhetian Turks, Armenians, Koreans** *check* locations: home languages outside the titular pair.
- **Diaspora repatriates** (Syria, Turkey, Jordan): Kabardian learned abroad meets Kabardian learned at home.
- **Nalchik against mountain villages; Soviet-schooled against post-1991 generations**: where and how fast Russian replaces the home language.
- **The Deaf community** (Russian Sign Language; regional branch of the All-Russian Society of the Deaf *check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kabardian etiquette and folklore (a strong Soviet ethnographic school); thinner for the Balkar side and small minorities; current data mostly in Russian.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora and Ottoman sources; German for older travelers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation of the Balkars and their return; the Russo-Circassian war and its remembrance on May 21; 1990s land and status tensions between the titular peoples; Islamic learning after the security operations of the 2000s. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
