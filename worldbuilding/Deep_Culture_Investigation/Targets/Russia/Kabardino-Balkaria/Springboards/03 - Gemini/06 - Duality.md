## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardians and Balkars as the two halves of the republic's name**: how each describes the shared state and its own place in it.
- **Children of mixed Kabardian-Balkar and Kabardian-Russian marriages**: which side they are counted on, by others and by themselves.
- **Russian-dominant urban Kabardians and Balkars in Nalchik**: a national identity with Russian as the daily language.
- **Repatriates from Turkey and Syria**: Circassian at home abroad, foreigners on arrival, or both.
- **Terek Cossacks**: Cossack and Russian as two identities, and how each is claimed.
- **Meskhetian Turks** *check*: an identity tied to more than one homeland.
- **Khabze revivalists and Islamic revivalists**: two moral codes claimed for one people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on interethnic relations and bilingualism; thin on paired worldviews and on tolerance of contradiction as such.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish; French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the status of the two peoples within one republic and past calls to divide it; deportation; religious debate under security scrutiny; ethnic labels in surveys.
- **Held for review:** none.
