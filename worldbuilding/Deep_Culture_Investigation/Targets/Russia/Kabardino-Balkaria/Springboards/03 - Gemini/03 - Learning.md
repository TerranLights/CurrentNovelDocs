## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former Kabardian noble lineages and farming families**: whether fosterage followed rank, and whether any memory of it shapes how families hand children to relatives.
- **Balkar shepherding families of the high pastures and town families in Nalchik**: learning by pitching in against learning by schooling.
- **Balkars born in exile, 1944 to 1957, and their children**: whether a generation went without schooling in the home language, and what came after.
- **Russian and Cossack families of the plains**: their own folk pedagogy and crafts, apart from the titular peoples.
- **Repatriate children from Syria**: learning Russian and local Kabardian as newcomers.
- **Observant Muslim families**: mosque classes in Arabic and Quran reading next to state school.
- **Children with disabilities**: whether learning at home or in special schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnopedagogy writing (often idealizing, so treat as primary material); thin on observed practice today; craft and herding knowledge needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Arabic for madrasa sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exile schooling and its losses; unregistered religious classes, which authorities watch closely; native-language hours in schools may be a live dispute *check* (the policy side belongs to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
