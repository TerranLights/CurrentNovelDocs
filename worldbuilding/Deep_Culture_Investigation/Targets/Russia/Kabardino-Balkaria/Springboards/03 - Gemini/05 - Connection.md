# Kabardino-Balkaria: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kabardino-Balkaria, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish and Arabic for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kabardian lineage groups (лъэпкъ) of the old princely and commoner strata**: whether a surname still opens doors, and to whom.
- **Balkar villages that were resettled after 1957 and those rebuilt on their old sites**: ties rebuilt in a new place against an old one.
- **Nalchik newcomers from the villages**: city life against village networks.
- **Repatriate families from Syria and Turkey**: ties across borders, and how locals take them in.
- **Russian and Cossack residents**: whether church and Cossack-society ties do the work that lineage ties do elsewhere.
- **Ossetian, Armenian and Meskhetian Turkish families** *check*: small-community networks and intermarriage.
- **Elderly people living alone, and men in particular**: whether isolation hides behind dense kin networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich ethnography on ritual kinship and weddings; thin measured data on loneliness and networks for the republic itself.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Kabardian, Karachay-Balkar; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "connections" in public jobs and politics shade into corruption claims; diaspora politics and the memory of the Russo-Circassian war; ties broken by deportation.
- **Held for review:** none.
