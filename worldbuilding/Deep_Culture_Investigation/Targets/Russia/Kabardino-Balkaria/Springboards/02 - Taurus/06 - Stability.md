## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Balkar families: continuity broken in 1944 and rebuilt after 1957; separate those who returned to their own village from those settled lower down.
- Kabardian villages moved in the 19th century against villages on old sites.
- Families of the princely and taubiy houses: lineages traced for centuries against families that keep no written genealogy.
- Nalchik flat dwellers against villagers: rooted in a building or in an ancestral yard.
- Cossack stanitsas and Russian towns of the north: continuity through the empire, Soviet rule and the 1990s exodus of Russians.
- Ossetian, Mountain Jewish and German *check* families: small communities thinned by emigration, and those who stayed.
- Circassian repatriates and Meskhetian Turks: households seeking a fixed place after flight.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and lineage history; thin for daily routine, which needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and archival Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for genealogies, songs and village books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1991-1992 talk of splitting the republic, and claims about who came first to which land, are still raw. Genealogies can be used to back land claims; read them with that in mind.
- **Held for review:** none.
