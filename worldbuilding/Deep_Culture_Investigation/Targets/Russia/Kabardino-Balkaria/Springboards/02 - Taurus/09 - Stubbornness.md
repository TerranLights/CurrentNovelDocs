## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Balkar activists and elders: a decades-long claim to land and to a people's name.
- Kabardian language activists and Circassian organizations: holding to a language and a memory of 1864.
- Families of the old princely houses: pride in ancestors who resisted or who served.
- Terek Cossack revival groups: memory of refusal under early Soviet rule.
- Older villagers against Nalchik youth: firmness in custom read as principle by some, as rigidity by others.
- Muslims of different currents: who holds to which practice against the muftiate or against the state.
- Ossetian and Mountain Jewish communities: how small groups hold to their own ways inside a republic of two titular peoples.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and political conflict; thin for everyday stubbornness, which needs proverbs, tales and fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for songs, tales and activism; English for Khodarkovsky, King and Perović.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land protests, language activism and religious currents are watched by the authorities; do not name private persons. Feud as law goes to Libra, and the spirit of customary justice to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
