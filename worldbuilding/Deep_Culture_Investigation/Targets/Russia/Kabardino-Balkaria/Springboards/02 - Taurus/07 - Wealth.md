## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Descendants of princely and noble families: the memory of old wealth against Soviet leveling.
- Balkar sheep-owning households: riches in flocks and pastures more than in cash.
- Kabardian orchard and grain villages of the plains: new agricultural fortunes.
- Nalchik's officials and business families: wealth near power, and how it is talked about.
- Russian and Cossack towns of the north: industrial wages lost after 1991.
- Migrant workers' families: wealth built from wages earned outside the republic.
- Circassian repatriates and Mountain Jews: wealth carried across borders or left behind.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century and statistical sources; thin and politically guarded for present top wealth.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for tales and feast ethnography; Italian for Interiano; Ottoman Turkish for Evliya.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official wealth and corruption are risky to discuss locally. The Circassian slave trade to the Ottoman lands was a source of some past wealth and is a painful subject; treat it with care.
- **Held for review:** none.
