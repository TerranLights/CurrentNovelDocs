*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kabardian villages of the plains (Baksan, Zolsky, Urvansky, Tersky districts): grain, orchards and horses set the household economy.
- Balkar gorge communities (Cherek, Khulam-Bezengi, Chegem, upper Baksan): sheep, pastures and stone dwellings, with distinct stores and food.
- Russians and Terek Cossacks of Prokhladny and Maysky districts: a separate settler economy and Orthodox feast calendar.
- Nalchik as a city against the villages: wages, flats and markets against plots and herds.
- Ossetians, Mountain Jews (Nalchik), Meskhetian Turks and other small groups: their own food rules and goods.
- Circassian repatriates from Syria and the Balkar families who returned from Central Asian exile: holdings rebuilt from nothing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Soviet ethnography and Rosstat figures are rich; recent ethnography of the Balkar gorges and of the small minorities is thinner. The tracker header lists the pre-war lands only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet set is written for the core lands of the former Kabardino-Balkarian Republic.
- **Languages needed:** Kabardian and Karachay-Balkar for folklore and the dailies; Russian for nearly all scholarship; Ottoman Turkish for Evliya Çelebi and Ottoman trade records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation of the Balkars and the 19th-century expulsion of Circassians shaped what families lost; land and pasture between Kabardian and Balkar villages is disputed. Travelers' texts on Circassian slaves sold abroad need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
