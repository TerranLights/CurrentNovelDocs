## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Clan elders and teip councils against state judges:** who is felt to hold the right to judge a killing may differ by setting.
- **Families of people abducted or killed in the 2000s counterinsurgency:** they meet justice mainly through courts, petitions and Strasbourg.
- **The deported generation and its descendants:** historical justice is a family memory, not only a state matter.
- **Ingush displaced from Prigorodny district (1992):** redress for loss of homes and land frames their idea of justice.
- **Sufi brotherhoods (Qadiri, Batal-Hajji, Naqshbandi) and reform-minded younger Muslims:** each may rank forgiveness, retaliation and state punishment differently.
- **Chechen residents of Sunzha district:** cross-border feuds may run under two republics' authorities.
- **Women:** cases involving women may be handled by kin rather than courts; separate them with care.
- **Mountain Dzheyrakh villages:** the remembered sites of oath and judgment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on feud reconciliation and historical justice; mixed on court statistics (aggregated); human-rights material is plentiful but partisan on all sides.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for sermons, proverbs and tales; Arabic terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** living feuds, abductions, insurgency cases and the protest prosecutions involve named living people; record institutions and published cases only.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search news archives for "комиссия по примирению кровников Ингушетия" and list its reported settlements by year.
2. Search HUDOC for respondent Russia with "Ingushetia" in the facts; sort by year.
3. Search eLibrary.ru for "реабилитация ингушского народа" and "закон о реабилитации репрессированных народов 1991 Ингушетия".
4. Search the Russian State Library for Kovalevsky's *Закон и обычай на Кавказе* and Bugai's deportation collections.
5. Search sudrf.ru for the Supreme Court of the Republic of Ingushetia's published sentences under "убийство" (murder) to see how revenge cases appear in judgments.
