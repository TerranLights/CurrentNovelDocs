# Ingushetia: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Georgian for medieval chronicles; Ossetian and Chechen for neighbors' accounts. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders who lead delegations to neighbors or officials:** their protocol may differ from that of state negotiators.
- **Republic officials facing Moscow, North Ossetia and Chechnya:** three relationships with different histories.
- **Daughters-in-law in extended households:** the most practiced daily diplomacy may happen at home.
- **Young organizers of the 2018-2019 protests:** a newer style of talking to power.
- **Families displaced from Prigorodny district:** the parties most bound by Ingush-Ossetian talks.
- **Imams and Sufi leaders:** sometimes called in as go-betweens.
- **Diaspora in Jordan, Turkey and Europe:** relations with host states and with the homeland.
- **Chechen residents of Sunzha district:** daily neighbor diplomacy across a disputed line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; historical negotiations are well documented in tsarist and Soviet archives; household tact needs ethnography and fieldwork; little measured data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush; Georgian for medieval chronicles; Ossetian for the other side of the 1992 talks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ingush-Ossetian relations, the Beslan siege and protest negotiations are raw; give both sides' sources; avoid naming private mediators.
- **Held for review:** none.
