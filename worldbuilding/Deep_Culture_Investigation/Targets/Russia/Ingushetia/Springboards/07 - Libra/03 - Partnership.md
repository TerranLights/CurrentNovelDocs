*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Couples in Nazran and Magas against those in hill villages:** household size and the say of in-laws may differ.
- **Ingush-Chechen couples:** two closely related peoples whose political partnership ended in 1992 while family ties continued.
- **Ingush-Ossetian and Ingush-Russian couples from before 1992:** mixed marriages under the strain of the Prigorodny conflict.
- **Diaspora couples in Europe:** new laws on marriage and property meet old expectations.
- **Members of the same Sufi brotherhood:** shared devotion as a bond between unrelated men.
- **Business founders inside one teip and across teips:** whether trust follows kin lines is a question to test.
- **Widows and divorced women:** what happens to a partnership's goods and children when it ends.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ritual kinship and alliances are well covered in Russian ethnography; current marriage data is federal and aggregated; business data is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for poetry, theater and sermons; German for early travelers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** plural marriage under religious rite only, and marriages arranged under pressure, involve private people; the end of the Chechen-Ingush partnership remains politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
