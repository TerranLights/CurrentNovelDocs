# Ingushetia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for alms and offerings. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Large extended families and small households:** the burden of returning every wedding and funeral visit falls unevenly.
- **Teips with active mutual-aid funds and those without:** pooled reciprocity against household reciprocity.
- **Families with members working in other Russian regions or abroad:** remittances create new debts and duties.
- **Givers through the Muftiate and those who give privately:** two routes for religious giving.
- **Sufi communities that hold shared zikr and meals:** feeding as a return to God and to fellow devotees.
- **Chechen and Russian neighbors in Sunzha district:** whether exchanges cross ethnic lines.
- **Diaspora families:** gifts and money sent to kin in the republic, and what is expected back.
- **Women:** much of the gift-keeping work of weddings and funerals may fall to them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ritual exchange at weddings and funerals is well covered in ethnography; pooled funds and online giving are thin and need fieldwork; measured data is federal.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for ritual terms and tales; Arabic religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** money collected for blood settlements touches feuds; record practice, not families.
- **Held for review:** none.
