# Ingushetia: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for the vocabulary of shares and just weights. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sons and daughters, widows and heirs:** the shares each receives may follow sharia, custom or federal law; the same family may use more than one.
- **Large and small teips:** whether offices and jobs are expected to be shared out between clans is a question to test, not a given.
- **Land-poor mountain families and plains landholders:** the history of land shortage shapes what a fair allotment means.
- **Displaced Prigorodny households and long-settled residents:** compensation programs set them in competition for scarce housing.
- **Job-seeking young people:** pull and patronage weigh most where work is scarce.
- **Chechen and Russian residents of Sunzha district:** minority access to posts and land as a fairness question.
- **The wrestling and martial-arts community:** fair play is debated in public there most often.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; history of land division is rich in Russian archives; current measures are mostly federal and aggregated to the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for rhymes and proverbs; Arabic terms for inheritance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patronage and election fraud allegations name living officials; inheritance disputes are private family matters; cite only public cases.
- **Held for review:** none.
