# Ingushetia: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (ГӀалгӀай мотт, Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ), Russian; Chechen for the Sunzha-side villages; Arabic for Islamic legal vocabulary; Georgian and Ossetian for older outside records. **Concept in one line:** partnership and justice: spouses and partners, contracts, open enemies, dispute settlement, marriage and gender ways, and the letter of the law; the question is what counts as a fair bargain and a good partnership.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plains towns (Nazran, Magas, Karabulak, Malgobek):** where courts and registries sit, so written law meets custom most often.
- **Dzheyrakh district and the old mountain societies (Feappi, Galgai, Tsorin, Khamkhi, Metskhal):** the pre-modern oath and council sites.
- **Sunzha district's Chechen and remaining Russian (Terek Cossack) residents:** rules for deals and marriages may differ by village.
- **Orstkhoy (Karabulak) descendants:** a group both Chechens and Ingush claim, so who mediates its quarrels is worth separating.
- **Families displaced from Prigorodny district (1992):** their claims to land and redress make "open enemies" and "justice" concrete.
- **Sufi brotherhoods (Kunta-Hajji's Qadiri followers, the Batal-Hajji community, Naqshbandi lines) and reform-minded younger Muslims:** each may weigh sharia, adat and state law differently.
- **Returnees from Kazakhstan and diasporas in Turkey, Jordan and Western Europe:** exile may have reshaped marriage and dispute rules.
- **Women:** marriage, divorce and inheritance rules bear on them differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for customary law and feuds (Russian ethnography, human-rights reporting); mixed for measured data; thin in English.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush (folklore, sermons, local media); Arabic; Georgian.
- **Lands:** the tracker's pre-war lands are broad ("Russia"); the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (Prigorodny district and the Chechnya border strip especially). The sheets are written for the core republic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation, the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict, the 2018-2019 border protests and their prosecutions, and active feuds involve living people; name no private families.
- **Held for review:** none.
