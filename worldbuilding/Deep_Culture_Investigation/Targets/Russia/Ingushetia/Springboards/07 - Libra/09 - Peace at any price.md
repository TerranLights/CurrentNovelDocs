# Ingushetia: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for the religious vocabulary of strife and calm. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women in extended households:** where silence about mistreatment may be expected for the family's peace.
- **Young protesters and the elders who urged them home:** a public split over calm against standing up.
- **Followers of Kunta-Hajji's Qadiri line:** an inherited teaching of non-resistance.
- **Families who settled abductions or injuries privately:** who bore the cost of the settlement.
- **Descendants of the deported generation:** silence kept under Soviet rule, then broken.
- **Prigorodny families:** pressed to accept compensation in place of return.
- **Russian-speaking and Chechen neighbors in Sunzha district:** keeping heads down as a minority strategy.
- **Diaspora descendants of 1865 emigrants in Turkey and Jordan:** a family history of leaving rather than resisting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; history and court data are reachable; silence itself leaves few records, so testimony and NGO work are central and partial.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for literature and family speech; Arabic religious terms; Ottoman Turkish for the emigration archive.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor-related violence, abductions and extortion involve victims; cite reports, never identify people; protest prosecutions are ongoing in effect.
- **Held for review:** none.
