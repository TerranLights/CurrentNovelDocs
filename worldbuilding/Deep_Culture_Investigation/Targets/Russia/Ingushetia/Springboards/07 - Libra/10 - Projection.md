*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for the vocabulary of sorcery, healing and repentance; Ossetian for the other side of 1992. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself; and how a culture places unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of the deported:** a people once charged as a whole, whose memory of that blame shapes how they see accusers.
- **Prigorodny displaced families and Ossetian neighbors:** the mirror images of 1992 are the core case.
- **Students and workers in other Russian cities:** where they meet the "Caucasian" label.
- **Sufi brotherhoods and reform-minded younger Muslims:** each side can be labeled by the other.
- **Households that consult ruqya healers:** misfortune laid on sorcery and on named people.
- **Chechen neighbors:** jokes and images traded between closely related peoples, handled with care.
- **Russian residents and descendants of Terek Cossacks:** older images of Ingush on the Sunzha line, and the reverse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; collective blame and enemy images are well documented in law and human-rights work; household blame (evil eye, sorcery) needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush; Arabic healing terms; Ossetian for the other side's sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic images of Ossetians, Chechens and Russians are explosive; record them only as claims with sources from all sides; accusations of sorcery can harm named people; the "Wahhabi" label has led to prosecutions.
- **Held for review:** none.
