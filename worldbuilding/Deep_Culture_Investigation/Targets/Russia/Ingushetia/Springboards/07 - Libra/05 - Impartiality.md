# Ingushetia: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for the classical vocabulary of judgeship. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Civil servants in Magas and Nazran:** they apply written merit rules daily and meet kin requests most.
- **Clan elders asked to arbitrate:** neutrality may depend on being from outside the quarrel.
- **Sufi brotherhood members:** loyalty to a sheikh's line may rank beside or above neutral rule.
- **Students and their families at exam time:** blind testing against pressure for help.
- **Displaced Prigorodny families dealing with housing offices:** who gets served first becomes an impartiality question.
- **Chechen and Russian residents in local courts:** whether non-Ingush parties expect a neutral hearing.
- **Returnees and diaspora members:** comparisons with offices abroad shape their expectations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; election and court data are public but federal in design; everyday favoritism is reported mainly in opposition media and needs careful reading.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ingush for tales and elders' speech; Arabic for classical judgeship texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption and election-fraud claims name living officials; cite the published claim, not the person; opposition journalists have faced violence.
- **Held for review:** none.
