# Ingushetia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (гӀалгӀай мотт), Russian; Chechen in Chechen-speaking villages; Arabic for Islamic terms. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and what they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families with the oldest deportation memory** against those born after the return in 1957: different objects stand for loss and for home.
- **Prigorodny families still in temporary settlements** *check*: whose belongings have moved more than once.
- **Mountain lineages with ancestral towers** in Dzheyrakh and the Assa valley, against lowland families with no stone holdings.
- **Women's things** (trousseau, jewelry, cloth) against **men's** (weapons, cars, tools), and who keeps the household's keepsakes.
- **Chechen-speaking villages** in the Sunzha district, whose funeral customs for a dead person's things may differ.
- **Ingush in Kazakhstan** who never returned, and **post-2000 refugees in Western Europe** who left with almost nothing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Archaeology and deportation records are rich; everyday possessions are thin in print and best found in fieldwork and social media.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for some villages; Arabic for Islamic terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation and 1992 losses are painful; weapons ownership is legally and politically delicate; family heirlooms may be private. The division of a dead person's estate as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
