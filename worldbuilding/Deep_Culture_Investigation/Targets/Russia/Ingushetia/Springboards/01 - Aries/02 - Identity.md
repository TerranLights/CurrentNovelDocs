# Ingushetia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; Georgian and Turkish or Arabic for diaspora and border material. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland societies (Galgai, Fyappi, Dzheyrakh, Tsorin, Khamkhi):** identity placed in a named valley as well as in the nation.
- **Families who identify as Orstkhoy:** a group claimed by both Ingush and Chechen narratives.
- **Prigorodny-district families and their descendants:** a homeland outside the present borders.
- **Kazakhstan- and Kyrgyzstan-born elders:** an identity formed in exile.
- **Sufi and Salafi-leaning Muslims:** whether faith or nation is named first.
- **Chechen and Russian residents of the Sunzha district:** minority identities inside the republic.
- **Kists of Pankisi (Georgia) and muhajir descendants in Turkey and Jordan:** related groups outside the lands, as a check.
- **Young urban Ingush in Magas and Moscow:** pan-Caucasian, Russian and online identities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on history, deportation and political identity; thin on surveys of what people name first.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; Ossetian and Georgian for border debates; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Alan claim and Prigorodny are live disputes with Ossetians; the 2018 Chechen-Ingush border deal is a live dispute; handle both sides' sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
