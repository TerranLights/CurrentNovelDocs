## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural households with plots and livestock, and town households on wages:** two routes to self-provisioning.
- **State-sector employees and private workers:** dependence on public budgets against market earnings.
- **Labor migrants and their wives and parents at home:** independence through absence.
- **Women earning their own income:** a separate story of economic independence inside the household.
- **Pensioners and disabled residents:** support from kin and the state.
- **Highland resettlers in Dzheyrakh:** self-sufficiency as a choice of land.
- **Cossack-descended residents of the stanitsas:** a different household and garden tradition.
- **Diaspora households in Europe:** living under another welfare system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on budget and labor statistics; thin on attitudes to self-reliance; ethnography of the clan safety net is uneven.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ingush.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "subsidized region" talk carries outside stereotypes; do not read budget data as a claim about people; political independence is a sensitive topic under federal law.
- **Held for review:** none.
