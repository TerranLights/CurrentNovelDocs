**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (Гӏалгӏай мотт, *Ghalghaj mott*), Russian; Chechen for Chechen-speaking residents; Russian for the Cossack-descended and other Russian-speaking residents. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain: the self, the body, appearance, beginnings and initiative; what counts as a self and how a self shows itself, with naming ways as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain Ingushetia (Dzheyrakh district) and its old societies** (Dzheyrakh, Galgai, Fyappi, Tsorin, Khamkhi): lineages whose origin is tied to named tower villages.
- **The lowland towns** (Nazran, Malgobek, Karabulak, Magas, Sunzha): urban and resettled populations where clan and civic identities mix.
- **Sufi brotherhoods** (Kunta-Hajji's zikr circles; Batal-Hajji's followers): a self also placed in a wird.
- **Cossack-descended and other Russian-speaking residents** of the Sunzha stanitsas: other naming and dress.
- **Chechen residents and families with mixed ties**: the Vainakh "we" seen from inside the republic.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district**: a lost homeland.
- **Returnees from Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan**: deportation-era generations.
- **Diasporas** in Moscow, Europe, Turkey and Jordan: naming and dress kept or reworked.
- **Women and men, elders and youth:** separate voices on modesty and self-display.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker lists the pre-war lands only as "Russia," so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Data:** mixed: rich Russian-language ethnography, thin Ingush-language scholarship, almost no republic-level self-construal surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ingush; Chechen; Georgian for medieval mountain sources; Arabic and Ingush *ajam* script for older religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Prigorodny conflict; the 2018–19 border protests and prosecutions; counterinsurgency years; the contested Alan heritage claim and Orstkhoy status. Family honor topics need consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
