# Ingushetia: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen; Ossetian for the cross-group strand. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women:** who may weep in public and who laments formally likely differs.
- **Elders and the young:** etiquette-based reading of others against therapy-language "empathy" on social media.
- **Ingush near the Ossetian line (Prigorodny families, Vladikavkaz ties):** empathy across a conflict boundary is a distinct question.
- **Mixed Ingush-Chechen households of Sunzha:** two closely related idiom sets.
- **The Russian Cossack-descended minority:** Russian-language idioms of sympathy and Orthodox mourning.
- **Children raised in displacement camps (1990s-2000s):** the group most outside psychosocial work aimed at.
- **Mountain Dzheyrakh and the plain:** different density of kin and neighbor ties.
- **Moscow and St. Petersburg diaspora:** exposure to Russian urban psychology talk.
- **Kazakhstan Ingush:** a community whose neighbors were Kazakhs, Germans and Koreans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Etiquette and lament are documented; direct measures of empathy at republic level are rare and scattered in small student samples.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, some Ossetian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 conflict and its victims on both sides; children's trauma records; women's mourning, which may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
