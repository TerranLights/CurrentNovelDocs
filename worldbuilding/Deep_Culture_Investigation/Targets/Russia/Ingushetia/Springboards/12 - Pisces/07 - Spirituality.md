# Ingushetia: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, Arabic; Georgian for the medieval Christian layer. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; this entry takes experience, practice and the sacred outside institutions (institutions are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kunta-Hajji's line, the Batal-Hajji line and the Naqshbandi families:** different rites (loud or silent zikr) and sacred places.
- **Salafi-leaning believers:** their stance on shrine visits and Sufi rites is a key contrast to check.
- **Soviet-educated secular families:** belief and practice that may be private or lapsed.
- **Women:** home мовлид, назмы and mourning practice, often out of sight of the mosque.
- **The Sunzha Orthodox Russians:** a Christian calendar and parish life.
- **Mountain villages and returning townspeople:** the mountain sites as places of homecoming and reverence.
- **Chechen residents:** shared brotherhoods, different shrines.
- **Kazakhstan, Jordan and Turkey Ingush:** continuity away from the land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Plenty on brotherhoods and history; inner experience in believers' words is thin outside video and song.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic, Chechen; Georgian and Turkish for side sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** brotherhood rites and lineages may be closed to outsiders: respect, do not intrude; religious disputes between Sufi and Salafi currents tied to past violence; state labeling of "extremism".
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLIBRARY.ru for «баталхаджинцы» and «суфизм Ингушетия».
