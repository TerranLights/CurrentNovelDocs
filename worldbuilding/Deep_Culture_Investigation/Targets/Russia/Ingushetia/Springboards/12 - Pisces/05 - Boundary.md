# Ingushetia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen; Georgian and Ossetian for the neighbors across the lines. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Border villages with Chechnya (Galashki, Arshty, Sunzha, Malgobek district; *check*):** where the line is newest and most disputed.
- **Ingush families from the Prigorodny district:** a boundary they were pushed across.
- **Dzheyrakh border-zone villagers:** living behind a permit line beside Georgia.
- **Nazran and Magas households against mountain villages:** walled yards on the plain against tower complexes.
- **The Sunzha Russian minority:** different fence, visiting and touch customs.
- **Chechen residents:** close kin across a contested line.
- **Older and younger women:** the line of modesty and movement in public may be moving.
- **Qadiri vird families with strict in-group marriage, against others:** group boundaries drawn by lineage, to check.
- **Moscow diaspora:** urban privacy norms meeting family ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on territory and kinship lines; thin on personal space and privacy as people describe them.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, Georgian, Ossetian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Prigorodny and Chechen border disputes are live and prosecuted; honor-related harm to women; security services and checkpoints; border-zone photography. All legal sides of borders go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian State Library for «Смирнова Семья и семейный быт народов Северного Кавказа» and read its Ingush chapters on avoidance.
2. Search eLIBRARY.ru for «тамги ингушей» and «Семёнов горная Ингушетия».
3. Search the web for «Джейрахский район пограничная зона пропуск».
4. Pull digitized maps of Checheno-Ingushetia from the 1930s and the 1957 restoration for the line around Sunzha and Malgobek.
5. Read *Host and Guest* in a Russian or English translation, then search Georgian sources on Khevsur-Kist contact.
