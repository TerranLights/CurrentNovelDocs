## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men and young women:** the places and permitted forms of escape likely differ sharply.
- **Magas, Nazran and Karabulak against villages:** commercial leisure against kin-bound evenings.
- **Malgobek and the oil-town legacy:** a Soviet industrial town with its own leisure history.
- **The Sunzha Russian minority:** different drinking norms and access to alcohol.
- **Sufi vird communities against Salafi-leaning circles:** where dance, music and zikr gatherings sit as rest or as wrong.
- **Youth raised in the displacement years:** a group named in drug-crisis reporting, to check.
- **Mountain Dzheyrakh residents:** the destination of others' escapes.
- **Labor migrants and asylum seekers in Europe:** escape by leaving, and the pull back home.
- **Kazakhstan Ingush:** whose families remember escaping, or not, from the settlements.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official drug and alcohol data exist at regional level but may be undercounted; leisure surveys at republic level are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; German and French for diaspora; Arabic for religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug users and their families; elopement and abduction cases; asylum seekers' safety; police operations against youth.
- **Held for review:** none.
