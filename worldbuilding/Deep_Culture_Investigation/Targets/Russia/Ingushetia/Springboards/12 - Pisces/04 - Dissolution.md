# Ingushetia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, Arabic (Sufi vocabulary), Turkish (muhajir descendants), Georgian (early sources). **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (working definition proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Loud-zikr Qadiri communities, silent-zikr Naqshbandi families, and Salafi-leaning circles:** three answers on whether losing oneself in rite is holy or wrong.
- **Ingush-speaking villages against Russian-dominant urban youth in Magas and Nazran:** the language-shift line.
- **Orstkhoy descendants:** a society split between Ingush and Chechen identities and partly lost to emigration.
- **The Sunzha Russian minority:** a community thinning out.
- **Chechen residents of Sunzha:** whose own republic split from the shared one.
- **Women married into another clan:** the bond-to-bond passage.
- **Mountain and plain:** older crypt-and-tower memory against plain cemeteries.
- **Muhajir descendants in Turkey and Jordan, and Ingush of Kazakhstan:** how far identity has dissolved into a host society.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Language and census data and archival records are good; the inner side (trance, self-loss) is documented mostly by outsiders or on video, rarely in participants' words.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic, Turkish, Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sufi rites are not for filming everywhere; the Ingush-Ossetian and Ingush-Chechen splits are live disputes; religious divorce touches family privacy.
- **Held for review:** none.
