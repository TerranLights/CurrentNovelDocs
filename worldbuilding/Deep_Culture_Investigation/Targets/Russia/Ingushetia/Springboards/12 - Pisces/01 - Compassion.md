# Ingushetia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, Arabic (Islamic charity terms). **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages against Nazran, Magas and Karabulak:** village mutual aid and urban foundations may relieve suffering in different ways.
- **Sunzha district's Cossack-descended Russians:** Orthodox almsgiving and parish charity beside Muslim sadaqa.
- **Chechen villages in Sunzha and the border:** shared Vainakh customs with their own variants.
- **Qadiri vird communities against muftiate and Salafi-leaning circles:** sadaqa at zikr gatherings and shrine visits against stricter readings of almsgiving.
- **Families who hosted displaced Chechens in 1999-2000:** direct experience of mass hospitality.
- **Prigorodny-displaced households:** the receivers, not only the givers, of help.
- **Mountain Dzheyrakh villages:** thin settlement, where kin help may stand in for any services.
- **Diaspora in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan:** whose memory of the exile holds help received from strangers.
- **Women's charity circles and men's mosque-based giving:** likely separate channels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good ethnography of custom and much humanitarian reporting on the displacement years; almost no republic-level survey data on giving or helping.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen; Arabic for zakat terms; Kazakh for exile archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blood-feud cases (named families); displacement and humanitarian aid politics; security scrutiny of Islamic charity funds; the privacy of the poor named in appeals.
- **Held for review:** none.
