# Ingushetia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen, Arabic (theological terms). **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of Kunta-Hajji's line and families who honor armed resistance:** two models of yielding and refusing.
- **Former fighters who surrendered, and their kin:** surrender as lived, with its stigma or relief.
- **Deportation survivors and their grandchildren:** endurance spoken of across three generations.
- **Women and men:** who is expected to yield in a household quarrel or a marriage.
- **The Sunzha Orthodox Russians:** смирение as a Christian virtue.
- **Chechen residents:** a parallel history of surrender and resistance with different outcomes.
- **Mountain and plain:** older legends of yielding to plague and death in the mountains.
- **The young urban generation:** "never give up" sports and self-help talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Theology and history are rich; data on how people accept illness or death are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic, Chechen.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** former fighters and their families; deportation memory; custody disputes; "fatalism" as an outsiders' label to record, never adopt.
- **Held for review:** none.
