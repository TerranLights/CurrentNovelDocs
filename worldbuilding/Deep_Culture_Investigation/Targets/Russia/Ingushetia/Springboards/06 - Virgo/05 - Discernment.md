## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders and the young on messaging apps:** different habits of trusting forwarded news.
- **Women who arrange or vet matches and the young couples themselves:** who judges a match.
- **Sufi disciples and Salafi-leaning Muslims:** whether a shaykh's insight or the text alone guides choice.
- **Pensioners and rural households:** frequent targets of phone fraud.
- **Livestock traders and smiths:** practical connoisseurship in animals and metal.
- **Ingush in European asylum communities:** judging rumors about papers and return.
- **Ingush working in Moscow and other Russian cities:** meeting prejudice and learning to read strangers.
- **Chechen residents and Russian residents:** neighbors whose own signs of trust may differ.
- **Mountain and lowland families:** lineage knowledge used to place a stranger.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore and religious guidance are rich; data on scams and misinformation exist only at the federal or press level.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush for proverbs and epic, Russian for fraud data and the press, Arabic for prayer and Sufi terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** opposition media and the 2019 protests carry legal risk for sources; matchmaking inquiries touch family honor and privacy; some pre-Islamic practices may be disowned today.
- **Held for review:** none.
