## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young people and elders:** the age line decides who stands and serves at gatherings.
- **Daughters-in-law in a husband's household:** service obligations tied to marriage and residence.
- **Sufi disciples and reform-minded Muslims:** service to a shaykh against service to God alone.
- **Veterans of imperial, Soviet and Russian service, and their families:** what military service meant to each generation.
- **Civil servants in Magas and the districts:** state service as job, duty or patronage.
- **Households in Sunzha, Karabulak and Nazran that hosted the displaced from Chechnya:** service on a large scale in crisis.
- **Guest-house keepers and guides in the mountain district:** hospitality as a paid service to tourists.
- **Waiters, caterers and market sellers:** paid service work and its standing.
- **Russian Orthodox residents of the Sunzha stanitsas:** parish and Cossack forms of service.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Hospitality and household service are well covered in ethnography; modern service work and volunteering are thin outside social media.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for Sufi texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conscription and service in security forces are bound up with the counterinsurgency years; women's household roles are a private family matter; the displaced of 1999 to 2000 and of 1992 are trauma topics.
- **Law:** rules of conscription and civil service as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
