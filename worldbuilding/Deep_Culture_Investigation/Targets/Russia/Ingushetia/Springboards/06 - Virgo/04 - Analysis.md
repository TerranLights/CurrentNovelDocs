## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **University-trained families in Magas and Nazran and village households:** formal education may change who is trusted to work things out.
- **Religious scholars trained in madrasas and secular-trained engineers and teachers:** two schools of reasoning from evidence.
- **Elders and family councils:** how far the senior voice stands in for analysis.
- **Oil engineers and technicians in Malgobek:** a technical culture with Soviet roots.
- **Generations schooled in exile, in Soviet Grozny and after 1992 in the republic:** three school systems.
- **Students and graduates in Moscow, Rostov and Stavropol:** analytic training gained away from home.
- **Russian residents and former residents of the stanitsas:** different school traditions and networks.
- **Women teachers of mathematics and science:** whose analysis is heard at school and at home.
- **Young freelancers and online learners:** new routes into analytic work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Education data exist but past exam-integrity scandals complicate them; the history of reckoning and measures is thinly documented.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ingush; Arabic for the madrasa tradition.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** election and census numbers are politically charged; religious authority is a delicate topic since the counterinsurgency years.
- **Law:** reasoning in Islamic jurisprudence goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up the numeral chapter in Nichols's *Ingush Grammar*; note the counting words and any older measures.
2. eLibrary.ru: "меры длины ингуши" and "тамги ингушских башен".
3. FIOKO site: download the latest regional ЕГЭ math results for the Republic of Ingushetia.
4. Library catalog: "Kemper Arabic scholarship North Caucasus" and "Семёнов археологические разведки Ингушетия".
5. Search the regional press for "перепись Ингушетия спор" and "явка Ингушетия 2008".
