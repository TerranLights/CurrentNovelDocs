*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders in village households and elders in Magas apartments:** an elder's tasks may shrink or grow with the house.
- **Young men without work and young men on rotation jobs:** being needed at home against providing from afar.
- **Daughters and sons, brides and unmarried girls:** different measures of being helpful.
- **Disabled people and their families:** where dependence and contribution are argued over.
- **Exile-born grandparents:** skills learned in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan and passed on, or not.
- **Builders, welders and mechanics in the plain towns:** handiness as a paid identity.
- **Mountain herders and households on the edge of the reserve:** practical skill in a thinly peopled area.
- **Russian dacha and garden households in the Sunzha stanitsas:** a separate thrift and reuse tradition.
- **Pious households and secular households:** usefulness framed as worship or as civic duty.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin. Census and pension data exist; how people feel about being useful is mostly in social media and conversation, not studies.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for hadith sources; Georgian for Vakhushti.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability certificates and pensions are politically charged; families may guard information on disabled or elderly members; exile memories are traumatic.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Rosstat census portal: download household-size and multigenerational tables for the Republic of Ingushetia.
2. eLibrary.ru: "этнопедагогика ингушей" and "воспитание детей у ингушей".
3. Kunstkamera collection site: search "ингуши" and list household objects with dates.
4. YouTube: "проповедь на ингушском языке пайда" and log speakers and dates.
5. Search the press for "справки об инвалидности Ингушетия" and note outlets and years.
