## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families in Nazran and Magas and families in mountain or border villages:** access to clinics differs.
- **New mothers and their kin:** the postpartum period as a family health regime.
- **Deportation survivors and their descendants:** illness memory from exile.
- **The displaced of 1992 and of 1999 to 2000:** camp health and war trauma.
- **Sufi communities and Salafi-leaning preachers:** shrine visits and ruqya accepted or rejected.
- **Young athletes and gym-goers:** a newer fitness culture.
- **Patients who travel to Moscow, Stavropol or abroad:** care sought beyond the republic.
- **Russian residents of the stanitsas:** Orthodox and Soviet folk remedies of their own.
- **Chechen residents:** close healing traditions, separate networks.
- **Ingush in Europe:** care in host health systems and in Ingush-language networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Federal health data exist but their quality in the region is disputed; folk and religious healing are documented mainly in journalism.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ingush, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mental illness, addiction and reproductive health are private; war injuries and torture are trauma topics; religious healing is contested between Muslim currents.
- **Held for review:** none.
