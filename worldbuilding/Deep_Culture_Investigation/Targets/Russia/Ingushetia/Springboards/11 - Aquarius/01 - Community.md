# Ingushetia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush and Russian; Chechen for the eastern villages; Ossetian for Prigorodny-related sources. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), who recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plain villages settled from the mountains** (Nazran district, the Sunzha valley) against **the near-empty mountain villages** of the Dzheyrakh district, which families revisit in summer.
- **Nazran's old quarters**, **Karabulak** and **Malgobek** (an oil town with a mixed past) against **Magas**, a capital built from the 1990s without an inherited community.
- **Sunzha (formerly Sleptsovskaya) and the Assa valley**, including Galashki and Arshty, where Ingush, Chechen and former Cossack histories meet.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district** and the settlements built for them.
- **Hometown associations** in Moscow and in Siberian oil towns, and the **diasporas** in Kazakhstan, Turkey and Jordan.
- **Women's networks** (condolence visits, wedding work), which may run apart from the men's assemblies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on rites and village life in Russian ethnography; thin on measured belonging, since surveys rarely sample the republic deeply.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush and Russian; Chechen and Ossetian for the edges.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1992 Prigorodny violence; the 1999-2004 displacement; mourning gatherings are private occasions, so observe only by invitation.
- **Held for review:** none.
