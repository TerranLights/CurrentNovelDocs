# Ingushetia: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush and Russian; Arabic for the contemplative vocabulary of Sufism; Georgian and Kist Chechen for Pankisi. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings or from outcomes, in forms judged as calm and composure or as coldness and withdrawal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women**, for display rules at funerals and before elders.
- **Daughters-in-law and their in-laws**, where avoidance rules apply most.
- **Qadiri followers with loud zikr**, **Naqshbandi silent practice** and **Salafi-leaning Muslims** who reject both.
- **Elders who lived through exile** against **grandchildren**, who know the deportation as told memory.
- **Mountain herders and last mountain residents** against **town dwellers**.
- **Kists of Pankisi** as a cross-border comparison for religious practice.
- **Urban professionals** (doctors, teachers), for whom distance is a duty of the job.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Family etiquette is well documented in Soviet ethnography; inner and religious detachment is reported mainly through Sufi practice; measured data on emotion and loneliness are almost absent for the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic; Georgian for Pankisi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grief and trauma interviews need care and consent; wird practices may be private to members; in-law customs touch on family honor.
- **Held for review:** none.
