# Ingushetia: The domain, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (ГӀалгӀай мотт, Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ), Russian; Chechen for the mixed eastern villages; Georgian, Turkish, Arabic and Kazakh for kin and diaspora groups. **Concept in one line:** the domain asks what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, groups, hopes and ideals toward humanity, with settlement, migration, association and affiliation as its folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain-origin and lowland families** (Dzheyrakh district, the Assa gorge, against Nazran and the plain): different settlement histories and gathering places.
- **The old mountain societies** (Fyappi, Galgai, Tsorin, Dzherakh, Metskhal, Khamkhi, Nazran): groupings older than the republic, *check* the list.
- **Malgobek and the Sunzha area**: oil-town and former Cossack-stanitsa settings with mixed pasts.
- **Chechen residents and the Arshty question**: neighbors sharing language roots and some villages.
- **Families from the Prigorodny district** and their descendants, a displaced group with its own memory.
- **Returnees' memory of Central Asia** and the **diasporas** in Moscow, Turkey, Jordan and Georgia's Pankisi gorge.
- **Sufi brotherhoods (вирд)** and **Salafi-leaning youth**: two very different religious groupings.
- **Women, youth and urban Magas**, so that elders' voices are not taken as the whole.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Ethnography and folklore are rich in Russian; survey data are thin, since national samples rarely hold enough Ingush respondents. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (the pre-war lands carried are listed only as "Russia"); this sign is written for the core republic's lands.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush and Russian; Chechen, Georgian, Turkish, Arabic and Kazakh for the edges.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Prigorodny conflict of 1992 and the 2018-2019 border protests (activists were prosecuted); Sufi-Salafi tension; family honor matters. Shrines and lineage lore may be held by families, so ask before publishing.
- **Held for review:** none.
