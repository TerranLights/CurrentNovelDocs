# Ingushetia: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush and Russian; Georgian for highland neighbors' sworn-brother customs; Kazakh for exile-era ties; Chechen for shared terms. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men's and women's friendships**: meeting places and public visibility may differ sharply.
- **Village friends from childhood** against **friendships made at Ingush State University or in the army**.
- **Mountain-origin families** with old cross-range ties to Georgian highlanders, against **plain families** with Russian and Ossetian neighbors in the past.
- **Returnees and elders** who remember Kazakh and Kyrgyz neighbors, against **the young**, who did not live in exile.
- **Prigorodny-displaced families**, whose cross-ethnic friendships were broken.
- **Chechen residents** and **Kists of Pankisi**, linked by shared words and kin, as a question of where friendship and kinship blur.
- **Diaspora in Moscow, Turkey and Jordan**, friends made outside the republic.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ritual friendship and guest-friendship are well covered in older ethnography; everyday friendship today is barely studied; national polls rarely break out the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Georgian, Kazakh and Chechen.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendships across the Ossetian line; private chats need consent; friendship between unmarried men and women may be a sensitive topic.
- **Held for review:** none.
