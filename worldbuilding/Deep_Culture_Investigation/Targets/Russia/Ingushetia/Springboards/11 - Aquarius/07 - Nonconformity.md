*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men and young women**: departures likely sanctioned differently.
- **Wird members, Salafi-leaning Muslims and the secular**: each may count the others as the nonconformists.
- **Political dissenters of 2018-2019** against **officials and loyal elders**.
- **Urban Magas and Nazran** against **villages**, where gossip and kin may weigh more.
- **Batal-Hajji followers** as a group often described as apart.
- **Chechen and Russian residents**, who follow different norms inside the same republic.
- **Diaspora youth in Moscow and abroad**, who may live by other norms and face judgment on return visits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political and religious dissent is well documented by rights groups; everyday departures (dress, marriage choice) are documented mainly by journalists and in private talk.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush and Russian; Arabic for religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** high. Activists and religious dissenters face prosecution; sexual and gender nonconformity carries real danger, and Russian law since 2023 treats LGBT activism as extremist, so any research must protect sources and avoid naming persons; honor-related violence against women.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian State Library's newspaper scans of *Терские ведомости* for "абрек" with Nazran or Sunzha place names.

3. Read Kodzoev's biography and note each sanction he faced.
4. Search the archived Memorial site for "ингушское дело" and list the defendants' charges.
5. Search Daptar for "Ингушетия" and tag each story by the norm departed from.
