# Ingushetia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic (funeral prayer and fiqh); Chechen for Sunzha-area villages; Kazakh and Turkish for diaspora graves. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life: dying, the dead, grief and mourning, and what death is taken to mean.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families whose elders died in exile (1944-1957)** against **families without exile graves**: mourning without a grave to visit.
- **Families of the 1992 Prigorodny dead and missing**: grief tied to land loss and unidentified remains.
- **Families of those killed or disappeared in the 2000s**: violent death, martyr language and state pressure on funerals.
- **Mountain-descended lineages** with ancestral crypts against **lowland families** with only Islamic cemeteries.
- **Sufi wird followers** against **Salafi-leaning households**: disputes over loud grief, grave visits and memorial meals.
- **Sunzha stanitsa Russian Orthodox residents**: a separate calendar of the dead (Radonitsa) within the same lands.
- **Young urban Magas residents** against **village elders**: who sits with the dying and how death is spoken of.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on archaeology and violent death; thin on private grief, end-of-life care and suicide.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic; Kazakh and Turkish for diaspora graves.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** counterinsurgency deaths, the 1992 conflict and deportation deaths are politically live; suicide is likely under-reported and stigmatized; never press families on unrecovered remains. Assisted dying as law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
