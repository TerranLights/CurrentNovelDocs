*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain residents of the Assa and Armkhi valleys** against **lowland towns**: exposure to mudflows, isolation and slow rescue.
- **Chechen displaced families who settled in Ingushetia** against **Ingush host families**: two sides of the same humanitarian crisis.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district in 1992**, still in temporary housing years later: crisis that never ended.
- **Russian Cossack families who left the Sunzha stanitsas** in the 1990s and 2000s, and those who stayed.
- **Labor migrants and their households**: economic crisis met by leaving.
- **Families of victims of the 2004 Nazran attack and of later bombings**: political violence as personal crisis.
- **The elderly who were children in exile** against **the young who know it from stories**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich humanitarian reporting for 1992-2005; thin for personal and mental-health crisis.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic; Kazakh for exile-era archives; English for NGO reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** displacement and political violence are living trauma; avoid retraumatizing interviews and follow psychosocial protocols. Emergency law and state powers in crisis go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
