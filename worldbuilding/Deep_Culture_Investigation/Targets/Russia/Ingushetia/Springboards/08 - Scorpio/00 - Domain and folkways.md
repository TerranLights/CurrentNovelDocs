*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain Ingush of the Dzheyrakh and Assa valleys** against **lowland Nazran and Malgobek villages**: crypt and tower heritage versus plains Islamic cemeteries.
- **The old mountain societies' descendants** (Fyappi, Metskhal, Khamkhi, Tsori, Galashki): separate local rites and lineage burial grounds.
- **Orstkhoy (Karabulak) families**: a group between Ingush and Chechen identities.
- **Sunzha-area Chechens** and **the remaining Russian and Cossack residents of the stanitsas**: different funeral and inheritance customs inside the same lands.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district in 1992** and **families born in Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan exile**: death and loss carried through displacement.
- **Sufi wird communities** (Qadiri, Naqshbandi) against **Salafi-leaning circles**: disputed funeral and grave practices.
- **Diaspora** in Turkey and Jordan (19th-century emigrants) and in Western Europe (post-2000 refugees).

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and its siblings are written for the core lands of the Republic of Ingushetia.
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for archaeology and 19th-century ethnography; thin for surveys on sex, money in marriage and secrecy.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic; Chechen and Georgian for some sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation, the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict and the 2000s counterinsurgency involve living grief and political risk; sex and sexual diversity are highly sensitive; interview subjects need protection. Law topics go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
