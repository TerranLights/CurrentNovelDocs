## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Soviet-educated urban professionals:** belief held privately after decades of atheist schooling; compare stated and practiced belief.
- **Followers of Kunta-Hajji's wird:** belief in saints' wonders and intercession.
- **Revival-minded and Salafi-identifying believers:** rejection of amulets, shrine visits and saints' wonders as unwarranted.
- **Women who keep household protective customs:** folk belief carried in the home.
- **Mountain-village families:** survival of older beliefs about spirits, places and the dead.
- **Orthodox residents of Sunzha:** a minority creed in a Muslim setting.
- **Chechen residents and families who fled Chechnya in the 1990s and 2000s:** shared Vainakh beliefs, to see where local versions differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and religious practice; thin for surveys of belief.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labeling someone Salafi or "Wahhabi" can endanger him; possession and healing are private matters; Soviet sources are hostile and need careful reading. Folk magic in depth is Pisces's.
- **Held for review:** none.
