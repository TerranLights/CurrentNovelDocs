## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kazakhstan Ingush:** the exile journey as the founding story, and visits back as a second journey.
- **Descendants of the 1865 emigrants in Turkey and Jordan:** a journey 160 years deep, told from the far end.
- **Labor migrants in Moscow and the oil towns of western Siberia:** travel as income and its effect on family life.
- **Recent asylum seekers in Austria, Belgium, France and Germany:** leaving under pressure, and contact kept with home.
- **Dzheyrakh highland families:** seasonal moves between valley and mountain, and the old routes over the passes into Georgia.
- **Women:** travel norms (company on the road, study and work away) that may differ sharply by family.
- **Hajj returnees:** the standing that pilgrimage confers.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district:** forced travel over a short distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for deportation, emigration and pilgrimage; mixed for labor migration; thin for leisure travel.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Kazakh, Turkish, Arabic, Georgian; Polish and German for the European route.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asylum seekers and their families risk exposure; travel to Chechen shrines and the border dispute are politically charged; deportation memory needs care.
- **Held for review:** none.
