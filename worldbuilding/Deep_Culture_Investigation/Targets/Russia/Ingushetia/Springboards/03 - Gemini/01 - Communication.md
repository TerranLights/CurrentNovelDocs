# Ingushetia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen and Arabic secondary. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, with the norms and purposes attached to it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders and the young:** respect registers and who speaks first are likely age-graded.
- **Married women in their husbands' households:** check whether, and how far, avoidance speech applies to them, by village and generation.
- **Families schooled in Russian in exile against rural Ingush-first households:** which language carries intimate or formal talk.
- **Mixed Ingush-Chechen villages of the Sunzha valley:** close kin languages, and code choice between neighbors.
- **Sufi brotherhood members and Salafi-leaning worshippers:** differing views on chant, sermon language and religious speech.
- **Deaf Ingush:** a separate channel with its own etiquette.
- **The European diaspora:** voice notes and video calls with home, and the drift of their spoken Ingush.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Ethnography of etiquette is likely available in Russian; recorded conversation data (pauses, turn-taking) is probably thin or absent.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for sermons; Chechen for the Sunzha valley.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family avoidance customs touch private household life; women's speech should be researched with women researchers or sources. Public political speech carries legal risk for speakers since the 2018-2019 protests.
- **Held for review:** none.
