## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Officials and staff in Magas against village households:** distance from the sources of official data.
- **Telegram-reading young adults against elders relying on the mosque and visits:** different trusted channels.
- **Readers of Ingush against Russian-only readers:** which news reaches each, and in which form.
- **Families of the disappeared from the 2000s:** information sought from and refused by authorities.
- **Families displaced from Prigorodny District:** property and civil records lost or held elsewhere.
- **Mountain villages of the Dzheyrakh district:** coverage gaps in signal and print delivery.
- **Exile journalists and activists abroad:** information produced from outside for those inside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Media outlets and NGO reports are plentiful in Russian; reliable audience and trust surveys at the republic level are probably absent (federal polls give country figures only).
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Ingush for local channels; Arabic for rulings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reading or citing outlets labeled "foreign agent" or "undesirable" carries risk inside Russia; identities of sources must be protected. Lineage genealogies can touch feud history and are family property.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. List the republic's registered media in the Roskomnadzor register, filtered by region.
2. Pull Ingushetiyastat's latest regional yearbook and its notes on methods.
3. Search web archives for Ingushetiya.ru (2006-2008) and note its sections.
4. Search eLIBRARY.ru for "СМИ Ингушетии" and "медиапотребление Северный Кавказ".
5. Ask the National Library which Ingush-language press runs it holds.
