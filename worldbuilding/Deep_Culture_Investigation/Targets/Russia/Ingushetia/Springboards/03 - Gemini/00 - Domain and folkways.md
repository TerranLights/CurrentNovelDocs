# Ingushetia: The domain: communication and learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (ГӀалгӀай мотт), Russian; Chechen and Arabic as secondary. **Concept in one line:** the domain of how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech ways, languages in use, neighbors and kin nearby, short journeys and early learning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nazran plain and the lowland villages:** the densest settlement, where most schooling and markets sit.
- **Mountain Ingushetia (Dzheyrakh and Assa gorges, the old Galgai, Tsori, Fyappi and Khamkhi societies):** tower-village heritage and sanctuaries, likely distinct customs of gathering.
- **Malgobek and its oil towns:** a Soviet industrial setting with mixed workforces.
- **Sunzha valley (the former Cossack stanitsas):** Ingush, Chechen and remaining Russian residents side by side.
- **Magas and Karabulak:** new or fast-grown towns with mixed origins.
- **Families from Prigorodny District and the 1992 displacement:** neighbor ties rebuilt after loss.
- **Returnee generations:** those schooled in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan against those born after 1957.
- **Orstkhoi-descended families:** a lineage group between Ingush and Chechen identity.
- **Sufi brotherhoods (Qadiri *virds*) and Salafi-leaning circles:** different settings of religious speech and teaching.
- **Women's and men's spaces of talk; the Deaf community (Russian Sign Language); the diaspora in Europe and Central Asia.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Russian-language scholarship and census data are fairly rich; Ingush-language material and fine-grained surveys are thin. This nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core Ingush lands.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for mixed areas; Arabic for religious texts; some Georgian for medieval sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation, the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict and the Prigorodny question, the 2018-2019 border protests and the prosecution of activists, and independent journalists' safety. Some Russian NGOs and pollsters carry "foreign agent" labels; treat their archives with care for the people named.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Rosstat 2010 and 2020 tables on language knowledge and mother tongue for the Republic of Ingushetia, by district.
2. Search the Russian State Library catalog for "ингушский речевой этикет" and "ингуши этнография".
3. Read Akhriev's essays and the Ingush entries of the *Сборник сведений о кавказских горцах* for the nineteenth-century baseline.
4. Ask the Ingush Research Institute of Humanities and Ingush State University for their folklore and language series lists.
5. Scan *Сердало* and GTRK Ingushetiya program schedules for Ingush-language content by audience.
