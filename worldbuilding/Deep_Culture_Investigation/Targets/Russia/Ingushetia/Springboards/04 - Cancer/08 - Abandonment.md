# Ingushetia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for shared laments and tales. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families who once held mountain seats now empty:** abandonment imposed in 1944 and the duty some feel to return.
- **Divorced mothers and fathers' families in custody disputes:** each side's account of who left whom is needed.
- **Households of shift workers and of migrants in Moscow or the European Union:** the family left at home and its waiting.
- **Children in state care, and children raised by grandparents or uncles:** two routes when parents are gone.
- **Russian families who left Sunzha and the few who stayed:** a community thinned by departure.
- **Chechen families who stayed after the camps closed:** left by others who returned.
- **Elders in the mountain Dzheyrakh villages:** whether the young have moved to the plain and left them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Archives on the 1944 emptying are rich; social data on divorce and orphans exist at federal level; everyday experience of being left needs testimony.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for shared laments; Kazakh for those left in exile.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** custody disputes and divorced mothers' accounts involve real people at risk; deportation trauma; religious and customary rules on divorce and custody go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit). Death and grief proper belong to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none.
