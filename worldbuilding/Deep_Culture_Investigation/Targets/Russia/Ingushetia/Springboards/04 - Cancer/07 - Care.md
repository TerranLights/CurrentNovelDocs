# Ingushetia: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for religious healing and charity terms. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Multigenerational households with in-married women and nuclear households in Magas or Nazran apartments:** who does the daily work of caring for elders.
- **Families caring for members disabled in the conflicts of the 1990s-2000s:** long-term care without much outside help.
- **Elders whose children work in Moscow or abroad:** care sent by money and phone.
- **Families who turn to religious healers and families who rely on clinics only:** two models of tending the sick.
- **Russian elders in the Sunzha district:** whether care comes from kin, neighbors or state services, as a question.
- **Chechen households in the republic:** shared mourning and visiting customs with their own variations.
- **Mountain Dzheyrakh villages:** distance from hospitals and pharmacies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Federal statistics exist but are thin for this subject; everyday care sits in households and in Ingush talk.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for religious healing texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** illness, disability and mental illness are private; religious healing is contested between currents; the burden on daughters-in-law is a sensitive question to ask, not assume. Inheritance tied to care goes to Scorpio; any rules to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
