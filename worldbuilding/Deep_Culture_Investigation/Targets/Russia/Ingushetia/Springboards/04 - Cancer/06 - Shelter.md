# Ingushetia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for the displaced of the Chechen wars; Kazakh for the exile years. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Host villages near Sleptsovskaya (now Sunzha) and Karabulak in 1999-2004:** places that took in camps and those that took families into homes.
- **Prigorodny forced migrants still in compact settlements or rehoused:** shelter that became a long wait.
- **Chechen families who stayed on in Ingushetia after the camps closed.**
- **Elders who remember dugouts and barracks in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan:** shelter in exile as a living memory.
- **Shepherd families using summer pastures in the Dzheyrakh mountains:** a seasonal shelter few others know.
- **Mosque communities and religious brotherhoods:** whether they lodge travelers and the stranded.
- **People leaving the region for safety:** those for whom shelter means flight to another country.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for displacement in 1992 and 1999-2004 (many agency reports); thin for everyday lodging of guests and strangers, which needs ethnography and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Chechen; Kazakh and Kyrgyz for exile-era hosts; English for agency reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** people helped by crisis networks are at risk if named; camp closures and returns were contested; the Prigorodny question is a live dispute. Refugee and housing law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
