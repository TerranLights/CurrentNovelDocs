*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain-origin families and lowland town families:** hospitality may be organized differently where houses are far apart and guests rare.
- **Sufi brotherhood circles and Salafi-leaning congregations:** different rules for alms, for feasts at shrines and for giving in the name of the dead.
- **Wealthy donors and households living on transfers:** giving as standing against giving as shared survival.
- **The deportation generation and young adults:** memory of being helped in exile may shape a different ethic of giving.
- **Chechen and Russian neighbors in Sunzha:** whether help crosses community lines, and on what terms.
- **Ingush communities in Kazakhstan and Moscow:** mutual aid among migrants far from kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Hospitality is heavily described in ethnography; measured giving is thin and mostly informal; online appeals are abundant.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic; Georgian for Vakhushti in the original.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fundraising for medical care involves private health details; donations can be linked to political patronage; avoid naming private donors.
- **Held for review:** none.
