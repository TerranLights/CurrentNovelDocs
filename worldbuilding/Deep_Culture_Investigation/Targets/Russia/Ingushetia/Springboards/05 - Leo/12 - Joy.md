# Ingushetia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; Arabic for religious terms of gladness. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how it is named, felt, shown, sought and shared.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families who keep mawlid and families who reject it:** the mawlid dispute may divide neighbors, so joy in worship needs both sides.
- **Children and the old:** Eid sweets and games against the quieter gladness elders describe.
- **Mountain-district households and lowland town households:** outdoor seasonal joy may matter more where life follows the slopes and herds.
- **Families in mourning and their neighbors:** how one household's grief limits others' celebration.
- **Young people in Magas and Nazran:** cafés, malls and car processions as new places of shared fun.
- **The Chechen minority and Russian residents of Sunzha:** separate festival calendars, and whether they join each other's feasts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Celebrations are visible and filmed; measured happiness data at republic level is likely thin or absent; depression is rarely discussed openly.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the mawlid dispute; mental-health stigma; celebratory gunfire is illegal and may draw police attention to people in videos.
- **Held for review:** none.
