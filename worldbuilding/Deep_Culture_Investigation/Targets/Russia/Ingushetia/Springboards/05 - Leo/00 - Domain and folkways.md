*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (ГӀалгӀай мотт, Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ), Russian; Chechen for the Chechen minority and the shared Vainakh vocabulary; Arabic for religious writing. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity, play, pleasure, romance, children, fame, celebration and sport: how people shine, play and take pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland towns (Nazran, Magas, Karabulak) against the mountain districts (Dzheyrakh, Assa valley):** gathering places likely differ between new towns and tower country.
- **The old societies (shahars: Fyappi, Ghalghai, Tsori, Dzheyrakh, Metskhal, Orstkhoy):** regional song, dance and festival forms may survive by society.
- **Families who returned from Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, and those born after 1957:** exile may have reshaped festivals and leisure across generations.
- **Ingush from the Prigorodny district displaced in 1992:** a community whose celebrations moved with it.
- **Chechens of the Sunzha district and the Russian and Cossack residents of the stanitsas:** separate festival calendars inside the republic.
- **Sufi brotherhood followers (Kunta-Hajji and Batal-Hajji lines) and Salafi-leaning youth:** sharply different views of music and dancing to separate, not average.
- **Women and men:** the domain's public spaces are likely divided; weddings, sport and online display need both sides.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. The 19th- and 20th-century ethnography is rich; republic-level survey rows may be missing or thin; online material is plentiful. Russia-wide values surveys likely hold too few Ingush respondents for any breakdown; *check* samples before use. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (its tracker carries "Russia"); these sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Ingushetia.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush and Russian; Chechen for shared vocabulary; Arabic for religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation, the 1992 Prigorodny conflict and the 2018–2019 border protests color any public talk of pride and celebration; religious disputes over music; privacy of wedding footage, especially of women.
- **Held for review:** none.
