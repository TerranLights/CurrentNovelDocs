## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village children and town children:** fields, rivers and streets against courtyards and new playgrounds may yield different games.
- **Boys and girls:** horse, ball and wrestling play against games kept at home, a split to measure rather than assume.
- **Older men at backgammon and chess, and young men online:** two generations of adult play.
- **Families who follow strict religious rulings and secular families:** different lines on music, cards and betting.
- **Children of the mountain districts:** games tied to slopes, snow and herding may survive there.
- **Ingush children in Kazakhstan and Europe:** play shaped by host-country schools and how it mixes with heritage games.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folk-game ethnography exists; current children's play is thinly studied; online play is visible but hard to measure.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for rulings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling and betting are religiously condemned and may be hidden; photographing children at play needs consent; avoid naming minors from online videos.
- **Held for review:** none.
