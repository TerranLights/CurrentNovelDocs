*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of the mountain societies (Dzheyrakh, Ghalghai, Tsori, Fyappi):** tower and stone traditions are tied to particular valleys, so making may be remembered locally.
- **Women and men makers:** felt and embroidery against metal, stone and woodwork; different paths of teaching and credit.
- **Soviet-trained professionals and self-taught makers:** the art school and union against the family workshop.
- **Sufi brotherhood families and Salafi-leaning circles:** likely to judge music, images and new forms differently.
- **Artists and writers raised in Kazakhstan exile:** making shaped by another landscape and by Russian-language schooling.
- **Cossack and Russian craft in the Sunzha stanitsas:** a separate tradition inside the republic.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on medieval architecture and literature; thin on everyday improvising and on crafts' current economics; online material is large but unsorted.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for the innovation debate; some Georgian for the medieval churches' scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heritage sites in border areas; archives lost in the Chechen wars; religious disputes over images and music.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian State Library catalog for Krupnov 1971 and Semyonov's Ingush reports.
2. Look up кхолла, говза and исбаьхьалла in Nichols's and Kurkiev's dictionaries.
3. Search "ингушский войлок истанг" and "золотое шитье ингушей" for craft studies.
4. Find the 7-НК tables for "Республика Ингушетия" on the Ministry of Culture's statistics pages.
5. Search "бидаӀат" with "ингуш" in Telegram and on the Muftiate site.
