# Ingushetia: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; Arabic for religious titles. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Religious honorees (hajjis, ustaz, Sufi sheikhs' families) and holders of state titles:** two separate ladders of recognition that may not rank people the same way.
- **Women whose hosting, care and mourning work runs the tezet and the wedding:** labor that may go unthanked in public lists.
- **Returnees and their children against families who lived through the deportation:** who is counted among the honored survivors.
- **Displaced Prigorodny families:** recognition of loss is itself a contested claim.
- **Chechen residents of Sunzha:** whether they appear on the republic's honors lists and memorial rolls.
- **Diaspora leaders in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan:** honored by host states in ways the home republic may or may not echo.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** fairly rich for state honors and commemoration (published lists); thin for everyday credit and workplace recognition.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for titles; Kazakh or Russian for diaspora honors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation victims' names and family records; toponyms in disputed areas; honors linked to security-service careers.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the republic's official portal for "указ о награждении" and sample one year.
2. Search "Книга памяти депортированных ингушей" for compilers and holdings.
3. Search "переименование сел Ингушетии 1944" for lists of renamed villages.
4. Look up "Ingush" in the UNESCO endangered-languages atlas.
5. Search CyberLeninka for "тезет ингуши" and "обычаи гостеприимства порядок".
