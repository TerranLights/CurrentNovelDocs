# Ingushetia: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; Arabic for religious terms of modesty. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brides and young wives against older women:** avoidance rules weigh most on the newly married, and relax with age.
- **Young urban social-media users in Magas and Nazran against village elders:** different stages and different audiences for showing the self.
- **Zikr participants and Salafi-leaning believers:** the first allows loud ritual expression, the second may reject it.
- **Protest participants from 2018–2019 and their families:** political voice and its costs.
- **Emigrants in the European Union:** a test of how much restraint travels.
- **Chechen families of the Sunzha district and Russian residents:** other display rules inside the same towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Restraint is well described in ethnography; personal expression today shows mostly online and in news, and needs careful sampling.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen; some French and German for diaspora material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest prosecutions and the risk to people named online; women's dress and images; the killing of an opposition site's owner (2008) means some voices are anonymous on purpose; no names of private people should be copied into notes.
- **Held for review:** none.
