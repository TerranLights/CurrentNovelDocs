# Ingushetia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for sermons on strife; Chechen for the refugee and Sunzha literature. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Residents of planned Magas** against **old Nazran and the villages**: different built orders and different complaints about disorder.
- **Families displaced from the Prigorodny district** and **Chechen refugees who stayed**: order lost and rebuilt after violence.
- **Households of police and security officers** against **neighbors of counterterrorist operations**: one order seen from two sides.
- **Young men at weddings and festivals** against **elders and officials** who curb them: where festive disorder is allowed.
- **Women who keep the home's order** against **men who keep public order**: whether these are separate domains is to be researched.
- **Mountain villages, now mostly emptied or seasonal**, against lowland towns, and the **remaining Russian and Cossack-descended residents** of Sunzha district.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; pre-modern cosmology and conflict history are rich; crime and safety figures exist but are unreliable for this republic.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic, Chechen.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** counterterrorism operations, the 1992 violence and refugee experiences are traumatic and politically charged; protect informants. Old sanctuary rites may still be family matters.
- **Held for review:** none.
