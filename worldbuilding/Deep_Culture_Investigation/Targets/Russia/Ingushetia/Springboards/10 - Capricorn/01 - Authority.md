# Ingushetia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Chechen for Sunzha district; Arabic for Sufi and Salafi teaching. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State officials and security staff** against **clan elders**: two chains of command over the same family, and people who belong to both.
- **Sufi murids** against **Salafi-leaning young believers**: authority carried by a living sheikh against authority read from the text.
- **Women inside the household** (the mother-in-law, the eldest wife): authority over the home runs on other lines than authority in public.
- **The 1944-1957 exile generation** against those raised after 1992: whether the Soviet and post-Soviet state ever held voluntary acceptance.
- **Chechen families of Sunzha district**: a neighboring republic's leader is a second authority in their lives.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district**, governed by North Ossetian officials, and **European diasporas** outside the Russian state's reach.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in news and rights reporting since 2007, thin in surveys; old ethnography is outsider-heavy.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for sermons; Chechen for shared Sufi texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2019 prosecution of protest leaders, security operations and rival religious authorities are live and risky topics; informants' names stay out. Sufi inner teaching may be restricted to initiates.
- **Held for review:** none.
