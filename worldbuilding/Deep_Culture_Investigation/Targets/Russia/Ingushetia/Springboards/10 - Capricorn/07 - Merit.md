# Ingushetia: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for religious contests; French, German and Belgian Dutch for diaspora credential recognition. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Large, well-placed lineages** against **small lineages**: the reported weight of kin in getting a post may fall unevenly.
- **Graduates of Moscow and St. Petersburg universities** against **local graduates**: whether an outside degree is read as merit or as a family's means.
- **Athletes**, whose results are public, against **office workers**, whose merit is harder to see.
- **Women** against **men** in teaching, medicine and the civil service: who is credited for the same work.
- **Religious students** (Quran memorizers, graduates of foreign Islamic schools) against **secular graduates**: two separate ladders of deserved standing.
- **Chechen, Russian and Cossack-descended residents** of Sunzha district, and **European diaspora** professionals retraining abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; awards, sports and contest results are easy to find; evidence on paid posts and nepotism is anecdotal or partisan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ingush, Arabic; French, German and Dutch for diaspora cases.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims of bought posts or diplomas can defame named people and whole clans; record them as claims with sources only.
- **Held for review:** none.
