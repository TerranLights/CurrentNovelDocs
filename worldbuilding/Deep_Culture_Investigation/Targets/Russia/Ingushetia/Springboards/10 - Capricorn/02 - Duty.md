# Ingushetia: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush, Russian; Arabic for religious obligation; Chechen for shared customs in Sunzha district. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The son who keeps the parental house** against his brothers, and **sons** against **daughters**: whether care and inheritance duties fall on one child is a question to settle in the data.
- **New daughters-in-law** against **older women of the household**: the duties of a bride and how they change with years and children.
- **Mountain-origin clans** with ancestral towers and crypts against **lowland families**: duties to an ancestral place may differ.
- **Sufi-observing** and **Salafi-leaning** households: they dispute which mourning and memorial duties are owed.
- **Men in Russian military, police or contract service** against those who avoid it: service to the federal state as duty or as a job.
- **The Kazakhstan and Moscow diasporas** and **Prigorodny families**: duties to the homeland and to lost villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; family and mourning customs are well covered in Russian-language ethnography; measured data on caregiving or volunteering for the republic is likely thin.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush, Russian, Arabic; Kazakh and Turkish for diasporas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blood-revenge obligations belong under Order and Law (this sign); disputes over mourning customs can be sharp; family inheritance is private.
- **Held for review:** none.
