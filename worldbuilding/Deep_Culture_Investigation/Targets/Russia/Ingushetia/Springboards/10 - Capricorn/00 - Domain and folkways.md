# Ingushetia: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ingushetia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ingush (Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ), Russian; Chechen for the mixed eastern districts; Arabic for Islamic sources. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys and what is owed, through career, status, reputation and the age, time, rank, order and power folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The old mountain societies (Fyappi, Dzheyrakh, Khamkhi, Tsori, Orstkhoy)** against lowland **Nazran**: herding calendars and tower-house seniority against a market town's rhythm.
- **Lowland towns (Nazran, Magas, Karabulak, Malgobek)** against hill villages: salaried careers, clock time and state titles may weigh differently.
- **Sunzha district's former Cossack stanitsas (Ordzhonikidzevskaya, Troitskaya, Nesterovskaya)** and their Chechen and Ingush residents: Orthodox and Muslim feast calendars on one ground.
- **Kunta-Haji and Batal-Haji wird members (Qadiriyya), Naqshbandis, the official Muftiate and Salafi-leaning youth**: who sets the religious year and whose word on duty counts.
- **Survivors of the 1944-1957 exile** to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan against those born after return: whether exile lived through adds to an elder's standing.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district and Vladikavkaz** (in North Ossetia), and diasporas in Kazakhstan, Moscow, Turkey, Jordan and Western Europe: careers and offices on another republic's or country's ladders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for 19th-century ethnography, clan structure and Soviet-era folklore; thin for measures, since national surveys rarely sample the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Ingush and Russian; Chechen for shared sources; Arabic for Islamic writing; Turkish and Kazakh for diasporas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict, the 2018 boundary deal with Chechnya and the jailing of protest leaders, and the killing of the Ingushetiya.ru founder (2008) are live matters; protect informants; cite clan names only as published. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the tracker carries "Russia"); this pass writes for the core republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
