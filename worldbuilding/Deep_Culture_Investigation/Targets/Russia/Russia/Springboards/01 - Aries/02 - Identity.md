## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ethnic Russians in the capitals, the provinces and the North** (Pomors, Old Believers of the Russian North): layered regional self-names.
- **The carried Ukrainian lands:** Galicia and Volhynia; the center around Kyiv; the Russian-speaking east and south; each kept apart, since language and declared identity diverge.
- **Crimean Tatars; Karaites and Krymchaks:** Crimean peoples with separate histories.
- **Greeks of the Azov coast (Rumei and Urum); Bulgarians and Gagauz of the southwest; Hungarians and Rusyns of Transcarpathia:** border minorities with kin states abroad.
- **Karelians, Veps, Izhorians, Votes, Ingrian Finns; Komi; Nenets; Kola Sami:** Finno-Ugric and Arctic peoples, each with its own revival movement.
- **Jews, Roma, Russian Germans:** diaspora identities shaped by Soviet categories and emigration.
- **Tatars and other Volga and Caucasus diasporas in Moscow; Central Asian labor migrants:** identity away from a homeland.
- **Generations and mixed families:** Soviet-born against post-Soviet; children of mixed marriages choosing a nationality.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich and abundant, but much of it is political. Identity is the most contested topic in this nation's lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian essential; Old Russian for the chronicles; minority languages for the small peoples' own voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Russian-Ukrainian identity, Crimea, and the "Russian world" idea are war-charged; read every source for its side and date. Crimean Tatar institutions face bans in Russia. Heritage and tradition belong to Capricorn, belonging to Cancer; keep them as neighbors.
- **Held for review:** none.
