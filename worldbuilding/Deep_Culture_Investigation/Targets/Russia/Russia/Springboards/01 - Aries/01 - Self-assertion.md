## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow office culture** against small-town and village life: assertion at work may follow imported scripts in one and older ones in the other.
- **Men and women; older and younger generations:** who may raise a voice in the family, and who is told to keep quiet.
- **Conscripts, prison-experienced men and street groups:** subcultures with explicit codes of standing one's ground.
- **The carried Ukrainian lands:** the Cossack heritage of the south and center; Galicia; the industrial east.
- **Odesa and other Jewish urban milieus:** a distinct humor and talk tradition.
- **Crimean Tatars:** collective claim-making by a deported and returned people.
- **Old Believers:** a long tradition of refusal on principle.
- **Karelians, Komi and Nenets:** check whether ethnographies describe quieter speech norms, and avoid assuming them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for speech, complaint letters and media; mixed for survey measures; thin for the minority peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Old Russian; Yiddish and Crimean Tatar for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hazing, prison codes and protest are sensitive topics in the current climate; disputes with authorities touch the law, so the legal side goes to Libra (the letter) and Capricorn (obedience). Hierarchy and politeness belong to Capricorn and Gemini; keep them as neighbors here.
- **Held for review:** none.
