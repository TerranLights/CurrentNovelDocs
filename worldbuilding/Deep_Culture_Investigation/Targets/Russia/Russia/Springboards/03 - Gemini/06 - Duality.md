# Russia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic for the diglossia question; Yiddish and Tatar for minority strands. **Concept in one line:** how a culture lives with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how it treats people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ukrainians in Russian-speaking and Ukrainian-speaking settings**, if carried: daily switching, and mixed families across the line.
- **Children of mixed Russian-Ukrainian marriages:** belonging to both, and pressure to choose.
- **Old Believers and the official church:** the 17th c. schism as a lasting pair.
- **Karelians and Veps:** between Finnic heritage and Russian daily life.
- **Sami and Nenets:** Indigenous and state identities.
- **Jews, Tatars and Crimean Tatars:** minority identity held with a Russian or Ukrainian one.
- **Former Soviet professionals:** official and private selves before and after 1991.
- **Migrants:** two homes, two languages, two calendars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theory and literature; mixed for measured identity; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Yiddish, Tatar, Crimean Tatar, Finnic and Sami languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Russian-Ukrainian identity, mixed speech and "two Ukraines" are deeply political; surzhyk is often a slur on its speakers; many big theses here (dual models, dual faith, double-think) are contested and must not become a national portrait.
- **Held for review:** none.
