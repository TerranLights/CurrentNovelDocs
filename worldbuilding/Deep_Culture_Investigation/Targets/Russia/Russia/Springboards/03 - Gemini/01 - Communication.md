# Russia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; minority languages for their own speakers. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow against provincial towns:** speech pace and the use of first names against name and patronymic may differ.
- **Older and younger generations:** Soviet address forms ("товарищ", "гражданин") against post-Soviet gaps where no neutral address exists.
- **Village speakers of northern dialects:** greetings to strangers and the order of talk at gatherings.
- **Ukrainian-speaking and Russian-speaking Ukrainians**, and speakers of the mixed speech between them, if the row carries Ukrainian lands.
- **Odesa and the south:** a multilingual port speech with its own humor.
- **Finnic and Arctic peoples** (Karelians, Veps, Sami, Nenets): their own norms of silence and turn.
- **Crimean Tatars and Tatars in the cities**, **Jews**, **Roma**: each with a home language and its own etiquette.
- **Deaf signers**, and children who interpret for migrant parents from Central Asia and the Caucasus.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian (a long Soviet and post-Soviet field of speech etiquette and pragmatics); mixed for Ukrainian; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic for church texts; Tatar, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Finnic and Sami languages for minority strands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** which language one speaks in public, and to whom, is politically loaded between Russian and Ukrainian; avoid any single "Russian style" (the sources themselves often generalize); the law on official languages belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
