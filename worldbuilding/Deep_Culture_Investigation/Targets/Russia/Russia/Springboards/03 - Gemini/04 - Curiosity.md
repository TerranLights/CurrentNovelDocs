# Russia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic for monastic texts; Yiddish and Tatar for minority strands. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore; and how far a culture encourages, channels, limits or punishes it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban intelligentsia families:** hobby circles, magazines and quiz clubs as household habits.
- **Village families:** curiosity about neighbors and strangers in a small place, and its limits.
- **Science towns** (наукограды such as Dubna, Obninsk, Pushchino): curiosity as a profession and a town identity.
- **Old Believers and devout Orthodox households:** teaching on vain curiosity.
- **Ukrainian regions**, if carried: school research culture and folklore of the center and west against the east.
- **Karelians, Veps, Sami and Nenets:** peoples long studied by outsiders; what they allow to be asked.
- **Jewish families:** study culture and Soviet science careers.
- **Collectors and kraevedy (local-lore enthusiasts)** in provincial towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science popularization, folklore and children's literature; thin for small peoples' own views on asking.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic, Yiddish, Tatar; Finnic and Sami languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnographic collections hold objects and remains of other peoples, and their return is a live question; some knowledge of Sami, Nenets and Karelian peoples is not open to every asker; asking about past repression can still carry risk.
- **Held for review:** none.
