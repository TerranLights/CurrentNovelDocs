## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow and the central provinces** against **St. Petersburg and the Northwest**: the two capitals' speech norms and neighborhood life differ.
- **The Russian North** (Arkhangelsk, Vologda, the Pomors): okanye dialects, older village visiting customs.
- **Southern Russian provinces** (Kursk, Voronezh, Belgorod): akanye speech shading into Ukrainian.
- **Old Believer communities**: separate reading traditions and church-script literacy.
- **Finnic peoples of the Northwest**: Karelians, Veps, Izhorians, Votes, Ingrian Finns; small languages under pressure.
- **Sami of the Kola Peninsula** and **Nenets** of the far north: Indigenous languages, oral tradition, boarding-school histories.
- **Ukrainian regions**, if the row carries them: Kyiv and the center; Galicia and Volhynia; Zakarpattia (Rusyns, Hungarians); Bukovina (Romanians); the south (Odesa, Bessarabian Bulgarians, Azov Greeks); the Russian-speaking east; Crimea (Crimean Tatars, Karaites, Krymchaks).
- **Jews** (Yiddish past, Russian present), **Roma**, and **migrants from Central Asia and the Caucasus** in the big cities.
- **Deaf communities**, signing Russian or Ukrainian Sign Language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian and Ukrainian (censuses, corpora, a deep tradition of dialectology and pedagogy); thin for the small Finnic and Arctic peoples. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled: the header carries both Russia and Ukraine, so where the line with the Ukraine row runs decides which Ukrainian strands belong here.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian throughout; Church Slavonic and Old Russian for pre-modern sources; minority languages for their own communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language use between Russian and Ukrainian is politically charged, and answers to language questions shift with events; some research bodies are legally labeled in Russia and their sites may be blocked; archive access varies; Sami and Nenets sacred places and stories are theirs to share, and boarding-school memories are told in their voices first.
- **Held for review:** none.
