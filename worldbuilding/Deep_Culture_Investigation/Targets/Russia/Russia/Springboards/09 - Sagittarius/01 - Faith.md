# Russia (core state): Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Crimean Tatar, Komi, Khanty, Buryat, Yiddish for community sources. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Churched against nominal Orthodox**: the gap the surveys keep finding needs separating everywhere.
- **Post-1991 adult converts** against families who kept faith through Soviet years (the older women who kept churches alive).
- **Old Believers**, priested and priestless: faith kept by refusing reform.
- **Greek Catholics of Galicia** and the **Protestant belt** of Volhynia and Rivne: underground memory and a different piety.
- **Crimean Tatars**: faith tied to return from deportation.
- **Komi** and **Karelians**: Orthodox with older layers.
- **Khanty, Mansi, Nenets**: sacred sites and sacrifices alongside or instead of Orthodoxy.
- **Buryats**: Buddhism and shamanic practice side by side.
- **Jews**: Chabad-linked and other communities in Moscow and St. Petersburg.
- **Urban professionals** with secular faith in science or progress.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodoxy; mixed for Ukraine's churches; thin and partly restricted for Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Buryat and Khanty for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Moscow Patriarchate's place in the war and Ukraine's measures against its branch (law: Libra or Capricorn); Indigenous sacred sites and rites that communities keep private; Soviet persecution and martyrdom.
- **Held for review:** none.

4. Look up PSTGU's sociology of religion publications.
5. Search RISU and the Lviv archive for "катакомбна церква спогади".
