## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believer and merchant milieus**: the reputation of a merchant's word.
- **Soviet-era workers** and today's employees: pilfering and its justifications.
- **Students and teachers**: copying and its tolerance.
- **Officials and citizens**: everyday bribes as each side tells it.
- **Ukrainian lands**: anti-corruption civic movements, east and west.
- **Crimean Tatars** and **Jews**: trading traditions and their own codes.
- **Rural villages** and **big cities**: honor systems, locks and trust among strangers.
- **Northern peoples** (Nenets, Komi): exchange with traders, as their accounts tell it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corruption and *blat* studies, literature and satire; mixed for behavioral measures; thin for minorities' own codes.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish, Tatar, Crimean Tatar for community proverbs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption talk risks stereotyping a people; keep it to measured variation. Anti-corruption investigators and NGOs face pressure; corruption law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
