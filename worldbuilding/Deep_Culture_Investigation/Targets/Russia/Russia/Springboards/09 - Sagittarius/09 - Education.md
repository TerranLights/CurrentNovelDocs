## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow and St. Petersburg university families** (the intelligentsia's heirs) against first-generation students from small towns.
- **Vocational-school graduates and skilled workers**: the trades' standing.
- **Rural schools** of the North and center, many closing or merged.
- **Ukrainian lands**: Ukrainian-language schooling, west and east, and the national test.
- **Crimean Tatars**: reform schooling past and Crimean Tatar-language schooling now.
- **Jews**: traditional religious schools and the push into higher education.
- **Nenets, Evenki, Komi, Khanty**: boarding schools and native-language teaching.
- **Old Believers**: home and manuscript learning outside state schools.
- **Orthodox schools and seminaries** against secular ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and statistics; mixed for families' own views; thin and painful for Indigenous schooling.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Nenets, Evenki for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school memory; the language of instruction is fiercely disputed in both pre-war countries; schooling in occupied areas; school and language law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
