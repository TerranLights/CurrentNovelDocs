*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Academic and scientific families** (selective schools, olympiads) against workers' and rural families.
- **Moscow against provincial universities:** where the ladder starts.
- **Ukrainian regions** and the post-2014 reformers against older administrative networks.
- **Jewish families** with memory of quotas; **Crimean Tatars** with deportation-era exclusion.
- **Sami, Nenets, Komi, Karelians** under quota policies, as each people describes it.
- **Military and security careers,** where seniority and patronage may weigh differently.
- **Women in science and management,** against the male track, to research.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet history and honors; mixed for opinion data on merit; thin for minorities' views.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar; 16th-c. Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antisemitic admissions practices and nationality quotas are painful topics; use survivors' and scholars' accounts. Investigations of officials' families can be legally risky to republish in Russia; some outlets are banned there.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Peresvetov's petitions in a modern edition with commentary.
2. Search FOM and VTsIOM archives for «социальные лифты» and «по знакомству»; note years and wording.
3. Locate the "Ukrainian Society" monitoring volumes and the success items.
4. Search the Russian National Corpus for «по заслугам», «кумовство» and «социальный лифт», by decade.
5. Read Martin (2001), chapters on Ukraine, before any study of quotas.
