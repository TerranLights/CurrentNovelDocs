# Russia (core state): Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar, Karelian, Komi, Sami for minority layers; Church Slavonic for the old layer. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow professionals against small-town and rural central Russia:** the distance to local officials differs.
- **Ukrainian lands by historical region** (Hetmanate left bank, Galicia, Transcarpathia, the south): different imperial and church authorities behind each.
- **Orthodox (two jurisdictions), Greek Catholics, Old Believers, Protestants, Muslims (Crimean Tatars), Jews:** whose religious authority counts.
- **Crimean Tatars** with their own assembly; **Kola Sami and Nenets** with herding and kin authority; **Karelians, Veps, Komi** village structures.
- **Military families and the security services** against civilians.
- **Generations:** those raised under Soviet discipline against the post-Soviet young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for polls and political history; mixed for minorities; legal texts are full and searchable.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar; Church Slavonic for older sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** answering polls about power is risky in wartime; treat answers with care and note fieldwork dates. The Mejlis is banned in Russia; Crimean Tatar sources should be heard in their own voice. Criminal-world authority (vory) is a real subject but sensational press dominates it.
- **Held for review:** none.
