# Russia (core state): Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar for the deportation-memory layer; Old East Slavic for the earliest legal layer. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Corporate Moscow and St. Petersburg** (KPI culture, "ownership" language) against state offices and factories.
- **Village central Russia and the North:** commune memory, mutual help and blame.
- **Ukrainian regions,** including families from the Chernobyl zone resettled elsewhere.
- **Crimean Tatars,** for whom collective blame was a deportation charge.
- **Conscripts' families and veterans:** accountability of the army.
- **Old Believers and Protestant congregations:** church discipline and confession as answering.
- **Karelians, Komi, Sami, Nenets:** kin and brigade answerability, as each people describes it.
- **Women and men** in households: the split of the mental load, to research.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, memory politics and Soviet practice; mixed for surveys on personal responsibility; thin for minority-language terms.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Old East Slavic for *Russkaya Pravda*; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memory of repression and of the Holodomor is legally and politically charged; Memorial is liquidated and its archives are hard to reach. Speech about the war since 2022 is legally restricted in Russia, so Russian sources on blame for it will be skewed.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian National Corpus for «круговая порука», «стрелочник» and «крайний», by decade.
2. Locate the USK questionnaire and any regional norms published with it.
3. Read *Russkaya Pravda* (Short and Expanded) on *vira* with a scholarly commentary.
4. Search Schetnaya palata reports for «ответственность должностных лиц», then NABU annual reports for the Ukrainian side.
5. Search Ukrainian sources for «моя хата скраю» in press commentary to see how the proverb is used.
