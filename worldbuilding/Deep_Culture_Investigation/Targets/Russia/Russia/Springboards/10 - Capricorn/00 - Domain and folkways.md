## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow and St. Petersburg** against provincial central Russia: capital service culture against small towns.
- **The Russian North:** Pomors and Old Believer communities, with a different land-tenure past from the estate center (*check*).
- **Ukrainian lands:** Cossack Hetmanate and Zaporozhian traditions of elected command; Galicia and Transcarpathia (Habsburg past); the imperially settled east and south.
- **Crimean Tatars,** with their own pre-imperial khanate and their deportation history.
- **Karelians, Veps, Izhorians and Votes, Seto, Komi, Sami, Nenets:** village, kin and herding-brigade authority, as each people describes it.
- **Rusyns, Hutsuls and Boikos; Hungarians, Romanians, Bulgarians, Gagauz, Azov Greeks (Urum and Rumei):** other states' rank traditions.
- **Jewish communities, Karaites and Krymchaks; Roma;** labor migrants from the Caucasus and Central Asia in the big cities.
- **Generations:** Soviet-educated against post-1991 cohorts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian and Ukrainian national data and imperial history; mixed for the Finnic and Arctic peoples; thin for Crimean Tatar sources in survey form. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (its header carries Russia and Ukraine, and the line with Ukraine is open), so these sheets are written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian are essential; Old East Slavic and Church Slavonic for the pre-modern layer; Crimean Tatar; Karelian and Komi help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survey answers about power in Russia since 2022 carry fear bias; Levada and Memorial carry official labels (Memorial liquidated 2021). Russian-Ukrainian framing is contested; keep both sides' sources apart. Crimean Tatar and Arctic Indigenous knowledge on the peoples' own terms.
- **Held for review:** none.
