# Russia (core state): Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; Karelian and Finnish for the rune-song layer; Crimean Tatar; Nenets and Sami for sacred-site rules. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow** (heavy city improvement) against **provincial and single-industry towns.**
- **St. Petersburg,** a planned city, against old Moscow and old Kyiv.
- **Old Believers** against the main church: the rite as order.
- **Ukrainian regions:** the Maidan generation against older residents; front-line and evacuated communities.
- **Crimean Tatars:** order kept by the community after return and in later years.
- **Karelians, Veps, Komi, Sami, Nenets:** order in the land and the household.
- **Youth subcultures** (football fans, punks) against older residents.
- **Labor migrants,** and how "order" campaigns touch them, to research.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history, literature and urban policy; mixed for safety and disorder surveys; thin for Arctic world-views in Russian-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic, Karelian (Finnish helps), Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "restoring order" is a political slogan in Russia and a contested one in Ukraine; protests and their policing are legally risky to research inside Russia. Sacred sites of the Northern peoples may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian National Corpus for «навести порядок», «бардак» and «смута», by decade.
2. Read Palitsyn's *Skazanie* beside Dunning's chapters on the same years.
3. Download the Russian Crime Victimization Survey report; find the safety and police-contact items.
4. Search Ukrainian sources for «Руїна» in school textbooks and the press.
5. Search Moscow press archives for «Моя улица» and «снос киосков» to collect both sides.
