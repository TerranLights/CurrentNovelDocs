# Russia (core state): Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar, Nenets, Yiddish, Seto for minority self-names and group words. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-stock city dwellers** against **incomers** in Moscow and St. Petersburg (from provinces, the Caucasus and Central Asia).
- **Villages and small towns** of the North and center, where "local" may mean generations.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galicia, the center, the Russian-speaking east and south; Polissia's *tuteshni*; Transcarpathian Rusyns and Hungarians; Bukovina Romanians.
- **Crimean Tatars**, **Karaites**, **Krymchaks:** small peoples with sharp membership lines.
- **Nenets**, **Setos**, **Karelians and Veps:** Indigenous and Finnic belonging to land and kin.
- **Old Believers** and other closed religious communities; **Jewish communities** before and after emigration.
- **Returnees and Soviet-era internal migrants** whose "home" is split.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national-identity surveys and Soviet history; mixed for local belonging; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar; Yiddish for diaspora records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Russian-Ukrainian identity is a war issue; Crimean Tatar bodies banned in Russia; ethnic slurs about newcomers; minority membership disputes. Name variation, never one voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
