# Russia (core state): Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish, Crimean Tatar, Nenets for their families; Old East Slavic for kin vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian villages of the North and the center** against **Moscow and St. Petersburg**, where kin networks thin.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galicia (Greek Catholic), the center and east (Orthodox), the steppe south; Carpathian Hutsul and Boyko families; Transcarpathian Rusyns and Hungarians.
- **Old Believers:** closed, religious kin networks.
- **Jewish families**, then and now; **Karaites** and **Krymchaks**, very small and kin-bound.
- **Crimean Tatars:** extended kin and family memory of deportation.
- **Azov Greeks**, **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz**: separate kin customs.
- **Nenets:** clan exogamy and herding families.
- **Karelians, Veps, Setos:** Finnic kin terms and border families.
- **Mixed Russian-Ukrainian families** split by politics and war.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; strong survey, literary and historical-demography material; minority kin systems thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish; Crimean Tatar; reading of pre-reform spelling for 19th-century sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "traditional family values" as state policy; families divided by war and politics; repression records (some archives restricted); same-sex families under Russian law (no law research here; see Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
