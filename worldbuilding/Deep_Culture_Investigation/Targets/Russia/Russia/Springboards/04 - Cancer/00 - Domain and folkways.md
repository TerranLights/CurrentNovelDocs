*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia; pre-war lands carried: Russia and Ukraine. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian and Ukrainian (both Cyrillic); Crimean Tatar, Karelian, Veps, Komi, Nenets, Yiddish, Rusyn, Hungarian and Romanian for minorities; Church Slavonic and Old East Slavic for pre-modern texts. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family: household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Russian North** (Arkhangelsk, Vologda, Pomor coast): large northern farmhouses; distinct building and kin ways.
- **Central Russia and the Black Earth belt:** the peasant commune household and its Soviet breakup.
- **The two capitals:** communal flats, small families and the dacha as a second home.
- **Ukrainian regions separately:** Polissia, Podillia, Sloboda Ukraine, the steppe south, Galicia and the Carpathians (Hutsuls, Boykos, Lemkos), Transcarpathian Rusyns.
- **Finnic peoples:** Karelians, Veps, Izhorians, Votes, Setos of the Pskov border; Komi if their lands fall inside.
- **Nenets** of the Arkhangelsk tundra: mobile dwellings and herding families.
- **Crimean Tatars:** deportation in 1944 and return.
- **Jews, Karaites, Krymchaks, Azov Greeks, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Transcarpathian Hungarians, Bukovina Romanians, Poles:** separate family systems and memory.
- **Orthodox, Old Believer, Greek Catholic and secular families:** religious divides in household practice.
- **Caucasus and Central Asian migrants** in the cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian and Ukrainian; thinner for Finnic and Tatar groups. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (above all the line through Ukraine); the Cancer sheets are written for the core Russian and Ukrainian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian first; Church Slavonic reading for pre-modern texts; Crimean Tatar, Karelian, Yiddish for their groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportations, famine (Holodomor), Gulag and wartime separations sit inside family memory; current-war displacement; politicized "traditional family values" rhetoric; some archives closed or reorganized.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
