## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Capital dwellers** in communal flats, Soviet blocks and new towers, against **small-town and village** households in wooden houses.
- **The Russian North and Pomors:** the large house with yard under one roof.
- **Ukrainian regions:** the whitewashed *khata* of the center, Polissia log houses, Carpathian farmsteads of Hutsuls and Boykos, Transcarpathian Rusyn and Hungarian villages.
- **Crimean Tatars:** homes rebuilt after return, often self-built.
- **Nenets herders** and settled Nenets.
- **Karelians and Veps** in lake villages; **Setos** on the Pskov border.
- **Old Believer villages**, and **Jewish families** whose old town homes (*shtetl*) are now memory sites.
- **Displaced people:** refugees from war, Chernobyl zone evacuees, the homeless.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban housing and Soviet history; folk belief material rich; Nenets, Seto and Crimean Tatar material thinner, partly in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar; Nenets and Komi for local terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** loss of homes to war, deportation and evacuation; Crimea's status; registration disputes; homelessness stigma.
- **Held for review:** none.
