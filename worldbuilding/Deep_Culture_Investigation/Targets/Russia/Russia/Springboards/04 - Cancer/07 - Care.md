# Russia (core state): Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic for monastic healing texts; Yiddish and Hebrew terms for Jewish care societies. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daughters and daughters-in-law** as carers against **paid carers** (often migrant women) in the big cities.
- **Village elders living alone** in the North and center, depending on neighbors and social workers.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Polissia healers; Galician church charity; the industrial east.
- **Orthodox church charity** against **secular charities and the state**.
- **Jewish community care** (Hesed); **Old Believer** mutual care.
- **Crimean Tatar** families: care of elders at home.
- **People with disabilities** in institutions against those cared for at home.
- **Men as carers**: rarely studied; worth a separate look.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church and nursing history and the hospice movement; mixed for family care (little survey data on carers); thin for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; some Yiddish and Hebrew.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conditions in residential institutions; care of the wounded and of war-disabled; folk healing (respect healers' own limits on what they share).
- **Held for review:** none.
