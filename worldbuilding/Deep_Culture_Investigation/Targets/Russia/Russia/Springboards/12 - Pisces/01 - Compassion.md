# Russia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ukrainian; Tatar, Buryat and Sakha for minority material. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox parish charity against secular foundations:** different reasons given for helping.
- **Big cities against villages and small towns:** organized giving against neighbors' help "всем миром" (by the whole community).
- **Muslim communities** (Tatars, Bashkirs, North Caucasus peoples where carried): sadaqa and zakat as duty.
- **Buddhist Buryats, Kalmyks and Tuvans:** compassion in monastic teaching and lay practice.
- **Old Believer communities:** closed mutual aid.
- **Ukrainian lands:** the volunteer movement, Greek Catholic charity (Caritas) in the west, Orthodox parishes elsewhere.
- **Northern and Siberian Indigenous peoples** (Nenets, Evenki, Khanty): sharing of food and help in hardship, as each describes it.
- **Generations:** those who remember the Soviet state as the sole provider, against post-1991 donors and volunteers.
- **Animal-welfare activists** against municipal policy on stray dogs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and for giving surveys since the 2000s; mixed for minority practice.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic for early texts; Tatar, Buryat.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** charities labeled "foreign agents" or closed since 2012 and especially since 2022; mercy toward prisoners touches political prisoners; amnesty and pardon belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru and CyberLeninka for "милосердие" plus "история благотворительности" and sort by region.
2. Pull the HSE civil-society center's monitoring reports on private donations and volunteering, all available years.
3. Read Fedotov's *Стихи духовные* chapter on almsgiving and list the verses it cites, then find them in Bessonov.
4. Search Levada's archive for "нищим подаете" and "бездомные" for trend items.
5. Search the Marfo-Mariinsky Convent's site and Rusfond's archive for their own histories and appeal texts.
