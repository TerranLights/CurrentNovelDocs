# Russia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; Tatar for minority material. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox believers against secular, Soviet-formed families:** two vocabularies (podvig of faith, podvig of state).
- **Old Believers:** a memory of martyrdom and self-immolation of their own.
- **Women and men:** the self-denying mother and wife against the soldier.
- **Military towns and families** against large cities.
- **Ukrainian lands:** their own canon of the fallen, and the Chernobyl zone.
- **Tatars and other Muslim peoples:** shahid and fidakärlek in religious and national memory.
- **Siberian and northern Indigenous peoples:** sacrifice in hunting, herding and survival lore, as each describes it.
- **Leningrad blockade survivors and their families.**
- **Generations:** WWII children, Afghan-war veterans (1979-1989), Chechen-war veterans, the post-2000 young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the theme pervades literature, church history and war memory.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic, Old Russian; Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory is state-managed and polarized; the war from 2022 and its dead; debunked legends (the "Panfilov 28") are contested in public; political hunger strikes; the Holodomor.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the PSTGU New Martyrs database by diocese for the core lands, and note the categories it uses.
2. Search CyberLeninka for "жертвенность" plus "материнство" and "русская культура" to find studies of the sacrificial-mother ideal.
3. Read Tumarkin's chapters on rituals of remembrance, then search news archives for "Бессмертный полк" by region and year.
4. Search for "гари старообрядцев" in eLibrary.ru for histories of self-immolation.
5. Pull the Rosstat time-use survey tables on care for family members.
