# Russia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; Hebrew and Yiddish for Hasidic material; Tuvan and Evenki for shamanic material. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monastic and hesychast circles** (Optina, Valaam, the Pskov Caves) against ordinary parishioners.
- **Pentecostal and charismatic congregations** (strong in parts of Ukraine and Siberia): ecstatic worship of another kind.
- **Descendants of sect communities** (Molokans, Doukhobors where they remain).
- **Siberian peoples with shamanic traditions** (Evenki, Tuvans, Buryats, Sakha, Khanty): trance as each community describes it.
- **Hasidic and other Jewish communities** of the Ukrainian lands.
- **Finno-Ugric peoples undergoing language shift** (Veps, Karelians, Erzya, Moksha, Komi).
- **Ukrainian lands:** identity shift in both directions.
- **Generations:** those who lived the USSR's end as adults against those born after.
- **Club and festival scenes** of Moscow and St. Petersburg (from the early 1990s raves).
- **Families of flooded villages** (Upper Volga, Angara).

## 4. Flags
