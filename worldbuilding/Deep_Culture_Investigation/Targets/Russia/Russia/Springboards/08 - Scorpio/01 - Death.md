## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Orthodox believers against the nonreligious and the Soviet-raised; Old Believers (their own teaching on dying, to check); the Russian North (Arkhangelsk, Vologda, Olonets), for lament and memorial survivals; big cities against villages emptied by migration; Ukrainian regions, especially Polissia and the Carpathians (Hutsul wake customs, to check), and Greek Catholics in Galicia; Karelians, Vepsians and Komi (Finnic soul beliefs); Nenets and Kola Sami (tundra burial); Muslims (Tatars, Crimean Tatars, migrants from Central Asia and the Caucasus); Jews; Baptists and Pentecostals; families of soldiers and of the repressed. Why: afterlife belief, who may grieve aloud and where people die may differ along these lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history, literature and demography of death; mixed for current belief and grief (surveys exist, qualitative work is newer); thin for Finnic and Nenets afterlife beliefs outside specialist archives.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Karelian and Komi for the North.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war dead since 2014 and 2022, and official limits on reporting casualties; the Holodomor as a contested memory between Russian and Ukrainian sources; suicide statistics' reliability; Nenets and Sami beliefs about burial places, to be recorded only as the peoples share them.
- **Held for review:** none.
