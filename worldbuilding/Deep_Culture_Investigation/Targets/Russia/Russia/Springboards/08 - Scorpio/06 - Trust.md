## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Moscow and St. Petersburg against small towns and villages (the radius of trust); generations who lost savings in 1991-1992 and 1998 against younger ones; Old Believers and their closed networks; Orthodox, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations as trust circles; Muslim communities and Central Asian and Caucasus migrants (*земляки*, people from the same home place); Jews; Roma; Ukrainians in the east, center and west, on trust in Kyiv, Moscow and neighbors; Crimean Tatars; Karelians, Komi and Nenets in small northern communities; women and men in small business. Why: whom one trusts, and through what bonds, may vary by home place, faith and experience of loss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in surveys (Russia and Ukraine are well covered since 1992); rich in fiction; mixed for qualitative work on godparent and migrant networks.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Tatar and Crimean Tatar for community studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** polls under political pressure (answers about trust in leaders may not be candid); Levada's label; wartime collapse of trust between the two peoples. Trust in police and courts belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
