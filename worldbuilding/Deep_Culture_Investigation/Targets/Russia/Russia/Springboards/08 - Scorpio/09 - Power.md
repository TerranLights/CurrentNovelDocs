## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Moscow's political and business elites against the regions and single-industry towns; families of security-service and military officers; the Orthodox hierarchy, Old Believer merchant dynasties and evangelical congregations; Ukrainians in the east, center and west (views of oligarchs, presidents and Moscow); Cossack-heritage communities; Crimean Tatars with their own representative bodies (the Kurultai and Mejlis); Tatar and other Muslim communities and their spiritual boards; Karelians and Komi in their republics; Nenets clan communities (*родовые общины*) and their say over land; women in business and politics; the generation of the 1990s against those born after 2000. Why: who holds power, and whether it is admired, feared or mocked, may differ by place in the hierarchy and by people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elites and history; mixed for everyday power (polls under pressure, qualitative work rarer); thin for minority self-government outside specialist work.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic for early texts; Crimean Tatar and Tatar for community bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** research on elites and security services is risky inside Russia; the Mejlis is banned in Russia; wartime politics color every source on both sides. Legitimate authority and power ways belong to Capricorn; the letter of the law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
