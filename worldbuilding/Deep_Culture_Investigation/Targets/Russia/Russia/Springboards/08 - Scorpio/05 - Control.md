## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Generations raised under Soviet, 1990s and post-2000 conditions; men and women, on control in couples, as a question; Moscow and St. Petersburg professionals against small towns and villages; Orthodox households that follow teaching on headship, and Old Believers with their own household rules; evangelical families; Muslim families (Tatars, Crimean Tatars, Central Asian and Caucasus migrants); Ukrainian regions; Nenets and Komi reindeer herders (control of herd and tundra); disabled people in residential institutions and their advocates; former prisoners and conscripts (*дедовщина*, hazing, as control among soldiers). Why: who controls whom, and how much is left to fate, may differ by generation, faith, gender and institution.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state control and Soviet history; mixed for couples and parenting (surveys exist); thin for minority ideas of fate outside folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Tatar and Crimean Tatar for Muslim communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence is politically charged in Russia, and NGOs face pressure and labels; surveillance research can be risky for informants; psychiatric and institutional records are restricted. The letter of family-violence and registration law goes to Libra, the spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
