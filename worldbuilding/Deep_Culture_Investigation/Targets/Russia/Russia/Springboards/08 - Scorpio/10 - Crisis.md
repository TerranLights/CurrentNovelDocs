## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Generations who lived through 1991-1992, 1998, 2008 and 2014 against those who did not; Moscow and St. Petersburg against single-industry towns and dying villages; dacha households against flat-only households; Chernobyl evacuees and liquidators; people displaced from Donbas and Crimea since 2014, and Crimean Tatars; Komi and Nenets on oil and gas land; Karelians and Vepsians in shrinking villages; Old Believers and others with end-times beliefs, as they describe them; volunteers and search-and-rescue groups; men and women (who copes, who cares, who drinks), as questions. Why: whether a crisis is felt as normal, as a test or as an ending likely follows generation, place and history of loss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for economic crises, Chernobyl and post-Soviet everyday life; mixed for personal crisis and mental health; thin for Finnic and Nenets experience of disasters.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Komi and Crimean Tatar for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2022 war and its displacement; censorship of disaster reporting; Chernobyl health data contested; volunteers in both countries may face legal or physical risk. Crisis as a test of law and courts goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
