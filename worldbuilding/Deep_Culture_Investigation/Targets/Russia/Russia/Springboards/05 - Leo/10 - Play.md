## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City courtyard childhoods** of the Soviet microdistricts against village childhoods with fields and rivers.
- **Generations:** yard play, then the 1990s arcades and video salons, then screen play.
- **The Russian North:** Yuletide games and winter play that differ from the center.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galician spring games; Bukovina's Malanka; Carpathian shepherd games; southern steppe play.
- **Crimean Tatars:** games at Hıdırlez and other festivals, as the community shares them.
- **Jewish families:** Purim plays (purimshpil) and Hanukkah games.
- **Karelians, Vepsians, Komi, Kola Sami:** northern children's games and play with reindeer and boats.
- **Roma:** music and dance as play in family life.
- **Boys and girls;** toys and games marked by gender.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's folklore and Soviet-era play; mixed for present-day children's free time; thin for minority games outside ethnographic collections.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish and Crimean Tatar for those communities; Finnic languages for northern games.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military-patriotic games for children have revived and are tied to the war; read them for side. Children's play in wartime and in displacement is a sensitive topic; use published studies only. Gambling rules as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
