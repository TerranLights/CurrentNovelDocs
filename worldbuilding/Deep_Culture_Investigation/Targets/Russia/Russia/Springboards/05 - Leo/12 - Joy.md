# Russia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Russia (core state), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic for liturgy; Yiddish, Hebrew and Crimean Tatar for minority strands. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Churchgoing Orthodox and Old Believers:** Paschal joy and fasting before it.
- **Secular urban households:** New Year as the main family joy.
- **Villages** of the north, center and Ukrainian lands: seasonal festivals and round dances.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galicia's church-centered feasts; Carpathian music; the east and south.
- **Hasidic and other Jewish communities:** joy in worship.
- **Crimean Tatars:** Hıdırlez, weddings and music.
- **Roma:** music at family celebrations, as Roma musicians describe it.
- **Karelians, Komi, Kola Sami, Nenets:** feasts of the herding and fishing year.
- **Generations, men and women:** how freely joy is shown in public, to test in each group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, liturgy, folklore and happiness polls; mixed for everyday joy; thin for the smaller peoples' feasts outside ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Yiddish and Hebrew for Hasidic sources; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** happiness polls in wartime, on both sides, are shaped by fear, grief and censorship; date every figure. Celebration during mourning and war is itself contested. Avoid any line that a people is "dour" or "merry"; report outsiders' claims as claims.
- **Held for review:** none.
