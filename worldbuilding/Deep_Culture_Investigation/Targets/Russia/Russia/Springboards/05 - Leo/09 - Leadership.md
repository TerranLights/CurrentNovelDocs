## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cossack-heritage districts** of Zaporizhzhia and the Dnipro, with traditions of electing leaders.
- **Village Russia** of the north and center, with commune elders and assemblies.
- **Factory towns,** where the plant director long acted as town leader.
- **Moscow and St. Petersburg business and civic life** against provincial administrations.
- **Ukrainian regions:** local self-government reforms produced new leaders; compare regions.
- **Hasidic and other Jewish communities;** Old Believer communities with lay leaders.
- **Crimean Tatars,** with elected national bodies.
- **Roma:** respected elders (barons, as outsiders call them; check their own terms).
- **Kola Sami and Nenets:** herding brigades and community councils.
- **Women leaders,** from Princess Olga in the chronicles to present-day mayors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state leaders, political myth and elite history; mixed for informal leadership; thin for minority community leadership in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish, Hebrew and Crimean Tatar for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leadership of the state and army is at the center of the war; polls on leaders in Russia carry fear effects, and wartime Ukrainian polling differs. Avoid any line that one people "wants a strong hand"; report it as a polling item only. Formal authority and command go to Capricorn; office as written to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
