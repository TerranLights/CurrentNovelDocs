*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Conscripts and their families,** and the regions that send most of them.
- **Prisoners and former prisoners:** a separate code of degradation that leaks into street speech.
- **Schoolchildren and teenagers,** by city size and school type.
- **Women in rural areas,** where reputation shaming of brides and wives is recorded in the folklore.
- **Ukrainian regions:** memories of language bans and of being mocked as "provincial" differ by region and by home language.
- **Crimean Tatars and Jews:** humiliation of a people as remembered by its members.
- **Roma:** shame rules inside the community against humiliation by outsiders.
- **Devout Orthodox and Old Believers:** humility and chosen lowering as religious practice.
- **LGBT people,** who face public shaming and legal pressure in Russia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, folklore and NGO reports; mixed in surveys; thin for minority shaming customs in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Romani for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** army hazing and wartime treatment of prisoners and civilians are current and partisan; use published reports, never informants at risk. Prison-caste words are slurs; cite them as terms of study only. Collective humiliation is a war narrative on all sides; read for side and date. Punishments as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
