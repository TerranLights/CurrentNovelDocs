*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** Soviet-born, the 1990s cohort and those raised online; each had different outlets.
- **Capitals against small towns:** subcultures, nightlife and visible style cluster in Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galicia, the center, the east and the south; embroidered shirts (вишиванка) and language choice as identity statements.
- **Old Believers and devout Orthodox** against secular households: dress and speech rules differ.
- **Crimean Tatars, Jews, Roma:** dress, music and speech in a minority's own register.
- **Northern Finno-Ugric peoples:** lament and song traditions with their own rules for showing grief.
- **Men and women;** LGBT people, whose public expression is legally restricted in Russia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, Soviet practice and youth culture; mixed for present-day opinion, since polls on speaking freely are themselves shaped by fear of speaking.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar and Yiddish for minority voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political speech, protest art and gender expression are under legal pressure in Russia, and wartime censorship affects what sources say; Maidan and wartime protest material is partisan on all sides. The letter of speech rules goes to Libra; their spirit to Capricorn. Some lament texts are family and funeral property; ask before quoting.
- **Held for review:** none.
