## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Officials and the public:** the *chinovnik* at the counter against the citizen in line; state service as a career.
- **Military families and garrison towns:** service as a family tradition, including Cossack service heritage.
- **Orthodox parishes and monasteries:** *sluzhenie* as religious vocation.
- **Muslim Tatar communities** in Moscow and St. Petersburg: charity and mosque service in their own terms.
- **Ukrainian lands:** a separate volunteer culture with its own recent history.
- **Domestic workers:** nannies and cleaners, many from Central Asia, Ukraine and Moldova, and their employers.
- **Service-sector youth in big cities** against older people who remember Soviet shops and queues.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state service and Orthodox charity; mixed on volunteering (official counts and independent ones differ); thin on domestic workers' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Church Slavonic for monastic texts, Ukrainian, Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service and conscription in wartime; hazing in the army; volunteer and charity groups facing official pressure; any Ukrainian volunteering tied to the war.
- **Held for review:** none.
