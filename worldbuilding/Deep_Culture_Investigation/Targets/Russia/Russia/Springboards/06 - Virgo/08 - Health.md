## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cities and villages:** access to clinics against the feldsher post and the *babka*.
- **Men and women:** a wide life-expectancy gap that the scholarship discusses; men's use of doctors as a research question.
- **Orthodox believers:** prayer, fasting and pilgrimage beside medicine.
- **Ukrainian lands:** whisper healers, wax-pouring and their own health-system history.
- **Komi, Karelians and Vepsians:** Finnic healers and bath customs.
- **Nenets:** health in reindeer-herding life, as the people describe it.
- **Tatars and other Muslims:** fasting and religious healing.
- **Migrant workers:** access to care and how they speak of it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on mortality statistics, Soviet medicine and folk-medicine archives; mixed on mental health; thin for the far-north peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Komi, Karelian, Nenets and Tatar for minority healing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** punitive psychiatry in Soviet history; alcohol, HIV and drug use; healers' charms shared only as they choose; war wounds and trauma.
- **Held for review:** none.
