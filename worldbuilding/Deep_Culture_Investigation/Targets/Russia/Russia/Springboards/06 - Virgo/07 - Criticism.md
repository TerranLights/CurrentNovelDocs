# Russia (core state): Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The intelligentsia and officials:** critic as conscience against critic as troublemaker.
- **Workplaces:** Soviet-style meetings against present corporate feedback, as employees describe both.
- **Families:** nagging, reproach and teasing across generations.
- **Old Believers:** a long tradition of critique of the official church.
- **Ukrainian lands:** their own satire and a press culture that diverged after 1991.
- **Tatars and the Jewish heritage communities:** satire in their own languages.
- **Online younger users** against older television audiences.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on literary and Soviet sources; mixed on present-day feedback norms; thin on minority satire outside Kazan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Tatar and Yiddish for minority sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** criticism of the state and army is legally risky in Russia now; dissident archives and some media are blocked or labeled; denunciation (*donos*) history touches painful memory. The legal limits on speech belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Kharkhordin's chapters on self-criticism, then search the Russian National Corpus for «критика и самокритика» by year.
2. Read the first Kurbsky letter and the tsar's reply.
3. Browse a scanned *Krokodil* run from the 1960s and list the targets of its satire.
4. Search Otzovik for complaint vocabulary («ужасный сервис», «не рекомендую»).
5. Search CyberLeninka for «жалобная книга советская торговля» to start the complaint line.
