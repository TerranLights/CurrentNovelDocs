## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow and St. Petersburg against small towns and villages:** office and service rhythms against farm and garden-plot ones.
- **The Russian North and the Pomors:** sea, timber and fishing rounds.
- **Industrial towns** (Ivanovo textiles, Tula metalwork): factory-town cultures.
- **Ukrainian lands:** their own peasant, craft and labor histories.
- **Karelians, Vepsians, Komi and Nenets:** forest, reindeer and fishing rounds, as each people describes them.
- **Old Believers:** their own rules of household purity.
- **Tatars, Jews, Roma, Armenians, Azerbaijanis:** trades and community economies.
- **Central Asian labor migrants** (Uzbek, Tajik, Kyrgyz): construction, cleaning, service.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for Russian-language sources, surveys and Soviet labor history; mixed for minorities; thin for small northern peoples outside ethnographic archives. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (the row carries pre-war Russia and Ukraine, and the line toward Ukraine is open); this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Ukrainian; Tatar, Karelian, Vepsian, Komi and Yiddish for minority sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced labor (serfdom, the Gulag) and wartime matters; organizations designated "foreign agents" or "undesirable" in Russia (Levada, the former Memorial) may be blocked or risky for in-country contacts; healers' knowledge shared only as they choose.
- **Held for review:** none.
