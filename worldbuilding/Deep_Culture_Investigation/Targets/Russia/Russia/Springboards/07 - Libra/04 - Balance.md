## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox fasters, Old Believers and secular majorities:** the strictness of the yearly rhythm.
- **Greek Catholics in western Ukraine:** a different church calendar and fasting discipline.
- **Crimean Tatars and other Muslims:** Ramadan and their own feast cycle.
- **Rotation workers of the North (вахта) and their families:** weeks apart, weeks home.
- **Dacha-goers:** a city week and a garden weekend, and the long summer.
- **Chernobyl evacuees and those who returned (самосели):** life after the land was thrown out of balance.
- **Hutsuls and other Carpathian highlanders:** seasonal herding rhythms.
- **Nenets, Komi and Karelians:** relations with land and seasons in their own words.
- **Big-city professionals against small-town workers:** burnout and rest as class questions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church calendar, constitutional texts and planning history; mixed for work-life data; thin for Indigenous teachings.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Nenets and Karelian for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "culture of extremes" writing (Berdyaev and heirs) slides easily into stereotype: treat it as a claim to test; Chernobyl testimony needs care; Ukrainian media norms changed under wartime rules.
