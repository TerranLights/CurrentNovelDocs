## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village communities of central and northern Russia:** the assembly that sought one voice.
- **Cossack descendants:** the rada's open, loud decisions.
- **Komi and Karelian villages:** their own assembly customs, likely thinly documented.
- **Old Believers:** councils that took long over doctrine.
- **Crimean Tatars:** collective decisions through the Kurultai.
- **Western and eastern Ukraine:** voting choices and late-deciders across regions.
- **Moscow and St Petersburg youth against small towns:** choice overload against narrow options.
- **The Soviet-educated:** decisions once made "from above" and the habit or relief that left.
- **Families:** who has the final word in household choices, by region and faith.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and poll records; mixed in psychology; thin for minority assemblies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar, Komi and Karelian for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Oblomovism" and "avos'" are often used as national stereotypes: treat them as claims to test, never as findings; current wartime polling in both lands has known limits on candor.
- **Held for review:** none.
