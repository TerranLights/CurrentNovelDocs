## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian heartland and the Russian North:** spell and sorcerer lore.
- **Polissia and the Carpathians:** dense witch and evil-eye beliefs.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** Islamic and older beliefs about envy.
- **Mari, Udmurt, Komi, Karelians:** village sorcerers and their own causes of misfortune.
- **Chechens, Ingush, Karachays, Balkars, Kalmyks, Crimean Tatars:** deported as traitor peoples.
- **Sakha, Buryats, Evenks, Chukchi:** shamanic accounts of harm; butts of jokes.
- **Jews:** blood libel, pogroms and Soviet campaigns.
- **Roma:** charges of theft and sorcery.
- **Russian Germans and Mennonites:** "enemy aliens" in two wars.
- **Central Asian and Caucasus migrants:** blamed for crime.
- **Old Believers:** once branded schismatics.
- **"Foreign agents" and LGBT people:** present official others.
- **Western and eastern Ukraine, Crimea, the Donbas:** mutual images, to test.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trials, propaganda and polls; mixed for folk belief today; thin for minorities' own words on being blamed and for non-Slavic evil-eye terms.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Old East Slavic, Middle Russian; Polish, Latin, Tatar, Bashkir, Komi, Crimean Tatar and Yiddish for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the war makes enemy images live; slurs only as data; "foreign agent" and LGBT topics carry legal risk, and some monitors are labeled or closed; the study must not become a projection onto either land.
- **Held for review:** none.
