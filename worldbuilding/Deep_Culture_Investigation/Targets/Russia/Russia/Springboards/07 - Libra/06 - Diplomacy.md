## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moscow and St Petersburg elites against provincial towns:** courtly and plain styles.
- **Ukrainian regions:** Galicia's Habsburg-era civic habits against the east and south.
- **Crimean Tatars:** heirs of a state with its own diplomatic tradition.
- **Jewish communities:** the shtadlan as a go-between with power.
- **Cossack descendants:** the Hetmanate's memory of treaty-making.
- **Karelians and Sami:** borderland peoples long caught between Novgorod and Sweden (the Treaty of Orekhov, 1323, *check*).
- **Transcarpathia's Rusyns, Hungarians and Romanians:** daily brokering among several languages.
- **Russian speakers in Ukraine and Ukrainian speakers in Russia:** language choice as a tactful or a pointed act.
- **Old Believers:** a community long negotiating with a hostile state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy, archives and memoirs; mixed for everyday tact; thin for northern peoples' own protocols.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Middle Russian; Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Hungarian for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2014-2022 agreements and the war are contested and live; outsider "negotiating style" books risk stereotype; some Russian archives restrict access to foreigners.
- **Held for review:** none.
