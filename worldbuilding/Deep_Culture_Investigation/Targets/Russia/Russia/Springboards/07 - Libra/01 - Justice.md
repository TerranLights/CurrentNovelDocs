## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Crimean Tatars:** the 1944 deportation and return; redress claims of their own.
- **Jews of Ukraine and Russia:** pogroms, the Holocaust (Babyn Yar), and memory work.
- **Russian Germans, Karelians and Ingrian Finns:** deported or repressed peoples, each rehabilitated on different terms.
- **Komi and the northern camp regions (Vorkuta, Ukhta):** places shaped by the Gulag, where former prisoners and guards' families live side by side.
- **Cossack descendants:** decossackization as a claim of wrong.
- **Western against eastern Ukraine; Moscow and St Petersburg against small towns:** different courts, memories and trust.
- **Old Believers and Orthodox, Greek Catholics in Galicia, Muslims, Jews:** divine justice in different scriptures.
- **Former prisoners and the criminal underworld:** a code of their own beside the state's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court statistics and the repression record; mixed for minority redress; current data on the war's justice questions is partisan on both sides.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian, Church Slavonic; Crimean Tatar and Yiddish for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Memorial is liquidated in Russia and its archive's access is uncertain; war crimes and the occupation are active matters; testimonies of victims need care; some court data were restricted after 2022.
- **Held for review:** none.
