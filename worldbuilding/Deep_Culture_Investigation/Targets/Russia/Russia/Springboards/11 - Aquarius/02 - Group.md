## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** those raised in Pioneer and Komsomol collectives against post-Soviet cohorts.
- **Conscripts, prisoners and their kin:** two total groups with their own codes.
- **Russian- and Ukrainian-speaking Ukrainians**, and east against west, where in-group lines have moved since 2014.
- **Crimean Tatars**, with their own representative bodies.
- **Nenets and Kola Sami herders**, with kin groups and brigades.
- **Roma families**, Old Believers and Jehovah's Witnesses (banned in Russia since 2017) as closed or closed-off groups.
- **Football ultras, bikers and other subcultures** in Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kyiv, Kharkiv, Lviv and Odesa.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the Soviet collective and on prison and army groups; mixed on survey-based collectivism; thin on Indigenous kin groups in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Old East Slavic; Crimean Tatar; prison argot (*fenya*) for criminal sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Mejlis is banned in Russia; Jehovah's Witnesses are prosecuted; ultras and battalions touch the war. Avoid one-voice claims about "Russian collectivism"; measure it by group and cohort.
- **Held for review:** none.
