## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Capital bohemias** (Moscow's Arbat and Kitai-gorod, St. Petersburg's Pushkinskaya 10, Kyiv's Andriyivskyy Descent, Lviv's cafés, Kharkiv's underground) against small towns.
- **Late-Soviet *neformaly*** against post-Soviet subcultures (goths, emo, rappers).
- **Religious dissenters:** Old Believers, Molokan and Doukhobor descendants, Baptists and Pentecostals, Jehovah's Witnesses.
- **Ukrainian, Crimean Tatar and Jewish dissidents** of the Soviet era, each with separate histories.
- **Karelian, Komi and Sami** cultural activists outside official folklore circles.
- **LGBT people,** whose standing now differs sharply between the two states.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dissent, art and youth subcultures; mixed for measured tolerance; thin for minority-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; slang dictionaries of each decade.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBT topics fall under Russian "propaganda" and "extremism" rules; Jehovah's Witnesses are prosecuted; Pussy Riot members carry labels. Protect people named in online sources. Do not frame either country as one voice on difference.
- **Held for review:** none.
