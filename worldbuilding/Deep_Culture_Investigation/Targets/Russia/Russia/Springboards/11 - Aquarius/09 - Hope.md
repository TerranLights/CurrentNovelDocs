## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** those who grew up under the "radiant future," the 1990s cohort, and the young since 2022.
- **Ukrainian front-line regions, the rear and refugees abroad;** families of soldiers and of the missing on both sides.
- **Single-industry towns and depopulating villages** against capital regions.
- **Believers** (Orthodox of each jurisdiction, Greek Catholics, Protestants) against the nonreligious.
- **Crimean Tatars,** with the hope of return and its present limits.
- **Jewish communities,** with emigration as one form of hope.
- **Karelians, Komi, Sami and Nenets,** with language-revival hopes in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, religion and survey data; mixed in present measures under war conditions; thin in minority sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Church Slavonic; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hope and despair in wartime are raw; survey optimism under censorship may be performative. Families of the missing need care. Keep Russian and Ukrainian war hopes apart. The "fatalistic Slav" is a stereotype; never adopt it.
- **Held for review:** none.
