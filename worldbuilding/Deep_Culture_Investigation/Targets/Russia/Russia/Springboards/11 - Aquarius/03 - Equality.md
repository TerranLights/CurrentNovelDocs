## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former estates' heirs:** descendants of serf villages, Cossack lands (where serfdom came late) and state peasants of the North, who were never serfs.
- **Capital elites against single-industry towns** (*monogoroda*) and depopulating villages.
- **Women and men** across Soviet and post-Soviet cohorts.
- **Jewish, Roma and Crimean Tatar communities,** each once ranked apart by the state.
- **Nenets, Sami, Veps and Izhorians,** whose status as small-numbered peoples gives distinct standing.
- **Labor migrants and internally displaced people,** often outside the circle of equals.
- **Ukrainian west, center, east and south,** where oligarchic and post-Soviet class orders differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in income data and Soviet history; mixed in survey items on attitudes; thin on Indigenous peoples' own equality claims.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ukrainian; Yiddish helps for Jewish sources; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** FBK is labeled "extremist" in Russia; citing it carries risk inside the country. LGBT topics are restricted by Russian "propaganda" rules (equality for gay people can be studied only from outside sources there). Never treat "Soviet equality" as one voice; test by group.
- **Held for review:** none.
