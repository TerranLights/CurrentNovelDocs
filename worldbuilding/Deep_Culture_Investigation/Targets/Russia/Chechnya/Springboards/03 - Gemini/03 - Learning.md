*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Girls and boys**: separate paths through household tasks, schooling and religious learning; whether early marriage cuts schooling short is a question to check.
- **Highland herding families** (Itum-Kali, Sharoy, Vedeno), where children may learn on the pastures.
- **The exile generation**, schooled in Central Asia.
- **Children of the war years** (about 1994 to 2004), whose schooling was broken off.
- **State-school pupils and madrasa students**, often the same children at different hours.
- **Kumyk, Nogai and Avar pupils** in Chechen-majority schools, learning Chechen as a third language.
- **Russian-speaking families** of the Terek left bank.
- **Children with disabilities**, and where they are taught.
- **Heritage learners abroad**, learning Chechen as a second language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Soviet and Russian education statistics and ethnopedagogy writing are plentiful; independent study of present schooling is thin; wartime school records are patchy.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kazakh for exile schooling records; German and French for diaspora schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious schooling is a political subject; girls' schooling and early marriage need care; the war years remain painful. Higher education and schooling as an institution go to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** none.
