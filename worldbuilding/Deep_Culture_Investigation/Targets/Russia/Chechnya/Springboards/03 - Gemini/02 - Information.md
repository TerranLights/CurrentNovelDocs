## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian-language readers in Grozny and Argun** against **Chechen-language listeners in the villages**: different channels and possibly different trust.
- **Highland settlements** (Itum-Kali, Sharoy, Galanchozh) where signal and broadband may be weaker.
- **Former Soviet professionals** (teachers, engineers) and **those schooled during the wars**, whose access to schooling and news differed.
- **Religious scholars trained in Arabic** (alims, Ӏеламнах *check*) as keepers of a separate body of knowledge.
- **Elders who hold teip histories**.
- **Families of the missing** from the wars, for whom records are a live question.
- **Russian and Nogai residents** of the Terek left bank and **Kumyk villages** near Gudermes.
- **Diaspora readers in Europe and Turkey**, who may follow émigré channels instead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official statistics and state media are plentiful; independent reporting exists but is contested; archives were heavily damaged.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic; English, German and French for monitors and diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the information space is tightly controlled; some outlets (including separatist and insurgent sites) are banned under Russian law and must not be accessed in ways that endanger anyone; informants who discuss media or the missing may be at risk; teip histories may be guarded by families.
- **Held for review:** none.
