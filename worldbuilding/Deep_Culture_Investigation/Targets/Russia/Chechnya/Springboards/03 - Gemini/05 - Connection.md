*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Teips with large and small memberships**, and families with weak teip ties: unequal reach of connections.
- **Members of different Sufi brotherhoods**, and those outside them.
- **Mountain families resettled on the plain** (after 1957 or after the wars), whose ties to ancestral villages may be stretched.
- **Women**, whose ties may run through both the birth family and the husband's family.
- **Russian, Kumyk and Nogai residents**, outside the teip system.
- **Akkin Chechens** of Dagestan and **Kists** of Georgia, kin across borders.
- **The long-settled diaspora** (Turkey, Jordan, Syria) and **the recent one** (Europe), with different kinds of ties home.
- **Widows, orphans and the elderly** from the war years, at risk of isolation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Kin structure and diaspora history are well studied; loneliness and isolation are likely unmeasured locally.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Turkish, Arabic, Georgian; Polish, German and French for European studies; Ottoman Turkish for archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** teip standing and disputes are delicate; diaspora informants may fear contact with home authorities; connections to officials can be politically sensitive. Friendship and community as such belong to Aquarius; belonging to Cancer.
- **Held for review:** none.
