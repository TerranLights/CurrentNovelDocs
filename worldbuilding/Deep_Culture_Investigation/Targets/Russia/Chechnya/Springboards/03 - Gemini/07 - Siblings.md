# Chechnya: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Ingush; Kumyk and Nogai for minority kin terms; Arabic for religious brotherhood. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Large village families** and **smaller urban families** in Grozny: different sizes of sibling sets.
- **Eldest sons, youngest sons and daughters**: possibly different duties, to be checked, not assumed.
- **Highland families** with older lineage structures (Itum-Kali, Sharoy, Cheberloy).
- **Families who lost members in the wars**, where elder siblings may have raised the younger.
- **Kumyk, Nogai and Avar families**, with Turkic or Dagestani kin systems.
- **Russian families** of the Terek left bank.
- **Mixed families** with half-siblings from second marriages.
- **Diaspora families** split across Europe, Turkey and Central Asia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Kinship and clan structure are well documented; studies of sibling feeling, rivalry and care are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Ingush; Kumyk and Nogai; Arabic for religious brotherhood.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** brothers' roles toward sisters touch on "honor" and may involve violence reported by human-rights groups; handle with care. Inheritance among siblings is law: point to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
