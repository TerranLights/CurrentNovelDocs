# Chechnya: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Ingush, Kumyk and Nogai for minority villages; Arabic for sermons and pre-1925 writing. **Concept in one line:** how meaning passes between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; questions to research: which kinds of talk count as good here, who judges, and what talk is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women's and men's settings** (mosque yard, kitchen, wedding): speaking rules may differ.
- **Elders and youth**: who speaks first.
- **Mainly Chechen-speaking villages** against **Russian-dominant workplaces in Grozny**.
- **Highland** (Itum-Kali, Sharoy) and **lowland speakers**: dialect may mark a speaker.
- **Kumyk, Nogai and Russian residents** of the Terek districts: Chechen as a second language.
- **Ingush-speaking families** near the western border.
- **Sufi brotherhood followers** and **Salafi-leaning youth**: ideals of religious speech.
- **Deaf Chechens**.
- **Diaspora families** (Europe, Turkey, Jordan, Kazakhstan).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Etiquette and ethnography exist in Russian; turn-taking and infant-directed talk are likely thin or unstudied.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic; German, Polish or French for diaspora studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public speech is constrained and criticism can endanger informants; avoidance customs need consented fieldwork; some foreign broadcasters are blocked in Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.
