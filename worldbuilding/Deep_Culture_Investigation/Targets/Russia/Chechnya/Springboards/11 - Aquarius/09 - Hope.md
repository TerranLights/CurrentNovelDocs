# Chechnya: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for religious teaching; Turkish and Arabic for the old emigrant communities; Polish and German for recent asylum seekers. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: what people in Chechnya's lands hope for (for themselves, their families, their people, the dead) when the good is uncertain, through which practices they hope, and how despair and false hope are named.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Religious** hope (Sufi shrine visitors; Salafis, who may reject shrine petitions) against **secular** hope for work, study and safety.
- **The exile generation**, with memories of waiting to return, against the **war generations** and the **post-2009 young**.
- **Families of the disappeared** and of the dead, against families whose men serve in the security forces.
- **Highland** families hoping to return to emptied villages, against **Grozny** residents.
- **Russians** who left in the 1990s and the few who returned; **Kumyks** and **Nogais** of the north.
- **Old diasporas** (Turkey, Jordan, Syria's displaced) and **new asylum seekers** in Europe; the **Kists**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich on religious practice and exile memory; thin and unreliable for measured optimism or life satisfaction in the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Turkish; Polish, German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared, the deportation date and leaving the republic are politically charged; protect searching families. Shrine practice is disputed between Muslim camps; record each side's view.
- **Held for review:** none.
