## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sufi brotherhoods** (Kunta-Hajji's and other Qadiri branches, Naqshbandi) against **Salafi** reformers: renewal of faith by opposite roads.
- **Soviet-trained intelligentsia** of Grozny, against **village religious scholars**.
- **The exile-born generation**, who lived Soviet reform in Kazakhstan, against the **post-war young**.
- **Women**, as targets of reform campaigns in the 1920s and again now.
- **Terek left bank**: Cossack revival, Nogai cultural revival.
- **Diaspora**: émigré reformers and critics in Europe, Turkey and Jordan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich history of reform from above and religious renewal; very thin on public opinion about change, since free polling and elections are lacking.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; French and English for the scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Wahhabi" is used as an accusation with real consequences; treat religious-reform labels with care. The reform of courts and sharia in the 1990s is law: point to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
