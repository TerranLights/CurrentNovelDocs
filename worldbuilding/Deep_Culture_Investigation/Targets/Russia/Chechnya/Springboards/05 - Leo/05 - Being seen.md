## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Daughters-in-law in a husband's household, where avoidance customs limit who may see and address whom.
- Young unmarried women against married and older women: different rules for being seen in public.
- People disabled by the wars and by mines, who may be overlooked in public life.
- The few remaining Russian residents of Grozny, visible as a small minority.
- Nogai and Kumyk villagers, seen mostly in census tables and rarely in republic media.
- Diaspora families in Europe, who may draw police attention as Chechens.
- LGBT Chechens, for whom being seen can be a danger; handle with great care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on customs and rights reports; thin on open opinion; census figures themselves are disputed.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for religious texts; Georgian; German and French for diaspora press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance and rights cases; women's images; photographs of the dead and the disappeared; ask before using any person's picture.
- **Held for review:** none.
