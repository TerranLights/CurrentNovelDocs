*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Men and women: different rules for dress, voice in public and showing feeling.
- Loud-zikr Qadiri lines (Kunta-Haji, Vis-Haji, whose women also hold zikr *check*) against silent Naqshbandi practice.
- Grozny's online youth against village elders, who may hold speech to stricter forms.
- Russian-speaking residents and Cossack families of the northern districts, under the same state rules but other customs.
- Ingush in the Sunzha border villages *check*: a Vainakh kin with its own politics of speech.
- The European diaspora (Austria, France, Germany), free of home rules but watched by them.
- The generation born in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan exile, raised in Russian-language schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rights reports and online media are rich; open survey data inside the republic are thin and answers may be guarded.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for religious texts; Georgian for Pankisi; German and French for diaspora media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** high. Speaking to researchers can carry risk; anonymize all contacts; LGBT and political material needs special care; do not reshare private posts.
- **Held for review:** none.
