# Chechnya: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for Islamic teaching on human worth; Kazakh and Russian for exile-era records. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how a person's worth as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it, are named and practiced in Chechnya's lands, and whether that worth is held to be inborn or earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Elders, from whom composure is expected, against young men, of whom it may not be.
- Families who lived in the 1999–2004 camps, for dignity under aid.
- Descendants of the special settlers, whose family stories begin in exile.
- Russian residents who left in the 1990s for Stavropol and elsewhere, with their own accounts of indignity.
- War-disabled men and mine victims, and how they are treated in public.
- Migrant workers from the republic in Moscow and Siberia, facing checks and suspicion.
- Asylum seekers in Austria, Poland and France, for dignity in foreign procedures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in testimony and humanitarian reports; mixed in statistics; ethics writing is often prescriptive rather than descriptive.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic; Kazakh for exile-era local records; Polish and German for asylum material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war trauma and remains; testimony of degradation; consent for any use of survivors' words.
- **Held for review:** none.
