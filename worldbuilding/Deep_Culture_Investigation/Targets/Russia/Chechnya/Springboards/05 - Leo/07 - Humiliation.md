# Chechnya: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for religious terms of abasement; English for rights reporting. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others, and the act of lowering someone, are named, used, remembered and answered in Chechnya's lands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Women and girls, on whom a family's shame is often said to rest: a claim to test, not adopt.
- Young men made to apologize on camera, and how their kin respond.
- Former detainees of the filtration camps, and families of the disappeared.
- Akkin returnees whose villages in Dagestan were resettled after 1944 *check*: humiliation tied to lost homes.
- Russian and Cossack families who left in the 1990s, with their own memories of being driven out.
- Chechen migrants in Moscow and Siberia facing slurs and police checks.
- Diaspora youth in European schools, for humiliation as foreigners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in rights reporting; thin in neutral surveys; much testimony is from people who fled.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; English for rights reports; German, French and Polish for diaspora material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** very high. Victims' identities, apology videos and gender violence; do not republish faces; consult survivors' groups before contact.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Caucasian Knot for "публичные извинения" and tabulate cases by year and by reason given.
