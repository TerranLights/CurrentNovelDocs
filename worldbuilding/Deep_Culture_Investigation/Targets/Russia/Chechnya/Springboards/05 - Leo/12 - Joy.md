# Chechnya: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Ingush for the old Vainakh festival record; Kumyk and Nogai; Arabic for religious joy. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive, are named, felt, shown, sought and shared in Chechnya's lands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Women's own gatherings at weddings and births against the men's side: separate spaces of celebration.
- Families greeting a son and a daughter: whether the joy differs is a question to test.
- Elders who lived the 1957 return against the generation born after 2000.
- Zikr circles of the Qadiri lines against households that keep celebrations quiet and music-free.
- Russian and Cossack residents of the north, with Orthodox Easter and Maslenitsa.
- Kumyk villages near the Dagestan line, which may keep Navruz *check*.
- Diaspora weddings in France and Austria, where joy is staged before host neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in custom and online media; thin and uncertain in well-being statistics.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Ingush, Russian; Kumyk; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official rules on wedding conduct and music; joy beside war loss; family photos and children online.
- **Held for review:** none.
