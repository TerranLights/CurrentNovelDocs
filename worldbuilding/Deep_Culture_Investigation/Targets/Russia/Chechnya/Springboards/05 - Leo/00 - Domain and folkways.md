## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Terek and Sunzha plain villages against the mountain gorges (Argun, Sharo-Argun, Itum-Kale): wealth and isolation may shape festivity.
- The Ichkeria foothills (Vedeno, Nozhay-Yurt): heartland of nineteenth-century resistance songs.
- Terek Cossack and Russian residents of the Naur and Shelkovskaya districts: a separate festival calendar and song stock.
- Nogai and Kumyk villages on the northern and eastern edges *check*: Turkic wedding forms.
- Akkin (Aukh) Chechens and the Kists of Pankisi: kin across the borders, useful contrasts.
- Sufi brotherhoods (Qadiri and Naqshbandi lines) against Salafi-leaning circles: opposed views on music and dance.
- Grozny's youth against village elders; those born in Central Asian exile; the European refugee diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and online video; thin in statistics (republic-level breakdowns rare; prewar archives damaged in the wars).
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the tracker carries "Russia" as pre-war lands); this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the present Chechen Republic.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen (Cyrillic, some Arabic-script manuscripts), Russian; Arabic and Turkish for the diaspora; Kumyk and Nogai for the edges.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official rules on music, dress and conduct; policing of speech and contacts' safety; women's and LGBT expression; war trauma.
- **Held for review:** none.
