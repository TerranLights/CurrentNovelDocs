# Chechnya: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for alms terms; German and Turkish for diaspora charities. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how giving freely beyond what is owed (money, goods, time, food, attention or praise) is practiced, expected, displayed and judged in Chechnya's lands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Highland villages with long hosting customs against Grozny's apartment neighborhoods.
- Wealthy patrons and officials against households that give little but must give: the weight of expected gifts.
- Members of Sufi wird groups, who may pool for zikr and feasts, against those outside them.
- Cossack and Russian villages in the north, with their own mutual-help customs (помочь *check*).
- Ingush and Chechen families linked by guest-friendship across the Sunzha border.
- Kists in Pankisi, hosts in 1999–2000.
- European diaspora senders and the home households that depend on them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in custom and press; thin in neutral numbers; the line between gift and obligation, and between charity and political tribute, will need careful sources.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic; German and Turkish for diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reports of forced "donations" to official funds; do not name ordinary donors without consent.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for "белхи" and "тезет" and collect the ethnographic articles.

5. Search Austrian association registers and press for "tschetschenischer Kulturverein" and charity drives.
