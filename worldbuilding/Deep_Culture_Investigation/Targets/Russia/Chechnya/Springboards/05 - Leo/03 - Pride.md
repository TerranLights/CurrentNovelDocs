## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The nine tukkhums and their teips (Orstkhoy, Cheberloy, Sharoy, Shatoy, Melkhi and others): rival claims of origin and antiquity.
- Highland families who trace descent to tower villages against lowland families of later settlement.
- Families stigmatized by descent claims (the slur "лай", servile origin *check*): pride and its denial; handle with care.
- Terek Cossacks in the northern stanitsas, with their own frontier pride and grievances.
- Akkin Chechens of Aukh, whose pride is tied to a homeland now in Dagestan.
- Women, through pride in sons and family, against men's public pride.
- Muhajir descendants in Turkey and Jordan, proud of a homeland few have seen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and folklore; mixed in opinion data; group pride is a political subject now.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic and Turkish for the diaspora; Swedish for some fighters' coverage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** symbols of the 1990s state may count as banned in Russia; descent slurs; LGBT matters carry danger; avoid national clichés, which outsiders often apply to Chechens.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for "дозалла" and "куралла" to find linguistic or ethics studies using the terms.
2. Read Gammer (2006) for chapters on the wolf image and list his primary sources.
3. Search for teip histories with "тайпа истори" and record which tukkhums publish most.
4. Search "Нурадилов" and "Брестская крепость чеченцы" for memorial and museum materials.
5. Search Turkish-language sites for "Çeçen derneği" and note their heritage events.
