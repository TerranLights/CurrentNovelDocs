*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Georgian for the Kists and for Georgian literature about them. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women:** the k'onakh ideal is male-coded; women's endurance needs separate reading.
- **Sufi vird lineages:** the Kunta-Haji line against traditions of armed resistance.
- **Highland and lowland villages:** different war histories and memorials.
- **The Soviet-era generation, the 1990s generation and the post-2000 generation:** whose courage counts shifts with each war.
- **Kists** (Georgian-language sources) and **Akkin Chechens**, if in the lands.
- **Russians and Terek Cossack descendants:** their own courage tradition on the same frontier.
- **Nogais:** a separate steppe heroic-epic tradition (the Edige epic *check*).
- **Diasporas:** courage retold in exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in narrative (songs, memoirs, war writing); thin in measured data.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** courage stories are tied to wars and insurgency; some sources glorify violence, and some media are banned in Russia. Separate praise of armed resistance from analysis; protect living witnesses. Military honor as duty is in Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Chechen-language sites for "къонахалла" and "майралла"; collect the definitions offered.
2. Search the Russian State Library for "илли" with "илланча" for recordings and studies.
3. Find a history of Dadi-Yurt (1819) and note how women's acts are told.
4. Search for "Кунта-Хаджи" with "ненасилие" (non-violence).
5. Read Tolstoy's "The Raid" alongside a Chechen-language account of the same campaigns.
