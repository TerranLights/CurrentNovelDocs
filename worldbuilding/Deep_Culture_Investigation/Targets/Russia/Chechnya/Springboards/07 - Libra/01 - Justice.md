## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of the disappeared and of the 1990s and 2000s dead,** set apart from families tied to the current security forces: justice claims may run in opposite directions.
- **Deportation-era elders and their descendants:** historical justice for 1944 as a separate question from the wars.
- **Highland lineages with long feud memories** (Itum-Kale, Sharoy, Shatoy districts) against lowland towns such as Gudermes, Urus-Martan and Shali.
- **Followers of Kunta-Haji's Qadiri line, Naqshbandi lines and Salafi-leaning believers:** different answers on who may punish and by which law.
- **Russians and Cossack descendants** displaced in the 1990s from Naursky, Shelkovskoy and Grozny: their own injustice narrative.
- **Nogai and Kumyk villages:** whether their disputes go to their own elders, to Chechen bodies or to state courts.
- **Women** as plaintiffs, victims and kin of the accused, a category distinct in every source.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in rights reporting and war scholarship; thin and politically shaped in official statistics; imperial court files survive mainly outside the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk and Nogai for minority cases; English for the scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extrajudicial killings, torture, disappearances and collective punishment; living perpetrators and victims; any interview carries risk to informants.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian State Library for «горские словесные суды Терской области» and request the Vladikavkaz archive's fond list.
2. Find the 1996 Ichkeria criminal code text («Уголовный кодекс ЧРИ 1996») and compare it with the 1960 RSFSR chapter on local customs.
3. Read Souleimanov and Aliyev (2015), then follow its Chechen-language citations.
4. Pull the ombudsman's last ten annual reports and list the complaint categories.
5. Search eLibrary.ru for «кровная месть чеченцы» and «самосуд Чечня» to find regional journal articles.
