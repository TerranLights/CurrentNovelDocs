## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland villages and lowland towns:** exchange labor in hay, stone and building against paid help in towns.
- **Lineages and wird brotherhoods:** each may keep its own circle of obligatory help at funerals and weddings.
- **Families who stayed through the wars and those who fled and returned:** debts of shelter and help incurred in camps and in Ingushetia, Dagestan and abroad.
- **Recipients of state and foundation aid and those outside it:** obligations created by giving from above.
- **Nogai, Kumyk and Russian or Cossack communities:** their own gift and work-bee customs.
- **Migrant workers and the European diaspora:** remittances and what they buy back home in standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** custom is rich in oral and ethnographic sources but rarely measured; household-transfer and remittance statistics exist only at region level; no trust or gift-exchange experiments in the republic are known to the agent.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Nogai and Kumyk; German and French for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** giving tied to loyalty to the authorities; forced "voluntary" contributions, if reported, need careful sourcing; funeral practice is private.
- **Held for review:** none.
