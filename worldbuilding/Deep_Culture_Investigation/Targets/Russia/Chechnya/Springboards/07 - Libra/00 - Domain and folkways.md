## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland and lowland communities** (Argun, Sharoy, Itum-Kale, Vedeno against the Terek-Sunzha plain): lineage councils may differ in the old tower country.
- **Tukkhums and teips** (Akkiy, Melkhiy, Ch'ebarloy, Shatoy, Orstkhoy and others): the units that negotiate marriage and settlement.
- **Sufi wird brotherhoods** (Qadiri lines of Kunta-Haji, Batal-Haji, Vis-Haji, Chimmirza; Naqshbandi) and a Salafi-leaning current: different authorities on law and marriage.
- **Terek and Greben Cossack descendants and Russians** of Naursky and Shelkovskoy districts: Orthodox custom, much reduced after the wars.
- **Nogais and Kumyks** of the northern steppe and eastern villages (Braguny, *check*): Turkic marriage ways.
- **Grozny against rural districts;** families shaped by the 1944 deportation to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan; the diaspora (Jordan, Turkey, Western Europe, Pankisi Kists) for comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich imperial ethnography (colonial, read critically) and rights reporting; thin independent surveys; many archives lost in the wars of 1994-2009. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled** (its tracker carries "Russia" as pre-war lands); this sign is written for the core lands of the former Chechen Republic, with Ingush, Akkin and Kist lands noted only for comparison.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk and Nogai for minorities; Georgian, Turkish and German for diaspora material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war crimes, disappearances, honor violence, persecution of LGBT people, political control; informants' safety first. Memorial was liquidated in 2021-2022 (*check*); its archive may sit abroad.
- **Held for review:** none.
