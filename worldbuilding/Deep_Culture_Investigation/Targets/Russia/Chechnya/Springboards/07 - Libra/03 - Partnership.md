# Chechnya: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (partnership contracts in fiqh); Kumyk and Nogai. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how committed bonds of shared aims, risks and returns are formed and judged here, in marriage, guest-friendship and sworn brotherhood, business, and alliances between lineages and peoples, and who leads inside each bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lineages that marry within their tukkhum and those that marry across it:** the scale at which marriage builds alliances.
- **Grozny professionals and rural extended households:** whether a couple lives apart from parents, and who decides.
- **Nogai and Kumyk families,** and Chechen-Nogai or Chechen-Kumyk mixed marriages in the north and east.
- **Russian and Cossack-descended residents:** civil-registry marriage only, and the remembered Cossack-Chechen guest-friendships.
- **Households with more than one wife:** a sensitive, separate case under federal and religious rules.
- **Traders, builders and diaspora-linked firms** (Moscow, Istanbul, Amman, Western Europe): business partnership beyond the republic.
- **Kists of Pankisi and Akkin Chechens** next door: comparison cases only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: written law, registers and Soviet ethnography are good; the inside of marriages and business partnerships is thinly documented and private.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Nogai and Kumyk; Turkish, Arabic and German for diaspora firms and marriages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced marriage, bride abduction, plural marriage, divorced women's custody; firms tied to officials.
- **Held for review:** none.
