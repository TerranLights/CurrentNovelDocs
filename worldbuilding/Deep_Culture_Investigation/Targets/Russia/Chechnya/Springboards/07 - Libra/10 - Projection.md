*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (envy, the evil eye, labeling as unbeliever); Nogai and Kumyk; Georgian for the Kists. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how faults, force and fears are placed on others here (rivals, neighbors, outsiders, internal dissenters, spirits), how outsiders have placed theirs on Chechens, and which practices of owning one's faults stand against both.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chechens living in Russia's large cities and those at home:** who faces outside images daily.
- **Generations shaped by 1944, by 1958 and by the 1990s wars:** each with its own remembered accuser.
- **Sufi brotherhoods and Salafi-leaning believers:** labels each side may apply to the other.
- **Terek Cossack descendants and Russians:** the mirror images of the frontier.
- **Nogais and Kumyks:** images held of and by the Chechen majority.
- **Ingush and Akkin neighbors, and Pankisi Kists:** kin peoples as rivals and as mirrors.
- **Supporters of the authorities and exiled critics:** each names the other as the enemy within.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on outside images of Chechens (literature, media, polls, archives); thin on Chechen images of others and on everyday blame; evil-eye and jinn beliefs are documented mainly by journalists and a few ethnographers.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Georgian; Nogai and Kumyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic slurs and enemy images must be quoted only as needed; extremist websites are banned in Russia and should not be accessed from there; naming dissenters can endanger them.
- **Held for review:** none.
