## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mothers, wives and daughters:** expected sacrifices, and women's own descriptions of them.
- **Youngest sons and eldest sons:** different duties toward parents and siblings.
- **Veterans' families of 1941-1945:** memory shaped by deportation.
- **Families of those killed in the 1990s and 2000s wars:** whose dead may be mourned in public.
- **Sufi murids and scripturalist Muslims:** different framings of fasting, vows and martyrdom.
- **Terek Cossacks and Russians:** Orthodox Lent and soldiers' sacrifice for Russia.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** Turkic offering customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for war memory and religious practice; thin and politically shaped for recent martyrdom talk.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgent martyrdom material is banned and dangerous to hold; use only scholarship. Commemoration of the wars is controlled, and informants face risk; families' grief is private. Sacrifice demanded by law belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
