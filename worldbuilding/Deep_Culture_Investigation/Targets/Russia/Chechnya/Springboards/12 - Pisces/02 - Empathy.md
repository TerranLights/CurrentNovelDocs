# Chechnya: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for religious teaching on consoling others; Ingush and Kumyk for border and northern communities. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how feeling or grasping what another person feels, from that person's side, is named, trained, expected or withheld in Chechnya's lands, including across group and enemy lines.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women:** who may show feeling for others in public, and in which settings (the *tezet*, the hospital).
- **Elders and the young:** older codes of tact against online emotional talk.
- **Grozny families who lived through the sieges and those who fled:** shared memory against outside accounts.
- **Russian and mixed families in Grozny:** a view from both sides of the war.
- **Former displaced people from the Ingushetia camps:** what being understood, or not, meant to them.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the north:** neighbors whose own losses are less told.
- **Diaspora youth in Austria, France and Norway:** empathy across a family split by borders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in war literature and testimony; thin on measured empathy and everyday practice.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Ingush for border talk; Arabic for religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory and interethnic tension; avoid treating any one memoir as the voice of a people. Informants may not speak freely about the authorities or past fighters.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Chechen-Russian dictionaries for "кхета", "синхаам" and idioms with "дог".
2. Search eLibrary.ru for "тезет чеченцы обряд" and list ethnographers.
3. Read Laudaev's chapters on etiquette and guests; mark passages on reading others.
4. Collect reviews of Sadulaev's and Makanin's books from Russian literary journals.
5. Search the press for "сопереживание" with "Чечня" to see how the word is used locally.
