# Chechnya: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk and Nogai for the northern Muslim minorities; Russian Church Slavonic terms for the Orthodox and Old Believer north. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how people in Chechnya's lands relate to what they hold sacred in experience and practice (prayer, remembrance, pilgrimage, sacred places), as distinct from religion as an institution, which is Sagittarius's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The main vird lines (Kunta-Haji's Qadiri followers, the lines of Bammat-Girey-Haji, Chimmirza and Vis-Haji, and Naqshbandi lines):** whether practice and dress differ is to be checked, as are the names.
- **Women and men:** separate circles, separate forms of remembrance.
- **Scripturalist young Muslims:** who may reject shrine visits and the zikr.
- **Elders who kept practice underground in Soviet times:** a memory of hidden faith.
- **Nogais and Kumyks:** Turkic-language Muslim practice in the north.
- **Old Believers, Orthodox Cossacks and Russians:** a separate Christian sacred landscape.
- **Kists of Pankisi:** outside the lands; a near case with its own Sufi-Salafi divide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on Sufi history; thinner on lived personal practice and on women's practice; one regional survey at most.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk and Nogai; Church Slavonic for the Old Believer north.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** zikr and some shrine rites are not for outsiders' cameras without leave; the Sufi-Salafi divide is security-sensitive and informants face risk. Religion as an institution is Sagittarius's.
- **Held for review:** none.
