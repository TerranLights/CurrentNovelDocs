# Chechnya: Domain and folkways (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen (Cyrillic with the palochka Ӏ; older Arabic- and Latin-script texts), Russian, Arabic; Kumyk, Nogai and Avar for minorities; Turkish for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: the domain of belief and philosophy, i.e. which faiths and worldviews are present in Chechnya's lands, where its peoples say truth and meaning are found, and the two folkways filed here, religious ways and freedom ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland and highland Chechens:** the Terek-Sunzha plain against the Argun, Sharoy, Itum-Kali and Vedeno highlands, to test whether shrine customs differ by altitude.
- **The tukhums and their home districts:** Cheberloy, Sharoy, Shatoy, Melkhi, Ichkerian; each may carry its own saints and sacred sites.
- **Followers of different virds:** Kunta-Hajji's Qadiri lines against the Naqshbandi lines; worship style may divide them.
- **Salafi-leaning believers and secular Soviet-trained families:** two outer edges of the landscape.
- **Terek Cossacks and other Russians** of Naursky and Shelkovskoy districts: Orthodox and Old Believer heritage.
- **Kumyks, Nogais and Avars:** neighboring Muslim traditions with their own languages and teachers.
- **Returned exiles of 1944-1957 and returned refugees from Europe:** faith shaped in Kazakhstan or in Austria and France.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for the 19th-century wars and Sufism; thin for numbers (no census religion item, few surveys sample the republic); much Grozny archive material was lost in the 1990s wars.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian essential; Arabic for manuscripts and teachers' lines; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religion and freedom are politically charged; public dissent carries real risk, so interviews need care and anonymity. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled (its row carries "Russia"); this sign's sheets are written for the core Chechen lands.
- **Held for review:** none.
