## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Grozny and district towns against highland villages:** school access and the pull of the capital.
- **Women and men:** enrollment, fields of study and what families expect after a degree.
- **Secular-track and religious-track students:** university against institute and madrasa.
- **Soviet-trained intelligentsia families and war-era cohorts:** schooling interrupted by 1994-2009.
- **Kumyk, Nogai and Russian pupils:** language of instruction and access.
- **Diaspora students in Europe and the Middle East:** degrees earned abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good official statistics and university websites; much Soviet-era archive material lost; independent assessments of quality are scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Chechen; Arabic for religious learning; German or French for diaspora schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** study abroad at some religious institutions draws security attention, so treat such students' identities with care. Exam and degree integrity is politically touchy.
- **Held for review:** none.
