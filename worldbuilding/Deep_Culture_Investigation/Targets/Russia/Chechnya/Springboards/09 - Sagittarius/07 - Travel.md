## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland families moved to the plain after 1957:** journeys of return that ended short of home.
- **Men and women:** who may travel alone, for work, study or pilgrimage.
- **Hajjis and ziyarat pilgrims against tourists:** sacred and leisure travel.
- **Shepherd households on seasonal rounds:** mobility tied to herds.
- **Nogais and Kumyks:** steppe and plain histories of movement.
- **Returnees from Europe and those who stayed abroad:** the road as exile or as escape.
- **Diaspora branches in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, Georgia and Kazakhstan:** each with its own journey story.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for deportation and emigration; mixed for pilgrimage and tourism; thin for women's travel experience.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Turkish, Arabic; German, Polish or French for European asylum.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugees and returnees may fear exposure; asylum records are confidential. Travel abroad by critics has been watched, so protect informants.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Ottoman archive catalog for "Çeçen muhacir" and the 1865 date range.

4. Download Eurostat asylum tables for Russian citizens, 2003-2015, as a proxy for the refugee route.
5. Search Georgian and Russian sources for "кистинцы Панкиси" and cross-border visits.
