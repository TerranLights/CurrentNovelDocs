# Chechnya: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Nogai and Kumyk for minority Muslims; Church Slavonic and Russian for the Orthodox and Old Believers. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how trust in and commitment to the unseen (God, saints, a sacred order, and secular objects such as the nation) is named, practiced, kept, lost and changed in Chechnya's lands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Qadiri followers of Kunta-Hajji's lines against Naqshbandi followers:** different zikr and possibly different ideas of devotion.
- **Families of Vis-Hajji, Bammat-Girey-Hajji (Avtury) and Chimmirza (Mairtup) lines:** to see whether a vird shapes household faith.
- **Women and men:** separate spaces and repertoires of devotion.
- **Generations:** Soviet-raised elders, war-years youth, post-2007 youth under the official religious policy.
- **Orthodox Russians and Cossack descendants:** a small Christian community.
- **Nogais, Kumyks and Avars:** neighboring Muslim traditions with their own teachers.
- **Returnees from Europe and diaspora visitors:** faith formed abroad meeting faith at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in religious and devotional material; thin and uneven in survey numbers.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen (hymns, sermons), Russian (scholarship, archives), Arabic (doctrinal texts).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** faith outside the official line can draw state attention; protect sources. Vird membership is personal and should not be asked casually.
- **Held for review:** none.
