## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of highland teips** (from Itum-Kale, Sharoi, Cheberloi) against **lowland families**: how much the clan and the ancestral village still organize kin.
- **The clan unions (тукхумы)**, such as Cheberloi, Shatoi, Malkhi and Akki: do they still mean anything to families? An open question.
- **Orstkhoy and Akkin families**, whose kin cross republic borders.
- **Ingush families**, should the nation's lands take in Ingushetia: a neighboring kin system with clan names of its own.
- **Terek and Grebensky Cossack families** and Old Believers: a Russian kin system with its own memory.
- **Kumyk and Nogai families**, with Turkic lineage terms.
- **Mixed Chechen-Russian and Chechen-Dagestani families**, mostly in Grozny and border towns.
- **Families split by the wars**, between officials and fighters, and between the republic and refugees in Europe.
- **The old diaspora** in Turkey, Jordan and Syria, and the Kazakhstan Chechens.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography and oral tradition; thin in independent survey data from inside the republic. Teip lists posted online vary widely and need checking against scholarship.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for religious material and Jordan; Turkish; Kumyk and Nogai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collective punishment of insurgents' kin and honor-based violence appear in human-rights reports; some genealogies are family property, to be used only with consent.
