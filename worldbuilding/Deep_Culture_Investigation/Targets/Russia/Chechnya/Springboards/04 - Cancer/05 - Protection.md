## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men, women and children**: who guards, who is guarded, and who decides.
- **Highland villages** against **lowland towns**: weapons at home, distance from police and rescue.
- **Families of the security forces** against **families of former fighters**: protection by the state against fear of it.
- **Terek Cossacks** and the remaining Russian residents: a minority's sense of who protects it.
- **Kumyk and Nogai villagers** in the northeast and steppe.
- **Grozny residents** against villagers, with cameras, gates and guards in the city.
- **Refugees abroad** who sought protection in Europe, including LGBT Chechens.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Human-rights and wartime material is rich; independent data on everyday protection at home is thin, and official sources are one-sided.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for prayer and amulet texts; English, German and French for asylum and human-rights material; Kumyk and Nogai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor-based violence, collective punishment of kin, persecution of LGBT people and reprisals against informants; sources and interviewees must be protected.
