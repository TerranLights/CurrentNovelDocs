# Chechnya: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Kumyk, Nogai and Ingush for minorities; Arabic for religious community; Turkish, German and French for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** to research what it takes, in Chechnya's lands, to be accepted as part of a group or a place and to feel so, and how outsiders and newcomers are placed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old lowland villages** of the Sunzha and Argun valleys against **highland societies** (Malkhi, Maisti, Terloi, Cheberloi): who counts as a local in a village, and who is a newcomer from the mountains.
- **Teips of outside origin** against those with long-told Chechen genealogies.
- **Orstkhoy and Akkin Chechens**, who belong to two republics at once.
- **Ingush**, the other Vainakh people, whose sense of being a separate people matters if their lands join the nation.
- **Terek Cossacks and the remaining Russian residents**: belonging as a minority in their own stanitsas.
- **Kumyks and Nogais**, with Turkic languages and ties to Dagestan.
- **Members of different Sufi brotherhoods** and young Muslims outside them (a sensitive line).
- **Returnees** from Europe, Kazakhstan and the Middle East, and the **children of mixed marriages**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich history and political writing; thin survey data on felt belonging; the official discourse on national identity is loud and needs balancing with private voices.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for religious material; German, French and Turkish for the diaspora; Kumyk and Nogai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious affiliation is policed and labels such as "Wahhabi" can endanger people; the separatist period's symbols are banned in Russia; the persecution of LGBT people bars them from open belonging. Citizenship rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
