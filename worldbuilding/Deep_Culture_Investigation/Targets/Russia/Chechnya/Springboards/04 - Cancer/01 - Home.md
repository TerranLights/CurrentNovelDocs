# Chechnya: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Ingush, Kumyk and Nogai for border villages; Arabic and Turkish for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** to research the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means: the dwelling, the household's base, and the wider place called home (village, homeland).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland families with an ancestral tower village** (Itum-Kale, Sharoi, Shatoi) against lowland families: is home the house one lives in or the village one comes from?
- **Families resettled from the mountains to the plains** after the 1957 return: a home that is not the ancestral one.
- **Grozny flat-dwellers** against village households with yards.
- **Terek Cossack and Old Believer stanitsa households** in the north: a different house type and a different homeland story.
- **Kumyk and Nogai villagers** in the northeast and the steppe.
- **Families displaced by the wars** who lived in temporary accommodation centers, and **returnees from Europe**.
- **The 1944 deportation generation**, for whom Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan was also home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: housing statistics and literature are good; first-hand accounts of what home means are scattered across memoirs, songs and the press.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Turkish and Arabic for emigrant memory; Kumyk and Nogai for minority villages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime destruction of homes and human-rights reports of relatives' houses demolished as punishment (*check*) are politically charged; some songs of homeland are banned in Russia as extremist, so avoid them as pointers and handle any reference with care. Property and ownership go to Taurus; housing law and compensation rules to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the 2021 census housing tables for the Chechen Republic and set dwelling type against urban or rural setting.
2. Search library catalogs for «Сулейманов Топонимия Чечено-Ингушетии» and list the volumes covering highland districts.
3. Search «родовое село» together with «чеченцы» in Russian scholarship and the press to find accounts of ancestral villages.
4. Search Memorial's published deportation testimonies for "дом" and "возвращение" (return).
5. Run «дом» with district names on Avito and save twenty listings as a sample of how family homes are described.
