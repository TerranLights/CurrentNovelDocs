# Chechnya: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (scriptural duties); Ingush, Kumyk and Georgian for kin and minority communities; Turkish and German for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: what a person in Chechnya's lands is held to owe, unasked, to parents, kin, guests, neighbors, the dead, God and the state, and how failing that is met.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland villages and the plains:** communal work and herding duties survived differently.
- **Families with deportation dead buried in Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan:** reburial as an unfinished duty.
- **Sufi vird members:** duties to the sheikh's line and its shrines (зиярт pilgrimages *check*).
- **Women:** duties as daughters-in-law (нускал, *check*) and as widows after the wars; separate their own accounts from men's accounts of them.
- **Younger urban Chechens** in Grozny and Moscow: duties weighed against work and study elsewhere.
- **Russian and Cossack remnants** of Naursky district: Orthodox and military-service duties.
- **Nogai and Kumyk villages:** Turkic kin obligations.
- **Diaspora** in Turkey and Jordan (old emigration) against Europe (refugees of the 2000s).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography of kin duties; present-day data thin and filtered.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Georgian, Turkish and German for borderland and diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blood-revenge duty touches living feuds; war service and coerced volunteering; gendered duties and violence against women; family liability for relatives' acts. Protect informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
