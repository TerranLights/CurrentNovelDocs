# Chechnya: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (Islamic jurisprudence); Ingush, Kumyk and Georgian for kin and minority communities; French and German for refugee communities. **Concept in one line:** to be researched, for the spirit of the law only (Capricorn): what people in Chechnya's lands take law to be for, whose law each of custom, sharia and Russian state law is held to be, and when people obey, use, bend or avoid each. The wording of statutes belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland and lowland villages:** where custom or state law reaches first.
- **Qadiri and Naqshbandi communities and Salafi-leaning believers:** different views of sharia's reach and of custom.
- **Women:** how each order treats inheritance, divorce and custody, from women's own accounts.
- **Former Ichkerian officials and fighters:** memories of the sharia courts.
- **Security-force members and officials:** law as an instrument of the present rule.
- **Nogai, Kumyk and remaining Russian communities:** different customary layers.
- **Ingush kin; Kists of Pankisi; diaspora in Turkey, Jordan and Europe** under foreign legal systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the nineteenth century and the Soviet campaigns; for today, mostly Lazarev, ECHR files and human-rights reporting; official statements must be read as claims.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian first, Arabic for qadi and sharia-court sources; Georgian for Pankisi, French and German for refugee mediation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extrajudicial punishment; so-called honor killings; disappearances; informants who describe avoiding state courts may be at risk. Keep to the spirit of the law; the text of codes goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Bobrovnikov (2002) for the custom-sharia-state framework.
2. Search eLibrary for "правовой плюрализм Чечня" and "адат шариат Чечня".
3. Search HUDOC for "Chechnya" and sort judgments by year.
4. Search Kavkaz-uzel for "суд по адату" and "шариатский суд Чечня".
5. Search the Judicial Department's site for the Chechen Republic's regional tables.
