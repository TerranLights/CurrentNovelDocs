## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Veterans of the separatist forces and members of the republic's current security forces:** each may call the other traitors; separate their accounts.
- **Families who lost members to informers:** memory of betrayal at village level.
- **The deportation generation:** the state's charge of collective treason as personal history.
- **Sufi brotherhoods and Salafi believers:** mutual charges of hypocrisy.
- **Women and men:** whose infidelity is named betrayal, and how each is punished.
- **Highland and lowland teips:** old alliances and grudges between kin groups.
- **Russians and Cossacks of the northern districts:** loyalty to Russia, and how neighbors saw them in 1991-94.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** minorities caught between sides.
- **European diaspora and Pankisi Kists:** loyalty tested from outside the republic.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war-era side-switching and the deportation charge; thin for everyday betrayal and infidelity; no survey data likely.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic terms; Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** calling any living person a traitor is dangerous here; separatist media and some insurgent songs are banned in Russia as extremist (legal risk to hold or share); diaspora killings. Treason as a criminal charge goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
