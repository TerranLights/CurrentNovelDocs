## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The ruling family's circle and security units:** power at the center.
- **Teips with members in the elite against those without:** access by kin.
- **Field commanders' families from the 1990s:** former power holders, some reconciled, some exiled.
- **Sufi sheikhs and saintly lineages:** spiritual power with its own following.
- **Salafi preachers:** a rival religious claim, largely suppressed.
- **Women:** informal power in households and rare public roles.
- **Highland and lowland communities:** distance from the center of power.
- **Russians, Cossacks, Kumyks, Nogais:** weight of minorities in the districts they live in.
- **Exiles in Europe and Pankisi Kists:** power claimed or felt from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in political science and journalism; thin on how ordinary people speak of power among themselves; official sources are self-presentations.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for the imamate chronicles; Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** criticism of the ruler and his circle; exiles at risk; field sources must stay anonymous. Legitimate authority, hierarchy and obedience go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
