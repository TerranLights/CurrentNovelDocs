## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland teips (Shatoy, Itum-Kale, Sharoy areas):** older feuds tied to land and pasture.
- **Lowland and foothill towns:** feuds amid dense, mixed settlements.
- **Families split by the wars (separatist and loyalist sides):** blood debts across political lines.
- **Families of those killed by state forces:** revenge aimed at institutions rather than families.
- **Sufi brotherhood members and Salafi believers:** different religious arguments for forgiveness or retribution.
- **Women:** the mothers who demand or refuse revenge; women as mediators.
- **Kumyk and Nogai neighbors:** their own feud customs and cross-ethnic disputes.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** a minority outside the feud system.
- **Pankisi Kists and European exiles:** feud kept, changed or dropped abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and political science; mixed in present-day ethnography; statistics weak.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen first, then Russian; Arabic for the vocabulary of vengeance and pardon; Georgian for Kist feud studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** active feuds put named people at real risk; never publish names of families in a feud. The feud's rules as law, and private vengeance set against the state's punishment, go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
