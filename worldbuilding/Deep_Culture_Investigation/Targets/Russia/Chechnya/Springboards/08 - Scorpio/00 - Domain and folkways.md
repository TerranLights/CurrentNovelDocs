## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland societies (Itum-Kale, Shatoy, Sharoy, Cheberloy, Melkhi):** crypt burial and older rites.
- **Lowland and foothill villages (Urus-Martan, Shali, Gudermes):** larger communities, other land holding.
- **Grozny:** urban funerals, rebuilt cemeteries, state ceremony.
- **The tukkhums (Nokhchmakhkoy, Akkiy, Orstkhoy, Terloy and others):** teip federations that may differ in custom.
- **Sufi wird brotherhoods (Kunta-Haji, Vis-Haji, Batal-Haji, Chimmirza; Naqshbandi lines):** different funeral zikr and saint-tomb practice.
- **Salafi-leaning believers:** dispute tomb visits and mourning customs.
- **Terek Cossacks and Russians (Naursky, Shelkovskoy districts):** Orthodox and Old Believer death ways in the north.
- **Nogais and Kumyks:** Turkic-speaking Muslim minorities with their own funeral and inheritance customs.
- **The deportation generation, born in Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan:** graves left behind, reburial.
- **Kists in Pankisi (Georgia); diasporas in Jordan, Turkey, Syria, Western Europe:** death ways kept or changed abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century ethnography, deportation and war documentation; thin for present-day surveys of sex ways, household money and inheritance; Soviet sources skew to a "survivals" frame.
- **Lands:** the tracker gives the pre-war lands as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the core Chechen lands, with the Kist and diaspora communities as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic; Kumyk, Nogai, Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual diversity (the 2017 persecution reports), honor violence, disappearances, the 1944 deportation, criticism of the government; informants may face real risk. Blood feud and inheritance as law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
