## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Close kin and teip members against unrelated Chechens:** where the radius of trust may stop.
- **Guests and hosts:** hospitality as trust given to an outsider.
- **Traders at Berkat and in the bazaar towns (Shali, Gudermes, Urus-Martan):** commercial trust on credit.
- **Families who lost members to informers or detention:** trust in neighbors and authority after war.
- **Sufi brotherhood members:** trust in a sheikh and fellow murids.
- **Young people raised on social media:** trust in online voices over older institutions.
- **Russians, Cossacks, Kumyks and Nogais:** trust across ethnic lines in shared districts.
- **Pankisi Kists:** trust in the Georgian state and in kin across the border.
- **Refugees and the older diaspora (Jordan, Turkey):** trust kept among exiles and toward the homeland's authorities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin on measured trust (no reliable regional survey likely); richer in ethnography of hospitality and in political accounts. Official figures on elections and opinion need context.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Arabic for religious terms; Georgian for Pankisi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asking about trust in authorities can endanger respondents; anonymity is essential. Trust in police and courts is the spirit of the law, researched under Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
