## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The deportation generation and their children:** crisis as the founding memory.
- **Families displaced to Ingushetia, Dagestan and abroad in 1999-2004:** camp life and return.
- **Grozny residents:** a city destroyed twice and rebuilt.
- **Highland villages:** cut off in war, hit by landmines and floods.
- **Terek and Sunzha lowland settlements:** flood and earthquake exposure.
- **Russians and Cossacks who fled in the early 1990s:** their own crisis, seldom told in the republic.
- **Kumyk and Nogai communities:** how minorities fared in shared disasters.
- **Pankisi Kists:** host community in a refugee crisis.
- **Older diaspora (Jordan, Turkey, Syria) and recent refugees in Europe:** crises carried across generations and borders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war, displacement and deportation (humanitarian and human rights records); thinner for natural hazards and for everyday personal crises; official figures may be contested.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen and Russian; Ottoman Turkish and Arabic for the 1860s; Georgian; English and Polish for European records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war trauma, contested death tolls, commemorations tied to the current authorities; survivors' testimony needs care and consent. Emergency law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
