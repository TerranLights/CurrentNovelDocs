## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Qadiri wird members (followers of the Kunta-Haji line, Vis-Haji, Batal-Haji) and Naqshbandi followers:** different routes of initiation and repentance.
- **Salafi converts within Chechen families:** rebirth as a break with family religion; a sharp contrast.
- **Former fighters who changed sides or were amnestied:** a contested redemption.
- **Returnee families from Central Asia:** the 1957 return as a lived new start; some never returned.
- **Grozny residents and rural villagers:** rebuilt city against slowly rebuilt villages.
- **Highlanders resettled in the lowlands:** whole villages moved in the 1940s and 1950s and later.
- **Russians, Cossacks and Armenians of old Grozny:** those for whom the "revival" may mean loss.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** their own revival movements since 1990.
- **Young people born after 2000:** rebirth discourse aimed at them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official renewal discourse is plentiful; independent accounts of repentance, recovery and the return of fighters are thinner and partly hidden.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic terms; Georgian for Pankisi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** amnesty and "returned" fighters; converts out of Islam (a risk to them); official framing of reconstruction. Rehabilitation of repressed peoples as law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
