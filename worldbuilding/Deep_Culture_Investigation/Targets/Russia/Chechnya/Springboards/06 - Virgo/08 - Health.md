## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain villages far from hospitals** against **Grozny:** access and the place of home cures.
- **Families who use ruqya and hijama** alongside clinics, and those who do not.
- **Wird communities and text-literal believers:** different views of shrines and amulets.
- **War survivors, the displaced and returnees:** trauma and chronic illness.
- **Women:** childbirth, the postpartum period and who decides on care.
- **Men:** whether doctors are avoided; to research, not assume.
- **Oil-belt and refinery-area residents:** environmental illness.
- **Terek Cossack descendants:** Orthodox healing prayer and Russian folk medicine.
- **Nogai and Kumyk communities:** Turkic healers and steppe remedies.
- **Ingush residents:** their own healers and shrine visits.
- **Diaspora in Europe:** health under other systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Federal statistics and NGO reports exist; folk-medicine ethnography is moderate; reliable mental-health data is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk, Nogai; Norwegian, German and Polish for refugee studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mental illness and war trauma; jinn and possession beliefs; HIV and addiction; reproductive health; the healers' own knowledge only as they share it.
- **Held for review:** none.
