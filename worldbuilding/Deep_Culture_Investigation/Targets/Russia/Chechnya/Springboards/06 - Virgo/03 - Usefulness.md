# Chechnya: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Kumyk, Nogai, Ingush; Arabic for hadith. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how being of practical value to others, and being a burden, are named in Chechnya's lands, and what standing the old, the sick, the disabled and the jobless are given by those words.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders in mountain villages** against **elders in Grozny apartments:** whether a role survives the move to the city.
- **War-disabled men and landmine survivors:** usefulness after injury.
- **Jobless young men** in lowland towns: idleness as a social worry.
- **Women in multi-generation households:** grandmothers' and daughters-in-law's daily use.
- **Rural children** (herding, water, fields) against **urban schoolchildren:** what tasks are given.
- **Formerly displaced families** who returned from camps.
- **Terek Cossack descendants, Nogai and Kumyk communities:** their own words and tales of the useful and the idle.
- **Ingush residents:** whether elders and the disabled hold different roles in Ingush households.
- **Diaspora asylum seekers in Europe:** usefulness under work bans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore and ethnopedagogy are rich in Russian; disability and pension data exist federally; attitude data on feeling useful is likely thin.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for hadith; Kumyk, Nogai; German and Polish for diaspora studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability and war injury; mental health; male unemployment; displaced-person histories; research with the disabled needs consent and care.
- **Held for review:** none.
