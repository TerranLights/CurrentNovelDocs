## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain host households** against **Grozny's apartment households:** whether guest service keeps its form in the city.
- **Qadiri wird groups** (for example the Kunta-Hajji, Vis-Hajji and Batal-Hajji lines) and **Naqshbandi followers:** different forms of service to a teacher.
- **Young brides, unmarried daughters, sons and grandsons:** who serves at table and at gatherings.
- **Veterans** of Soviet, Russian and other armies: service remembered differently by era.
- **Civil servants and police:** state service as a job and as a status.
- **Terek Cossack descendants:** service as an estate's duty to the state.
- **Nogai and Kumyk villages:** Turkic hospitality and service terms.
- **Ingush residents:** guest and elder service to test separately.
- **Diaspora in Jordan, Turkey and Europe:** service to host states and communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on hospitality and custom (ethnography is plentiful in Russian); thin on paid service work and volunteering as measured; war-era humanitarian records exist but are scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for Sufi texts; Kumyk, Nogai; Arabic and Turkish for diaspora service.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current military and security service is politically charged; the daughter-in-law's duties touch gender pressures; historical bondage (лай) is a lineage-status matter some families keep quiet; informant safety.
- **Held for review:** none.
