*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian; Arabic for self-refinement; Nogai, Kumyk, Ingush. **Concept in one line:** to be researched: how making something better (oneself, a house, land, a town, a people) is named in Chechnya's lands, which improvements were chosen and which were imposed by outside powers, and who judged what "better" meant.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain-origin families resettled to the plain:** whether "betterment" is remembered as gain or loss.
- **Grozny and the rebuilt towns** against **villages:** who saw the new buildings, who did not.
- **Wird members:** self-refinement under a teacher.
- **Young people in vocational and university programs:** betterment through study.
- **Women:** home improvement, schooling and the limits set on change.
- **Terek Cossack descendants:** de-Cossackization and revival.
- **Nogai herders:** sedentarization and its memory.
- **Kumyk villages:** their own development story.
- **Ingush residents:** how their own postwar rebuilding is spoken of.
- **Diaspora:** improvement of children's prospects abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian for Tsarist and Soviet policy; official sources dominate the postwar period; independent evaluation is thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Nogai, Kumyk; Turkish and Arabic for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** imperial conquest and deportation framed as "improvement"; postwar reconstruction and its politics; state moral-upbringing programs; never present official claims of progress as findings.
- **Held for review:** none.
