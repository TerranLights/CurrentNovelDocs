*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain districts** (Itum-Kale, Sharoy, Shatoy, Vedeno, Nozhai-Yurt, Cheberloy): terraces, herding and tower-building; a different yearly round.
- **Lowland Terek and Sunzha villages:** field farming and the collective-farm legacy.
- **Grozny and Gudermes:** state and oil employment; an urban, commuting day.
- **Tukkhum and teip affiliations:** whether mutual-help duties differ by lineage.
- **Terek Cossack stanitsas** (Naursky, Shelkovskoy districts): Russian-speaking Orthodox farming tradition and its *помочи*.
- **Nogai and Kumyk communities** (north and northeast; Braguny): Turkic-speaking steppe and village work ways.
- **Ingush residents:** close kin, separate customs to test.
- **Qadiri and Naqshbandi followers:** how the wirds' rules shape the day.
- **Women and men; elders and the young:** separate household and public work worlds.
- **Generations:** the exile-born, the Soviet return generation, the postwar young.
- **Diaspora:** Jordan, Turkey, Kazakhstan, Austria, France, Germany, Belgium, Norway, Poland: how work ways traveled.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in Russian for Tsarist and Soviet times; thin in Chechen print; war damage (1994-2000) may have destroyed Grozny's local archives (*check*). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this Virgo set is written for the core Chechen lands.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Kumyk, Nogai, Ingush; Turkish, German and French for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the political climate; employment in security structures; human-rights reporting (some groups now closed); women's work; informant safety; war trauma.
- **Held for review:** none.
