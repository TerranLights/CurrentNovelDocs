# Chechnya: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (for old chronicles and gravestones); Kazakh and Arabic for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families who returned to their own highland villages** against **families resettled to the plains** after 1957 and never allowed back up.
- **Grozny's pre-war residents** against **newcomers from the villages** who now fill the rebuilt city.
- **Wird communities** whose weekly *zikr* runs back generations, against **converts to newer practice**.
- **Old Believer Cossack descendants** in Chervlyonnaya and nearby stanitsas, and the **Russians who left** after 1991.
- **Nogai** households, settled herders whose own changes came with collectivization.
- **Chechens who stayed in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan**, and the **old diaspora** in Jordan, Turkey and Syria.
- **Akkin Chechens** of Dagestan, who returned in 1957 but not always to their own villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on genealogical memory and upheaval; good census and migration series; little direct study of routine and daily continuity.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (teptars and gravestones); Kazakh and Arabic for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memorial dates and the deportation memorial are politically charged; teip genealogies can be contested between families, and some are kept private; the official "stability" story is not safe to dispute on record.
- **Held for review:** none.
