## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Veterans of the 1990s separatist side**, those who later joined the republic's forces, and **those who never fought**: holding firm, changing sides and staying out.
- **Feuding families** and the **elders and imams who reconcile them**.
- **Highland villages that refused to move down** after 1957 against those that resettled.
- **Women who held households together** through the wars: tenacity without arms.
- **Old Believer Cossack descendants** and **Nogai herders**.
- **Exiles in Europe and Turkey** who reject the present government, against **returnees** who made peace with it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history, legend and outsiders' images; thin on everyday stubbornness (habits, quarrels, haggling); psychological data is sparse.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; English, French and Georgian for outsiders and neighbors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "unconquerable" image is a stereotype with a long colonial history; treat it as a source to examine; anything that praises or blames armed resistance is politically dangerous to discuss on record.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search library catalogs for "Шейх Мансур" and "Байсангур Беноевский" and list folklore sources among the results.
2. Read Layton's chapters on Lermontov and Tolstoy, then set them beside Chechen-authored accounts.
3. Search Grozny-Inform's archive for "примирение кровников" and note the years and numbers reported.
4. Search dissercat.com for "жизнестойкость чеченских подростков".
5. Search regional news for "Хасуха Магомадов" and compare official and family accounts.
