*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland teips** whose ancestral villages were emptied after 1944 and never fully resettled, against the **lowland villages** where most now live: a claim kept without use.
- **Akkin (Aukh) Chechens** in Dagestan, with Laks and Avars as the other side of the same land.
- **Cossack descendants and Russian residents** of Naursky and Shelkovskoy, and those who left in the 1990s and their claims to houses.
- **Nogai herders** of the northern steppe: open pasture against fenced plots.
- **Grozny's returnees** whose apartments were destroyed, against those rehoused in new blocks.
- **Women as owners**: houses and plots held in a woman's name, as a question to check.
- **Kists of Pankisi**, who hold land under Georgian rule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good imperial and Soviet archives; modern registry data exists, but much holding is informal; public opinion on private against state ownership is almost never broken out for Chechnya.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian; Lak and Avar for the Aukh dispute; Arabic for waqf and the Jordanian diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land quarrels between teips and with neighboring peoples can still turn violent; property tied to the ruling family is dangerous to research on the ground; the letter of property law, and *adat* and sharia rules on land, belong to Libra; the spirit of law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
