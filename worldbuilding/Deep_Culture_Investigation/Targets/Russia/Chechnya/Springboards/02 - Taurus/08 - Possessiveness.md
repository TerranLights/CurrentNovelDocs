# Chechnya: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chechnya, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic (religious terms); Nogai; Ingush for the border question; Georgian and German for neighbors and the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Grozny's educated women and men under thirty-five** against **their parents' generation**: whether jealousy and control are read as love.
- **Families after divorce**, mothers and fathers separately: who keeps the children, and how each side tells it.
- **Village communities guarding pasture and forest** against neighbors, including across the Ingush and Dagestani lines.
- **Akkin Chechens** of Khasavyurt and Novolaksky, where a territorial claim is part of daily life.
- **Nogai and Cossack households** with their own wedding and family customs.
- **Kists** in Georgia and **Chechen families in Austria, Germany, France and Belgium**, where local law and custom pull the other way.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin on measured jealousy and control; richer in rights reports, news and ethnography of weddings.
- **Languages needed:** Chechen, Russian, Arabic; Ingush and Dagestani languages for the borders; German and French for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** among the most dangerous topics for informants, especially women; never name individuals from rights reports in a public file; avoid reading one family's control as a people's character; the legal side of custody and marriage goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
