# Dagestan: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgian, Lak, Azerbaijani, Chechen, Tabasaran; Arabic for the judge's ethics. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar and Dargin officials' networks**, since the largest peoples hold most posts and face the most favoritism charges.
- **Kumyks**, who often speak as a plains people treated unfairly by highland officials.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans of the south**, for whether the capital is seen as fair to distant districts.
- **Laks**, a small people strong in the professions, to test favoritism against merit.
- **Nogais and Rutuls**, small peoples who may argue for quotas as protection against bias.
- **Akkin Chechens**, where teip ties may cross the border with Chechnya.
- **Andi-Tsez villages**, where everyone in a court case may be related.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis and Mountain Jews**, minority communities in a city with its own networks.
- **Kizlyar Russians**, outsiders to the kin networks, for how they judge them.
- **Sufi brotherhoods and their followers**, whose loyalty to a sheikh may shape appointments.
- **Salafi communities**, who often claim the state favors one religious current.
- **Young professionals and returning migrants**, for views of hiring by kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; much press and expert comment, few measured surveys; court and election data exist in federal systems.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for classical judge ethics; Avar and Dargin for oral tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming families tied to office is dangerous; journalists have been killed (Gadzhimurad Kamalov, 2011 *check*); Golos and Transparency International Russia are labeled by the state, so their reports carry legal risk to cite inside Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.
