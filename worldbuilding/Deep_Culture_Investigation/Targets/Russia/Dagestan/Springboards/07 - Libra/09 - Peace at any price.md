# Dagestan: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families in blood-feud reconciliations**, since the victim's side bears the price of peace.
- **Women in highland Avar and Dargin villages**, where silence about violence may protect a family's name.
- **Sufi murids**, taught patience and loyalty to the ruler.
- **Salafi communities**, who often reject that quietism, and some of whom were pressed by state peace programs.
- **Former militants and their families**, the direct subject of the adaptation policy.
- **Laks and Akkin Chechens of Novolak**, where a land settlement asks both sides to give way.
- **Kumyks**, for whether plains villages accept highland settlers quietly.
- **Lezgins**, for acceptance of the 1990s border and what was given up.
- **Tabasarans and Aguls**, small peoples who may avoid quarrels with larger neighbors.
- **Mountain Jews**, a small minority with a long habit of caution.
- **Kizlyar Russians**, for frontier Russian sayings of endurance.
- **Women protesters of 2022**, who broke the rule of silence in public.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rights groups and journalists document hidden harm; scholarship covers historical quietism; surveys of conflict avoidance in the republic are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for Sufi teaching; Avar and Dargin for village interviews; Azerbaijani for Derbent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor violence, female cutting and feud pressure endanger informants; insurgent extortion of businesses and the adaptation program involve security services; mobilization protest is prosecuted.
- **Held for review:** none.
