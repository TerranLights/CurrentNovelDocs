# Dagestan: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgian, Lak, Chechen, Azerbaijani, Nogai, Tabasaran; Arabic for legal and religious texts. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar highland villages**, because the Imamate's sharia courts were rooted there.
- **Dargin communities of the Akusha union**, for a long record of their own qadi courts and union rulings.
- **Kumyk plain towns**, where princely courts gave nobles and commoners unequal remedies.
- **Lezgins on both sides of the Samur**, since justice ties reach into Azerbaijan's courts.
- **Laks of Novolak and Kumukh**, for the redress dispute over resettled land.
- **Akkin Chechens of Khasavyurt and Novolak**, as deported and rehabilitated people with claims.
- **Andi-Tsez villages**, where elders' rulings may outlast state courts.
- **Nogais of the steppe**, for bii judges and pastoral wrongs.
- **Shia Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, for a separate jurisprudence.
- **Mountain Jews**, for rabbinical and community settlement.
- **Kizlyar Russians and Cossacks**, for state-court habits.
- **Families of counterinsurgency victims**, for justice as unanswered grievance.
- **Salafi and Sufi communities**, because each claims the true sharia of punishment.
- **Migrants in Surgut, Tyumen and Moscow**, for disputes that follow them away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on nineteenth-century adat and the Imamate; mixed on current courts; current self-help justice sits in rights reports and press, not surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for fiqh and chronicles; Avar, Lezgian and Dargin for oral tradition.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** counterinsurgency victims, blood feud cases and anti-extremism trials; interviewees risk harm; Memorial was liquidated by Russian courts (2021) and its archives sit abroad.
- **Held for review:** none.
