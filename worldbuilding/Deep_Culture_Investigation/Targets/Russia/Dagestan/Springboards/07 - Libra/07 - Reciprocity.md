*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar highland villages**, where guest law and work parties are best described.
- **Dargin villages**, including Kubachi, to test whether craft wealth changes gift sizes.
- **Laks**, whose migrants returned money and goods to home villages for generations.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**, for work-party customs and wedding gifts in the south.
- **Kumyk towns**, where plain-dwellers host highland relatives moving down.
- **Nogais**, for steppe hospitality and livestock gifts.
- **Akkin Chechens**, whose collective-help forms tie them to Chechnya.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, for wedding cash gifts and Shia mourning offerings.
- **Mountain Jews**, for gift and hospitality customs within a small community.
- **Kizlyar Russians and Cossacks**, for Orthodox feast-day exchange.
- **Sufi brotherhoods**, for gifts to sheikhs and the help they give back.
- **Salafi communities**, who may reject shrine vows and costly weddings.
- **Migrants in the north and their home villages**, for remittances and returns.
- **Urban Makhachkala families**, as a test of whether the village's accounts survive in the city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hospitality and wedding custom; mixed for funerals and shrines; thin for measured household transfers at the republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for waqf and gift law; Avar, Lezgian and Lak for oral tradition; Juhuri for Mountain Jewish terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wedding spending is a public quarrel between the muftiate, Salafis and families; shrine visits are disputed between Sufis and Salafis; online fundraising can expose the sick.
- **Held for review:** none.
