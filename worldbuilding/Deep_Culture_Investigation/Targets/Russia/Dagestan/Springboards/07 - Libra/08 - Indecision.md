## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sufi murids**, whose sheikh may make choices for them.
- **Salafi believers**, who may reject sheikhs and decide by text alone.
- **Avar highland villages**, where elders' councils may keep questions open until all agree.
- **Dargin unions of villages**, with old assembly habits.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plain**, for an older princely or steppe chain of command.
- **Laks**, whose long migration made deciding to leave or stay a household question.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**, for southern families' choices across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis**, where Shia scholars may be asked instead.
- **Mountain Jews**, whose emigration choices split families.
- **Kizlyar Russians**, a test of a less kin-bound pattern.
- **Young women in Makhachkala**, whose choices of study, work and spouse may be most contested.
- **Returning migrants**, torn between the north and home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin on indecision as such; better on who decides (sheikh, elders, parents) and on ways of handing a choice to God or chance.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for prayer manuals and manuscripts; Avar and Dargin for literature and proverbs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** divination is condemned by many religious authorities, so practitioners may not speak openly; personal choices of women and young people are a family flashpoint.
- **Held for review:** none.
