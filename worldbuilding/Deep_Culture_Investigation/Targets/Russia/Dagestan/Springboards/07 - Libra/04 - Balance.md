## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The largest peoples (Avars, Dargins, Kumyks, Lezgins)**, whose shares of posts are the core of the political balance.
- **Laks**, a smaller people with an outsized role in office and learning, which tests the arithmetic.
- **Smaller peoples (Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs, Nogais)**, who may feel left out of the balance.
- **Andi-Tsez peoples**, folded into Avars, so their weight is hidden.
- **Lezgins of the Samur valley**, for whom water and the border are a live balance.
- **Nogais and Kumyks of the plains**, for land degraded or crowded by newcomers' herds.
- **Highland villages emptied by migration**, for the mountain-plain balance.
- **Lak tightrope walkers of Tsovkra**, for balance as a body skill.
- **Kaitag and Kubachi craftspeople**, for balance as form.
- **Sufi and Salafi believers**, who define the religious middle differently.
- **Kizlyar Russians**, a declining share in the ethnic balance.
- **Working women in Makhachkala**, for time balance between job and home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the political balance among peoples; mixed on folk medicine and ecology; thin on personal work-life balance.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for medical and religious texts; Lezgian and Azerbaijani for Yaran Suvar; Lak for Tsovkra.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic shares of power are a touchy subject; "moderate" and "extremist" labels carry legal risk; abandoned highland villages are a grief for many families.
- **Held for review:** none.
