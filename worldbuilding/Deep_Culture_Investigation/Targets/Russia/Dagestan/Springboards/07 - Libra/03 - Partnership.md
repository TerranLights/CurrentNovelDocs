## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kubachi Dargins**, a village whose craft partnerships are its livelihood.
- **Laks**, for long-distance work crews and trading partnerships.
- **Avars of the highlands**, where marriage within the village and kin shapes who partners whom.
- **Kumyk princely families**, for alliances by marriage and fosterage with neighboring houses.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**, where women's carpet weaving creates partnerships among households.
- **Nogai pastoralists**, whose herding partnerships span long steppe moves.
- **Akkin Chechens**, for teip-based alliances that cross into Chechnya.
- **Mountain Jews**, with trade partnerships tied to a worldwide diaspora.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, for bazaar and family-firm partnerships.
- **Kizlyar Russians and Cossacks**, whose couple and brotherhood forms come from Orthodox tradition.
- **Mixed urban couples in Makhachkala**, as the main place partners cross ethnic lines.
- **Young believers using Islamic finance**, for religion reshaping business partnership.
- **Migrant crews in the north**, where village partners work as one team.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; craft and kinship partnerships are well described in ethnography; couple equality and business partnership need fresh fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for fiqh; Dargin (Kubachi dialect) and Lak for craft histories; Avar for poetry.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic and interfaith marriage can bring family pressure; unregistered religious marriages and polygamy are touchy; Islamic finance is politically watched.
- **Held for review:** none.
