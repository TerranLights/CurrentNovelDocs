## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and Dargins of the former Imamate's heartland,** because memory of serving the Imam may color service to later states.
- **Kumyks of the plain,** because shamkhals' and beks' households kept retainers and dependents, so service had a rank.
- **Laks,** because the Kazikumukh khanate and a line of officers make a separate service history.
- **Lezgins and southern Azerbaijanis,** for service vocabulary shared with Azerbaijan across the border.
- **Terek Cossacks around Kizlyar,** for whom military service was an inherited estate duty.
- **Akkin Chechens,** because the 1944 deportation shapes how service to the state is spoken of.
- **Mountain Jews,** for communal and synagogue service and for an emigrant diaspora that serves the home community from abroad.
- **Sufi disciples and Salafi-leaning communities,** because they disagree about service to a living master.
- **Daughters-in-law, youngest sons and elders,** because household service runs by age and sex.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Imamate and imperial service; mixed for household service; thin for volunteering and paid service.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin, Dargin, Lak; Azerbaijani; Hebrew and Juhuri for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service in current wars, police casualties in the insurgency, disputes between Sufi and Salafi communities, and patronage in public posts are all sensitive; some of the debate happens on banned or designated platforms. Murids' practice may be shared only on the brotherhood's terms. Public office as law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
