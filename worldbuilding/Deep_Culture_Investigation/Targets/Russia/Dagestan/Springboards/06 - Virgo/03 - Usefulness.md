## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland villages with a living *godekan* against Makhachkala apartment blocks,** because elders' daily usefulness depends on a place to give it.
- **Kumyks and Nogais,** to test whether Turkic elder vocabulary carries a different weight.
- **Avars and Dargins,** for large highland families where children's tasks are many.
- **Laks,** because absent migrant fathers may leave the useful work to those at home.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans,** for women's loom work as income and household value.
- **Mountain Jews,** because emigration may leave elders without the young.
- **Kizlyar Russians,** whose household shapes may differ from their neighbors'.
- **Disabled people and their families,** because "burden" talk falls on them first.
- **Unemployed young men and returning rotational workers,** because being needed and being idle meet in them.
- **Speakers of the small highland languages** (Andi and Tsez groups, Archi), because a language's usefulness decides whether it is passed on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folk-pedagogy literature is plentiful but often idealized; disability and elder-care data are federal and thin by people.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin, Dargin, Lak; Arabic; Azerbaijani.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability can carry family shame and marriage concerns; questions about elders as a burden need care; native-language decline is a political topic. End-of-life care touches religion.
- **Held for review:** none.
