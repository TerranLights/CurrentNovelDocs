## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars,** high-mountain against resettled lowland villages: leaving the terraces changed the work itself.
- **Dargins,** with Kubachi and Kaitag apart: whole villages lived by one craft.
- **Laks:** migrant artisanship set the year and who stayed home.
- **Kumyks:** lowland farming and an old estate order decide who served whom.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans:** carpet weaving as women's work; Lezgins also live across the Azerbaijani border.
- **Nogais:** steppe herding on a different calendar from the mountains.
- **Akkin Chechens** (Khasavyurt, Novolaksky): the 1944 deportation and return broke and rebuilt work ties.
- **Mountain Jews and Tats of Derbent:** urban trades and vineyards, with a large emigrant diaspora.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent:** a southern, trade-based urban round.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks around Kizlyar:** vineyards and a frontier service tradition.
- **Small highland peoples** (Andi and Tsez groups, Rutuls, Aguls, Tsakhurs, Archi): isolated pastoral economies, thin records.
- **Makhachkala, the villages, and rotational workers in the north:** three different daily rounds.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian-language ethnography and crafts; mixed in statistics (republic level, little by people); thin for small highland peoples and native-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak; Arabic for manuscripts; Azerbaijani and Turkish for cross-border and diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgency, counter-extremism policing and Sufi-Salafi tension touch service and religion; some outlets carry Russian legal designations. Healers' and Sufi orders' knowledge is shared on their terms.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (its tracker carries "Russia"); this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Dagestan.
- **Held for review:** none.
