## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avars, Dargins and Laks,** for bone-setting and folk surgery far from clinics.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans and Azerbaijanis of the south,** for healing at *pirs* and springs.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plain,** for herders' remedies and different access to towns.
- **Akkin Chechens,** for healing customs shared with Chechnya.
- **Mountain Jews,** for remedies of their own and a diaspora that seeks care in Israel.
- **Kizlyar Russians,** for Orthodox and secular approaches to care.
- **Sufi and Salafi-leaning believers,** because they disagree about shrine visits and healers.
- **Women,** for childbirth, postpartum customs and the care of the sick at home.
- **The very old,** because the long-lived are both a health ideal and a statistic to check.
- **Rotational and migrant workers,** for health and injury far from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk medicine and official statistics; thin for mental health; quality of some vital records needs checking.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Dargin, Lak, Lezgin, Kumyk; Azerbaijani; Hebrew for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mental illness and spirit-possession beliefs carry stigma. A rights group's 2016 report on genital cutting of girls in some highland districts needs great care for the women concerned. Shrine visits are a live dispute between communities. Healers may share knowledge only on their terms. Medical law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
