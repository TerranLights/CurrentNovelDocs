# Dagestan: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Tabasaran, Azerbaijani, Chechen, Nogai; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar villages against resettled lowland ones,** because the same people's daily work changed with the move.
- **Laks,** because a long tradition of leaving home to work may shape how staying and leaving are judged.
- **Kubachi and Untsukul,** as craft villages where one trade defines a working life.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plain,** because steppe herding and irrigated farming set different work calendars.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans,** to separate women's loom work from men's work away.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis and Mountain Jews,** for urban trade, workshops and vineyards.
- **Akkin Chechens,** because deportation interrupted work and land for a generation.
- **Kizlyar Russians,** for a vineyard and cannery working tradition on the Terek.
- **Rotational workers and their home villages,** because the worker and the waiting household see the same job differently.
- **Women and men, young and old,** because unpaid farm and household work is rarely in the labor statistics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Soviet records and ethnography are rich; current statistics exist at republic level; unpaid and informal work is thinly measured.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak; Azerbaijani; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unemployment among young men is tied in the press to recruitment by armed groups; keep that as a research question, not a frame. Informal and unregistered work can expose people to tax or police trouble.
- **Held for review:** none.
