## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highlanders moved to the plain against those who stayed,** because the same promise of betterment was lived two ways.
- **Laks of Novolaksky and Akkin Chechens,** for whom improvement of land was a loss for one people and a gain for another.
- **Kumyks,** whose plain received resettled villages and winter pastures for highland herds.
- **Nogais,** whose steppe was reshaped by pasture and irrigation plans.
- **Lezgins,** split by a border and active in language revival.
- **Mountain Jews,** for emigration as a family's route to a better life.
- **Kizlyar Russians,** for Soviet industrial and farm modernization and later outmigration.
- **Women,** because the uplift campaigns were aimed at them in particular.
- **Sufi and reformist or Salafi-leaning believers,** because they define spiritual improvement differently.
- **Urban young strivers,** for education migration and self-help as present-day betterment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet campaigns, resettlement and infrastructure; mixed for current self-help; thin for how families judge the results.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Lak, Kumyk, Avar, Dargin, Chechen, Nogai; Azerbaijani.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land disputes arising from resettlement (Novolaksky; the plain) are live and sometimes violent; the 1944 deportation must be told in the Akkin Chechens' own voices; women's "liberation" campaigns met violent resistance. Land law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
