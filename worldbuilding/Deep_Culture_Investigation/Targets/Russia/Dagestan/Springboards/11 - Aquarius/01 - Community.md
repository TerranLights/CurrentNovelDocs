# Dagestan: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Tabasaran, Nogai; Azerbaijani and Juhuri in Derbent; Arabic for mosque and manuscript usage. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar villages against resettled lowland villages** (Kizilyurt and Khasavyurt districts), to see what a moved community keeps.
- **Kubachi and other single-craft villages**, where trade and village may form one community.
- **Laks of Kumukh and the Laks of Novolaksky**, for a community moved in 1944 and asked to move again.
- **Lezgins of the Samur valley**, whose village ties cross a state border.
- **Derbent's quarters**: Azerbaijanis, Mountain Jews, Lezgins and Tabasarans sharing one old city.
- **Kumyk villages such as Tarki, Endirey and Aksay**, old settlements beside newcomer districts.
- **Nogai auls**, for community spread thin across the steppe.
- **Cossack stanitsas of Kizlyar and Tarumovka**, for a community built around a Cossack circle and an Orthodox parish.
- **Rutul, Tsakhur and Aghul villages**, for very small communities facing emptying.
- **Makhachkala's self-built suburbs**, where community forms among strangers from many villages.
- **Migrants in the northern oil towns**, for community rebuilt away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on village festivals and ritual; mixed on urban neighborhoods; thin on measured "sense of community".
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Dargin, Lak and the small southern languages; Azerbaijani; Juhuri; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land quarrels between villages and peoples; the Novolaksky return; congregations divided between Sufi and Salafi mosques; the weight of village opinion on women and dissenters, as those affected describe it.
- **Held for review:** none.
