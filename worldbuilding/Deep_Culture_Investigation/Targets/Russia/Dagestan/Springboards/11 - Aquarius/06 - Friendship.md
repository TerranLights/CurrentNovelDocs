# Dagestan: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (including the republic's own Russian slang); Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Dargin, Lak, Nogai, Azerbaijani; Arabic for religious brotherhood. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar and Dargin men**, for kunak and sworn bonds between villages.
- **Kumyks**, for lowland friendships with Russian and highland neighbors.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis, Lezgins and Mountain Jews**, for friendship across faiths in one city.
- **Tsakhurs and Rutuls**, for friendships kept with neighbors across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Nogais**, for friendship with Cossack and Russian neighbors on the steppe.
- **Laks and Akkin Chechens of Novolaksky**, for friendships tested by a land dispute.
- **Women**, because their friendships appear less in the classic sources and need their own voices.
- **Students and young men in Makhachkala**, for city friendships that mix peoples.
- **Wrestling and sports teams**, for friendship formed by training together.
- **Migrants in the north**, for friendships made among Dagestanis abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and travel writing; mixed in ethnography; thin in survey data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Dargin, Lak, Azerbaijani; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendship between men and women; cross-faith friendship in tense times; friends recruited into armed groups; favors between friends in office.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Russian folklore and ethnography catalogs for "побратимство Дагестан" and "молочное родство горцы".
2. Read *Аммалат-бек* and the critical literature on it ("Аммалат-бек дружба").
3. Search the Avar and Lezgin dictionaries for "гьудул" and "дуст" and collect derived words and proverbs.
4. Search memoirs with "Дербент соседи горские евреи".
5. Search wedding videos and articles for "друзья жениха Дагестан" to list friends' roles by district.
