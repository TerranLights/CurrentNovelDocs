# Dagestan: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Nogai, Tabasaran, Chechen (Akkin dialect); Arabic for brotherhood and religious usage. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars**, where imamate memory and Sufi networks press on kin loyalty.
- **Dargins of Akusha and the Kaitag lands**, a large league beside a former ruler's domain.
- **Laks**, for the khanate of Kazikumukh and how one small people draws its group line.
- **Kumyks**, for the lowland line drawn against highland newcomers.
- **Nogais**, to test whether steppe divisions and youth congregations follow highland patterns.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**, for southern leagues and a cross-border national movement.
- **Akkin Chechens**, for clan (taip) membership inside a mostly non-Chechen republic.
- **Sufi murid circles and Salafi congregations**, two kinds of group that cut across peoples.
- **Terek Cossacks**, for an Orthodox group with its own circle.
- **Sports clubs**, for a men's group that mixes peoples.
- **Mountain Jews**, for a minority group bound by faith and kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and political science on clan and ethnic groups; mixed in ethnography; thin in surveys of conformity or group weight.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Kumyk, Dargin, Lak, Lezgin, Nogai; Chechen for the Akkins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** counterinsurgency, "Wahhabi" listings and pressure on relatives; blood feud; land and ethnic clashes; the two regional news sites carry official designations in Russia, so read them alongside other sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
