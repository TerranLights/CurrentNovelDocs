# Dagestan: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Arabic for prayer and religious hope; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Nogai, Dargin, Lak, Tabasaran, Azerbaijani; Hebrew and Juhuri for the Mountain Jews; Turkish for the emigrant diaspora. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar and Dargin highland villages**, for farming rites that ask for a good year.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**, for the spring festival as a rite of renewal.
- **Kumyks**, for lowland farming hopes and the hopes of a growing urban middle class.
- **Nogais**, for herders' hopes tied to rain on the steppe.
- **Mountain Jews**, for hope of return and hope of staying.
- **Russians and Cossacks**, for Orthodox Easter and its hope of resurrection.
- **Shia Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, for petitions tied to their own holy figures.
- **Families of labor migrants**, for hope sent and received over distance.
- **Young men without work and students**, to compare hopes by schooling.
- **Akkin Chechens**, for hope of return to the villages left in 1944.
- **Families of the disappeared**, for hope kept in waiting.
- **Speakers of the smallest languages (the Andi-Tsez peoples, Archi)**, for hope that a language will last.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ritual and religious material; mixed in migration studies; thin in measured hope or expectation.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Nogai, Dargin, Lak, Tabasaran, Azerbaijani; Hebrew; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared and counterinsurgency; suicide data; charities that may be under official scrutiny; written amulets and folk rites that reformist preachers condemn, to be described without taking sides.
- **Held for review:** none.
