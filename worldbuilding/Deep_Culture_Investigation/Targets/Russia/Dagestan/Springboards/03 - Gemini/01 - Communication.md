# Dagestan: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgian, Lak, Tabasaran, Chechen, Nogai, Azerbaijani, Juhuri; Arabic for religious teaching on speech. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women,** and talk in mixed and single-sex settings (godekan, wells, kitchens).
- **Elders and young people,** and Russian-dominant city youth against village speakers.
- **Highland Avar, Dargin and Lak villages** against **Kumyk and Nogai plain and steppe** communities.
- **The southern peoples** (Lezgins, Tabasarans, Agul, Rutul, Tsakhur) and the **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, where an Azerbaijani-language ashug tradition crosses ethnic lines.
- **Akkin Chechens**, with Chechen speech norms.
- **Mountain Jews and Russians of Kizlyar**, as minority speech communities.
- **Migrants in Moscow and the oil north,** who may shift norms when talking with outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Linguistic and folklore material is good; recorded everyday conversation is scarce outside the Higher School of Economics expeditions.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Avar, Lezgian, Dargin and Kumyk for proverbs and poetry; Arabic for letters and sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** speech between in-laws and between sexes touches family honor; religious teaching on speech is contested between Sufi and Salafi groups, so a single muftiate source is not the whole picture.
- **Held for review:** none.
