*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland jamaats** with dense kin ties against **resettled lowland villages** and **Makhachkala neighborhoods** of newcomers.
- **Avars, Dargins, Laks, Kumyks, Lezgins:** compare how far each people's kin and village networks reach into posts and trades.
- **Small southern peoples** (Rutul, Tsakhur, Agul, Tabasaran) and **Lezgins and Avars split by the border** with Azerbaijan.
- **Nogais** of the steppe, linked to Nogai kin in Stavropol and Astrakhan.
- **Akkin Chechens,** with ties into Chechnya.
- **Mountain Jews,** mostly abroad now, with ties to Derbent.
- **Rotation workers and Moscow migrants,** and **descendants of emigrants in Turkey, Jordan and Syria.**
- **Women and men,** who keep different parts of the network.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Ethnography of kin and ritual ties is good; measured data on loneliness and social support at republic level is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Turkish for the Turkish diaspora; Azerbaijani for cross-border kin; Hebrew and Juhuri for Mountain Jews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patronage and clan networks touch corruption; Sufi networks were caught up in violence (a well-known sheikh was killed in 2012); ties to the Middle East can draw security attention.
- **Held for review:** none.
