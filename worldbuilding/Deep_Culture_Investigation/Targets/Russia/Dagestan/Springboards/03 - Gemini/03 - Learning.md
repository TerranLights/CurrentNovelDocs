## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar, Dargin and Lak villages,** with old madrasa traditions, against **lowland and city families** schooled mostly in Russian.
- **Kumyks and Nogais,** with Turkic primers and their own folk pedagogy.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Rutul, Agul and Tsakhur,** where very small languages face school in Russian.
- **Craft villages** (Balkhar, Untsukul, Kubachi, Gotsatl) against farming and herding villages.
- **Girls and boys,** since crafts, sport and religious learning are often split by sex.
- **Sufi-linked families, Salafi-leaning families and secular families,** each with its own idea of a well-raised child.
- **Mountain Jews and Russians of Kizlyar,** with their own home learning.
- **Akkin Chechens,** and **migrant children** raised in Moscow or the oil north.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. The ethnopedagogy genre is large but idealizing; hard data on home learning are thin; the Arabic manuscript record of learning is rich.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for the madrasa record; Avar, Lak and Lezgian for folklore; Azerbaijani for al-Alqadari.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered religious schooling and the state's view of it; children studying abroad in religious schools; disputes over native-language teaching (the law side goes to Libra).
- **Held for review:** none.
