## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Makhachkala, Kaspiysk and Derbent** against **highland villages** with weak signal and older channels.
- **Readers of the national-language press** against **Russian-only readers,** by people.
- **Religious networks:** followers of Sufi sheikhs and Salafi-leaning communities, each with its own trusted voices.
- **Southern Lezgins and Azerbaijanis,** who can also follow media from Azerbaijan.
- **Mountain Jews,** linked to Russian-language media in Israel; **Kizlyar Russians,** closer to federal television.
- **Nogai steppe villages,** remote and small.
- **Young people on Telegram and Instagram** against **elders on television and the mosque.**
- **Migrants in Moscow and the oil north,** for whom village chats carry home news.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Press archives and the linguistic record are good; trust and news-use data at republic level are thin and federal surveys rarely break the republic out.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the national languages for local papers; Arabic for chronicles and inscriptions; Azerbaijani for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** journalists in the republic have faced violence; counter-terror reporting is restricted; some outlets and pollsters carry Russian "foreign agent" labels or are blocked, which affects both access and how locals view them; the October 2023 Makhachkala airport riot and the online channels tied to it are a sensitive case; the legal side of censorship goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
