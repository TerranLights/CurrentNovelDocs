## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars** with the Andi and Tsez (Dido) peoples counted among them: many small languages under one label.
- **Dargins**, with Kubachi and Kaitag speakers (separate languages, by some counts).
- **Laks**, including those resettled to Novolakskoye.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Agul, Rutul and Tsakhur** of the south, near Azerbaijan.
- **Kumyks and Nogais** of plain and steppe: Turkic-language market languages.
- **Akkin Chechens** of Khasavyurt and Novolak districts.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**, **Mountain Jews (Juhuro)** and **Tats**: urban Turkic-language and Iranian-language traditions.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** around Kizlyar.
- **Highland villages against their resettled lowland offshoots**, and **Makhachkala** against home jamaats.
- **Diasporas:** Moscow and the oil north; emigrants' descendants in Turkey; Avars and Lezgins in Azerbaijan; Avars in Georgia; Mountain Jews in Israel.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich: linguistics deep and partly in English; everyday ethnography of talk thinner, mostly Russian. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (its tracker carries "Russia" broadly); this sign is written for the Republic of Dagestan as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgian and Lak for folklore; Arabic for manuscripts; Azerbaijani and Turkish for the southern and diaspora records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the years of insurgency and counter-terror raids; disputes between Sufi and Salafi communities; risks for local journalists; blood feud; male-only spaces such as the godekan, which limit who can observe.
- **Held for review:** none.
