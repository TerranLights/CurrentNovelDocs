## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dargins of Kudutl and Akusha**: an old high-learning tradition to trace into modern schooling.
- **Laks of Kumukh**: Kumukh's Arabic learning, and whether it carried into a Soviet-era intelligentsia.
- **Avars of the mountain districts**: village madrasas, then Soviet schools, then Islamic institutes.
- **Andi, Tsez, Archi and other small-language peoples**: whether children are schooled in Avar, Russian or their own tongue, and what that does to the standing of learning.
- **Kumyks and Nogais**: Turkic-language schooling and the reform movement.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: written languages of different ages, and ties to Azerbaijani schooling.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis and Juhuro**: a city tradition of schooling, and minority schools.
- **Russians of Kizlyar**: Russian-language schooling as the norm.
- **Graduates of foreign Islamic schools** against **graduates of state universities**: two kinds of educated person.
- **Girls and boys**: rates and paths to compare, not assume.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and statistics; mixed for madrasa life today; thin for small-language schooling outcomes.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Arabic for manuscripts and ijazas; the native languages for textbooks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** study abroad is tied to security files; religious schools face licensing checks. Education law goes to Libra; Gemini keeps early schooling and learning.
- **Held for review:** none.
