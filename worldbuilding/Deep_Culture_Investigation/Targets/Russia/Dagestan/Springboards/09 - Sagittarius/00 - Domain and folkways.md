## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars of the central highlands (Khunzakh, Gunib, Gergebil)**: the Imamate's heartland, and their memory of it.
- **Andi and Tsez (Dido) peoples of the western valleys (Botlikh, Tsumada, Tsunta)**: smaller languages, distinct shrine and village traditions.
- **Dargins, with Kubachi and Kaitag apart**: the old village unions and a craft town with its own lore.
- **Laks (Kumukh) and Lak resettlers in Novolak**: an early center of Islamic learning; resettlement changes the story.
- **Kumyks of the plains** and **Nogais of the northern steppe**: Turkic speakers, with steppe and khanate layers.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls and Tsakhurs of the south**: shrines, mountain pilgrimages and ties to Azerbaijan.
- **Azerbaijanis and Tats of Derbent**: the Shia minority.
- **Juhuro (Mountain Jews)** and **Russians and Terek Cossacks of Kizlyar**: the non-Muslim faiths.
- **Akkin Chechens (Khasavyurt, Novolak, Kazbekov)**: Chechen Sufi orders inside Dagestan.
- **Makhachkala's newer urban population** and **diasporas** (West Siberia, Moscow, Turkey, Israel): belief away from the village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Islam's history and manuscripts; mixed for present-day belief (surveys are few, and the census asks no religion); thin for non-belief and for the smaller peoples.
- **Lands:** the tracker gives the pre-war lands only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands of the former Republic of Dagestan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Arabic for the manuscripts; the local languages for folk and village sources (Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak above all).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sufi-Salafi tension and counterinsurgency make belief a security subject; some texts are on Russia's banned-materials list; murids' instruction from a shaykh is personal and not public. Law questions go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
