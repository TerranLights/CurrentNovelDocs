# Dagestan: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Chechen, Azerbaijani, Nogai, Judeo-Tat; Arabic for devotional texts. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Murids of the Shadhili-Naqshbandi lineages** against **non-affiliated Sunni villagers**: faith through a shaykh, or without one.
- **Avar highlanders**, **Dargins** and **Laks**: the three oldest centers of learned devotion, each with its own shrines.
- **Kumyks and Nogais**: a Turkic, plains and steppe path to the same faith.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Rutuls, Aguls, Tsakhurs**: mountain pilgrimages and saints' tombs.
- **Derbent's Shia Azerbaijanis and Tats**: mourning rites, a different calendar of devotion.
- **Juhuro (Mountain Jews)**: faith kept as a small community, then mostly carried abroad.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar and Tarumovka**: Orthodox and Old Believer faith on the northern plain.
- **Akkin Chechens**: Qadiri dhikr, Chechen language.
- **The Soviet-educated older generation** against **post-1990 believers**: faith inherited, interrupted or newly chosen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sufi practice and shrines; mixed for measured religiosity (no census question); thin for unbelief and for secular faith in science or the state.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Arabic, Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin, Azerbaijani; Juhuri and Hebrew for the Jewish strand.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a shaykh's instruction to a murid is private; shrine visits are disputed by Salafis (see 10, Fundamentalism); hold back any family's private devotion.
- **Held for review:** none.
