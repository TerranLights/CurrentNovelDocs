*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sufi disciples:** trust in a sheikh's insight sets their idea of hidden knowledge apart.
- **Salafi-leaning believers:** suspicious of claims of *kashf*, so they draw the line between insight and error elsewhere.
- **Lak and Dargin craft villages (Balkhar, Kubachi):** tacit skill passed by hand within a family or village.
- **Tabasarans and southern weavers:** women's pattern knowledge.
- **Highland herders of Avar and Andi villages:** weather and animal lore.
- **Kumyks and Derbent's Azerbaijanis:** Turkic fortune-telling words and practices, and the Shia frame.
- **Mountain Jews:** their own dream and omen customs, to record from community sources.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar:** Russian folk omens beside the local ones.
- **Elders and the young:** grandmothers' omens against phone-searched dream meanings.
- **Women and men:** who is said to "feel" trouble coming, and whose hunch the family follows.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for Sufi and dream literature, fair for crafts, thin for measured intuition; omens are mostly in older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Russian; Lak for Balkhar; Tabasaran for weaving; Lezgian and Azerbaijani for ashug verse; Kumyk for fortune-telling words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clerics condemn fortune-telling, so practitioners may hide; a sheikh's claimed insight is a matter of faith for followers; record divination as belief, never as evidence.
- **Held for review:** none.
