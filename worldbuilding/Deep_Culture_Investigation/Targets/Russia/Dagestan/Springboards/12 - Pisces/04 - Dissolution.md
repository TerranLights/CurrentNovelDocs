# Dagestan: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Arabic for Sufi terms; Avar, Dargin, Lezgian, Lak, Kumyk, and the small Andi-Tsez and Archi languages for language shift; Juhuri. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Naqshbandi-Shadhili disciples of Chirkey and other sheikhs' circles:** self-loss as a sought spiritual goal.
- **Salafi-leaning believers:** who reject *rabita* and loud dhikr, so they draw the line between union and error elsewhere.
- **Small Andi-Tsez and Archi peoples:** whose languages face the sharpest fading.
- **Laks and Avars in Makhachkala:** children raised in Russian, where the city dissolves village speech.
- **Resettled villages on Kumyk lands:** two communities pressed together, each fearing absorption.
- **Mountain Jews:** a community that has largely left, seen from those who stayed and those abroad.
- **Russians and Cossacks of the north:** emigration as the dissolving of an old frontier society.
- **Soviet-era elders:** the end of a Soviet identity in 1991, a dissolution they lived through.
- **Derbent's Shia Azerbaijanis:** Ashura processions as crowd merging of another kind, to set beside dhikr.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sufi doctrine and for language shift; mixed for mystical experience in participants' own words; thin for dementia and the self's fading in old age.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Russian for Sufism; the small languages themselves, through linguists' corpora; Juhuri for the Jewish community.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sufi-Salafi disputes over *rabita* and dhikr have turned violent; some disciples treat the inner practices as not for outsiders; resettlement and flooding are tied to land disputes. Religious divorce as law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
