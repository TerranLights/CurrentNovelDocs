# Dagestan: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Arabic (the core vocabulary of yielding to God); Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgian, Lak; Azerbaijani and Persian for Derbent's past. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars of Gunib, Gimry and Khunzakh:** Shamil's surrender is their family history.
- **Derbent's peoples:** an old city that changed hands many times, with surrender as civic memory.
- **Kumyks of the plain:** the shamkhalate's earlier accommodation with Russia (*check*) may give yielding another telling.
- **Lezgins of the old Kurin khanate:** another path into the empire, to compare.
- **Dargins of the Kadar zone:** the most recent village resistance and defeat.
- **Former fighters and their families:** surrender under the adaptation commission, and its aftermath.
- **Chechen-Akkins:** the 1944 deportation as forced yielding, distinct from defeat in battle.
- **Patients and families:** how a terminal diagnosis is accepted or fought, by age and faith.
- **Wrestlers and fighters:** a sport culture with strong words about giving up.
- **Sufi disciples and Salafi-leaning believers:** differing frames for yielding (to a sheikh, or to God alone).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Caucasian War surrender; mixed for religious acceptance (much doctrine, little first-person); thin for end-of-life and palliative care.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Russian; Avar for Gunib memory; Dargin for Kadar; Kumyk and Lezgian for the lowland and south; Persian for older Derbent records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the adaptation commission and Kadar touch on counterinsurgency and possible abuses; Shamil's surrender is debated as betrayal or wisdom; grief and dying families' privacy. Military and amnesty law go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
