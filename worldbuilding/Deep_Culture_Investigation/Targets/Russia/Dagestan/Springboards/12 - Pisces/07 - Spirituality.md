## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and Dargins of the Sufi heartlands:** sheikh-centered practice, with Chirkey and other circles.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans and Aguls:** mountain shrines and spring rites, to see how they sit with Islam.
- **Kaitag and Kubachi villages:** small communities whose local shrines may carry older layers.
- **Laks of Kumukh:** an old seat of mosque and scholarship.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** lowland and steppe Islam, each with its own school and older beliefs.
- **Derbent's Shia:** devotion through mourning, distinct from the Sunni majority.
- **Mountain Jews:** prayer, holy graves and a community spread across Israel and Moscow.
- **Orthodox Russians and Old Believers:** two Christian practices in the north.
- **Salafi-leaning believers:** often critics of shrine visits and mawlid, so their inner practice is worth hearing on its own terms.
- **Soviet-raised and secular Dagestanis:** spirituality through poetry, mountains and family memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sufi practice and doctrine; mixed for folk and syncretic practice (Soviet ethnography, often biased); some survey data, mostly older.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Russian; Lezgian, Avar, Kumyk for shrine and festival names; Hebrew and Juhuri; Church Slavonic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Sufi practices are taught only to initiates; shrine visits are a fault line between Sufi and Salafi believers; Shia and Jewish minorities face security concerns. Religious institutions and doctrine as such go to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** none.
