## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lezgins of the Samur valley:** a people divided by a state line since 1991.
- **Avars and Tsakhurs near Georgia and Azerbaijan:** kin across two other borders, each with its own crossing habits.
- **Kumyks:** a lowland people whose lines with resettled highlanders are disputed.
- **Laks and Chechen-Akkins of Novolak and Khasavyurt:** two claims on one piece of land.
- **Nogais of the northern steppe:** pasture lines against incoming herders.
- **Mountain Jews and Derbent's old quarters:** neighborhood lines inside one walled city, and how they have shifted.
- **Shia and Sunni in Derbent:** their own mosques and holy days; how firm the line is in daily life.
- **Sufi and Salafi-leaning congregations:** separate mosques and who may pray beside whom.
- **Village houses and Makhachkala apartments:** the guest room and the courtyard against shared stairwells.
- **Women and men:** different maps of where each may go and with whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state borders and land conflict; mixed for household and bodily boundaries (ethnography, some dated); thin in measured data on privacy for the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Lezgian and Azerbaijani for the southern border; Kumyk and Avar for land disputes; Arabic for purity terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land and border disputes have led to violence; Lezgin national claims are politically sensitive; honor and seclusion of women; the border zone limits fieldwork. Land law and border law go to Libra; custom as order to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
