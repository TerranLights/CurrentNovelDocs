## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **High-mountain villages** (Tsumada, Tlyarata, Charoda, Kulinsky districts) with terrace and herd economies, against **lowland market towns**.
- **Nogai steppe herders** and **Kumyk farming villages** of the plain.
- **Lezgin and Tabasaran** southern villages, with cross-border trade to Azerbaijan.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** of the Kizlyar lowland, with a different farm history.
- **Chechen-Akkin villages** near Khasavyurt, close to Chechnya's independence history.
- **Women and men**: who may earn and hold money in her own right.
- **Elders, disabled people and the young** inside the same household.
- **Migrants** in western Siberia and Moscow, and the households they support.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; statistics on plots and budgets are good; personal self-reliance as a value is thin outside ethnography and journalism. Historical political independence is well studied in Russian.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for Imamate documents; Kumyk, Avar, Lezgin for folk sayings and oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** any talk of political independence from Russia is legally risky for informants; the Chechen wars and the 1996 accords signed in Khasavyurt touch Dagestani lands. Regional subsidy debates carry ethnic slurs. Sovereignty in constitutional terms belongs to Libra; what independence is for, to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
