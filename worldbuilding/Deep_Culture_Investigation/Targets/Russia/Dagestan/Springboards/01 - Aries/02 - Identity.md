## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Each titular people against the republic-wide "Dagestani" label**: the base layering to test.
- **Andi, Tsez (Dido), Archi and Bezhta speakers** inside the Avar count; **Kubachi** and **Kaitag** inside the Dargin count.
- **Sunni and Shia communities**: Derbent's Azerbaijanis and the Shia Lezgin village of Miskindzha (*check*) against the Sunni majority.
- **Sufi followers, Salafis and secular townspeople**, across all peoples.
- **Lowland resettled villages** (mountain jamaats moved to the plain) against their home villages.
- **Nogais of the northern steppe** and **Russians and Cossacks** of the Kizlyar lowland.
- **Chechen-Akkins** of Khasavyurt and the Novolaksky area, with Chechen identity across the border.
- **Mountain Jews** at home and abroad; **Lezgins and Avars** in Azerbaijan.
- **Makhachkala, a multiethnic city**, against the single-people highland district.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; identity is a large theme in Russian-language scholarship and politics. English sources exist but lean on security and religion.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Nogai for organizations' own texts; Arabic for chronicles; Hebrew for parts of the Mountain Jewish diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic quotas and land disputes; the Lezgin border question; Salafi identity and state pressure; the Chechen-Akkin and Lak resettlement dispute in Novolaksky. Constitutional status of peoples is a Libra question.
- **Held for review:** none.
