## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aghuls and Azerbaijanis** of the south, whose spring new year may differ from the highland north.
- **Avar, Dargin and Lak** highland villages with first-furrow and other farm-opening rites.
- **Kumyks** and **Nogais** of the plain and steppe, with Turkic-language festival names.
- **Shia Azerbaijanis** of Derbent, whose religious calendar differs from the Sunni majority.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** keeping the Orthodox year; **Mountain Jews** keeping the Hebrew one.
- **Resettled lowland villages** and their home villages in the mountains.
- **Laks and Chechen-Akkins** in the Novolaksky area.
- **Descendants of emigrants** in Turkey, Jordan and Syria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and birth rites in Russian ethnography; thin on "starting over" as a personal value outside memoir and press.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for Islamization narratives; Lezgin, Lak, Avar and Kumyk for festival songs; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Novolaksky land dispute; the former-fighters program and insurgency memory; competing founding claims between peoples. Some rites may be judged un-Islamic by some believers, so informants may play them down.
- **Held for review:** none.
