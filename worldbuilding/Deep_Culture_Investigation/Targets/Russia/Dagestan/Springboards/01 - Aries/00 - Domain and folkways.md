# Dagestan: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (the shared language of the republic); Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Tabasaran, Nogai, Azerbaijani, Chechen; smaller Nakh-Daghestanian languages (Rutul, Aghul, Tsakhur, the Andi and Tsez group, Archi); Judeo-Tat; Arabic for the manuscript tradition. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here, and how it asserts itself: the person, the body, appearance, beginnings, naming.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars** and, separately, the **Andi, Tsez and Archi** peoples counted under them: the census grouping itself is a question of identity.
- **Dargins**, with **Kubachi** and **Kaitag** treated apart (distinct crafts and speech).
- **Laks**: a compact highland people with an old princely center at Kumukh (Gazikumukh).
- **Kumyks** and **Nogais**: Turkic-speaking lowland and steppe peoples; Nogai steppe life differs from the foothills.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aghuls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: the south, with kin across the border in Azerbaijan.
- **Azerbaijanis** of Derbent; **Mountain Jews**; **Russians and Terek Cossacks** around Kizlyar.
- **Chechen-Akkins** of the Khasavyurt and Novolaksky areas.
- **Highland villages, resettled lowland villages and Makhachkala**: Soviet resettlement to the plain made a third, mixed setting.
- **Diasporas**: Moscow and other Russian cities, descendants of nineteenth-century emigrants in Turkey, and kin communities in Azerbaijan and Georgia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian ethnography and folklore; thin in English; thin for the smallest peoples outside linguistics.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries "Russia" as the pre-war lands, which is broad; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. These Aries sheets are written for the core lands of the former Republic of Dagestan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk and Lezgin for primary texts; Arabic for manuscripts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic tension over land and representation; Sufi and Salafi disputes; the insurgency years and later attacks; honor-related violence against women; the 1944 deportation of the Chechen-Akkins and the later resettlement of Laks. Avoid the stock "proud highlander" figure; name variation.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Institute's publication list and the Russian State Library catalog for "родильные обряды" and "имянаречение" with each people's name (аварцев, даргинцев, кумыков, лезгин, лакцев).
2. Read Gamzatov's «Мой Дагестан» and note every passage on names, language and belonging, as leads only.
3. Pull the 2010 and 2021 census ethnicity tables for the republic and list how the smaller peoples were coded.
4. Search folklore anthologies for «Хочбар», «Шарвили» and «Эдиге» and log the editions and their compilers.
5. Ask the Takho-Godi museum for its costume and portrait catalogs.
