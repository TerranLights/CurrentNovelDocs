## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland craft villages** (Kubachi, Balkhar, Untsukul, Gotsatl) against **farming and herding villages**.
- **Lowland towns** (Khasavyurt, Buynaksk, Izberbash, Kizlyar) with markets and trade routes, against **highland districts**.
- **Kumyks and Nogais** of the plain and steppe, against **Avar, Dargin and Lak** highland communities.
- **Derbent**: Azerbaijanis, Lezgins, Tabasarans and Mountain Jews in one old trading city.
- **Women and men**: who may start a business in her own name, and who acts unasked at home.
- **Young urban founders** in Makhachkala and Kaspiysk, against **older collective-farm generations**.
- **Migrants** in Moscow and western Siberian oil towns, starting ventures far from home.
- **Chechen-Akkin villages**, with their own work-party customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; statistics exist but miss much informal activity; ethnography of work parties is rich in Russian; little in English.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kumyk, Avar, Lezgin for folk terms and tales; Arabic for endowment deeds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shadow economy, land seizures, fishing and caviar poaching, and contested big-business cases; informants may avoid naming income. Contracts, property and registration belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
