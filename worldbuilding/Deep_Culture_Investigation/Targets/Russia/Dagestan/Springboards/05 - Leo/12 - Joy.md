## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and Andi-Tsez peoples:** highland feasts, with small villages' own songs.
- **Dargins and Kubachi:** craft-village celebrations.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** a Turkic spring calendar and steppe music.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs:** the southern spring festival and Azerbaijani-border kin.
- **Laks:** city-dwelling and highland Laks may celebrate differently.
- **Mountain Jews and Azerbaijanis of Derbent:** other holiday calendars and music.
- **Chechen-Akkins and Russians:** Chechen dance and Orthodox holidays.
- **Sufi, Salafi and secular families:** what counts as proper joy.
- **Men and women:** separate halls and different ways of showing delight.
- **Young people in Makhachkala and migrants abroad:** joy away from village rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and weddings; mixed for religious joy; thin for private and everyday happiness.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the national languages for songs; Arabic for teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** music and dance divide religious currents; joy during mourning is policed by neighbors; some Sufi practice is not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
