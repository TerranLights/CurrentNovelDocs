## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and Dargins:** highland strength and agility contests; compare village by village.
- **Andi-Tsez peoples and Archi:** small-village games that may survive only in folklore records.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** horse games and knucklebones of the plain and steppe.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs:** spring-festival games of the south.
- **Laks:** a performing tradition in Tsovkra beside ordinary children's games.
- **Mountain Jews and Azerbaijanis of Derbent:** city games and festivals.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** Maslenitsa and riding games.
- **Boys and girls:** sport schools for boys; what girls play, and where.
- **Pious and secular families:** different limits on music, games and screens.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport; mixed for folk games; thin for girls' play and adult leisure.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the national languages for game names and rhymes; Arabic for teaching on amusement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious debate over games and music; boarding sport schools raise child-welfare questions.
- **Held for review:** none.
