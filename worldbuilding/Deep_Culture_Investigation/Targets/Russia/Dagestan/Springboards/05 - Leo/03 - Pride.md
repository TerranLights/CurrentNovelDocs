# Dagestan: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Nogai, Tabasaran; Arabic; Turkish for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars:** the largest people, with Shamil's memory; check whether others see Avar pride as dominance.
- **Andi-Tsez peoples and Archi:** small groups whose pride may be in a separate identity from the Avars.
- **Dargins and Kubachi:** a large people and a craft village with its own pride.
- **Kumyks:** a lowland people whose pride in land and past statehood meets resettled highlanders.
- **Lezgins, with Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls and Tsakhurs:** divided-nation feelings across the Azerbaijani border.
- **Laks:** pride in learning and trade.
- **Nogais:** a steppe minority at the republic's northern edge.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis and Mountain Jews:** competing claims to the city's past.
- **Chechen-Akkins and Russians or Cossacks:** pride bound to neighbor republics and to Russia.
- **Village against village:** jamaat rivalry may matter more than ethnic pride.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective pride and its rivalries; thin for personal pride, which modesty may hide.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic for chronicles; each people's language for local histories; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic rivalry, land disputes and the 1990s movements are live; pride in Shamil is contested; avoid painting any people as one voice.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the al-Qarakhi chronicle in Russian translation and list what it praises.
2. Search press archives from 1990 to 1999 for «Садвал», «Тенглик» and «Бирлик».
3. Search library catalogs for «история селения» books from five villages of different peoples.
4. Collect press on the Derbent jubilee and note who spoke for the city.
