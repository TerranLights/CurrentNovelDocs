*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men:** different channels (laments and lullabies against boasts and toasts); compare by age as well.
- **Avars and the Andi-Tsez peoples:** whether small highland communities keep tighter limits than the larger Avar towns.
- **Kumyks and Nogais:** Turkic singer-poet traditions with their own license to criticize.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans:** the ashug line and a southern lyric tradition.
- **Laks:** satire and a long urban-trading habit of travel.
- **Mountain Jews and Derbent's Azerbaijanis:** a city setting with different rules on dress and music.
- **Chechen-Akkins and Russians of Kizlyar:** neighbors with other dress and speech norms.
- **Makhachkala youth against village youth:** online display against face-to-face control.
- **Sufi-leaning, Salafi-leaning and secular families:** each draws the line on music, dress and speech differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for poetry and folklore; mixed for dress; thin and guarded for private feeling and politics.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak for poetry; Arabic for religious writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political speech carries legal and physical risk; women's images online; religious disputes over dress.
- **Held for review:** none.
