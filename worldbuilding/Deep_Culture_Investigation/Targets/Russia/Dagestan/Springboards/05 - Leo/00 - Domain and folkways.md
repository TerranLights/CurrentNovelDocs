## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and the Andi-Tsez peoples** of the western highlands: the small groups' own songs and feasts can vanish under the Avar label.
- **Dargins, with Kubachi and Kaitag:** craft villages whose display is their livelihood.
- **Kumyks and Nogais** of plain and steppe: a Turkic festive vocabulary; for Nogais, a steppe heritage of horse and song.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls and Tsakhurs** of the south: a spring-festival calendar and kin across the Azerbaijani border.
- **Laks:** an old trading people, home of the Tsovkra tightrope tradition.
- **Derbent's Azerbaijanis and Mountain Jews (Juhuro):** city music and feasts, and a diaspora in Israel and the United States.
- **Chechen-Akkins** near Khasavyurt and **Russians and Cossacks** of Kizlyar: other calendars, other sports.
- **City against highland and resettled lowland villages:** setting may divide play more than people do.
- **Sufi-leaning against Salafi-leaning families:** different lines on music and dance.
- **Migrants in Moscow and western Siberia, and the Turkish diaspora:** display at home by those who left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for weddings, crafts, dance and wrestling; mixed for romance and children; thin for pleasures custom frowns on.
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries only "Russia", which is broad; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. These sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Dagestan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin for folklore; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sufi zikr and religious disputes over music; the insurgency years color public festivity; some families keep women off camera.
- **Held for review:** none.
