*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar and Dargin highland villages that feed the wrestling schools**: a sports discipline joined to village and family pressure.
- **Lak villages (Tsovkra, Kumukh)**: discipline of a craft and a stunt, kept in one village.
- **Kubachi**: a master craftsman's discipline in metal.
- **Balkhar (Lak) women potters**: a women's craft discipline.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plain and steppe**: Turkic-speaking peoples with other upbringing customs.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans of the south**: carpet weaving as a household discipline.
- **Sufi disciples of different shaykhs (Chirkey, Shadhili and Naqshbandi lines)**: different rules of the path.
- **Salafi-leaning youth**: a stricter self-discipline that rejects the brotherhoods.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar**: Orthodox fasting and cadet drill.
- **Mountain Jews of Derbent**: Sabbath and fast-day discipline.
- **Girls and boys**: whether discipline is imposed differently on each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in sports media and folk-pedagogy writing; thin in measured data on child discipline.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Arabic, Avar, Lak, Dargin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** harsh punishment of children; hazing; ethnic stereotypes about conscripts and fighters (name variation, never one voice); religious dress disputes.
- **Held for review:** none.
