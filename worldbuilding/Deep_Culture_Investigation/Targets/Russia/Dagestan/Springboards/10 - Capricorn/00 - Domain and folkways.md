# Dagestan: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Tabasaran, Nogai, Chechen (Akkin speech), Rutul, Agul, Tsakhur, Azerbaijani, Tat and Judeo-Tat; Arabic (the language of the republic's Islamic learning and older chronicles). **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed: career, status, reputation, authority and responsibility, with the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars**, with the **Andi and Tsez peoples** (Andi, Botlikh, Karata, Akhvakh, Bagvalal, Tindi, Chamalal, Tsez, Hinukh, Bezhta, Hunzib and others) apart: a khanate core beside free societies; small peoples counted as Avar.
- **Dargins**, with **Kubachi** and **Kaitag** apart: unions of villages beside hereditary rule.
- **Kumyks**: an estate society of the plain under the Shamkhal.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: different khanate pasts, kin across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Laks**: careers made away from home through craft and trade.
- **Nogais**: a once-nomadic steppe order.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: the 1944 deportation and return reshaped what is owed.
- **Azerbaijanis, Mountain Jews (Juhuro), Tats** of Derbent: urban Shia and Jewish community authority.
- **Russians and Terek-Kizlyar Cossacks**: Cossack self-rule and parish life.
- Highland villages, Soviet-era resettled lowland villages, Makhachkala; the Moscow and West Siberian diaspora; Sufi followers, Salafi-leaning congregations and the secular; men and women.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 19th century and post-Soviet politics; mixed for everyday life; thin in surveys that separate the peoples.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries the pre-war lands as "Russia", which is broad; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the Republic of Dagestan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak; Azerbaijani and Persian for Derbent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgency and counterterror; Sufi-Salafi tension; the criminal sense of "authority"; land disputes; Russian "foreign agent" and ban designations that touch sources and endanger informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
