## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar villages (Botlikh, Tsumada)**: order kept by the village itself, from feud brokering to the 1999 militias.
- **Dargin villages of the Kadar valley**: a contest between two orders, state and religious.
- **Kumyks of the plain**: villages near the capital, where state order arrives first.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**: festival order and an epic of defense.
- **Kaitag and Kubachi Dargins**: ancient fortified villages and their layouts.
- **Nogais of the steppe**: open land with sparse policing.
- **Chechen-Akkins of Khasavyurt and Novolaksky**: land disputes that threaten local peace.
- **Mountain Jews and Shia Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: minorities whose safety is the test of order.
- **Russians of Kizlyar**: an older Russian civic order on the Terek.
- **Makhachkala**: a fast-grown city where order in streets and building is fought over.
- **Young men and the police**: the group most often stopped.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conflict and feud; thin on household order and everyday tidiness.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Arabic, Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk; Persian for Derbent's chronicle tradition.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgency, counterterror and attacks on minorities; feud cases (never name families); stereotypes about weddings and gunfire.
