## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Laks**: a long habit of rising through crafts and trade away from home.
- **Kubachi Dargins**: ambition through the fame of one village's craft.
- **Avar and Dargin highland youth**: sport and the city as the main ways up.
- **Kumyks**: plains landholders and urban professionals, with older estate standing to keep.
- **Lezgins**: work migration to Azerbaijan and Russian cities, and the pull of two states.
- **Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: small southern peoples whose rise may run through larger neighbors' networks.
- **Nogais**: a steppe people on the republic's edge, with fewer routes to office.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: rising after deportation and return.
- **Mountain Jews**: emigration to Israel, Moscow and the United States as a family project.
- **Russians of Kizlyar**: outflow to central Russia.
- **Women**: degrees, office and work against family expectation.
- **Students of Islam**: a scholarly path that the state may treat as a threat.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; good on migration and sport, thin on how ambition itself is spoken of.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Avar, Lak, Lezgin, Kumyk; Arabic for religious study; Hebrew for emigrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exam fraud and bought degrees; religious students under suspicion; avoid stereotypes about Caucasus businessmen.
- **Held for review:** none.
