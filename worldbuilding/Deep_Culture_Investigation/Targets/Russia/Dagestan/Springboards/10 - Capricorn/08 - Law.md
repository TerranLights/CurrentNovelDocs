*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avar and Dargin highland villages**: where custom and Islamic mediation may still outweigh state courts.
- **The Kadar villages and Salafi-leaning communities**: Islamic law claimed against state law.
- **Sufi-aligned congregations**: the muftiate's line on state law.
- **Kumyks**: plains peoples living close to Russian institutions since the 19th century.
- **Lezgins and other southern peoples**: Russian and Azerbaijani law on two sides of kin.
- **Nogais and Chechen-Akkins**: land disputes where custom and state title collide.
- **Makhachkala residents**: the city's daily dealings with police, courts and fixers.
- **Mountain Jews and Shia Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: their own religious law and its reach.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar**: Russian law closest to the letter, or not.
- **Women**: whether custom, Islamic law or state law serves them better in divorce, inheritance and custody, by their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on 19th-century custom and on legal pluralism; mixed for present practice; thin in measured trust in courts.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Arabic, Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin.
