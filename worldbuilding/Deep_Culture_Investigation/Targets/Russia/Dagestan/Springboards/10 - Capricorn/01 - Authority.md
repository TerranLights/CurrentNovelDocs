# Dagestan: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak and the other peoples' languages; Arabic for religious authority. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars of the old khanate lands (Khunzakh) and of the former free societies**: inherited rule against elected village office.
- **Andi-Tsez valleys (Botlikh, Tsumada, Tsunta)**: remote villages where elders and imams may weigh more than district officials.
- **Dargin unions of villages (Akusha-Dargo) and Kaitag**: a confederation against a hereditary Utsmi.
- **Kumyks of the Tarki and Endirey lands**: an estate past under the Shamkhal, and Endirey in the 2022 protests.
- **Laks**: authority once vested in the Kazikumukh khan, now spread through an urban diaspora.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans of the south**: authority read across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Nogais**: steppe nobles (*мурза*) and Soviet-era district rule.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: whose elders speak for the community in land disputes.
- **Derbent's Shia Azerbaijanis and Mountain Jews**: congregational and rabbinic authority outside the Sunni muftiate.
- **Kizlyar Cossacks and Russians**: atamans and Orthodox clergy.
- **Sufi followers against Salafi-leaning congregations**: two answers to whom a Muslim obeys.
- **Women**: authority inside the household and in the 2022 protests, apart from men's public authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on 19th-century and post-Soviet authority; thin on everyday household authority.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Arabic, Avar, Kumyk, Dargin, Lezgin; German for Kemper.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgency, counterterror and the killing of religious leaders; criticism of officials carries risk; the permission-share and correctional-control figures are scored from the law in force (Libra's letter of the law and Stage 2), not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
