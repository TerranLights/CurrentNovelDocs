# Dagestan: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars against the Andi and Tsez peoples of the western highlands**: remote villages may keep older lament and grave customs under the Avar name.
- **Laks of Kumukh**: an old center of learning, useful for comparing scholarly and folk teaching on death.
- **Dargins of Kubachi and Kaitag**: carved and inscribed grave stelae make them a key case for the dead marked in stone.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plains**: Turkic vocabulary of death and steppe memory, set against highland ways.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Rutuls, Aghuls and Tsakhurs of the south**: shrine (*pir*) visits and memorial days that may differ from the north.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: Shia mourning for the imams alongside private grief.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: a death history shaped by deportation and return.
- **Russians, Cossacks and Mountain Jews**: Christian and Jewish mourning, and emigration that leaves graves untended.
- **Families of soldiers, police and insurgents**: violent death is named and mourned in sharply different words by each.
- **Makhachkala against the villages**: city funerals may take new forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, war memory and religious texts; mixed for statistics, which need a careful look at how deaths are registered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar and Dargin for *ajam* verse and laments; Azerbaijani for Derbent; Chechen for the Akkin story.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martyrdom vocabulary is used by state, mosque and insurgents in different ways; deaths in custody and disappearances; suicide (stigma, likely undercounting); the Kunstkamera and other collections of remains; the "foreign agent" label on some sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
