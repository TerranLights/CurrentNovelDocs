## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sufi followers against Salafi-leaning Muslims**: each accuses the other of polluting the faith, so purity is a live dispute.
- **Avars and Dargins of the highlands**: heavy losses of scholars in Soviet purges; memory of hidden books.
- **Laks**: a reformist scholarly class among the purged.
- **Kumyks of the plain**: princely and landowning families singled out as class enemies.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans and Azerbaijanis of the south**: fire and water rites of the new year.
- **Nogais of the steppe**: herders under collectivization and drought.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: removed wholesale in 1944, the far end of purging a people.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar**: their own history of dekulakization and Cossack repression.
- **Mountain Jews**: purity rules and a community thinned by emigration.
- **Officials and police**: purges from above, and fear of being the next one removed.
- **Women and girls**: dress and conduct policed as "purity" by kin and by religious activists.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet repression and religious teaching; mixed for the present, where much lives in rights reports and social media.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Ottoman Turkish; Avar, Dargin, Lak for family memory; Azerbaijani and Lezgin for the new-year rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "preventive register" and security sweeps; some Muslim movements are banned in Russia, so their accounts carry legal risk; Human Rights Watch, Memorial's successors and some media carry "foreign agent" or "undesirable" labels; healing by exorcism can involve abuse.
