## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars of Khunzakh and the old free communities**: rival memories of khans and imams, so who "betrayed" whom differs by village.
- **Andi and Tsez villages**: border highlands that sometimes sided differently in the 19th-century wars.
- **Dargins and Kaitag**: princely and community traditions of alliance and defection.
- **Kumyks of the plains**: early ties with Russia, and their own view of those choices.
- **Laks and Chechen-Akkins**: the 1944 resettlement made them each other's grievance.
- **Lezgins and Azerbaijanis of the south**: a people split by a border and a mixed city.
- **Nogais**: steppe herders whose lands were taken by settlers from both sides.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: imperial and Soviet loyalties, and departures after 1991.
- **Police, soldiers and former insurgents**: each side names the other's informers and turncoats.
- **Women**: infidelity and "dishonor" judged by a stricter standard, to be tested, not assumed.
- **Diaspora in Turkey**: emigrants who left after the defeat, and those who stayed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century war history and folklore; mixed for marriage and infidelity, which are private; thin for informers, which are guarded.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic and Persian for the older papers; Avar, Kumyk, Lezgin for songs and proverbs; Azerbaijani for Derbent; Chechen for the Akkin side of the resettlement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informers and collaboration with security forces can mark a person for violence; exposure channels harm real women; leaving Islam carries risk; treason charges under Russian law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
