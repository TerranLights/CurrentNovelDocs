# Dagestan: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin, Lak, Nogai, Azerbaijani; Arabic and Persian for the older sources. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how the culture judges who holds it and how (legitimate authority is Capricorn's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars**: the largest people and the largest share of posts, with rival districts inside.
- **Dargins**: long-held top offices in the post-Soviet decades, so a key case of a people in power.
- **Kumyks**: former rulers of the plain who now share it with resettled highlanders, so land and power are linked.
- **Lezgins**: a people split by a state border, with its own elite networks in Derbent and Baku.
- **Laks**: a small people with a strong share of the Soviet-era intelligentsia and officials.
- **Tabasarans, Rutuls, Aghuls and Tsakhurs**: small peoples who may feel left out of the sharing.
- **Nogais**: a northern district with its own grievances about power over land.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: city politics in the south.
- **Chechen-Akkins**: power over Khasavyurt and the return of lost districts.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar**: a minority's share of offices.
- **Mountain Jews**: past role in Derbent's commerce and administration.
- **Sheikhs, imams and secular officials**: three kinds of power in the same villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political science and the 19th century; mixed for informal power, which lives in the press and in rumor.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic and Persian for medieval sources; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk for village politics; Azerbaijani and Lezgin for Derbent and cross-border links.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming "clans" can stigmatize whole peoples; criticizing officials carries risk; some sources carry "foreign agent" labels. Legitimate authority and the spirit of law go to Capricorn; the letter of the law on offices to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
