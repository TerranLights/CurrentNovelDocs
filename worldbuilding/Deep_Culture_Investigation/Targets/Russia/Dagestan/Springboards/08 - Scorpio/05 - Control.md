## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar, Dargin and Lak villages against Makhachkala**: control by neighbors and elders is likely stronger where everyone knows everyone.
- **Andi, Tsez and Archi villages**: very small communities where kin control and isolation may combine.
- **Kumyks and Nogais of the plain**: different marriage and land histories, and closer Russian influence.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Rutuls, Aghuls and Tsakhurs**: southern highland norms that may differ from the north and center.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: urban Shia families with their own household rules.
- **Russians, Cossacks and Mountain Jews**: minorities under different religious and family expectations.
- **Sufi murids and Salafi-leaning communities**: obedience to a master against obedience to text, a central split.
- **Young men and women**: whose movements, phones and marriages are watched, and by whom.
- **Labor migrants and their families**: control from a distance by remittance and phone.
- **Districts under counterterrorism regimes**: state control layered over village control.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rights reports and field studies on gender and generations; thin for survey measures broken down by people.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk for village speech and proverbs; Azerbaijani for Derbent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence against women and harmful practices; informants risk retaliation; counterterrorism operations; some sources carry "foreign agent" labels. The law of policing and of family violence goes to Libra and Capricorn; self-discipline goes to Capricorn.
