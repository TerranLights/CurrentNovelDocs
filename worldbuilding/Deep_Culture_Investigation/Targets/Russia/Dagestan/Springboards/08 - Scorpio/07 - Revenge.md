## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars and Dargins of the highlands**: older feud and reconciliation customs tied to the jamaat and the lineage.
- **Andi, Tsez and Archi villages**: isolated communities where a feud can involve most of the village.
- **Laks**: a long tradition of men working away, which may change who carries a family's quarrel.
- **Kumyks of the plain**: princely and commoner families with different honor codes, and early Russian courts.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Rutuls, Aghuls and Tsakhurs**: southern highlands with their own elders' mediation.
- **Chechen-Akkins of Khasavyurt and Novolakskoe**: a feud tradition shared with Chechnya, set in Dagestan.
- **Nogais of the steppe**: herding peoples, a test of the "herding and honor" idea.
- **Azerbaijanis, Mountain Jews and Russians of Derbent and Kizlyar**: city and minority ways of settling scores.
- **Security forces and insurgents' families**: reprisal along political lines rather than kinship.
- **Women**: as targets, mediators and keepers of grievance, to be studied, not assumed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century ethnography and folklore; mixed for the present, where feuds are reported unevenly and settled privately.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lak and Chechen for songs and proverbs; Azerbaijani for Derbent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** live feuds endanger informants; naming families can restart quarrels; reprisals by security forces; *Новая газета* and other sources face restrictions in Russia. Blood money, retaliation as punishment and the state's monopoly on force are law, under Libra (the letter) and Capricorn (the spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
