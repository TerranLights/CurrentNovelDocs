## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars of Botlikh and Tsumada**: front line of 1999, and village self-defense.
- **Avars, Laks and Dargins of Andalal and Kumukh**: keepers of the 1741 victory story.
- **Dargins of Kadar and Karamakhi**: villages at the center of the 1999 operation, seen from the other side.
- **Kumyks of Endirey and the plain**: the 2022 protest and land under pressure.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**: southern districts with mudflow and border-crossing crises.
- **Azerbaijanis, Mountain Jews and Russians of Derbent**: the 2024 attacks hit a mixed city.
- **Russians and Cossacks of Kizlyar**: the 1996 raid and later emigration.
- **Chechen-Akkins of Novolakskoe and Khasavyurt**: a border zone in two wars.
- **Nogais of the steppe**: drought and grazing crises in the north.
- **Labor migrants' households**: crisis managed through remittances and absence.
- **Women**: carers in hard times and, in 2022, visible protesters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century and war history and for recent press; mixed for official disaster statistics; thin for personal and mental-health crises.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Lak, Dargin for songs and oral history; Kumyk for the plain; Azerbaijani for Derbent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1999 events and the Kadar villages are still disputed; terrorism cases; protest participants face prosecution; mental illness is stigmatized. Emergency law and the prosecution of protesters go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
