## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Avars**, with the Andi and Tsez (Dido) peoples counted under them: the largest people; small highland groups may keep their own rites under one census name.
- **Dargins**, with Kubachi and Kaitag apart: craft villages that hand down skills and objects in their own way.
- **Kumyks** of the lowland plain: a former princely order, so rank may show in funerals and marriages.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans** of the south: southern calendars and kin across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Laks**: men's long work away from home, which bears on household money.
- **Rutuls, Aghuls and Tsakhurs**: small highland peoples rarely studied apart.
- **Nogais** of the northern steppe: a steppe and herding land-use history.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: Shia mourning calendars in a mostly Sunni land.
- **Mountain Jews and Russians/Terek Cossacks of Kizlyar**: non-Muslim death and marriage ways.
- **Chechen-Akkins of Khasavyurt and Novolakskoe**: deported in 1944; graves and land lost and regained.
- **Highland villages, plain villages resettled in Soviet times, and Makhachkala**: rites may change with a move or city life.
- **Sufi brotherhood followers against Salafi-leaning Muslims**: open disputes over shrine visits and memorial meals.
- **The diaspora in Turkey, Jordan and Syria** (19th-century emigrants' descendants): customs kept or changed abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet and earlier ethnography and Arabic manuscripts; thin for recent surveys broken down by people. The post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands of the present Republic of Dagestan.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar, Dargin, Kumyk, Lezgin for press and folklore; Azerbaijani, Judeo-Tat, Hebrew for Derbent; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Sufi and Salafi divide; insurgency and counterinsurgency; violence against women; sexual minorities (a dangerous topic for informants under Russian law); sources labeled "foreign agents" in Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.
