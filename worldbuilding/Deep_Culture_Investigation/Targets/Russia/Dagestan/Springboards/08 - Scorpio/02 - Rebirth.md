# Dagestan: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Lezgin, Kumyk, Lak, Tabasaran, Azerbaijani; Arabic for Sufi and religious writing; Turkish for the returning diaspora. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, repentance, recovery, renewal after loss, cultural revival and belief in resurrection.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sufi brotherhood followers against Salafi-leaning Muslims**: each tells the story of becoming a "true" Muslim differently and doubts the other's rebirth.
- **Avars and Dargins of the central highlands**: the heartland of the 19th-century Sufi renewal.
- **Laks of Kumukh**: a center of early Islam and of Soviet-era intelligentsia, both kinds of new life.
- **Lezgins and Tabasarans**: the spring new-year festival as a renewal rite of the south.
- **Kumyks and Nogais**: plains peoples whose lands received the resettled highlanders.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: Shia mourning and renewal calendar in the same city.
- **Russians, Cossacks and Mountain Jews**: Christian and Jewish rebirth beliefs; Jewish emigration and the occasional return.
- **Former fighters and their families**: the most contested second chance in the republic.
- **Women who take up religious dress as adults**: a visible personal turn, judged in different ways by kin.
- **Urban young people in Makhachkala**: new forms of self-remaking (sport, business, social media).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious history and Sufi writing; mixed for present conversion and recovery, which sit in religious and personal accounts more than statistics.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Avar and Dargin for Sufi verse; Lezgin and Tabasaran for spring rites; Turkish and Arabic for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion out of Islam; former fighters and their security; some Muslim groups banned as "extremist" under Russian law, so their members' accounts carry legal risk; addiction is stigmatized.
- **Held for review:** none.
