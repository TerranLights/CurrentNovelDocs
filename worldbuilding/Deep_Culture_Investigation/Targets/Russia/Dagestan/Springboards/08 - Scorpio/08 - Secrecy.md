*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Naqshbandi and Shadhili followers against other Muslims**: silent and voiced practice, and different ideas of what may be shown.
- **Dargins of Kubachi and Kaitag**: craft secrets and closed men's societies.
- **Avars and Laks of the highlands**: buried books and hidden religion in Soviet times.
- **Andi, Tsez and Archi villages**: tiny communities where little can be hidden from neighbors.
- **Kumyks**: former princely society with remembered ranks, so origins matter at marriage.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans and Aghuls**: southern villages with their own lineage secrets and cross-border kin.
- **Mountain Jews**: identity concealed under another name in Soviet records.
- **Azerbaijanis of Derbent**: Shia discretion in a mostly Sunni land.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: a minority keeping its own counsel.
- **Women**: what is kept from men and from in-laws, and what is kept from women.
- **Sexual minorities**: lives that must stay hidden.
- **Labor migrants**: second lives far from the village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for manuscript history and the Soviet hiding of religion; thin for family secrets and private lives, by nature.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic; Dargin (Kubachi variety); Avar and Kumyk for status terms; Judeo-Tat and Hebrew for Mountain Jewish history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual minorities (great danger to informants under Russian law); hidden status of descendants of slaves; secret marriages; closed security archives; restricted Sufi teaching, which members may not share and which should not be pressed for.
