*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Avar, Dargin and Lak villages** against **Makhachkala**: births at home or in hospital, large and small families.
- **Andi, Tsez and Archi** communities: mothers in very small villages.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: birth and mothering words in five Lezgic languages; mothers whose kin live across the Azerbaijan border.
- **Kumyks** and **Nogais** of the plain and steppe: lowland, Turkic-language families to set against the highland villages.
- **Akkin Chechens** of the Khasavyurt area: mothers in families marked by the 1944 deportation and return.
- **Derbent Azerbaijanis** (Shia) and **Mountain Jews**: mothers in two religious traditions.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: a contrast in family size to test.
- **Mothers of sons in the security forces**, in the war, or **detained**: political motherhood.
- **Divorced mothers** and **widows**; **mothers raising children while fathers work away**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** strong official statistics on births; rich praise in poetry; women's own accounts fewer but growing online.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar for Gamzatov and Aliyeva in the original; Lezgin, Dargin, Kumyk, Lak for birth customs; Arabic for teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** son preference (to research, not assume); NGO reports on female genital cutting; mothers of detained men; mobilization protests; custody after divorce goes to Libra. Sacred praise of mothers is not evidence of mothers' lives; seek both.
- **Held for review:** none.
