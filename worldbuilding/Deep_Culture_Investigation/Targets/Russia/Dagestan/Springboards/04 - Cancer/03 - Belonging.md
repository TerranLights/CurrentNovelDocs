# Dagestan: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland jamaats** against **villages moved to the plain**: belonging to a place one has left.
- **Makhachkala** and **Kaspiysk**: city belonging across peoples.
- **Avars**, and the **Andi, Tsez and Archi** who may or may not call themselves Avar.
- **Dargins**, **Laks**, **Kumyks**, **Lezgins**, **Tabasarans**: each people's own idea of "ours".
- **Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: small peoples on a border, Tsakhurs and Lezgins with kin in Azerbaijan.
- **Nogais** of the northern steppe.
- **Akkin Chechens**: belonging to Dagestan and to the Chechen people at once.
- **Derbent Azerbaijanis** (Shia), **Mountain Jews**, **Tats**.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** of Kizlyar.
- **Followers of different sheikhs** and **other Muslim communities**: belonging by faith path.
- **Children of mixed marriages**; **returning migrants**; the **Turkish and Middle Eastern diaspora**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian ethnography and political science; identity surveys exist but are scattered; diaspora material mostly in Turkish.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Lezgin, Kumyk, Dargin, Lak; Turkish; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic land and representation quarrels; faith-based divisions and state pressure on some Muslim communities; Russian and Jewish emigration; violent episodes. Name many voices, never one.
- **Held for review:** none.
