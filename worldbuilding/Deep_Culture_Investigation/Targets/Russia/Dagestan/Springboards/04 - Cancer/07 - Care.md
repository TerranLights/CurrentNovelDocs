# Dagestan: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Dagestan, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Avar, Dargin, Lezgin, Kumyk, Lak, Chechen, Azerbaijani, Tabasaran; Arabic for religious teaching and medical manuscripts. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland villages** far from hospitals against **Makhachkala** with clinics and paid carers.
- **Avars** and the **Andi and Tsez** villages: healer traditions and care in isolation.
- **Dargins** and **Laks**: households where men work away, a case to check.
- **Lezgins, Tabasarans, Aguls, Rutuls, Tsakhurs**: collective-help customs to separate.
- **Kumyks** and **Nogais** of the plain.
- **Akkin Chechens**.
- **Derbent Azerbaijanis** (Shia charity customs to check) and **Mountain Jews**.
- **Russians** in the cities and Kizlyar: more reliance on state care homes, perhaps; a question.
- **Followers of different Muslim paths**: views on healers and shrines to compare.
- **Families with a migrant son** and those without.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** institutional figures available; family care mostly in small studies and the press; healing practices partly hidden or contested.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Avar, Lezgin, Dargin, Kumyk for village care; Arabic for manuscripts and religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disputes over healers and exorcism; disability stigma; the 2020 death toll; family secrets about mental illness. Do not present devoted family care as one people's trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
