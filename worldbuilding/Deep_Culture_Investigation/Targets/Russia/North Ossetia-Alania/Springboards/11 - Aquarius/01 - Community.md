# North Ossetia-Alania: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Iron and Digor Ossetian, Russian; Ingush, Kabardian and Armenian for the minorities. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Highland villages that remain (Kurtatin, Alagir and Digor gorges) against plains villages founded by resettlers: an old place community against a remade one.
- Digoria, where Christian and Muslim Digor share villages: faith community and place community may not match.
- Vladikavkaz districts and its old suburbs: urban, mixed and anonymous against village life.
- Beslan and the Pravoberezhny district: a community reshaped by mass grief.
- Ingush villages of Prigorodny district (Kartsa, Chermen, Tarskoye *check*): their own community lines after 1992 and the 1944 deportation.
- Mozdok district: stanitsas, Kabardian and Kumyk villages, Armenian families, a military town.
- Newcomers from South Ossetia and their землячества.
- Ossetians in Moscow and abroad: community kept without a shared place.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Soviet ethnography and local history in Russian; present-day measures of belonging are thin at republic level; minorities are thinly covered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Iron, Digor; Ingush; Kabardian; Armenian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan and Prigorodny: survivors and both peoples' testimony first. Some shrine rites limit who may attend.
- **Held for review:** none.
