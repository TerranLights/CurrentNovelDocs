# North Ossetia-Alania: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian in its two literary varieties, Iron (ирон æвзаг) and Digor (дигорон æвзаг); Russian; for minorities, Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk, Armenian and Georgian. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for: friends, groups, hopes and ideals toward humanity at large.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Iron gorge societies, Tagaur, Kurtatin, Alagir and Tual (Nar-Mamison): each had its own estate order and assemblies.
- Digor, Christian and Muslim, in the Digorsky and Irafsky districts: own literary variety and its own nobility (badeliata *check*).
- Highland villages against plains villages settled from the gorges: settlement shapes who meets whom.
- Vladikavkaz: a mixed city with Russian, Armenian, Georgian and other communities.
- Mozdok district: Russians and Terek Cossack stanitsas, Kabardians, Kumyks, Armenians.
- Ingush of Prigorodny district, written from their own sources.
- Migrants and refugees from South Ossetia (1990s, 2008).
- Faith lines: Orthodox, Muslim Ossetians, followers of the traditional faith (Uatsdin *check*).
- Diaspora: Moscow; descendants of 19th-century emigrants in Turkey and Syria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Iron folklore and Soviet ethnography in Russian; thinner for Digor, Muslim Ossetians and the Mozdok minorities; the Ingush of Prigorodny need their own sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Iron, Digor; Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk; French and English for Nart scholarship; the classical languages through Alemany; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this set of sheets is written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Prigorodny conflict (1992) and the Beslan school siege (2004): survivors first, no ranking of peoples. Some shrine rites may be closed to women or outsiders. Memorial was liquidated in Russia (2021) and some independent sites are blocked there.
- **Held for review:** none.
