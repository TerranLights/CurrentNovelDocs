# North Ossetia-Alania: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Iron and Digor Ossetian, Russian; Latin for the classical source. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are praised (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Men and women: lament and composure are often divided by sex at funerals.
- Elders and the young: avoidance and reserve before elders, and whether the young keep it.
- Highland shepherds and Vladikavkaz office workers: solitude by work against urban anonymity.
- Orthodox monastics, Muslim Ossetians and Uatsdin followers: different teachings on calm and letting go.
- Beslan survivors and rescuers: detachment as a wound and as a shield.
- Russians, Armenians and Ingush: their own display rules at funerals and in public.
- Migrants to Moscow: distance from home and kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on avoidance customs and mourning in ethnography; thin on present-day emotional style; measures are national, not regional.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Iron, Digor; Latin for Ammianus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grief and trauma after Beslan and Prigorodny; some mourning rites are family matters; recordings of laments may need the family's consent. Turnout figures in the region are disputed.
- **Held for review:** none.
