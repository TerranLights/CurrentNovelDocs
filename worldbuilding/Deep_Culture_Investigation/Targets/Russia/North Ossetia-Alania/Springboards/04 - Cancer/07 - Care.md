## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland districts** (Irafsky, Alagirsky, Kurtatin and Dargavs areas): care far from hospitals.
- **Vladikavkaz:** clinics, care homes and paid sitters within reach.
- **Iron and Digor folk-healing traditions:** different texts and healers.
- **Orthodox, Muslim and Uatsdin Ossetians:** different religious frames for care of the sick.
- **Families of Beslan:** care given over two decades to survivors.
- **Ingush, Kumyk and Kabardian families:** their own customs of tending elders.
- **Terek Cossacks and Russians:** Orthodox and secular Russian care norms.
- **Armenians and Georgians:** community-based care.
- **Families with members working in Moscow or abroad:** care at a distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely mixed: rich on folk medicine in old ethnography and on official social services; thin on unpaid family care.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (folk texts); Ingush, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan survivors and families are a heavily studied group; prefer their own words and approach with care. Healing incantations may be held by families who do not share them freely.
- **Held for review:** none.
