## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Digoria and the central gorges:** emptied villages, and families who left or stayed.
- **Plain villages that sent men to work far away:** wives, children and parents left behind.
- **Families of those who emigrated in the 1860s,** and Ossetians in Turkey who descend from them.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny:** homes left in 1944 and after 1992.
- **South Ossetians who arrived as refugees:** homes left across the range.
- **Christian, Muslim and Uatsdin Ossetians:** different rites for the kinless dead.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks:** Russian norms on fostering and children's homes.
- **Kumyks, Kabardians, Armenians, Georgians:** each minority's own ways of taking in orphans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on emptied villages and historical emigration; mixed on orphanhood statistics; thin on desertion within families, which is rarely written about.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (laments, tales); Ingush; Turkish for the emigrants' side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation, war orphans of Beslan and of 1992, and children in state homes are sensitive; use the affected people's own accounts first. Desertion of a spouse touches marriage (Libra) and custody (law, Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
