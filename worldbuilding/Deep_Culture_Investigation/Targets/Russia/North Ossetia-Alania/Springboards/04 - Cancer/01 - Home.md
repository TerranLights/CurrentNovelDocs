# North Ossetia-Alania: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Ingush, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian for minorities; Turkish for the diaspora. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families with an ancestral village still standing in the gorges** (Kurtatin, Dargavs, Alagir, Digor gorges) against families whose plain village is the only home they know: the old house may still count as home.
- **Digoria** (Digorsky and Irafsky districts), including Muslim Digor villages around Chikola: home words and rites may differ.
- **Vladikavkaz apartment dwellers:** home without a courtyard or plot.
- **Ingush and Ossetians of the Prigorodny district:** the same houses claimed by two memories.
- **South Ossetians who settled in the north:** home as the place left behind, across a border.
- **Terek Cossacks of the Mozdok stanitsas:** a separate settlement history on the plain.
- **Kumyks and Kabardians of Mozdok district; Armenians and Georgians of Vladikavkaz:** home in a republic named for another people.
- **Ossetians in Turkey and in Moscow:** home at a distance of generations or of one move.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on house types, highland depopulation and the conflict; thin on home as a feeling, which will need memoir, song and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Iron and Digor Ossetian; Ingush for the Prigorodny side; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** housing and property in the Prigorodny district remain a live grievance; use both peoples' sources. Shrines near old villages may have rules on entry.
- **Held for review:** none.
