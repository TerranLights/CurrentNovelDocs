## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland gorges with towers against plain villages:** different histories of self-defense.
- **Beslan and the Pravoberezhny district:** protection shaped by the siege.
- **Iron and Digor shrine traditions:** protector figures and their feasts.
- **Christian, Muslim and Uatsdin Ossetians:** saints, prayers or shrines asked for protection.
- **Ingush and Ossetian villages of Prigorodny:** each side's sense of who guards it.
- **Mozdok district** (Cossacks, Kumyks, Kabardians; a large military base): protection tied to the army and the Chechen wars.
- **Armenians and Georgians of Vladikavkaz:** minority protection through community and church.
- **Women and men:** who is guarded and who guards, as each group describes it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on towers, war memory and Beslan; thin on everyday protective practice in homes.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for official and memorial records; Ossetian for shrine and feast terms (Iron and Digor); Ingush for Prigorodny accounts; Kumyk for Mozdok villages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan (2004), the 1992 Prigorodny conflict and terrorist attacks in Vladikavkaz and Mozdok are recent and painful; read victims' words first. Some shrines restrict entry by sex. Blood revenge and customary settlements are law questions for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
