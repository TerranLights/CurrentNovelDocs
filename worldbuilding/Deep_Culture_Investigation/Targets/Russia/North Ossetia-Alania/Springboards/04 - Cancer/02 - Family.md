# North Ossetia-Alania: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Ingush, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian for minorities. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor families:** kin terms may differ.
- **Families descended from the old nobility** (Tagaur *ældar*, Digor *badeliatæ*) and from commoners: lineage memory and fosterage ties may differ.
- **Christian, Muslim and Uatsdin Ossetian families:** family teaching comes from different sources.
- **Mixed marriages in Vladikavkaz** (Ossetian with Russian, Georgian or Armenian spouses): whose kin system wins.
- **South Ossetian kin of North Ossetian families:** families split by the border.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** clan and lineage structure of their own.
- **Kumyks and Kabardians of Mozdok district; Terek Cossacks:** different lineage words and sizes.
- **Urban professionals in Vladikavkaz against village households:** how far kin reaches when relatives live far away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on kin structure in ethnography; mixed on present-day practice; surname councils are documented online but scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (both varieties); Ingush; Kumyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feuds between lineages and customary settlements touch law (Libra and Capricorn) and should not be researched here; the Prigorodny conflict split neighboring families.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search eLibrary.ru for "терминология родства осетинского языка" and "система родства осетин".
2. Read Dumézil on the Nart families, then the matching tales in an Ossetian edition.
3. Search the web and VK for "фамильный совет" with three or four common Ossetian surnames.
4. Find Dzutsev's surveys and note their family items.
5. Search for the *Ингуши* volume and Terek Cossack family ethnography for the two largest non-Ossetian groups.
