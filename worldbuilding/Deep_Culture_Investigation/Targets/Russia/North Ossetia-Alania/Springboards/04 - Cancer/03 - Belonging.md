## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ossetians who stress the Alan name against those who stress Iron and Digor:** two ways of naming the "us".
- **Digor speakers:** belonging to Digoria and to the Ossetian whole at once.
- **Muslim Ossetians** (some Digor villages and Tagaur families *check*): faith and nationhood may pull differently.
- **Uatsdin followers:** belonging through the old shrines.
- **South Ossetian newcomers:** kin who arrived as refugees.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** belonging to land and to a republic that is not named for them.
- **Russians born in Vladikavkaz; Terek Cossacks:** locals by birth who are not Ossetian.
- **Armenians, Georgians, Greeks, Kumyks, Kabardians:** each with its own history of settlement.
- **Diaspora in Turkey, Moscow and elsewhere:** belonging at a distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on identity politics and the Alan debate; mixed on felt belonging, which needs interviews and online material.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian; Ingush; Turkish; Armenian and Greek for small community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the right of Ingush to return to Prigorodny homes is contested; the Alan name is claimed by more than one people in the North Caucasus; keep every party's sources. Some shrines restrict who may enter.
- **Held for review:** none.
