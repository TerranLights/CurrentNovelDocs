## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland villages and Vladikavkaz:** village зиу against city charities and online appeals; where help still comes from neighbors.
- **Orthodox, Muslim and traditional-faith Ossetians:** three different teachings on alms and on mercy to the stranger.
- **Digor and Iron areas:** separate proverb and tale records; the Digor gorge has a long Muslim strand.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny:** own mosque-based and kin-based help, and a contested past that bears on mercy across groups.
- **Beslan families:** the receivers of the largest outpouring of aid; their view of compassion offered and withdrawn.
- **Refugees from South Ossetia and inner Georgia:** both helped and, at times, resented; ask both sides.
- **Terek Cossack stanitsas and Mozdok's Kabardians and Kumyks:** the north's own church, mosque and kin networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore and literature are rich, regional giving data are thin and mostly in national surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor); Ingush and Kabardian for minority networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan and the 1992 conflict; appeals for named sick children (do not reproduce names). Mercy in blood feud and pardon belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read «Сидзæргæс» in Ossetian and in Russian translation, then search the СОИГСИ journal for studies of pity in Kosta's verse.
2. Search «зиу» together with «осетины» and «взаимопомощь» in ethnographic journals.
3. Find the diocese's and the Muslim board's pages on charity, and list what each runs.
4. Pull the HSE civil-society monitoring for the republic's row on giving and volunteering.
5. Search UNHCR's archive for "North Ossetia forced migrants" for reception reports.
