*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women; elders and the young:** rules of restraint (æfsærm) may fall differently by sex and age; test, do not assume.
- **Iron and Digor stages and literatures:** two audiences, two repertoires of identification.
- **Ossetians and Ingush:** empathy across the 1992 line is the hardest case in the republic.
- **Beslan survivors and the wider republic:** who is felt with, and when the public stops.
- **Vladikavkaz Russians, Armenians and Georgians:** city peoples with their own churches, speech and mourning forms.
- **Mozdok district's Cossacks, Kabardians and Kumyks:** the plains north, with their own etiquette and languages.
- **Refugees from South Ossetia and inner Georgia:** insiders and outsiders at once.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin for measured empathy; mixed for literature and ritual, which are rich but scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for scholarship; Ossetian (Iron and Digor) for laments, theater and social media; Ingush for the other side of the conflict line.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan victims and survivors, and psychologists' case accounts; Ossetian-Ingush relations. Recordings of laments at real funerals: ask families.
- **Held for review:** none.
