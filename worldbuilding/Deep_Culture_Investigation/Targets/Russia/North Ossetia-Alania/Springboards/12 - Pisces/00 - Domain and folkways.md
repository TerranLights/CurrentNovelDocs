# North Ossetia-Alania: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Ossetian in both its Iron and Digor forms (Cyrillic script); Ingush, Kabardian and Kumyk for the minorities; Armenian and Georgian for older Vladikavkaz communities. **Concept in one line:** how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape: the hidden and the unconscious, confinement and institutions, sacrifice, compassion and transcendence, with magic ways as the sign's folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor speakers:** two literary standards and partly separate folklore records; Digor variants need their own search.
- **The old highland societies (Tagauria, Kurtatia, Alagiria, Digoria):** each gorge keeps its own shrines and feast calendar.
- **Plains villages resettled from the mountains:** what moved with people and what stayed with the land.
- **Christian, Muslim and traditional-faith Ossetians:** three religious frames for the dead and the unseen, often within one family line.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** own language, Islam and Sufi brotherhoods, contested recent history.
- **Terek Cossacks of the stanitsas and Mozdok; Mozdok Kabardians and Kumyks:** the north's own peoples and records.
- **Armenians, Georgians and Greeks of Vladikavkaz:** own churches and customs for the dead.
- **Refugees from South Ossetia and inner Georgia; Ossetians in Turkey and Syria:** displacement's effect.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries the pre-war lands only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core lands of the republic.
- **Data:** rich folklore and ethnography; thin survey data, the republic often only a row in Russian tables.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor) for folklore and the faith's texts; Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk; French and German for older scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Beslan siege (2004) and the Ossetian-Ingush conflict of 1992: never one side's account alone. Shrine-keepers' (дзуарылæг) prayers and some sanctuaries may be closed to outsiders; ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** none.
