## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Traditional-faith, Orthodox and dual-practice Ossetians:** many families mix shrine feasts and church rites; the mix itself is a question.
- **Digor Muslims and the Digor gorge:** Islam with local feasts; a separate strand.
- **Kurtatin, Tsey and other gorges:** each with its own sanctuaries and keepers.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny:** Sufi brotherhoods and mosque life.
- **Mozdok's Kabardians and Kumyks:** Muslim practice in the north, apart from Ossetian Islam.
- **Terek Cossacks:** Orthodox and possibly Old Believer lines, as their communities describe them.
- **Armenians and Georgians of Vladikavkaz:** their own churches and saints.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ethnography of feasts and shrines; mixed for church and mosque life; thin for personal experience and private prayer.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor) for prayers and feast speech; Ingush and Arabic terms for Sufi practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relations between the Orthodox diocese and the revived faith; some shrines and prayers are kept within families or lineages; Sufi brotherhoods are a sensitive subject in the North Caucasus. Ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search «осетинские молитвы» and «куывды ныхæстæ» for printed prayer texts.
2. Find «Ацæтæ» and any other registered traditional-faith organization, and read their own descriptions.
3. Search news archives for «Джеоргуыба» to see how the feast is reported, year by year.
4. Search «святилища Северной Осетии» with gorge names.
5. Search for studies of the Batal-Haji wird among the Ingush.
