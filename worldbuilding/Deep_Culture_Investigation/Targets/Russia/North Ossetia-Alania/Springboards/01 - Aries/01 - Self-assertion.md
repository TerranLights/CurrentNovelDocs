## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders and juniors:** speaking order at the feast and the *ныхас* follows age; how young men assert themselves inside it is the core question.
- **Men and women:** the women's side of the feast, the daughter-in-law, widows heading a household.
- **Vladikavkaz youth against mountain villages:** urban workplaces and campuses may set other rules for speaking up.
- **Digor and Iron communities:** separate gorge traditions of assembly.
- **Former noble and commoner lineages:** older ranks (aldars, *бæдилатæ*) may still shape who claims precedence.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district:** claims made as a minority in a contested district.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok district:** the stanitsa circle as their own forum.
- **Kabardian villages of Mozdok district:** another Caucasian code of speech etiquette.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnography of speech order is rich, survey data on assertiveness is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Ingush, Kabardian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political protest in the republic is policed; reporting may be partisan; Ossetian-Ingush claims are a live grievance.
- **Held for review:** none.
