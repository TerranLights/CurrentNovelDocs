## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor:** whether the Digor label sits inside or beside "Ossetian".
- **Christian, Muslim and traditional-faith Ossetians:** faith may split or not split the answer to "who are you?"
- **Mozdok Ossetians:** a historically separate Christian settler community (*check*).
- **Families from South Ossetia and inner Georgia:** identity shaped by flight and by the unification question.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district:** an identity tied to a disputed homeland.
- **Terek Cossacks:** a Cossack identity distinct from a Russian one.
- **Armenians and Georgians of Vladikavkaz:** old urban minorities with their own churches.
- **Kabardians and Kumyks (*check*) of Mozdok district:** a different self-name and kin people across the republic border, so identity labels need separate sampling.
- **Mixed Russian-Ossetian families:** where language shift is likeliest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; identity and origins are a favored topic of local scholarship.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian, Georgian (for the chronicles), Turkish, Ingush.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin claims (Alan, Scythian) are politically charged with neighbors; refugee and Ingush questions remain raw.
- **Held for review:** none.
