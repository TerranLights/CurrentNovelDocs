## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daughters and sons:** different degrees of say over marriage and leaving home.
- **Youngest son and elder sons:** inheritance of the father's house may tie one son more tightly.
- **Brides in a husband's household and widows:** the changing say of a woman over a lifetime.
- **Vladikavkaz professionals against mountain and plain villages:** how much say a person has over career, residence and marriage may differ by setting, so sample both.
- **Christian, Muslim and traditional-faith families:** religious rules about marriage and the body differ.
- **Ingush families of Prigorodny:** lineage councils as another layer of control.
- **Terek Cossacks:** stanitsa self-rule.
- **Russians and Armenians of Vladikavkaz:** other urban family norms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; marriage ethnography is rich; republic-level survey data on personal autonomy is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian, German (Klaproth), Ingush.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride abduction, coercion and domestic violence; individual cases in the press must not be re-exposed; the republic's political status.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search a catalog for "осетинская свадьба" and "семейный быт осетин"; pull the chapters on choosing a spouse.
2. Download the latest КОУЖ regional tables and find the share of 25- to 34-year-olds living with parents.
3. Read *Фатима*, then search for school curricula and essays that teach it.
4. Search the archive guide of the Central State Archive for Party files on "пережитки".
5. Search "упорядочение обрядов Осетия" for community rulings on feasts.
