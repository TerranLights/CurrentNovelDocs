# North Ossetia-Alania: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Ingush and Kabardian for minority traditions. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women:** a courage vocabulary built on *лæг* ("man") may or may not extend to women's endurance.
- **Generations:** WWII, the Afghan war, the 1990s conflicts, 2008, Beslan; each has its own heroes.
- **Soldiers and civilians:** military valor against civilian rescue and moral refusal.
- **Christian and traditional-faith families:** Uastyrdzhi as saint or as native patron.
- **Digor and Iron gorges:** separate song traditions of local heroes.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny:** the *къонах* ideal, and courage remembered on the other side of 1992.
- **Terek Cossacks and Kabardians of Mozdok district:** frontier warrior memories of their own, honored through other heroes and rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and folklore sources; thin for moral and social courage outside war.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first (archives, citations), Ossetian (Iron, Digor) for songs; Ingush and Kabardian for minority traditions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan victims and families; current-war mobilization is politically charged; heroes on one side of 1992 may be accused on the other; some Uastyrdzhi rites are men-only.
- **Held for review:** none.
