## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland and lowland hosts:** guest customs may differ with distance from the city and the road.
- **Christian and Muslim families, especially in Digoria:** feast-giving with drink and prayer against alms and charity by Muslim rule.
- **Wealthy sponsors and ordinary households:** giving that makes standing against giving that strains a budget.
- **Ossetians working in Moscow:** remitters and village benefactors.
- **Ingush, Kabardian and Kumyk neighbors:** separate guest codes, to keep distinct rather than merged into a "Caucasian hospitality".
- **Refugees and Beslan families:** people more often on the receiving side.
- **Russians and Armenians of Vladikavkaz and Mozdok:** urban church charity and trade-guild giving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hospitality and feasts; mixed for present-day cash giving; thin for measured charity at the republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron, Digor); German for Güldenstädt; Ingush, Kabardian, Armenian, Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** debts from memorial feasts can be a private shame; Beslan aid disputes; avoid the outsiders' cliché of a single "Caucasian hospitality". Law on bride-wealth belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
