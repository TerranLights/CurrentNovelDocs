# North Ossetia-Alania: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lineages (*мыггæгтæ*):** pride may sit with the family name first; compare large and small lineages.
- **Former noble lineages (Tagaur *æлдæрттæ*, Digor *бæдæлиатæ*) and commoner lineages:** older status pride, and how Soviet rule recast it.
- **Iron and Digor:** whether either side claims the older speech or the closer Alan descent; check both literatures.
- **Vladikavkaz and the villages:** city pride against valley and village pride.
- **Mozdok Russians and Terek Cossacks:** frontier and military pride of their own.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny, Georgians, Armenians:** minority pride under a titular majority.
- **War generation and post-Soviet youth:** war heroes against sport and stage stars as models.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epic, war memory and sport; mixed for lineage pride (mostly self-published); thin for surveys at the republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron, Digor); Georgian, Armenian, Ingush; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Alan descent is claimed by several North Caucasus peoples; record claims without judging them. War pride touches the 1992 conflict and the wars in South Ossetia; keep stereotypes of a "warrior people" out, as outsiders' claims only.
- **Held for review:** none.
