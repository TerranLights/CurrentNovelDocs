## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland craft villages and Vladikavkaz's art world:** household making against academy-trained art.
- **Women's embroidery and felt-making against men's smithing, carving and stone-building:** creativity split by sex, with different paths to credit.
- **Iron and Digor writers:** two literary norms with separate canons and readerships.
- **Mozdok Russians and Terek Cossacks:** a separate craft and song tradition on the plain.
- **Armenian and Georgian craftsmen of Vladikavkaz:** urban trades (jewelry, tailoring, building) with their own lineages.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** their own tower-building and craft heritage, kept apart in the sources.
- **Diaspora in Turkey:** crafts that did not pass through Soviet institutions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for archaeology and folk applied art; mixed for living professional art; thin for invention and the everyday making-do of households.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron, Digor); Armenian, Georgian, Ingush; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ossetian and Ingush claims to the same towers, crafts and Alan heritage are politically charged; report both, take no side. Patterns on dress and shrine objects may carry family or religious meaning; ask makers before publishing them.
- **Held for review:** none.
