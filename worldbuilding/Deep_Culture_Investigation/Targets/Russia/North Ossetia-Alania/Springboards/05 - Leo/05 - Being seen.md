# North Ossetia-Alania: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron and Digor), Russian; Armenian, Georgian, Ingush for minorities; Turkish and German for older and diaspora sources. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brides and young wives against elder women:** visibility rules that shift with marriage and age.
- **Men of the *ныхас* and men who work away:** being seen at home against being unseen as migrants in Moscow.
- **Digor and Iron:** whether Digor identity is visible in counts, media and schools.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny:** visibility tied to safety since 1992.
- **Russians of Mozdok and Vladikavkaz:** a majority in Russia but a minority here.
- **Refugees from Georgia and South Ossetia:** counted, housed and seen as a category.
- **The elderly in emptying highland villages:** who notices them.
- **Diaspora in Turkey:** hidden, then newly visible through associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wedding and mourning rites; mixed for media representation; thin for measured isolation and loneliness.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron, Digor); German for Klaproth; Armenian, Georgian, Ingush; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan survivors' privacy; the Ingush minority's safety; xenophobia data name victims; old photographs may show people who did not consent, so handle with care. Memorial was dissolved by Russian courts; archived copies only.
- **Held for review:** none.
