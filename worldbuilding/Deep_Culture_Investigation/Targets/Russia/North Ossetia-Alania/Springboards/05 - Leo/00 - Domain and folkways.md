## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor Ossetians:** two literary communities whose festival calendars and song repertoires differ.
- **Gorge societies (Tagaur, Kurtatin, Alagir, Digor; Tual on the border):** older highland identities that carry their own shrines and dances.
- **Highland villages against 19th-century lowland settlements:** festivals moved with resettlement.
- **Christian, Muslim and traditional-religion Ossetians:** different rules on dancing, drinking and feasting.
- **Vladikavkaz:** urban, multiethnic, with theaters, clubs and nightlife.
- **Mozdok district:** Russians, Terek Cossack stanitsas, Kumyks, Kabardians and Armenians, each with its own festive forms.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district** and **refugees from South Ossetia and Georgia**: separate communities with separate memories.
- **Diaspora in Turkey:** descendants of 19th-century emigrants whose festive forms escaped Soviet change.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epic, folklore, dance and wrestling; mixed for festivals (revival writing is partisan); thin for measured leisure and values. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the republic's core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor); French and German for older scholarship; Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict and the 2004 Beslan school siege; shrine rites that may be limited by sex or lineage (ask before recording); some regional outlets carry Russian "foreign agent" labels.
- **Held for review:** none.
