## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men and elders at the Ossetian feast:** service follows age, so age groups are the first split.
- **Daughters-in-law, mothers-in-law and daughters:** household service falls on women in different ways by role.
- **Shrine-keeping families and Uatsdin congregations:** service owed to a sanctuary by lineage or rotation.
- **Orthodox parishes and monastics; Muslim Digor and Ingush communities:** religious service in different forms.
- **Military families and cadets:** service to the state as a family tradition.
- **Terek Cossacks of the Mozdok and Vladikavkaz districts:** a service estate with its own history.
- **Armenian and Georgian parishes of the capital:** church service and charity of their own.
- **Workers in shops, cafés and guest houses:** paid service, often by younger and female staff.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and feast service; mixed for paid service work; thin for minority religious service.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian for etiquette and the press; Armenian and Georgian for parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan and Karmadon; shrine duties may be family matters not shared with outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
