# North Ossetia-Alania: Domain and folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron Ossetians of the gorge societies** (Tagaur, Kurtatin, Alagir): mountain herding against plains farming after the resettlement.
- **Digor Ossetians** (Iraf and Digora districts): their own words for work and health, and a separate gorge economy.
- **Christian, Muslim and Uatsdin (traditional faith) Ossetians:** rest days and purity rules may differ.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks** of the Mozdok district and the old stanitsas: a separate farming and service tradition.
- **Kumyk and Kabardian villages** of the Mozdok district: plains farming and household tasks under other languages.
- **Armenians** of Mozdok and Vladikavkaz; **Georgians** and **Greeks** of the capital: possible trade and craft niches.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** a separate language and household order (see flags).
- **Migrants from South Ossetia and inner Georgia** since the 1990s: working lives rebuilt after displacement.
- **Vladikavkaz** against the villages: the urban workday against the farm round; the **diaspora** in Moscow and Turkey: work away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for custom, folklore and the Soviet economy; mixed for the measured daily round; thin for minorities.
- **Lands:** the pre-war lands are listed only as Russia, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet covers the present republic's core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Iron and Digor Ossetian; Ingush and Kumyk; French for Dumézil.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict and the Prigorodny district; the 2004 Beslan school siege; some shrine rites are kept within families and villages.
- **Held for review:** none.

5. Search the «Рæстдзинад» archive for «куыст» and «лæггад» in obituaries.
