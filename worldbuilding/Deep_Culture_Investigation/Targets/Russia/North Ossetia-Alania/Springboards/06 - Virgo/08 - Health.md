## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain villages:** distance from clinics, old remedies and the health of herders.
- **Vladikavkaz and its industrial districts:** pollution, hospitals and private clinics.
- **Mothers and young children:** the old protective rites and the maternity system.
- **Older people:** chronic illness and the long-life claims.
- **Christian, Uatsdin and Muslim Ossetians:** different routes to healing beyond the clinic.
- **Beslan families:** long-term health after the siege.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** separate healers and health networks.
- **Mozdok's Cossacks and Kumyks:** folk medicine of their own.
- **Migrants from South Ossetia:** health after displacement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for vital statistics and folklore; mixed for folk healing today; thin for mental health.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Iron and Digor Ossetian for charms and songs; Ingush and Kumyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan; healing rites and shrines may be private; disease and addiction carry stigma.
- **Held for review:** none.
