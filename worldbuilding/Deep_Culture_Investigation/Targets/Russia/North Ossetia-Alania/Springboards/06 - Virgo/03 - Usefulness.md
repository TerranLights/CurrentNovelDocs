## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain households:** children's herding and haymaking tasks made them needed early.
- **Plains and city families:** schooling and services change what a child is useful for.
- **Elder men and elder women:** formal roles at the feast and in councils go mostly to men; women's roles may lie elsewhere.
- **Disabled people and the long-term sick:** how they are included or set apart.
- **Labor-migrant families:** grandparents and wives who carry the work at home.
- **Digor and Iron tale traditions:** separate records of the useful and useless son.
- **Ingush and Kumyk villages:** a shared loan word for benefit, but separate household orders.
- **Terek Cossack families of the Mozdok district:** a farming household with its own age roles.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk upbringing and tales; mixed for elders and disability; thin for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Iron and Digor Ossetian; Ingush and Kumyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability, old age and the sense of being a burden need care; data on migrant households may be informal.
- **Held for review:** none.
