## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old central Vladikavkaz and its newer outskirts:** public order and cleanliness may differ by quarter.
- **Mozdok, a garrison and airbase town:** order shaped by the military presence.
- **Herding villages around the national park:** state land rules meet customary grazing.
- **Christian and Muslim households:** separate calendars that set the order of the year.
- **Terek Cossack stanitsas:** Cossack patrols that claim a part in keeping order.
- **Prigorodny Ingush:** police patrols seen from the other side of the 1992 line.
- **Settlements built for South Ossetian refugees after 2008:** new neighborhoods with order still being made.
- **Young people at night and their elders:** who decides what counts as disturbance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cosmology, ritual and the built town; mixed for crime and safety data; thin for local views of noise, dirt and disorder.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian (Iron and Digor), Latin (Ammianus, translations exist), Ingush, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** terror attacks and the security response; war refugees; restricted shrine places and crypts, as communities share them; burial sites are not entered without permission.
- **Held for review:** none.
