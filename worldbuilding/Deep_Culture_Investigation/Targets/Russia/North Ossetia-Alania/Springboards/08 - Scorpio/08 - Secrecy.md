# North Ossetia-Alania: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Kumyk, Armenian and Georgian for minorities. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden, including privacy.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hunters and mountain herders:** the users of the secret hunting register; whether anyone still speaks it is open.
- **Women and men:** which rites and words are closed to one or the other, as a question to test.
- **Shrine-keeping lineages:** families who know what others may not.
- **LGBT people in the republic:** secrecy as survival rather than choice.
- **Families of the repressed and of the 1992 missing:** histories hidden for safety or still unknown.
- **Beslan families:** secrecy experienced as something done to them by the state.
- **Muslim and Christian Ossetians in mixed families:** what is told and not told across the faith line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the hunters' speech and avoidance customs in older ethnography; mixed for lineage secrets; thin for privacy as people feel it today.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian (Iron and Digor), Russian; Kumyk, Georgian, Armenian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shrine rites and some lineage knowledge are restricted: record that they exist, never their content, and only with the keepers' consent. Since 2023 LGBT advocacy is legally banned in Russia, so contacting sources may endanger them. State archives on Beslan and security matters are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
