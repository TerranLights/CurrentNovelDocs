# North Ossetia-Alania: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Ingush, Kumyk and Georgian for minorities. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** a people deported in 1944 and expelled again in 1992; their account of being purged differs from the Ossetian one.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok district:** descendants of a 1920 removal, with their own memory of who was cleared out.
- **Shrine-keeping lineages:** families who hold the rules for sacred places in their own villages.
- **Muslim Ossetians and Kumyks:** washing and fasting rules taken from Islam.
- **Vladikavkaz neighborhoods near Electrozinc:** pollution as daily life, and who campaigned against it.
- **Alagir gorge mining settlements:** a different industrial poisoning, far from the capital.
- **Families of the repressed:** the long effect of the 1930s purges on lineage memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political repression and the 1992 conflict; mixed for shrine and fasting rules; thin for personal cleansing and folk healing today.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian (Iron and Digor), Russian; Ingush; Greek for Herodotus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation and the 1992 expulsions are contested and still political; shrine rules may bar women or outsiders, so their keepers' consent comes first; Memorial is closed in Russia, so its archive is reached abroad.
- **Held for review:** none.
