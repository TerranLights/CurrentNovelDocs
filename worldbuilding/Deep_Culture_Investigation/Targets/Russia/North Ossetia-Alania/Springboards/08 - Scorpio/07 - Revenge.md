*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor Ossetians:** different gorges and nobilities, which may have had different feud histories.
- **Mountain and plains villages:** whether feuds clung to the gorges, and what moving to the plain did to them.
- **Ingush of the Prigorodny district:** their own feud and reconciliation customs, and the memory of 1992.
- **Ossetians from South Ossetia:** a recent war with Georgia that may shape talk of payback.
- **Women:** their part in feuds, as mourners who demand vengeance or as peacemakers, as a question.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok district:** a frontier tradition with its own codes of retaliation.
- **Vladikavkaz youth:** fights, street codes and online shaming as a modern form.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the epic and 19th-century ethnography; mixed for reconciliation custom; thin and sensitive for revenge today.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian (Iron and Digor), Russian; Ingush, Georgian; German for Güldenstädt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collective revenge after 1992 and Beslan is a live fear; naming families in feuds puts them at risk. The customary law of feud and payment (for example, Kovalevsky's study of Ossetian custom) is Libra's or Capricorn's, not this sheet's.
- **Held for review:** none.
