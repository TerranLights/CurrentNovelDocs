# North Ossetia-Alania: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Ossetian (Iron and Digor); Georgian for mixed families from Georgia. **Concept in one line** (proposed): how people live with two-ness: two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor speakers:** one people or two literary standards.
- **Christian, Muslim and folk-faith Ossetians:** different ways of holding two faiths.
- **Urban bilinguals in Vladikavkaz and village speakers:** mixing tolerated or scorned.
- **Ossetians from Georgia and South Ossetia:** Georgian as a third language; mixed families.
- **Mixed Ossetian-Ingush, Ossetian-Russian and Ossetian-Kabardian families:** belonging to two sides.
- **Terek Cossacks:** Russian and local identities at once.
- **Diaspora in Turkey and Moscow:** Ossetian with Turkish or metropolitan Russian lives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language status, faith and identity debates; thin for everyday "two faces" and mixed feelings.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor); Georgian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Iron-Digor question is politically charged; mixed Ossetian-Georgian and Ossetian-Ingush families carry war memories; folk-faith rites may be closed to outsiders. Never set one form of Ossetian identity as the true one.
- **Held for review:** none.
