## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Girls and boys:** separate histories of entry into school; tasks taught at home may differ.
- **Highland villages and lowland towns:** small schools and farm work against city schools.
- **Russian-medium urban families and Ossetian-speaking village families:** which language the first lessons come in.
- **Digor:** whether Digor is taught, or only Iron.
- **Ingush families of Prigorodny:** Ingush-language teaching and separate schools.
- **Russians and Terek Cossacks:** Cossack cadet classes and stanitsa traditions.
- **Kabardians and Kumyks of Mozdok district:** their own heritage-language teaching.
- **Armenians and Georgians:** Sunday and community schools.
- **Families displaced from Georgia and South Ossetia:** children who changed school systems.
- **Beslan:** a separate case after 2004.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on school history and ethnopedagogy (a large Russian literature, uneven in quality); mixed on present-day home learning.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian for primers and children's press; German for Sjögren and early scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Beslan touches every school topic; Ingush schooling in Prigorodny is tied to the 1992 conflict. Ethnopedagogy writing can present one ideal "Ossetian upbringing"; treat it as a source of questions, not a voice for all.
- **Held for review:** none.
