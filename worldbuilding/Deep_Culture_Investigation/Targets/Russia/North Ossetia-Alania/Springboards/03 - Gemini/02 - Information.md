## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vladikavkaz against high-gorge villages:** signal, broadband and newspaper reach differ.
- **Older and younger people:** television and the district paper against Telegram and Instagram.
- **Beslan and Pravoberezhny district:** a community with its own record of official and unofficial information.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district:** separate outlets (Ingush-run sites and papers) and contested accounts of 1992.
- **Mozdok:** a garrison town where some information is restricted; Cossack, Kumyk and Kabardian villages nearby.
- **Digor and Muslim Ossetian families:** religious channels as sources.
- **Migrants from South Ossetia and Georgia:** Georgian and South Ossetian media in the mix.
- **Armenians:** community and church notices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich historical press and documents; current media trust data mostly national, with thin regional detail.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Ossetian for the Ossetian-language press; Georgian for chronicles and migrant media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Russian media law, "foreign agent" labels and blocked sites shape what is published and who will talk; Beslan casualty disputes; the Ingush-Ossetian conflict; Mozdok military information. Censorship as law goes to Libra and Capricorn; here, only its effects on who knows what.
- **Held for review:** none.
