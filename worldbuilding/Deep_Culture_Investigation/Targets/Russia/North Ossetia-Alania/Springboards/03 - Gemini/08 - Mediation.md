## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland societies (Tagaur, Kurtat, Alagir, Digor, Tual):** each had its own go-betweens and assemblies.
- **Christian, Muslim and folk-faith Ossetians:** priest, imam or sanctuary keeper as intercessor.
- **Ingush villages of Prigorodny:** their own elders and reconciliation customs, and a recent history of failed mediation.
- **Kabardians and Kumyks of Mozdok district:** Adyghe and Kumyk peacemaking forms.
- **Terek Cossacks:** atamans and stanitsa assemblies.
- **Women:** whether women can act as go-betweens (a mother, a sister, an elder woman) and in which matters.
- **Ossetians from South Ossetia and Georgia:** the JCC years as lived experience.
- **Vladikavkaz professionals:** formal mediators and lawyers against kin and elders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich ethnography of feud and courtship go-betweens; official mediation of the 1990s conflicts well documented; thin on everyday mediation now.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian; Georgian for JCC sources; Ingush for Ingush accounts; Greek or translations for Byzantine texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Ossetian-Ingush conflict and the Georgian-Ossetian wars; Memorial was dissolved in Russia (2021), so its archives may be reached only abroad; sanctuary intercession may be restricted. Customary courts and reconciliation rules go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
