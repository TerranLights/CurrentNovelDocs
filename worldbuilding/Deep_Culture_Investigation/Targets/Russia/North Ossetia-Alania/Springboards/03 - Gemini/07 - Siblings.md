## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor:** different kin words; possibly different household customs.
- **Descendants of former noble and commoner families** (Digor badeliatæ, Tagaur aldars): older rules that ranked half-siblings.
- **Christian and Muslim families:** religious teaching on inheritance and on brothers' duties to sisters.
- **Large rural families and small urban ones:** the number and closeness of siblings.
- **Ingush families of Prigorodny:** their own brother-sister customs.
- **Terek Cossacks, Kabardians, Kumyks of Mozdok district:** different household and inheritance forms.
- **Armenians and Georgians:** church-based family models.
- **Migrant families from Georgia and South Ossetia:** siblings split across borders.
- **Beslan families:** sibling loss on a large scale.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in epic, kinship ethnography and census figures; thin on present-day sibling relations and rivalry.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor); Ingush and Kabardian for minority families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** brother-sister honor customs touch women's autonomy; historic rank and "unequal birth" labels may still wound families; the Satana motif needs careful framing; Beslan losses; inheritance law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
