## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron highland societies** (Tagaur, Kurtat, Alagir, Tual): separate gorge traditions and dialects.
- **Digor, Christian and Muslim:** a distinct language form, its own literature and theater, and two faith communities.
- **Vladikavkaz:** Russian-dominant city speech.
- **Plains villages of resettled highlanders:** did talk and neighbor ways move with them?
- **Ossetians from South Ossetia and inner Georgia** (refugees and migrants since the 1990s): Kudar speech, Georgian as a second language.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district:** a separate language and kin system, and a sensitive history.
- **Russians, including the Terek Cossack stanitsas;** Kabardians and Kumyks of Mozdok district; Armenians and Georgians of the towns.
- **Diaspora:** Ossetians in Turkey (19th-century emigrants) and Moscow, as contrast for language keeping.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in folklore, ethnography and philology; thin in survey data on talk styles and neighbor contact. The pre-war lands are given only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the others in this sign are written for the republic's core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron and Digor); German and French for older scholarship; Georgian, Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict over Prigorodny district; the 2004 Beslan school siege (any school topic touches it); sanctuary (дзуар) customs of the Ossetian folk faith, some of which keepers may not share. Name variation; never one "Ossetian" voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
