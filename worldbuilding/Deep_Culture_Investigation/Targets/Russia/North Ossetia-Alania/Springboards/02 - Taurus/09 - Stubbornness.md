## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain elders** who stay against **families who moved down.**
- **Traditional-faith keepers** against **church and mosque reformers:** steadfastness in rival directions.
- **Language activists** against **Russian-speaking urban families:** holding to Ossetian against shifting to Russian is a test case for refusal to give ground.
- **Iron and Digor communities;** Digor Muslims: holding to a dialect and a faith as minorities within the people.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok district:** memory of holding out in the Civil War.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district** and **Ossetian neighbors:** long-held collective claims.
- **Kabardians and Kumyks of Mozdok district;** **Armenians and Georgians of Vladikavkaz:** their own idioms of firmness, and of yielding.
- **Women and men:** who is called "firm" and who "obstinate".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in war and resistance history and folklore; thin on everyday stubbornness, apology and changing one's mind.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian; Latin and Persian (or translations) for the Mongol-era sources; Ingush and Kabardian for minority voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** resistance narratives are often used in present disputes; Prigorodny district; recent protests and their prosecution. Name no living private persons. Courts and protest law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
