*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain lineages** still in the gorges against **families resettled on the plains** in the 19th and 20th centuries.
- **Iron and Digor speakers:** different rates of language continuity.
- **Old Vladikavkaz families** against **newcomers** from the villages and from Georgia.
- **Mozdok Armenians and Terek Cossacks:** long-settled minorities with their own continuity.
- **Kumyk and Kabardian villages of Mozdok district:** their own measures of how long a family has held a place.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district:** broken and restored residence.
- **Refugee settlements** from South Ossetia and inner Georgia: continuity rebuilt after a forced break.
- **Christian and traditional-faith shrine communities;** Muslim Ossetians of Digoria: three lines of ritual continuity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on archaeology, folklore and population statistics; mixed on present routines; thin on people's own talk of steadiness and change.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Ossetian (Iron and Digor); Ingush for deportation testimony; Armenian for parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 deportation and 1992; claims of first settlement used in disputes; genetic genealogy used to rank peoples. Keep every claim to its source and community.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. On fedstat.ru, pull migration in and out of RSO-Alania by district for the last fifteen years.
2. On eLibrary, search «горные сёла Северной Осетии» with «миграция» or «запустение».
3. Search for Tekhov's Koban monographs and the Verkhny Dzhulat excavation reports in library catalogs.
4. Search «осетинский народный календарь» and note each feast's month and keepers.
5. Check UNESCO's atlas entries for Ossetian and Digor, then SOIGSI's language-situation studies.
