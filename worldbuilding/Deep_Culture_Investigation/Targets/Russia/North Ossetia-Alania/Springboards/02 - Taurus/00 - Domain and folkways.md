## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor:** two literary standards; Digoria has its own nobility and a Muslim strand.
- **Mountain gorges against the plains:** Tagaur, Kurtatin and Alagir gorges against resettled lowland villages (Ardon, Digora, Chikola): scarce land against grain farming.
- **Vladikavkaz:** multiethnic market and factory city.
- **Mozdok district:** Terek Cossack stanitsas, Kumyk and Kabardian villages, Mozdok Armenians and Ossetians: separate histories of trade and land.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district;** Armenians, Georgians and Greeks of Vladikavkaz: their own household goods, foods and church giving.
- **Christian, Muslim and Uatsdin (traditional-faith) Ossetians:** separate teaching on feasts and alms.
- **Refugees from South Ossetia and inner Georgia:** households rebuilt from little; **diasporas** in Moscow, Turkey and Syria: money sent home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich 19th-century and Soviet ethnography and regional statistics; thinner recent Ossetian-language material. The pre-war lands are given only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the republic's present lands.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian (Iron, Digor); Ingush, Kumyk, Kabardian; Georgian for chronicles; Latin for Ammianus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** housing and land in Prigorodny district after 1992; the Beslan school siege (2004); rival claims to the Alan heritage; some sanctuary rites are closed to outsiders or to women, so use only what keepers share. Property and inheritance law go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
