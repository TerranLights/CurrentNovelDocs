## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban Vladikavkaz couples and mountain-village households:** the nuclear couple against the joint household.
- **Iron and Digor Ossetians:** whether their noble lines made marriage alliances with different neighbors.
- **Muslim Ossetians and Christian or traditional-faith Ossetians:** different marriage rites and rules on mixed marriage.
- **Mixed Ossetian-Russian, Ossetian-Georgian and Ossetian-Ingush families:** partnerships across lines shaped by the region's conflicts.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok district:** stanitsa marriage and labor-sharing customs.
- **Kabardians and Kumyks of Mozdok district:** their own host-friend and marriage alliances.
- **The diaspora in Turkey:** marriage within a small emigrant community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for host-friend bonds and dynastic ties in older literature; mixed for modern couples; business-partnership data is likely thin beyond registers.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian, Russian; Georgian for medieval history; Kabardian for the dynastic ties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mixed families affected by the 1992 and Georgian conflicts; forced marriage and bride abduction, where cases involve named people; partnerships between the two Ossetias touch disputed statehood.
- **Held for review:** none.
