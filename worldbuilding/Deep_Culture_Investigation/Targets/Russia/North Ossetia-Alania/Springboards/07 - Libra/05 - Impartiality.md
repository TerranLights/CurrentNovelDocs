*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Large surname groups and small ones:** whether the size of a lineage changes access to offices.
- **Vladikavkaz and the district towns (Beslan, Alagir, Ardon, Digora, Mozdok):** whether recusal and distance work differently in towns where people know their officials.
- **Ingush residents of Prigorodny district:** their experience of officials and police as a minority in a disputed area.
- **South Ossetian arrivals:** their access to residence and posts.
- **Mozdok's Russians and Cossacks, Kabardians and Kumyks:** representation in a mixed district.
- **Muslim and Christian Ossetians:** whether religion affects treatment by officials, as each reports it.
- **Students and young professionals:** exams, admissions and first jobs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; official disclosures, court decisions and election data exist; studies of kin favoritism in the republic are likely thin and scattered in press reports.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian, Russian; Ingush for the Prigorodny side; Georgian and English for the peacekeeping materials; Latin or a translation for Ammianus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations against named officials and families; the legal status of election-observer groups in Russia *check*; the neutrality of peacekeepers is contested by Georgia.
- **Held for review:** none.
