*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron and Digor communities:** whether their petitions and envoys to the empire went through different channels.
- **Ossetians from South Ossetia:** whether family histories across the range shape how they view talks with Georgia.
- **Ingush residents of Prigorodny:** their own view of every agreement with Ingushetia.
- **Mozdok Kabardians and Kumyks:** links to Kabardino-Balkaria and Dagestan across the republic's borders.
- **Armenians, Georgians and Greeks of Vladikavkaz:** communities that act through cultural associations.
- **Terek Cossacks:** their own tradition of envoys and assemblies.
- **Families arranging marriages:** go-between customs in villages and in the city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 18th-century Ossetian-Russian negotiation and for Byzantine sources; mixed for current interethnic negotiation; thin for everyday tact in survey data.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian, Russian, Georgian; Greek or translations for Byzantine sources; Arabic or translations for the geographers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Georgian-Ossetian relations and the status of South Ossetia; the Prigorodny dispute; the 1774 joining to Russia, which Ossetian, Georgian and Russian writers may frame differently.
- **Held for review:** none.
