## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iron Ossetians by old society** (Tagaur, Kurtatin, Alagir): gorge shrine networks, different estate histories.
- **Digor Ossetians**, Christian and Muslim: a separate literary form and a Muslim noble layer.
- **Muslim Ossetians elsewhere** (Kurtatin and Tagaur villages, Vladikavkaz): the faith minority within the majority people.
- **Mozdok district**: Terek Cossacks and other Russians, Kumyks, Kabardians, Armenians, Ossetians; a steppe frontier with its own churches and mosques.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district**: separate faith, language and memory.
- **Vladikavkaz minorities**: Armenians, Georgians, Pontic Greeks, Mountain and Ashkenazi Jews, Germans, Azerbaijanis.
- **Migrants from South Ossetia** (since 1991 and 2008): different village and church ties.
- **Diaspora links**: Ossetians in Turkey (19th-century emigrants), Moscow and St. Petersburg.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in Russian-language ethnography, folklore and church history; thin in surveys and in Ossetian-language print on modern belief.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Ossetian Iron and Digor for terms, prayers and folklore; French and German for older Ossetology; Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk for minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 Ossetian-Ingush conflict over Prigorodny district; the Beslan siege (2004); church and traditional-faith disputes; Russian extremism law; shrine access customs set by keepers, not to be published without consent.
- **Lands:** the tracker gives the pre-war lands only as "Russia", so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the republic's core lands.
- **Freedom ways:** start from the old estate order (æлдар, фарсæглæг, цагъар) in sheet 03.
- **Law:** not here; customary law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
