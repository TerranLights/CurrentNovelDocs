## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former lordly and free-community societies** (Tagaur and Digor against Kurtatin and Alagir): estate memory may shape how freedom is spoken of.
- **Descendants of noble, free and bonded families**: whether old status still colors talk of freedom.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district**: freedom as return, after deportation (1944) and the 1992 conflict.
- **Muslim Ossetians**: freedom of faith in a largely Christian setting, and the muhajir memory.
- **Mozdok's Terek Cossacks**: the Cossack idea of free service, and Soviet decossackization.
- **Refugees from South Ossetia and Georgia**: freedom as safety, after war.
- **Urban young people in Vladikavkaz**: online speech and protest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; history and folklore rich; current freedom data rests on outside monitors, some now labeled or banned in Russia.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Ossetian for songs and estate terms; Ingush for the return question.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Prigorodny (1992) and the Ingush deportation; protest prosecutions; Russian laws on "foreign agents" and "undesirable" organizations make some monitors risky for local contacts; Memorial was liquidated (2021).
- **Held for review:** none.
