# North Ossetia-Alania: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Ossetian (Iron, Digor), Russian; Ingush, Kabardian, Kumyk, Armenian, Georgian for minorities. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ossetians who keep shrine feasts and those who are churched Orthodox**: two kinds of faith practice that may overlap in one family.
- **Women and men**: different roles in the toast-prayer, at the hearth and at shrines.
- **Muslim Digor and Muslim Iron villages**: whether faith practice follows the Muslim calendar, Ossetian feasts, or both.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny district**: Sufi brotherhood practice (virds) to separate from other Muslims.
- **Terek Cossacks of Mozdok**: an Orthodox devotion with its own icons and processions.
- **Protestant and new communities in Vladikavkaz** (Baptists, Pentecostals, Adventists): converts' accounts of faith.
- **Generations**: those raised under Soviet atheism, their children, and the young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore and church material plentiful, survey data on faith practice thin at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Ossetian for prayers and toasts; Russian for scholarship and media; Arabic terms for Muslim practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shrine rites and women's rites may be closed to outsiders; Jehovah's Witnesses are banned in Russia (2017), so their members' testimony is risky to seek; the Beslan memorial is a site of grief.
- **Held for review:** none.
