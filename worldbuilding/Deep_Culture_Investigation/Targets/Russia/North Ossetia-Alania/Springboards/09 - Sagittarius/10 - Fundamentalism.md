# North Ossetia-Alania: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** North Ossetia-Alania, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (most sources and labels), Ossetian (Iron, Digor); Arabic terms of Muslim debate; Ingush, Kumyk, Kabardian, Georgian. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox parishes**: strict and relaxed attitudes to Ossetian shrine practice.
- **Uatsdin adherents**: purist restorers against those who blend shrine feasts with church life.
- **Muslim Ossetians**: inherited village Islam against text-based currents.
- **Ingush of Prigorodny**: Sufi brotherhoods against Salafis.
- **Kumyk and Kabardian Muslims of Mozdok district**: their own currents.
- **Protestant converts**: literalist churches and the "sect" label.
- **Secular Soviet-educated elites**: the question of secular certainty.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; scholarship and press are plentiful on the North Caucasus generally; republic-level data is thinner, and much of it comes from security or rights sources with their own aims.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Arabic terms; Ossetian for the native-faith debate; Ingush.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** terrorism (Beslan 2004; the 2010 bombing); Russian extremism law, under which possessing or sharing some texts is an offense; labels ("ваххабит", "секта") are charged, never adopt them; protect believers' identities.
- **Held for review:** none.
