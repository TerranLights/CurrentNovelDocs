*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Arctic villages** (Chukotka, northern Sakha, Kamchatka north) against **southern towns** with roads and farms (Amur, Primorye, Irkutsk).
- **Reindeer and hunting households** (Even, Evenk, Chukchi, Koryak, Dolgan, Yukaghir) against village wage earners.
- **Sakha** cattle-and-horse households; **Buryat** steppe herders in Aga and Barguzin.
- **Coastal Yupik and Chukchi** whaling villages; **Aleuts** of the Commander Islands; **Nivkh** and **Ulchi** fishers.
- **Russians:** trappers and Old Believers, dacha gardeners, Soviet-era northerners who stay or leave, Gulag-descended Magadan families.
- **Diasporas:** Sakhalin Koreans, Birobidzhan Jews, Ukrainians, Chinese farm workers in Amur and the JAO.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Household and income data are rich by region; the self-reliance ideal itself is studied mainly through Chukotka and Arctic ethnography; regional independence history is well archived but politically awkward.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha for 1990s debates; Ukrainian for 1917-22; Korean for Sakhalin memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** talk of separatism is legally dangerous inside Russia now; keep living advocates out of named pointers. Quotas and land use touch law: the rules go to Libra, legal culture to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
