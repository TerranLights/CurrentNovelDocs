## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primorye and Khabarovsk** port and trade cities against **Sakha and Buryatia** republics, where ethnic networks may shape who starts what.
- **Border towns** (Blagoveshchensk, Zabaykalsk, Pogranichny) with daily trade across to China.
- **Northeast peoples** (Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Itelmen) starting community enterprises in hunting, fishing and tourism.
- **Amur peoples** (Nanai, Ulchi, Udege) and **Sakhalin Nivkh and Uilta** in fishing communities.
- **Evenks and Evens:** herding brigades and private herds.
- **Russians:** old merchant families of Irkutsk, Soviet builder generations, 1990s traders, young urban founders.
- **Sakhalin Koreans** and **Chinese and Central Asian migrants** in trade and farming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Historical initiative (explorers, merchants, Soviet builders) is rich; present-day business data is national with regional cuts; volunteer data is scattered in press.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Chinese for the border trade's other side; Sakha and Buryat for local enterprise and folklore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** volunteer and civic groups can be labeled by the state; take care with named living organizers. Explorer accounts carry a conqueror's view; read them against Indigenous memory of the same events.
- **Held for review:** none.
