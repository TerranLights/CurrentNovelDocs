## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding and hunting households** (Even, Evenk, Chukchi, Koryak, Dolgan) against village and city families of the same peoples.
- **Sakha:** cattle-and-horse uluses against Yakutsk; Arctic uluses (Anabar, Allaikha) against the central valleys.
- **Buryats:** clan-minded rural Aga against Ulan-Ude professionals; Buddhist and shamanist families.
- **Coastal Yupik and Chukchi villages** remade by resettlement; **Aleuts of Bering Island**.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples** (Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Nivkh, Uilta) in fishing and hunting communities.
- **Russians:** Semeiskie and other Old Believers; prospectors; shift workers; Magadan families; urban Vladivostok and Irkutsk middle class.
- **Sakhalin Koreans** (family-centered decisions across generations); **Birobidzhan Jewish families**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Collective autonomy of Indigenous peoples is well studied; personal choice in families and at work is thin, with few regional surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for family and community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous self-rule, separatism and federal control are politically charged; some Indigenous organizations have been closed or labeled. The legal status of clan communities and lands belongs to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet takes only lived self-rule.
- **Held for review:** none.
