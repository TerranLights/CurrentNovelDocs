## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha** (urban Yakutsk against rural uluses), **Buryats** (Buddhist Aga and Khori against Ekhirit-Bulagat of Irkutsk): different religious teachings on pride and restraint.
- **Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Itelmen:** small peoples speaking up inside Russian-run institutions; elders against youth.
- **Evenks and Evens:** dispersed herders and hunters, where speaking up may follow brigade and kin order.
- **Russian old-timers, Semeiskie Old Believers, Cossack descendants:** older registers of deference and defiance.
- **Magadan and Chukotka newcomer families:** Soviet-era northerners with their own speech habits.
- **Sakhalin Koreans; Birobidzhan Jews; Ukrainians of the Green Wedge:** diaspora claims.
- **Women against men; workers in extraction camps (shift rotation) against office staff.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Oral epic and literature are rich; conversation and workplace studies of the Far East are thin, so general Russian research must be tested against local sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for epic and proverb sources; Korean and Yiddish for the press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest archives and political speech carry legal risk for speakers inside Russia; quote only public material. Consumer complaints about public bodies shade into law: point to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
