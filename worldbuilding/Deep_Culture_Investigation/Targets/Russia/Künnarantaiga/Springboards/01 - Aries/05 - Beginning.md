# Künnarantaiga: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia: Sakha and Chukotka in the north, Buryatia, Irkutsk and Zabaykalsky in the west, the Amur, Primorye, Sakhalin and Kamchatka on the Pacific. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenk, Chukchi, Koryak and Itelmen festival names; Korean; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new years, birth and starting over.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** Ysyakh in Yakutsk (mass, staged) against ulus festivals; Christian and Aar Aiyy households.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist Sagaalgan households against shamanist Ekhirit-Bulagat practice; Aga against Irkutsk.
- **Northeast peoples:** Chukchi and Koryak herders, coastal Yupik, Itelmen and Kamchadal fishing villages, each with its own year's turn.
- **Evenks and Evens** across five or more units; **Dolgans**.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples:** Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Nivkh, Uilta (birth and cradle customs; fish-run beginnings).
- **Russians:** old-timers, Semeiskie (Old Believer calendar), Soviet-era arrivals, Gulag families; **Sakhalin Koreans; Birobidzhan Jews**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and origin legends; mixed for birth rites (much from mid-20th-century ethnography); thin for present-day "starting over" outside migration data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for ritual texts; Korean for the return program; Yiddish for early Birobidzhan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some birth and festival rites are kept within families or by practitioners; ask before using them. City founding stories are contested (Chinese names for Vladivostok and the Amur lands touch a live border dispute). Gulag memory is painful for Magadan families.
- **Held for review:** none.
