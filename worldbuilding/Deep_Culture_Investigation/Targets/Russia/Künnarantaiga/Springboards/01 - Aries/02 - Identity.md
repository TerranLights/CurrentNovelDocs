# Künnarantaiga: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia: twelve eastern units from Irkutsk and Buryatia to Chukotka and Sakhalin. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenk, Even, Nanai and other Indigenous languages; Yiddish; Korean; Ukrainian. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** Yakutsk urban youth against herding uluses; Sakha who speak mainly Russian against Sakha speakers.
- **Buryats:** Aga and Khori against Ekhirit-Bulagat and Tunka; ties to Mongolia against ties to Russia.
- **Small peoples:** Evenks (Orochen) of Amur and Zabaykalsky; Evens and Yukaghirs of the Kolyma; Chukchi against Yupik; Koryak against Itelmen and Kamchadal; Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Oroch and Negidal of the Amur; Nivkh and Uilta of Sakhalin; Tofalars and Soyots; Aleuts of the Commander Islands; Dolgans.
- **Russians:** old-timers, Semeiskie, Cossack descendants, Soviet-era newcomers and their children, Magadan families of Gulag origin.
- **Diasporas:** Sakhalin Koreans; Birobidzhan Jews; Ukrainians; Chinese and Central Asian migrant workers; Kuril residents with Japanese-era memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Identity is one of the best-studied topics for Sakha, Buryats and Chukotka; thinner for Russian old-timers and Ukrainians.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for insider press; Korean, Yiddish and Ukrainian for diasporas; Japanese helps for Karafuto sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** identity claims tied to Indigenous status carry benefits and disputes; ask who may speak for a people. Ethnic politics, separatism and the current war are risky subjects for speakers inside Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Find the published 1926 Polar census tables for Yakutia, Kamchatka and the Okhotsk coast.
