## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha** of the central valleys against the Viliui and the northern uluses: different herding and contact histories.
- **Buryats:** Khori and Aga (Zabaykalsky, mostly Buddhist) against Ekhirit-Bulagat of Irkutsk (more shamanist, more Russian contact); Barguzin.
- **Evenks and Evens** across Sakha, Amur, Khabarovsk, Buryatia and Magadan; **Dolgans** in Anabar.
- **Northeast:** Chukchi (coastal and reindeer), Siberian Yupik, Koryak (nymylan and chavchuven), Itelmen and Kamchadal, Aleuts of the Commander Islands, Chuvans, Yukaghirs (tundra and forest).
- **Amur and Sakhalin:** Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Oroch, Negidal, Nivkh, Uilta; Ainu descendants (small, *check*); Tofalars and Soyots of the Sayan.
- **Russians and Slavs:** old-timers (chaldony, gurany, karymy), Semeiskie Old Believers, Cossack descendants (Amur, Ussuri, Transbaikal), Ukrainians of the Green Wedge, Soviet-era and Gulag-descended Magadan families.
- **Others:** Sakhalin Koreans, Birobidzhan Jews, Chinese and Central Asian migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sakha, Buryats and the Northeast peoples (strong regional institutes, exile and Jesup ethnography); mixed for Russian settlers; thin for the smallest peoples (Negidal, Kerek, Uilta). **Lands:** the tracker header lists only "Russia"; these 12 units come from the territory reference, and the exact lands are still to be settled at Stage 0.
- **Languages needed:** Russian throughout; Sakha and Buryat for insider sources; Yiddish and Korean for two diasporas; Indigenous languages mainly through bilingual editions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic knowledge and soul concepts may be held as restricted by families or practitioners; museum human remains and sacred objects; Gulag descent in Magadan and Irkutsk; current war losses among Buryat and Sakha families.
- **Held for review:** none.
