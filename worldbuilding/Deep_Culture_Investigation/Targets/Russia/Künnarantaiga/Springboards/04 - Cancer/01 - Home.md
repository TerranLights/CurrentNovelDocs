# Künnarantaiga: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga (Күннарантайга), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Yiddish; Korean; Ukrainian for older Amur and Primorye villages. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Flat-dwellers and private-house dwellers** in Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Irkutsk and Yakutsk; the two may define home differently.
- **Sakha homesteads** on the alaas meadows of central Yakutia against Yakutsk apartment life.
- **Buryat village households** in the Aga and Ust-Orda districts against Ulan-Ude.
- **Reindeer-herding families** (Evens, Evenks, Chukchi, Koryaks), with a camp home and a village home.
- **Rotational workers** in mining and oil towns (Neryungri, Mirny, Bilibino) whose home is elsewhere.
- **Kuril and Sakhalin residents**, including Sakhalin Koreans and Nivkh villages.
- **Jewish families** of Birobidzhan and their descendants abroad.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers** and returnee Old Believers from South America in Primorye.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing statistics and Russian-language media; thin for small peoples' own words for home, which sit in dictionaries and monographs.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Yiddish for settler sources; Korean for the return story.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** village closures and forced settlement of nomads still hurt; ownership and inheritance belong to Taurus and Scorpio; home and land law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
