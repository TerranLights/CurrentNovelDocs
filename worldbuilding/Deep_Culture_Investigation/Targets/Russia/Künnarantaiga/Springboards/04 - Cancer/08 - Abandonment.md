*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villagers whose settlements were closed or flooded**: Angara Russians, Naukan Yupik, Nivkh and Nanai of consolidated villages.
- **Stayers in Kolyma, Chukotka and Kamchatka mining towns**, against those who left for "the mainland".
- **Families of rotational workers and fishermen** (Sakhalin, Kamchatka, Magadan).
- **Children in care**, with attention to which peoples are over-represented (a question).
- **Sakhalin Koreans** with kin left in Korea.
- **Jews who stayed in Birobidzhan** after the emigration of the 1990s.
- **Old people whose children moved away**, in Sakha, Buryat and Russian villages alike.
- **Descendants of exiles and Gulag prisoners**, many brought here unwillingly and then left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village closures, ghost towns and literature; mixed for orphan figures; thin for families' own words on parents who left.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Korean; Yupik and Nivkh oral histories through Russian or English editions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's data is protected; forced resettlement and Gulag history are painful and politically fraught; stray-dog culling divides opinion. Child support and divorce law point to Libra; betrayal and grief to Scorpio; leaving home to Aries.
- **Held for review:** none.
