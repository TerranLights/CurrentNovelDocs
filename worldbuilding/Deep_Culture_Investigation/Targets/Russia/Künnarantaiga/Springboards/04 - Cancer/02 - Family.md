*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga (Күннарантайга), region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Nivkh; Itelmen and Koryak (through Russian sources); Yiddish; Korean. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buryat clan families** (Khori, Ekhirit, Bulagat, Khongodor), where clan membership may still shape marriage choices.
- **Sakha rural kin networks** in the Vilyuy and central districts against Yakutsk families.
- **Evenk and Even clans**, scattered across several units.
- **Nivkh, Nanai, Ulchi and Udege**, with exogamous clan traditions.
- **Chukchi and Koryak reindeer camps**, built on groups of related families.
- **Old Believer and Cossack families**, often large and close-knit.
- **Soviet-era newcomer families** with kin thousands of kilometers away.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, many with kin in two or three countries; **Jewish families** of Birobidzhan with kin in Israel.
- **Military and rotational-work families** in Kamchatka, Primorye and the mining north.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demographic tables and Soviet kinship monographs; thin for how today's small peoples speak of family in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Korean and Yiddish; Nivkh and Tungus terms via dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan histories and genealogies may be held by families and not shared; repression and deportation broke many lines. Siblings point to Gemini, marriage to Libra, inheritance to Scorpio, family authority to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
