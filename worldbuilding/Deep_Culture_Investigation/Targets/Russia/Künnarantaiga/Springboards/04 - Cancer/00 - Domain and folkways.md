## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha**, separating central Yakutia's cattle-keeping districts from the Vilyuy basin and the northern districts.
- **Buryats**, separating western (Irkutsk-side, more shamanic) groups from eastern Buddhist ones and Aga and Ust-Orda Buryats.
- **Evenks and Evens**, spread across seven units; reindeer camps against village life.
- **Northeastern peoples**: Chukchi (tundra and coast), Siberian Yupik, Koryaks, Itelmens and Kamchadals, Yukaghirs, Chuvans, Kereks, Aleuts of the Commander Islands.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples**: Nanai, Ulchi, Negidal, Udege, Oroch, Nivkh, Uilta; Tofalars and Soyots in the Sayan foothills; the Taz of Primorye; Ainu descendants.
- **Russians**: old-settler Siberians (Russkoye Ustye, Pokhodsk, Markovo), Semeiskie Old Believers, Transbaikal, Amur and Ussuri Cossacks, Soviet-era newcomers in BAM, mining and military towns, and Old Believer returnees from South America in Primorye.
- **Others**: Jews of Birobidzhan; Sakhalin Koreans and Koryo-saram; Ukrainian-heritage villages of the old Zeleny Klin; Polish exile descendants in Irkutsk; Chinese and Central Asian migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Russian, Sakha and Buryat households (census, Soviet ethnography, local media); mixed to thin for small peoples, where much sits in Soviet monographs and regional archives. The tracker names only "Russia"; this sheet follows the twelve units of the territory reference, and the Stage 0 units are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Sakha and Buryat for local media; Yiddish and Korean for two diaspora streams; small-language terms through bilingual dictionaries from Yakutsk, Ulan-Ude and St. Petersburg's Herzen University *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet-era resettlement, boarding schools and Gulag history touch many families; some shamanic and clan knowledge is not for outsiders. Inheritance points to Scorpio, family authority to Capricorn, law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
