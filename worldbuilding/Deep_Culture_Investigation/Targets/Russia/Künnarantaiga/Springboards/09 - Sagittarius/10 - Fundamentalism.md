*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox mainstream against rejectionist currents** (Chukotka's former diocese; anti-tax-number groups).
- **Old Believers:** Semeiskie, Primorye returnees and other accords; boundary-keeping as custom, not militancy.
- **Protestant converts:** Korean-mission, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations among Russians, Koreans, Sakha and Chukchi.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses:** in Birobidzhan, Primorye and Khabarovsk.
- **Muslim communities:** Tatars with old mosques against newer Central Asian congregations.
- **Native revival leaders:** Sakha Aiyy and Buryat shamanic groups, and their critics.
- **Birobidzhan's small Jewish community** and its observant core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed and skewed. Much of what exists is written by anti-cult activists, state bodies or advocacy groups; each needs reading for its stance. Members' own voices are scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Korean; Central Asian languages through community contacts; Sakha and Buryat for revival debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "extremist" designations make contact dangerous for members; never treat a state or anti-cult label as a finding; the word "sect" is itself a sanction. No law research: prosecutions as law go to Libra, legal culture to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
