## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** Orthodox, Aiyy and unaffiliated people who may share the same spirit customs.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist belief in rebirth against shamanic belief in ancestral spirits, often in the same family.
- **Yukaghirs, Evens and Evenki:** hunters' beliefs, and those of their schooled children.
- **Nivkh, Ainu, Ulchi and Nanai:** bear rites and sea and river spirits.
- **Chukchi, Koryak, Itelmen and Yupik:** sea-mammal and reindeer belief.
- **Russian old-settlers** (Russkoye Ustye, Lena, Kamchadals) against Soviet-era newcomers raised on scientific atheism.
- **Sakhalin Koreans; Birobidzhan Jews; Central Asian Muslims:** creeds and folk belief brought from elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich classic ethnography for the small peoples; thin current survey data by unit.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Sakha, Buryat; Indigenous languages through specialists; Polish and English for Piłsudski; Japanese for Ainu scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and bear-rite knowledge may be restricted by the communities; early ethnographies were written by outsiders and need to be read beside the peoples' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** none.
