*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Reindeer-herding families** (Even, Evenki, Chukchi, Koryak, Dolgan): learning on the land against boarding schools.
- **Sea-mammal hunters** (Yupik, coastal Chukchi, Koryak): learning in boat crews.
- **Amur and Sakhalin fishing peoples** (Nanai, Ulch, Nivkh, Udege): river skills and crafts.
- **Sakha villages** with horse and cattle work, against Yakutsk's lyceums.
- **Buddhist Buryat families** (Aga, Khori) and **western Buryats**: datsan and secular school.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers**: reading from church books.
- **Sakhalin Koreans** and **JAO Jewish-heritage families**: heritage learning after closures.
- **Children of labor migrants** learning Russian.
- **Port and industrial cities** against **villages**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnopedagogy, much of it idealized; mixed on boarding-school experience; thin on present-day home learning among small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; classical Tibetan and Mongolian for monastic texts; Yiddish; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school memories; initiation and shamanic learning are restricted; children as informants need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
