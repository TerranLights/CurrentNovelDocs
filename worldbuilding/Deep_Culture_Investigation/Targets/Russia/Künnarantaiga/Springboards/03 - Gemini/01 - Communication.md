# Künnarantaiga: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Korean and Yiddish for heritage communities; Nivkh, Chukchi and other Indigenous languages where records exist. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Port-city Russians** (Vladivostok, Nakhodka, Petropavlovsk) against **old-timer villages** and BAM-era towns: pace, directness, swearing.
- **Sakha**, elders and youth; city and village: greetings, address, silence.
- **Buryats**, Khori, Aga and western: respect registers and good-wish speech.
- **Chukchi** coastal and reindeer families; **Siberian Yupik** of Sireniki and Novoye Chaplino: speech and gender.
- **Nivkh, Nanai, Ulch, Udege**: avoidance rules and kin speech.
- **Yukaghirs and Evens** of the Kolyma: written signs and spoken etiquette.
- **Sakhalin Koreans** by generation: speech levels, letters home.
- **Jewish-heritage families** of the JAO; **Deaf communities**; **labor migrants** and their children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Russian talk in general but centered on Moscow; mixed on Sakha and Buryat etiquette; thin on spoken style among small peoples apart from older ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Korean; Yiddish; Nivkh for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual speech and shamans' formulas; Koreans' separation history; migrant children's status.
- **Held for review:** none.
