# Künnarantaiga: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat (and Khalkha Mongolian for cross-border kin); Korean; Chinese; Yiddish and Hebrew for emigrant ties. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected (or not).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buryats** of Aga and Khori (Buddhist, close to Mongolia) against **Ust-Orda and western Buryats** (clan rites stronger).
- **Sakha** villagers and their Yakutsk relatives.
- **Evenki and Evens**, scattered across many units: kin ties over long distances.
- **Chukchi and Siberian Yupik** with kin in Alaska.
- **Old-timer Russians** against **Soviet-era incomers** who came for a contract and stayed or left.
- **Mining and shift towns** (Mirny, Aldan, Bilibino, Magadan's goldfields).
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, by generation; **Chinese traders** on the Amur; **JAO Jewish-heritage families**.
- **Cossack descendants**; **Semeiskie**; **Central Asian migrants**.
- **Closed and shrinking settlements** whose people moved away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on blat and border trade; mixed on Buryat and Sakha kin networks; thin on loneliness by unit.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat and Mongolian; Korean; Chinese; Yiddish and Hebrew for emigrant records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan rites may be closed to outsiders; family separation histories; the legality of favors and nepotism goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
