# Künnarantaiga: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Korean (whose sibling terms depend on the speaker's sex); Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha village families**, often larger, against **Yakutsk** families.
- **Buryats**: youngest-son and seniority customs, eastern and western.
- **Evenki and Evens**: brothers in herding brigades; older customs on widows.
- **Chukchi and Koryak reindeer families**; **coastal Yupik** households.
- **Nanai, Ulch, Nivkh**: kin terms that may group cousins with siblings.
- **Urban Russians** in Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Irkutsk: smaller families and only children.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers**: large families.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**: speaker-sex sibling terms, eldest-son duties.
- **Labor migrants** whose brothers follow one another north.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical kin terms and fertility statistics; mixed for adult sibling ties; thin for small peoples today.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Korean; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inheritance and widow customs touch law (point to Libra and Capricorn); boarding-school separations; gang histories; twin beliefs may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
