## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern Buryats** (Buddhism strong) against **western Buryats** (clan shamanism strong): how two faiths share one life.
- **Sakha** families: Orthodox, Aar Aiyy, both or neither.
- **Evenki and Evens in Sakha**, many of whom speak Sakha as well as Russian: three-way, not two-way, language lives.
- **Kamchadals, Karymy, Chuvans and Russkoustintsy**: mixed descent, named or unnamed.
- **Sakhalin and Kuril residents** living with a Japanese past; **Sakhalin Koreans**.
- **Amur border towns** (Blagoveshchensk, Pogranichny) facing China.
- **JAO residents**, Jewish and not.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers**: old rite and modern state.
- **Shift workers** with a town life and a field life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious coexistence among Buryats and for border-town studies; mixed for Sakha; thin for how mixed-descent people name themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Japanese; Korean; Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic cosmology and soul beliefs; gender-crossing roles among Chukchi and other shamans, known mostly through Bogoraz, are to be approached only through community voices; the Kurils' status; "half-blood" language.
- **Held for review:** none.
