## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** central alaas villages, Vilyui, Arctic uluses; Yakutsk.
- **Buryats:** western (Ust-Orda, Irkutsk; shamanist ties) and eastern (Khori, Aga; Buddhist ties); Soyots (Oka); Tofalars (Irkutsk).
- **Tungusic peoples:** Evenks (Sakha, Amur, Zabaykalsky, Buryatia, Khabarovsk), Evens, Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Oroch, Negidal, Uilta; Dolgans (Anabar).
- **Northeast:** Chukchi (reindeer and coastal), Siberian Yupik, Koryak (Nymylan and Chavchu), Itelmen, Kamchadals, Chuvans, Yukaghirs, Aleuts (Commander Islands).
- **Lower Amur and Sakhalin:** Nivkh; Ainu heritage on Sakhalin and the Kurils.
- **Old-settler Russians:** Semeiskie Old Believers (Transbaikal); Russkoye Ustye and Kolyma old settlers; Transbaikal, Amur and Ussuri Cossack descendants; Molokans (Amur).
- **Later arrivals:** Ukrainian-rooted Primorye villages; Jews of the JAO; Sakhalin Koreans; Chinese and Central Asian migrants; Gulag and Dalstroy-descended families (Magadan); BAM towns; naval closed towns.
- **Cities:** Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Irkutsk, Yakutsk, Ulan-Ude.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sakha, Buryats and the Jesup-era northeast; mixed for old settlers and newcomer towns; thin for Negidal, Taz, Oroch, Tofalars.
- **Lands:** the tracker header lists only "Russia"; this sign's sheets work from the 12 units named in the territory reference. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Yiddish; Korean; Indigenous languages, mostly via Russian-language ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and clan-sacrifice knowledge (ask communities first); Gulag and deportation memory (Koreans 1937; Japanese repatriation from Sakhalin); "foreign agent" and "undesirable" designations affecting pollsters, NGOs and Indigenous activists.
- **Held for review:** none.
