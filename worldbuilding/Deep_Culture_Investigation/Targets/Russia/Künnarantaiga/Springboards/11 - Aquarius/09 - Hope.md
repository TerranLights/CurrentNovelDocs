*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha** (blessing tradition; Ysyakh) against **Buryats** (Buddhist and shamanist forms of asking for luck); **Evenks and Evens** (hunters' luck customs).
- **Orthodox Russians** (icons, pilgrimages); **Old Believers**; Protestant converts in Sakha and Chukotka villages.
- **Newcomers' children** weighing "the mainland" against staying: Magadan, Chukotka, Kamchatka.
- **Sakhalin Koreans:** those who returned to Korea, those who stayed.
- **Small peoples with endangered languages:** Itelmen, Nivkh, Uilta, Udege, Negidal, Orochi.
- **Young people** in Vladivostok, Irkutsk and Yakutsk; families in shrinking towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in migration and mortality numbers; mixed in polls (national, little regional detail); thin on hope in Indigenous peoples' own words outside revival projects.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Korean; community languages for revival materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data in small communities (avoid pointing at a people); Buddhist and shamanic rites closed to outsiders; families of the missing; hopes tied to the war and mobilization.
- **Held for review:** none.
