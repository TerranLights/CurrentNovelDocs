## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders and hunters:** Evenks, Evens, Chukchi, Koryaks (collectivization, settlement in villages, boarding schools, 1990s collapse).
- **Titular peoples' reformers:** Sakha intelligentsia; Buryat Buddhist and secular reformers; western against eastern Buryats.
- **Old settlers:** Semeiskie and other Old Believers, a community born from refusing a church reform; Cossack descendants (abolished, then revived).
- **Penal and camp legacies:** Sakhalin, Kara, Kolyma.
- **Industrial towns:** Baikalsk, Bratsk, Ust-Ilimsk (dam and mill towns); BAM towns.
- **Cities:** Irkutsk's civic tradition against Vladivostok's port and naval culture; Yakutsk; Khabarovsk.
- **Sakhalin Koreans and Chinese traders** under shifting policies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-c. exile reform and Baikal activism; mixed for Indigenous self-led reform; thin for Uilta and Negidal.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; classical Mongolian script for older Buddhist texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** NGOs closed or labeled "foreign agents"; alcohol and Indigenous health (avoid stereotypes, take villages' own accounts); boarding-school memory; present-day activists' safety.
- **Held for review:** none.
