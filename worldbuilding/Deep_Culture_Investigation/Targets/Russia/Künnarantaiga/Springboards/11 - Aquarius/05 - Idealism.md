## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Believers** (Semeiskie; Primorye and lower Amur villages): religious purity as an ideal.
- **Sakha intelligentsia and Aiyy revivalists; Buryat Buddhist laity and lamas;** western Buryat shamanist circles.
- **Soviet newcomers and their children:** BAM towns (Tynda, Severobaikalsk), Komsomolsk-on-Amur, Magadan.
- **Indigenous northeast** (Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Even) as targets of Soviet idealists and as authors of their own ideals.
- **Birobidzhan's Jewish community** and its émigrés; **Sakhalin Koreans**.
- **Eco-settlers and back-to-the-land families** in Primorye and around Baikal; city youth in Vladivostok and Irkutsk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Soviet-era idealism and its disillusion; mixed on present-day values (national surveys, little regional detail); thin on Indigenous ideals in their own words outside folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat and Tibetan or classical Mongolian for Buddhist sources; Yiddish; English for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Buddhist teachings given under vows; Gulag memory; the romanticizing of Indigenous peoples (name it as outsiders' image); present-day volunteering tied to the war effort.
- **Held for review:** none.
