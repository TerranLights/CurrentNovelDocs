# Künnarantaiga: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga (Күннарантайга), region Russia, Baikal to the Bering Strait. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki, Even, Chukchi, Nanai, Nivkh; Yiddish; Korean. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest, cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sakha alaas villages against Yakutsk's newcomer districts; Even and Evenki herding brigades against their base villages.
- "National villages" of the lower Amur (Nanai, Ulchi, Nivkh) against mixed Russian river towns.
- Chukotka's coastal Yupik and Chukchi villages, merged in Soviet times, against Anadyr.
- Old Believer villages (Transbaikal; lower Amur and Primorye) against neighbors; Cossack-descended villages along the Amur and Ussuri; Ukrainian-rooted villages in Primorye.
- Monotowns and mining towns (Baikalsk, Bodaybo, Kolyma), BAM towns, naval closed towns (Vilyuchinsk, Fokino).
- Sakhalin Koreans; Birobidzhan; Buddhist, shamanist, Orthodox and Protestant congregations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sakha and Even villages (several English ethnographies); mixed for Russian settler villages; thin for Uilta, Negidal and Tofalar communities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Sakha and Buryat for village and festival media; Korean for Sakhalin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disaster grief; forced village closures and mergers (Chukotka, Soviet era); Gulag-built towns; ask before using clan-community records.
- **Held for review:** none.
