# Künnarantaiga: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Nivkh; Itelmen and Koryak; Chukchi. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Evenki and Evens**, whose sharing rules travel with herds and hunts across Sakha, Amur and Khabarovsk.
- **Nivkh, Ulchi, Nanai, Udege** and the **Ainu heritage**, through bear festivals and river exchange.
- **Itelmens, Koryaks, Chukchi, Yupik**, through sea harvests and festivals.
- **Sakha**, hunters and herders against city dwellers.
- **Buryats**, Buddhist and shamanist communities.
- **Old Believers** and other village Russians with labor exchange traditions.
- **City networks** in Vladivostok, Khabarovsk and Irkutsk; **migrant workers** and their remittances.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Indigenous sharing rules and ritual exchange in older ethnography; mixed for present-day favors; thin for measured regional data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Evenki; Nivkh and Itelmen terms through dictionaries; Japanese for Ainu comparisons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bear festivals and offerings are sacred to some communities; use what they publish or share; informal favors shade into bribery and may expose informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
