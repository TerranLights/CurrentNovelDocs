# Künnarantaiga: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia (Lake Baikal to the Bering Strait). **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Japanese for Ainu and Karafuto material; Korean and Chinese for heritage communities. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former Gulag towns** (Magadan, the Kolyma settlements, Vanino) against **old-settler villages** with older community discipline (Semeiskie, Russkoye Ustye).
- **Sakha**, rural districts against Yakutsk, on clan obligation and the state's courts.
- **Buryats**: Buddhist teaching on retribution against shamanist traditions of offense against spirits and ancestors.
- **Indigenous fishing and herding communities** (Itelmens, Koryaks, Nanai, Ulchi, Evenki, Evens), whose claims are collective.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, whose questions of return and redress differ from the Koryo-saram's.
- **Chinese and Central Asian migrants** as defendants and as victims.
- **Khabarovsk and Vladivostok** civic cultures against smaller towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Gulag and penal past and for court records; mixed for customary justice; thin and filtered for Indigenous peoples' own justice ways.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Japanese for Ainu sources; Chinese for 1900.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression, the Gulag and the 1900 killings; the Memorial society was dissolved by court order, so its materials may sit in mirrors or abroad; the 2020 protests and criminal-world sources carry legal risk for local informants; Indigenous restorative rites are shared only as the peoples choose.
- **Held for review:** none.
