# Künnarantaiga: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki, Nivkh, Nanai, Koryak; Korean and Japanese for Sakhalin heritage; Chinese for border business. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha**, villages where in-laws arrange much against Yakutsk couples who meet on their own.
- **Buryats**, Aga and Khori against Ekhirit-Bulagat, on in-law ties and sworn brotherhood.
- **Nivkh, Ulchi, Nanai, Udege, Oroch**, whose clan marriage rules differ river by river.
- **Koryaks, Chukchi, Yupik, Itelmens**, herders against sea hunters.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers** and other village Russians against city couples in Vladivostok, Khabarovsk and Irkutsk.
- **Sakhalin Koreans and the Japanese remnant**; **Birobidzhan's Jewish families**, many of mixed marriage.
- **Mining-town couples** separated by shift work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Decembrists, census figures and the Jesup-era peoples; mixed for current couples outside the cities; thin for the smallest Amur and Sakhalin peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Japanese and Korean for Sakhalin; some Indigenous kin terms only through dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence in the home (decriminalized in part in 2017) is a live and painful subject; mixed marriages under Soviet nationality rules; Indigenous clan rules shared only as the peoples choose.
- **Held for review:** none.
