# Künnarantaiga: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga ("Land of the Sunlit Taiga"), region Russia: Sakha, Irkutsk, Buryatia, Zabaykalsky, Amur, the Jewish Autonomous Oblast, Khabarovsk, Primorye, Sakhalin and the Kurils, Magadan, Kamchatka, Chukotka. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki; Itelmen; Korean; Yiddish; Ukrainian. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha**: summer joy at ysyakh against winter gatherings; Arctic districts and the return of the sun.
- **Buryats**: White Month and summer games; Buddhist and shamanist joy.
- **Evenki, Evens, Chukchi, Koryak, Itelmen, Yupik**: herders' and sea hunters' festivals.
- **Nanai, Ulch, Nivkh, Udege**: river festivals and fish-run seasons.
- **Russians**: Old Believers' church year; port cities' nightlife; dacha culture; northern towns.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, **Jews**, **Ukrainians**, **Chinese** and **Central Asian** migrants' holidays.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals; mixed for everyday joy, needing time-use, forums and interviews; national polls rarely break out these units.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki and Itelmen festival terms; Korean; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drinking at celebrations is a charged topic, often turned into stereotypes about Indigenous peoples; study it with care and never as a trait. Blessing texts may be the speaker's own.
- **Held for review:** none.
