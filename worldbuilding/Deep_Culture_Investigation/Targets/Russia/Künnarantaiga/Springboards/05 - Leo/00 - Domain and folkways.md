## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha** (central, Vilyuy and northern districts).
- **Buryats** (Buryatia; Aga and Ust-Orda Buryats): Buddhist and shamanist festive calendars to separate.
- **Evenki and Evens** (Sakha, Amur, Khabarovsk, Magadan): revived festivals, scattered groups.
- **Chukchi, Koryak, Itelmen, Yupik, Chuvan and Kamchadals** (Chukotka and Kamchatka): reindeer and sea-mammal festivals.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples** (Nanai, Ulch, Udege, Oroch, Negidal, Nivkh, Uilta): bear festivals, ornament, boat races.
- **Dolgans, Yukaghirs, Soyots and Tofalars**: small and easy to miss.
- **Russians**: Old Believers, Cossack descendants (Transbaikal, Amur, Ussuri), port cities, Magadan mining towns, military towns.
- **Ukrainians** of Primorye, **Jews** of Birobidzhan, **Sakhalin Koreans**, **Chinese** traders and workers, **Central Asian** migrants in the cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sakha and Buryat festivals and for city sport; mixed for statistics, which often stop at the federal-district level (the Far Eastern Federal District holds eleven of the twelve units, *check*; Irkutsk sits in the Siberian district); thin for the smallest peoples.
- **Lands:** the tracker header says only "Russia"; this pass follows the twelve units named in the territory reference. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled at Stage 0.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Yiddish and Korean; Japanese for Karafuto-era Sakhalin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some festivals are tied to shamanic rites whose details belong to their keepers; ask before using. Several pollsters are labeled under Russian law; weigh access.
- **Held for review:** none.
