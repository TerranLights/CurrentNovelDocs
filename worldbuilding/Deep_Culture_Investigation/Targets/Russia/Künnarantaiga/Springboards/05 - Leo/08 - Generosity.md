## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Evenki and Evens**: sharing in hunting and herding camps.
- **Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Itelmen**: coastal catch-sharing against herders' gifts.
- **Sakha**: festival sponsorship, hospitality in rural districts against Yakutsk.
- **Buryats**: Buddhist offerings, kin giving, guests.
- **Nanai, Ulch, Nivkh, Udege**: sharing fish and game in river villages.
- **Russians**: merchants' heirs in Irkutsk, Old Believer mutual help, miners' and fishers' collectives, city volunteers.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, **Jews**, **Ukrainians**, **Chinese** and **Central Asian** migrants' remittances home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for early ethnography of sharing and for philanthropy history; mixed for current giving data, often national only; thin for informal giving in cities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki terms; Korean; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** company giving is entangled with politics; offerings at shrines may be private. Avoid any "generous people" stereotype: compare practices, not traits.
- **Held for review:** none.
