## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha**: children's stick and pebble games; jumping contests; city against village.
- **Buryats**: ankle-bone games; summer games; Buddhist calendar holidays.
- **Evens, Evenki, Chukchi, Koryak**: herders' contests; coastal play; children in boarding schools against those in camps.
- **Nivkh, Nanai, Ulch, Udege, Uilta**: play at feasts; storytelling.
- **Russians**: Old Believers and Cossacks at Maslenitsa; city youth; sailors and shift workers.
- **Sakhalin Koreans**, **Jews**, **Ukrainians**, **Chinese** and **Central Asian** families: home games and holidays.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festival games and early ethnography; mixed for everyday adult play; thin for current children's play in small villages.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Nivkh and Nanai for feast terms; Korean; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the bear festival is a sacred rite for Nivkh and Amur peoples: details belong to their keepers. Gambling as play goes with the domain; its legal side goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
