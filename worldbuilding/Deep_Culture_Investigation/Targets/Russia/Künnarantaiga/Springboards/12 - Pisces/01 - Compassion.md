## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox settler majority** in Irkutsk, Primorye, Khabarovsk, Amur: parish charity and secular Soviet-era social care.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers** and Molokans: almsgiving tied to separate church life.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist east and Aga against western shamanist-leaning villages; Soyots.
- **Sakha:** central uluses against northern and Vilyui districts (leprosy and isolation history).
- **Northeast reindeer and sea-hunting peoples** (Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Evens): sharing rules, not charity.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples** (Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Nivkh): care for the old and the orphaned.
- **Jewish AO** and **Sakhalin Koreans:** community welfare tied to diaspora networks.
- **Kolyma and Magadan:** families of former prisoners and contract workers.
- **Recent migrants** (Central Asian, Chinese): as givers and as objects of public pity or blame.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-revolutionary and Gulag-era sources; mixed for present-day giving (mostly national surveys); thin for small Indigenous peoples outside ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Tibetan terms; Yiddish; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet children's homes and boarding schools; leprosy and stigma; NGO "foreign agent" status (affects which charities can be named or reached); mercy toward offenders is cultural here, while the law on it goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
