## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hunting and herding peoples** (Yukaghir, Evens, Evenks, Udege, Nanai, Koryak): empathy with animals and spirits as practical knowledge.
- **Sakha** in Yakutsk and in the uluses; **Buryats** in Ulan-Ude and in Aga and Ust-Orda villages: urban and rural registers.
- **Russian-speaking cities** (Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Irkutsk) against old-settler villages and Old Believer communities.
- **Sakhalin:** Russians, Koreans, Nivkh and Uilta, with the Japanese past as a layer.
- **Jewish AO:** Yiddish heritage against present Russian usage.
- **Generations:** Soviet-schooled against post-2000 young people, where the loanword эмпатия spreads.
- **Professions:** doctors, teachers, psychologists, priests and lamas, shamans (as each describes listening to a client).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich ethnography for hunters' perspective-taking; plenty of Russian-language psychometric papers; thin on the small Amur peoples outside folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Japanese for internment sources; Indigenous languages through bilingual texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hunting and animal-ceremony knowledge that communities keep among themselves; ethnopsychology papers that may cast peoples in fixed molds (read for variation, never for a type); internment memory in Japanese-Russian relations.
- **Held for review:** none.
