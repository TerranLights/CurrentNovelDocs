## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler Russians:** descendants of Cossacks (Albazin and Amur memory), Decembrist-linked Irkutsk families, Kolyma families.
- **Sakha:** war and home-front memory; large families and the "heroine mother" honor.
- **Buryats:** clan offerings; Buddhist monastic vows; recent military losses.
- **Chukchi, Koryak, Yupik, Evens:** elders' and hunters' duties to the camp, as they describe them.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples** (Nanai, Nivkh, Ulchi): offering customs and war service.
- **Kurils and Sakhalin:** Russian and Japanese memory of 1945; Sakhalin Koreans' wartime losses.
- **Jewish AO:** pioneer settlers of the 1930s and wartime service.
- **Women and men separately:** the self-denying wife and mother against the soldier and the rescuer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and Decembrist memory; mixed for caregiving and donation by unit; thin and sensitive for Indigenous accounts of voluntary death.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Church Slavonic for memorial texts; Sakha; Buryat; Japanese for Shumshu; Indigenous languages through bilingual texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current war losses and mobilization (risk to sources; blocked outlets); voluntary death among northeastern peoples (outsiders' framing; ask communities); the 1942-43 Arctic resettlement deaths; clan offerings that may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
