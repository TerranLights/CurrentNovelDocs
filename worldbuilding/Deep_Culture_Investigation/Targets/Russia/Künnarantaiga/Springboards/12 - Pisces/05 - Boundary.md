## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amur and Ussuri border towns** (Blagoveshchensk, Khabarovsk, Pogranichny) against inland settlements.
- **Chukotka coast:** Yupik and coastal Chukchi with kin in Alaska.
- **Kurils and southern Sakhalin:** Russian residents; Japanese former islanders as a contrasting voice.
- **Closed and border-zone towns** (naval bases on Kamchatka and Primorye).
- **Sakha and Buryats:** family avoidance customs, urban against rural.
- **Old Believers** against neighboring Orthodox and Buryat villages.
- **Nivkh, Nanai, Ulchi:** human-animal and sea-land boundaries.
- **Chinese, Korean and Central Asian residents:** where they are let in and kept out.
- **Communal-flat generations** against those raised in private flats and houses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state borders; mixed for personal and family boundaries (ethnography plus popular psychology); thin on measured personal space by region.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Chinese; Japanese; Sakha; Buryat; Yupik and Nivkh through bilingual texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Kuril dispute; border-zone and closed-town access; Chinese migrants (risk of feeding stereotypes; name variation); sacred-site restrictions, including gendered ones, as communities state them; the law of borders and property goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
