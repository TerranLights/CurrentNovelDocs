## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kurils, Sakhalin and Kamchatka:** quakes, tsunamis, volcanoes.
- **Amur and Ussuri river towns:** floods, border fears.
- **Taiga hunters** (Russian, Udege, Nanai, Evenk): tigers, bears and the forest's spirits.
- **Sakha:** smoke, cold and permafrost loss; fear figures in Sakha horror film and folklore.
- **Buryats:** spirits and Buddhist protective rites.
- **Primorye naval towns:** radiation and military secrecy.
- **Sakhalin Koreans and the Jewish AO:** memories of deportation and purges.
- **Kolyma families:** inherited fear from the camps.
- **Generations and genders:** what is spoken and what is hidden; who may admit fear.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and Gulag memory; mixed for anxiety by region; thin for small peoples' fear figures outside folklore volumes.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Korean; Yiddish; Udege and Evenk through ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war and mobilization fear (risk to informants); nuclear and military sites; fear of Chinese and Central Asian migrants (record claims, never adopt them); spirits that are dangerous to name, as communities say.
