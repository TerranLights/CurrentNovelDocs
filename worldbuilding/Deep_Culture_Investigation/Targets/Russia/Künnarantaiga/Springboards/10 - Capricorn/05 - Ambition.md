## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** rural youth heading to Yakutsk, Moscow or abroad, against those staying in the village.
- **Buryats:** monastic and scholarly careers against business and state careers; ties to Mongolia.
- **Incomers' families** in Magadan, Chukotka and Kamchatka: the "long ruble" generation against their children.
- **Primorye and the Amur:** traders and car importers against state-sector workers.
- **Irkutsk:** an old merchant city with its own pride, against newer industrial towns (Bratsk, Angarsk).
- **Indigenous peoples of the north and Amur:** herding and fishing enterprises against village wage work.
- **Sakhalin Koreans:** striving across migration and return to South Korea.
- **Chinese and Central Asian migrants:** ambition as a reason for coming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration and trade; mixed for attitudes; thin in English beyond a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Korean; Chinese for the border trade.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** out-migration and protest over trade are politically touchy; Chinese presence is a charged subject in regional media, so read for variation, not one line.
- **Held for review:** none.
