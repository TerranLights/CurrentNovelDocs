## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** village order around Aiyy rites and seasonal work against urban Yakutsk.
- **Buryats:** Baikal-shore communities keeping order with sacred sites; Buddhist east against shamanic west.
- **Evenki, Even, Chukchi and Koryak camps:** order of the tent and the herd against settlement order.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples:** riverside and coastal order against the state's fishing rules.
- **Port and border cities** (Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Blagoveshchensk): 1990s disorder memory.
- **Planned new towns** (BAM, mining, military) against old settler villages and Old Believer villages.
- **Disaster-struck communities** (Neftegorsk, Tulun, Amur flood towns).
- **Chinese, Korean and Central Asian residents:** order seen and policed from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime and disasters; mixed for household and cosmic order (strong for Sakha and Buryat, thin elsewhere).
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat and Mongolian; Chinese and Korean for minority views.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and Buddhist ritual knowledge may be restricted to practitioners; the 2010 Primorye case is politically charged; describe "disorder" claims about migrants as claims, not facts.
- **Held for review:** none.
