# Künnarantaiga: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat and classical Mongolian; Chinese and Korean (historic port quarters); Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. *Capricorn takes the spirit of the law only; the letter is Libra's.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha and Buryat communities:** clan custom and village assemblies against federal courts.
- **Reindeer herders and sea hunters** (Even, Evenki, Chukchi, Koryak, Yupik): quotas and land rules against custom.
- **Amur and Sakhalin fishing peoples** (Nanai, Ulch, Nivkh): fishing law against need and custom.
- **Gulag-heritage Magadan and Kolyma:** the long shadow of camp codes.
- **Primorye and Khabarovsk:** port cities with strong informal economies; border trade.
- **Old Believers:** a long history of distance from state law.
- **Chinese, Korean and Central Asian communities:** law as met by migrants.
- **Garrison and closed towns** under military rule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian legal scholarship and court statistics; mixed for regional legal consciousness; thin for small peoples' living custom.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat and classical Mongolian; Chinese and Korean for port history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** criminal codes and informal law are risky fieldwork; some customary rules are a people's own knowledge, to be taken only as they share it; criticism of courts carries legal risk in Russia. The letter of the law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
