*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha households:** central villages against Arctic uluses, where kin duty may reach farther.
- **Even and Evenki:** herding brigades against settled families.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist duty to parents and lamas against clan duties around shamanic rites.
- **Semeiskie and other Old Believers:** a community that keeps its own rules of work and remembrance.
- **Cossack families** (Transbaikal, Amur, Ussuri): hereditary service as a duty of the estate.
- **Soviet-era incomers:** BAM and Komsomol veterans, northern contract workers, Gulag families.
- **Shift workers (vakhta):** duty to family kept at a distance.
- **Sakhalin Koreans, Birobidzhan's Jewish families, Ukrainians of the Zeleny Klin, Chinese and Central Asian migrants** sending money home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Soviet-era writing on labor duty; thinner for private family duty outside Sakha and Buryatia; national surveys rarely break out the region.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki terms; Korean for repatriates; Yiddish for Birobidzhan archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military duty and the 2022 mobilization in Buryatia and other minority regions is politically sensitive; funeral and ancestor duties may be restricted knowledge in some families.
- **Held for review:** none.
