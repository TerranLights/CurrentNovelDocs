## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** central uluses, the Vilyuy basin and the Arctic north; Yakutsk against villages.
- **Buryats:** western (Ust-Orda; shamanic) against eastern Khori and Aga (Buddhist), Selenga and Barguzin.
- **Evenki and Even:** reindeer camps against settlements, across many units.
- **The northeast:** Chukchi and Siberian Yupik; Koryak; Itelmen and Kamchadals; Yukaghir; Dolgan of the Anabar.
- **The Amur and Sakhalin:** Nanai, Ulch, Udege, Orochi, Negidal, Nivkh, Uilta.
- **Small southern peoples:** Tofalars (Irkutsk), Soyots (Buryatia).
- **Old settlers:** Semeiskie Old Believers; Transbaikal, Amur and Ussuri Cossacks; Arctic old-timer communities.
- **Soviet-era incomers:** Gulag families of Magadan, BAM builders, military and naval towns.
- **Others:** Ukrainians of the Zeleny Klin; Birobidzhan's Jewish community; Sakhalin Koreans; Chinese and Central Asian labor migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian; mixed in English; thin for the smallest peoples. Many surveys report only the Far Eastern Federal District (Irkutsk sits in another) or Russia as a whole.
- **Lands:** the tracker header lists only "Russia" and no scope file exists yet; this pass writes for the twelve core units named above. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat (Cyrillic and Mongolian script); Evenki and other Indigenous languages; Yiddish; Korean; some Chinese and Japanese for Amur, Sakhalin and Kuril history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** research on authority in Russia carries legal risk ("foreign agent" laws; Memorial was dissolved); mobilization among minorities is sensitive; clan, shamanic and Buddhist knowledge may be restricted, so take only what each people shares.
- **Held for review:** none.
