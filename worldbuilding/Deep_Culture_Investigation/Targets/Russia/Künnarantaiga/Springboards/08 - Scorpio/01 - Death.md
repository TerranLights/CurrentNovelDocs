# Künnarantaiga: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Evenki, Nanai; Korean and Yiddish for Sakhalin and Birobidzhan. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** central farming districts against northern Arctic uluses; Orthodox against Aiyy-revival families.
- **Buryats:** Aga and Khori (Buddhist) against Ust-Orda and Ekhirit-Bulagat (shamanic) traditions.
- **Evens and Evenki:** herding camps, where death is far from hospitals, against village life.
- **Chukchi and Yupik:** tundra against coastal sea-hunting communities.
- **Nanai, Ulchi, Nivkhs:** Amur river and Sakhalin traditions of soul and memorial.
- **Russian Orthodox and Old Believers:** church against priestless funeral practice.
- **Sakhalin Koreans, Birobidzhan Jews:** ancestral and Jewish mourning kept in a minority.
- **Military and naval towns** (Kamchatka, Primorye): the military dead and their honors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Sakha and Buryats; thin and older on the small Amur and northeast peoples; statistics rich but bare.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for living voices; Korean, Japanese, Yiddish for specific sites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and alcohol-linked deaths among Indigenous peoples; graves and remains; the words for the dead, which some peoples avoid saying; shamanic death rites.
- **Held for review:** none.
