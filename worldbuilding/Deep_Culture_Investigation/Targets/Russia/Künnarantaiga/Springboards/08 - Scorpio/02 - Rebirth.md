## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** Aiyy-faith followers, Orthodox families, and those mixing both; urban Yakutsk against villages.
- **Buryats:** Buddhist revival against shamanic revival, and the tension between them.
- **Yukaghirs, Evens, Evenki:** beliefs about returning souls, set beside schooling and language loss.
- **Nivkhs, Nanai, Ulchi, Uilta:** cyclical renewal rites on the Amur and Sakhalin.
- **Chukchi and Koryaks:** recent Protestant conversion against older practice.
- **Russian Orthodox, Old Believers, Gulag descendants:** different stories of starting over in exile.
- **Sakhalin Koreans:** Protestant churches and a community remade after 1945.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sakha and Buryat revival; mixed for recovery; thin for conversion among the small northeastern peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat; Korean for Sakhalin churches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic initiation is restricted knowledge, so follow only what shamans and their kin have published; missionary sources carry a colonial frame; addiction data touches stigma.
- **Held for review:** classical Buddhist scriptures of South Asian origin in the Buryat canon, set aside under the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. Find Ksenofontov's collection in a library catalog and list the initiation accounts by people.

3. Read Willerslev's chapters on souls and naming, then search "реинкарнация юкагиры".
4. Search Sakha and Buryat news for "Ысыах" and "Сагаалган" reports from district events.
5. Search regional press for "трезвое село" Якутия and "реабилитационный центр" in each unit.
