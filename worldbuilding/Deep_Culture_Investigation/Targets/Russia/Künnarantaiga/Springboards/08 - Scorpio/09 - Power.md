*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat, Chukchi; Chinese and Korean for business networks. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how a culture judges who holds it and how. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** the republic's elite, diamond towns (Mirny, Udachny) and villages.
- **Buryats:** clan memory of taishas; Buddhist and shamanic authorities.
- **Chukchi and Koryak herders:** rich herd owners against poor households, past and present.
- **Evenki and Evens:** obshchina leaders and dispersed families.
- **Kolyma and Magadan:** camp-era power and its heirs.
- **Primorye and Khabarovsk:** port and timber money, organized crime, the navy.
- **Irkutsk:** industrial and hydropower towns.
- **Cossack revival groups:** claims to power through history.
- **Chinese and Korean business communities:** economic power without political voice, as a question.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on post-Soviet regional politics; mixed on Indigenous power; thin on pre-modern sources outside Sakha and Buryatia.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha, Buryat; Chukchi for herder vocabulary; Chinese for border business.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current power and criticism of officials is risky to research openly; some named figures are living; spiritual power is described only as believers present it.
- **Held for review:** none.
