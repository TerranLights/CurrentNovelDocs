# Künnarantaiga: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia (Lake Baikal to the Bering Strait). **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki, Even, Chukchi, Koryak, Nanai, Nivkh, Itelmen; Yiddish; Korean; Japanese for Karafuto-era sources. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nomadic herders** (Even, Evenk and Chukchi reindeer camps), whose goods must travel on sledges, against settled Sakha cattle farmers and Russian villagers.
- **Coastal sea-mammal hunters** (Siberian Yupik, coastal Chukchi, Koryaks) and the Commander Islands Aleuts: shared catch against personal gear.
- **Amur and Sakhalin peoples** (Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Nivkh, Uilta): objects collected by museums; boat and net ownership.
- **Buryats and Sakha:** home altars, silver and horse gear as kept heirlooms.
- **Port cities** (Vladivostok, Nakhodka, Korsakov): sailors' imported goods in the Soviet years.
- **Magadan and Kolyma:** camp survivors' and free workers' descendants.
- **Shift workers** in Mirny, Bodaybo and Chukotka mines keeping two households.
- **Jews of Birobidzhan; Sakhalin Koreans; Semeiskie Old Believers.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for ethnography and museum collections, thin for everyday modern possession outside Rosstat.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha and Buryat for local press; Korean and Japanese for Sakhalin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic objects and grave goods (some peoples restrict their handling); Gulag material; museum holdings taken without consent; property law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
