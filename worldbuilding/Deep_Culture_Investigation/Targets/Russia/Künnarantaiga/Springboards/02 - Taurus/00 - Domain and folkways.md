# Künnarantaiga: Possessions and value, domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga (Күннарантайга), region Russia: Sakha (Yakutia), Buryatia, Irkutsk, Zabaykalsky, Amur, the Jewish Autonomous Oblast, Khabarovsk, Primorsky, Sakhalin with the Kurils, Kamchatka, Magadan and Chukotka. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki, Even, Chukchi, Koryak, Nanai, Nivkh, Yukaghir and Siberian Yupik; Yiddish (Birobidzhan heritage); Korean (Sakhalin Koreans). **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how is it held?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sakha:** central cattle-and-horse districts against Arctic reindeer-and-fish districts; Yakutsk against villages.
- **Buryats:** Cis-Baikal (Irkutsk side) against Trans-Baikal (Khori, Aga): different herds and faith mixes; Soyots and Tofalars apart.
- **Tungusic peoples:** Evenks and Evens across Sakha, Amur, Khabarovsk, Buryatia and Magadan; herding against hunting groups.
- **Northeast:** reindeer Chukchi against coastal Chukchi and Siberian Yupik; Koryaks, Itelmens, Kamchadals; Aleuts of the Commander Islands; Yukaghirs.
- **Amur and Sakhalin:** Nanai, Ulchi, Udege, Oroch, Negidal, Nivkh, Uilta; Ainu heritage of Sakhalin and the Kurils.
- **Settler groups:** Semeiskie Old Believers of Transbaikalia; Russkoye Ustye old settlers; Cossack descendants of the Amur and Ussuri; Ukrainians of the "Green Wedge"; Soviet-era migrants and shift workers in Magadan, Mirny and Chukotka.
- **Diasporas:** Jews of Birobidzhan; Sakhalin Koreans; Chinese traders past and present; Tatars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Russian-language ethnography and Rosstat tables; thinner for small peoples and below the federal subject. The tracker header lists only "Russia"; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled, and these sheets are written for the twelve units above.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Sakha and Buryat for the press and folklore; dictionaries for the smaller languages; Yiddish, Korean, Japanese (Karafuto era) and Chinese (Qing-era Amur) for specific archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Gulag and Dalstroy dispossession; collectivization and "dekulakization" of Sakha and Buryat herders; the 1937 deportation of Primorye's Koreans; shamanic and Buddhist ritual objects held in museums; Indigenous land claims (law questions go to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
