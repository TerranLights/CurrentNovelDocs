*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **By faith:** Orthodox Russians; Semeiskie Old Believers; Buryat Buddhists; shamanic and Aar Aiyy followers; Protestant congregations (including Korean-led ones on Sakhalin and in Primorye); Jews of Birobidzhan; Muslims among Tatars and Central Asian migrants.
- **Sakha:** revival generation against Soviet-schooled elders; Yakutsk against districts.
- **Buryats:** Cis-Baikal against Trans-Baikal; urban Ulan-Ude.
- **Northeast and Amur peoples:** Chukchi, Yupik, Koryak, Itelmen, Nanai, Udege, Nivkh, Ulchi, each with its own teachings.
- **Settler towns:** Kolyma and Chukotka "Northerners"; Cossack descendants; Ukrainians of the Green Wedge.
- **Generations:** Soviet, 1990s and post-2000 cohorts.
- **University cities** (Irkutsk, Vladivostok) against villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography and literature; survey data by unit thin and mostly national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Yiddish and Korean for minority sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official "values" talk is political; shamanic teaching may be restricted by its keepers; Baikal disputes are live. Justice belongs to Libra, duty and tradition to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
