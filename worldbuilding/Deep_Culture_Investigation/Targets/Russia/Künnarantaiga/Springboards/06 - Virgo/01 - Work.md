# Künnarantaiga: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Künnarantaiga, region Russia (the twelve eastern units from Irkutsk and Lake Baikal to Chukotka). **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Sakha; Buryat; Evenki, Even, Chukchi, Koryak and the Amur languages through dictionaries; Yiddish; Korean. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders and sea hunters** (Even, Evenki, Chukchi, Koryak, Yupik) against **settled Sakha and Buryat stock keepers**: different years of work.
- **Old Russian peasant and Cossack settlers** of the Amur and Ussuri, against **Soviet-era contract recruits** chasing the "long ruble".
- **Mining and port towns** (Mirny, Neryungri, Susuman, Nakhodka) against **regional capitals** with office work.
- **Birobidzhan's Jewish settlers** and their farm-colony ideal; **Sakhalin Koreans,** many descended from wartime labor recruits.
- **Nanai, Ulch and Nivkh fishers,** and **Udege forest hunters** of the Bikin, against Russian logging crews nearby.
- **Gulag and exile descendants** in Magadan and Kolyma, whose family memory of work is shaped by forced labor.
- **Chinese and Central Asian migrant workers** in Primorye and Amur, as the region's newest labor force.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet labor policy and ethnography of the 1990s; thinner for present-day attitudes split by unit.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Sakha and Buryat for proverbs and press; Yiddish and Korean for minority memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced labor and deportation memory (Koreans, Gulag prisoners, Japanese prisoners of war); Indigenous unemployment, easily written up as stereotype; keep to people's own accounts.
- **Held for review:** none.
