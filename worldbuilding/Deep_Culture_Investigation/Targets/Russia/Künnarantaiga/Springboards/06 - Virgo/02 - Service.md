## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Military and border-service families** of Primorye, Kamchatka and the Kurils, against civilian port and fishing towns.
- **Orthodox parishes, Old Believer communities** (Semeiskie; Primorye taiga Old Believers) and **Buddhist Buryats:** three different religious ideas of serving.
- **Sakha** in Yakutsk against Sakha villages, where help is given through kin and the *alaas* community.
- **Evenki, Even and Chukchi** herding families, where serving elders and guests is part of camp life.
- **Birobidzhan Jews** and their community institutions; **Sakhalin Koreans**.
- **Workers in the tourism and trade economy** of Vladivostok, Irkutsk and Listvyanka against public-sector service in the capitals.
- **Cossack revival societies** of the Amur, Ussuri and Transbaikal, which speak of "service" as their identity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical state and church service; mixed for customer service and volunteering by unit.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, with Church Slavonic for older church sources; Sakha and Buryat; Yiddish and Korean for the minority communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service is a live and sensitive subject (conscription and recent deployments); handle it through published histories and stated attitudes only. Conscription rules are law (Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. RGADA online guide: find the Yakutsk office fond and its published inventories.
2. Rosstat: KOUZh tables on "добровольческая деятельность" by subject of the federation.
3. Search "Иннокентий Вениаминов якутский перевод" for his Sakha translations and mission letters.
4. eLibrary.ru: "служение" plus "буддизм Бурятия" for studies of lay and monastic service.
5. Search regional news for volunteer fire crews in Sakha and Zabaykalsky.
