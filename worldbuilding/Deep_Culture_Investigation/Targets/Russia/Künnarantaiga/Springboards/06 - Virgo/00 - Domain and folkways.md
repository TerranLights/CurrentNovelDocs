## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian old settlers and Cossacks** of the Amur, Ussuri and Transbaikal, against **Soviet-era migrants** who came for northern wages.
- **Semeiskie Old Believers** of Zabaykalye and Buryatia: a farming community with its own religious discipline of work and rest.
- **Sakha:** cattle and horse keepers of the central valleys, against northern Sakha in the reindeer zone.
- **Buryats:** Selenga, Khori, Aga and Barguzin groups, Buddhist and shamanist; Irkutsk (Ekhirit-Bulagat) Buryats are more Russified.
- **Evenki, Even, Yukaghir and Dolgan** herders and hunters spread across Sakha, Khabarovsk, Amur, Magadan and Buryatia.
- **Chukchi, Koryak, Itelmen, Siberian Yupik, Chuvan and Kerek:** reindeer and sea-mammal economies of the far northeast.
- **Amur–Sakhalin peoples:** Nanai, Ulch, Udege, Nivkh, Oroch, Negidal and Uilta (fishing and forest economies); Tofalars and Soyots in the Sayan foothills.
- **Jews of Birobidzhan,** **Sakhalin Koreans,** Ukrainian descendants in Primorye, and the military and port workforce of Vladivostok, Kamchatka and the Kurils.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet labor history and Northern ethnography; mixed for current attitudes (national surveys sample these units thinly).
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Sakha and Buryat for the press and folklore; dictionaries for the smaller Northern languages; Yiddish and Korean for two minority archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Gulag and deportation memory; shamanic healing knowledge, which healers may keep restricted; the Northern peoples' land and fishing rights (law belongs to Libra and Capricorn). Calendars and day-selection customs are classifiers only.
- **Scope:** the tracker header says only "Russia"; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled, and this sheet is written for the twelve units listed above.
- **Held for review:** none.
