## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay highland households and Cherkessk apartments:** extended-family control against urban nuclear living.
- **Circassian Besleney and Kabardian villages:** a past of princely estates may shape whose word governs a young person.
- **Abaza princely-society villages (Tapanta):** an old estate order and its present traces.
- **Nogai families of Nogaysky district:** steppe kin structures against highland ones.
- **Russian and Cossack families of the stanitsas:** Orthodox and Soviet norms on leaving home and choosing a spouse.
- **Women against men, and daughters against sons:** expect the widest gap on marriage, travel and work choices.
- **Young people who study or work in Stavropol, Krasnodar or Moscow:** autonomy gained away from home and its limits on return.
- **Returnees from exile (Central Asia) and from the Middle East:** different family norms brought back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; family control is well covered in news and NGO reports but sparse in measured data; collective-autonomy history is rich.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for songs and rites; Abaza and Nogai for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride abduction and coerced marriage; women's freedom of movement; the 1990s separatist claims and inter-ethnic tension. Marriage law and court practice go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
