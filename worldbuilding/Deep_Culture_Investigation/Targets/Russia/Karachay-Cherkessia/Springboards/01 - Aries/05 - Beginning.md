## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay herders of the high pastures against farming villages:** the year may begin at a different moment for each.
- **Karachay families who rebuilt in 1957:** a new start that is also a return; compare with those who stayed in Central Asia.
- **Circassian farming villages:** plowing openings and their spoken blessings may differ by sub-group.
- **Abaza villages:** first rites shared with, or kept apart from, Circassian neighbors.
- **Nogais:** steppe new-year and spring customs, perhaps linked to the wider Turkic spring holiday (*check*).
- **Russian and Cossack stanitsas:** Orthodox and Soviet New Year customs, and stanitsa founding days.
- **Diaspora returnees:** their own new start in an old homeland.
- **Muslim, Orthodox and secular households:** which new year a household keeps, and how.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; birth and calendar rites are fairly well recorded in Russian ethnography; present-day restarts (divorce, migration) are only in statistics.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for rite texts; Abaza and Nogai for depth; Turkish for emigrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** founding stories that leave out earlier peoples; the 19th-century expulsions and the 1943 deportation; whose "first" an ascent or a town was. Calendar divination customs, if found, are recorded as folk practice only.
- **Held for review:** none.
