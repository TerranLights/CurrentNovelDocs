## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of Big and Small Karachay:** upper Kuban, Teberda and Uchkulan gorges against the Malokarachaevsky lowlands; lineages differ.
- **Karachay generations born in Central Asian exile (1943 to 1957):** the return may have reshaped what a self names first.
- **Circassians (Cherkess):** separate Besleney and Kabardian-speaking villages in the Khabez, Adyge-Khabl and Ust-Dzheguta areas.
- **Abaza:** Tapanta and Ashkharua speakers, placed between both larger neighbors.
- **Nogais:** the Ak-Nogai of Nogaysky district, a steppe Turkic heritage distinct from the highland Karachays.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks:** stanitsas of Zelenchuk and Urup districts against urban Cherkessk.
- **Ossetians, Greeks and Armenians:** small communities (*check* locations).
- **Diasporas:** Circassians and Karachays in Turkey, Jordan and Syria, and Karachays in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, as outside mirrors.

## 4. Flags
- **Lands:** the tracker lists the pre-war lands only as "Russia"; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands of the former Karachay-Cherkess Republic.
- **Data:** rich in Russian for Circassian etiquette and Karachay rites; mixed for Nogais; thin for Abaza and the small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for primary texts; Abaza and Nogai for depth; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 deportation; the 19th-century wars and Circassian exile; disputes over the republic's ethnic balance and over who inherits the Alan past; religious practice under security scrutiny. Customary law (адат, тёре) belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
