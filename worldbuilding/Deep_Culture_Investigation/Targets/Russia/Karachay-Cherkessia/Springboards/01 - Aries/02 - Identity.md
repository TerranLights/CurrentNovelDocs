## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays against Balkars across the border:** one language, two republics; where the line of "us" falls is a question.
- **Circassians who answer "Adyghe," "Cherkess" or "Kabardian":** the label chosen may differ by village and generation.
- **Abaza of Tapanta and Ashkharua dialects:** do they place themselves nearer the Abkhaz, the Circassians or neither?
- **Ak-Nogai of Nogaysky district:** a steppe identity tied to a wider Nogai world (Dagestan, Stavropol).
- **Russians and Cossack descendants:** Cossack revival against plain Russian identity in the same stanitsas.
- **Karachays born in Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan exile:** exile as part of identity.
- **Urban Cherkessk against mountain and steppe villages:** mixed marriages and Russian-language identity in the town.
- **Diaspora returnees from Turkey, Syria and Jordan:** "who are you?" asked of those who came back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census categories and political identity talk; mixed for lived identity; thin for Abaza, Nogai, Ossetian and Greek identity inside the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin claims (Alan heritage) are politically charged; deportation; the 19th-century wars; ethnic balance in republic office; separatist talk. Handle as contested, never as one voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
