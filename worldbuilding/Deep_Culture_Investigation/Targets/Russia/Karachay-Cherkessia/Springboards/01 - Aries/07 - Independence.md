## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay highland herding households:** sheep and hay as the old base of self-sufficiency; compare with valley households who earn wages.
- **Circassian farming villages of the Khabez and Adyge-Khabl districts:** grain and orchards against Karachay pastoral life.
- **Abaza villages:** small farming communities between larger neighbors.
- **Nogais of the steppe:** grain and stock farming on flat land, close to Stavropol markets.
- **Russian stanitsas:** Cossack farming households and post-Soviet farms.
- **Cherkessk wage earners and pensioners:** urban dependence on jobs and transfers.
- **Rotation workers and their families:** households run on money earned elsewhere.
- **Disabled people and elders:** how independence is defined when help is needed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for measured economy (census, budgets, transfers); mixed for kin help; thin for Abaza and Nogai household ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for kin and help terms; Abaza and Nogai for depth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "subsidy region" talk can carry ethnic stereotypes about the North Caucasus; report the debate, never a verdict. Clan-based patronage. Political secession talk goes under Autonomy; welfare law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
