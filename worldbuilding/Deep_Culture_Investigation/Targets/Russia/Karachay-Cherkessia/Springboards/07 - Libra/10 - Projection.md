# Karachay-Cherkessia: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian (state documents, psychology, literature about the Caucasus); Karachay-Balkar, Kabardino-Cherkess, Abaza and Nogai for folk figures of blame; Arabic for repentance and the evil eye. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays:** the people charged with collective guilt in 1943, and how blame is spoken of in their own sources.
- **Circassians:** the 1860s expulsion and how outsiders and insiders are pictured in their accounts of it.
- **Abazins:** images of larger neighbors and of the Abkhaz kin across the mountains.
- **Nogais:** a steppe people's images of mountain neighbors, recorded apart.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** the settler image of the "mountaineer," and the images held of them in turn.
- **Rival claimants to the Alan past:** scholars and activists whose claims mirror each other.
- **Migrant workers and students from the republic in Russian cities:** where they are labeled from outside.
- **Healers and those who visit them:** misfortune explained by another's evil eye or by one's own wrongs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for the deportation and for literary images, thin for personal projection and present-day evil-eye belief.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the national languages for folk belief; Arabic for religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collective guilt and deportation; descent disputes between peoples; hate crimes; healers' and accusers' names in folklore records; SOVA and some outlets may be restricted in Russia.
- **Held for review:** none.
