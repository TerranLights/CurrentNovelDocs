## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of the upper Kuban and Teberda valleys (Uchkulan, Khurzuk, Kart-Dzhurt):** the highland core, where lineage-based bargains are likeliest to be recorded.
- **Karachays of the Malokarachayevsky and lowland districts, and urban Cherkessk:** post-1957 and later settlements, to separate from the highland core.
- **Circassians (Kabardians and Besleney) of the Khabezsky and Adyge-Khablsky districts:** Adyghe Khabze as their own code, with a distinct princely and noble past.
- **Abazins, Tapanta and Shkaraua groups (Abazinsky district and scattered villages):** a third language, near the Circassians in rank and custom yet documented apart.
- **Nogais of the Nogaysky district:** steppe Turkic marriage and council forms with their own history.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks of the Zelenchuksky, Urupsky and Prikubansky stanitsas:** Orthodox marriage and the Cossack assembly as a separate line of practice.
- **Smaller communities (Ossetians, Armenians, Greeks and others):** confirm by census before treating any as units.
- **Diasporas** (Karachays in Turkey and Central Asia; Circassians and Abazins in Turkey and the Middle East): bargains kept or changed abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Circassian custom and the Nart epic, mixed on Karachay custom, thin on Abaza and Nogai; mostly in Russian.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the four national languages; Arabic and Ottoman Turkish for religious and diaspora material.
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries only "Russia" as pre-war land, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the republic's core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 Karachay deportation and the 1864 Circassian expulsion; ethnic sharing of top offices and the 1999 election dispute; feud and bride-abduction cases; religious prosecutions; foreign-agent rules touching some sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
