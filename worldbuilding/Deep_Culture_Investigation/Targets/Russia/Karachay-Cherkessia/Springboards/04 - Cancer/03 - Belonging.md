## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**, whose shared exile in 1943-1957 may tie belonging to a common memory more than to a single village.
- **Circassians**, split between Besleney and Kabardian villages and linked to a large world diaspora.
- **Abazins**, a small people with kin ties to Abkhazia, to see whether belonging is drawn across a state border.
- **Nogais**, whose main communities lie outside the republic, in Dagestan and Stavropol.
- **Russians**, for whom the republic may be home without being "theirs" in the same way, a question to test.
- **Kuban Cossacks**, whose belonging is to a host and a stanitsa as well as to a people.
- **Newcomers**: workers from Central Asia and the South Caucasus, and repatriates from abroad.
- **Mixed-marriage families**, whose children must answer "which one are you?"

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnic-relations monitoring and press are plentiful, but they look at conflict more than everyday belonging.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish; Arabic for Middle Eastern diasporas; Ottoman Turkish for older travel texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** quarrels over the republic's status and leadership; claims about the 1860s expulsions; deportation memory; questions of citizenship rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
