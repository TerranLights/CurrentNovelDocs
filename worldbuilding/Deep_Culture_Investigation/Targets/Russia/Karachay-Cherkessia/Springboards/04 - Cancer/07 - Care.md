*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay households**, to see whether the youngest son's family is expected to stay with and care for the parents.
- **Circassian villages**, where the vigil and the folk-medicine record can be set against current home care.
- **Abazins**, whose small numbers may mean care networks cross into Circassian kin.
- **Nogais**, who may draw on care practices shared with the Nogais of Dagestan and Stavropol.
- **Russians and Cossacks**, more likely users of residential care homes, a point to test.
- **Labor-migrant families**, split between the republic and Moscow or the oil regions.
- **Families caring for disabled children**, whose route through state and kin care is its own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folk-medicine ethnography and state social-service data both exist; little survey work on unpaid family care in the republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic for religious healing texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious healing practices and claims about them; elder abuse; conditions in residential homes.
- **Held for review:** none.
