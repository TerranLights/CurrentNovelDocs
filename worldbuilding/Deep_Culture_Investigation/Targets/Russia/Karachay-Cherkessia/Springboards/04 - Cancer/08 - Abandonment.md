## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**, whose exile left houses, graves and cattle behind, and whose return with lowland resettlement may have meant a second leaving.
- **Circassians and Abazins**, to trace how far the 1860s exodus split lineages between those who left and those who stayed.
- **Nogais**, whose earlier moves on and off the Kuban steppes, dates to check, can be set beside the others.
- **Kuban Cossacks**, whose stanitsas lost families to repression in the early Soviet years.
- **Russians of the mining settlements**, left by industry after 1991.
- **Children in state care**, of every group, and the families who give them up or take them in.
- **Migrant workers' households**, where the line between leaving for work and leaving the family is drawn by the family itself.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective forced leaving (exodus, deportation) in memory and archives; thin for private desertion and child abandonment in this republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish; Ottoman Turkish for 19th-century migration records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether the 1860s events are to be called genocide is disputed; deportation memory; children's identities in care; desertion and support duties as law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
