## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of the high valleys**, where avalanche and flood risk may make guarding the household a matter of the whole village.
- **Circassians**, comparing the guest-protection ideal in 19th-century accounts with what villagers describe today.
- **Abazins**, to see whether a small people's protection rests on alliance with Circassian neighbors.
- **Nogais**, whose defender figures come from a steppe epic rather than a mountain one.
- **Kuban Cossacks**, founded as a frontier guard, for protection as a corporate duty of the stanitsa.
- **Russians in Cherkessk**, likely to lean more on state services than on kin, a point to check.
- **Women and girls** across all groups, since who escorts or guards them is a separate question from who protects the house.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; strong for folklore and military history, thin for present household practice.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; English for 19th-century travelers from Britain.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the charge of wartime collaboration used against the Karachays; blood revenge and honor violence, whose customary rules belong to Libra or Capricorn; domestic violence.
- **Held for review:** none.
