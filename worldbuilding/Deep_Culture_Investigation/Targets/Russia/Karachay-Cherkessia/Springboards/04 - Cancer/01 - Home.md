## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay families who rebuilt after 1957**, against those who resettled in lowland villages: the old aul and the new house may not mean the same.
- **Circassians in the Khabez district**, whose village houses come from a lowland, wattle-and-daub tradition.
- **Abazins of the Psyzh and Kubina area**, to see whether a small people's homeland is spoken of as a village, a valley or the whole republic.
- **Nogais of the Nogai district**, where a pastoral past may shape what counts as a fixed home.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks** in stanitsas such as Zelenchukskaya, with their own yard-and-house pattern.
- **Town dwellers of Cherkessk** in Soviet apartment blocks, against village households with land.
- **Families who left the republic** for Moscow, Stavropol or abroad and speak of it as "back home".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for house forms in Soviet ethnography; mixed for recent feeling about home; thin in Abaza and Nogai print.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Georgian for the Klukhori records; Turkish for émigré material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** houses taken over during the exile, and quarrels over land on return; disputes between communities over who the land belongs to; questions of property law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
