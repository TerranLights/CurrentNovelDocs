## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: the 1957 return fixes a date for collective renewal; compare first-generation returnees with their grandchildren.
- **Circassians**: Khabze revival and repatriates from the Levant reshape what renewal means.
- **Abazins**: renewal here is above all language survival for a very small speech community.
- **Nogais**: literary and language renewal in a steppe district far from the mountain institutions.
- **Cossacks of the Zelenchuk and Urup stanitsas**: revival of an estate abolished after 1920.
- **Orthodox Russians** at Arkhyz: pilgrimage and church restoration.
- **Young Muslims against their Soviet-raised elders**: return to faith as a break inside families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on the 1957 return and on medieval churches; thin on personal conversion and addiction recovery; the movements' own sites may be short-lived.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish and Arabic for repatriates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** competing Alan claims are politically charged; religious revival touches on extremism labels; addiction is stigmatized.
- **Held for review:** none.
