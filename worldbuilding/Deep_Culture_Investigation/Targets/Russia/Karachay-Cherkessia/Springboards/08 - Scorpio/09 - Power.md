## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: former princely and uzden houses, and today's political weight of the largest people.
- **Circassians**: noble descent against Soviet-era promotion as routes to standing.
- **Abazins**: power through alliances when numbers are small.
- **Nogais**: a steppe lineage of lords and a district far from the capital.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: federal links, town administration and Cossack self-government.
- **Women in public life**: few at the top, so their routes to power need separate search.
- **Business families in Cherkessk**: economic power that may not appear in office lists.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Pre-1917 estates and current office lists are documented; informal power and ethnic balance are often asserted, rarely measured.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Greek and Latin for Byzantine sources; Turkic and Persian for the Nogai Horde.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic shares of office are a flashpoint; naming clans in power can harm informants; business-politics ties may lead to defamation risk.
- **Held for review:** none.
