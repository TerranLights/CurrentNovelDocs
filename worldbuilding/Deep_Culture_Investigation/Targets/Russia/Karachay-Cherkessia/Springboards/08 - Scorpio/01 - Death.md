## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays born in exile** against those born after 1957: graves left in Central Asia and the dead remembered at a distance.
- **Upper-valley Karachay villages** (Uchkulan, Khurzuk, Kart-Dzhurt) against families resettled on the plain: old family cemeteries reached or lost.
- **Circassian villages**: lament and remembrance for a war of the 1860s held beside present mourning.
- **Abazins**: a small people whose dead and laments may be recorded only under Circassian headings.
- **Nogais** of the Nogaysky district: steppe burial heritage and Kypchak words for the dead.
- **Cossack and Russian stanitsas**: Orthodox memorial days and the war dead of 1942 nearby.
- **Cherkessk**: hospital death, municipal cemeteries and mixed families who must choose a rite.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich for laments, epitaphs and the deportation; thin for suicide and end-of-life care in any minority language.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic for gravestones; Greek-letter and Old Ossetic material for the Alan layer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation deaths and the Caucasian War dead are grievance topics; suicide is likely hidden; remains found on the passes and in old graves are handled by search teams and families.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Russian library catalogs for «Нарты героический эпос балкарцев и карачаевцев» and list the episodes in which heroes die.
2. Search «гъыбзэ» and «сыйыт» in KChIGI and KBIGI publications for lament collections and studies.
3. Find Lavrov's epigraphy corpus and pull the entries from Karachay and Circassian villages of the republic.
4. Query «книга памяти депортированных карачаевцев» for lists of the dead in exile.
5. Download Rosstat regional death-by-cause tables for the republic and note which causes are given by district.
