## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay families with deportation memory**: whether sacrifice is framed around survival in exile more than around war.
- **Circassians**: whether Caucasian War memory and the 1864 expulsion, not the Soviet war, shape the language of giving oneself.
- **Abazins**: their heroic lore may follow Circassian models or keep its own.
- **Nogais**: steppe epic heroes as a different template.
- **Kuban Cossacks**: sacrifice tied to imperial and Soviet military service as a caste ideal.
- **Ossetians** of the Georgievsko-Osetinskoe area: a poet-figure rather than a warrior as the local model.
- **Women and men**: unpaid care, fasting and household giving may fall unevenly, to be measured, not assumed.
- **Young men of service age today**: present wars and the honoring of the dead are live questions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich for war memory; thin for measured care and donation at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Nogai for the epic; Ossetian for Khetagurov in the original; Abaza.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the word "martyr" carries insurgency and counter-terror charge; coverage of current wars is restricted by Russian law and is a sensitive area for fieldwork; deportation memory is grief.
- **Held for review:** none.
