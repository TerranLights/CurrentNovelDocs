# Karachay-Cherkessia: Domain and folkways (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic for Islamic vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and transcendence: how suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape are met, plus the magic ways (healing, charms, divination, hidden forces).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of the upper Kuban and Teberda valleys** (Uchkulan, Khurzuk, Kart-Dzhurt) against **lowland Karachays** around Ust-Dzheguta: mountain shrines and older belief sites lie upstream.
- **Karachays born in Central Asian exile** against those born after the 1957 return: suffering and the unseen may be framed differently by deportation memory.
- **Circassians (Cherkess)**, separating Besleney-dialect villages from Kabardian-speaking ones around Khabez: the old pantheon is recorded unevenly by subgroup.
- **Abazins**, Tapanta and Ashkharua groups: a Northwest Caucasian people with their own Nart and spirit lore, often folded into Circassian studies.
- **Nogais** of the Nogai district: a steppe Turkic tradition close to Kazakh and Crimean Tatar belief.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks** of Zelenchukskaya and other stanitsas: Orthodox and Russian folk belief in the same valleys.
- **Ossetians** of Georgievsko-Osetinskoe *check*: an Iranian-speaking enclave with its own sacred places.
- **Diasporas** in Turkey, Jordan and Syria: communities that kept or reshaped beliefs abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore and epic are rich; republic-level measures of distress and belief in the unseen are thin. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the tracker carries "Russia" broadly); every sheet of this sign is written for the core lands of the present republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for folklore; Abaza and Nogai for their peoples; Turkish for diaspora work; Arabic for Islamic terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 deportation and the 1864 Circassian expulsion are living grief; neo-pagan revivals (Adyghe Khabze, Tengri movements) and Islamic currents are politically sensitive, and some Islamic literature is on Russia's banned-extremist list.
