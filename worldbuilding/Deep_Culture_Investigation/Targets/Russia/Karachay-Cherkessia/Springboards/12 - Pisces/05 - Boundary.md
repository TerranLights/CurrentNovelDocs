## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: avoidance practices such as not naming in-laws may be named and kept differently from Circassian ones.
- **Circassians**: the guest-house threshold and the code of restraint (укӏытэ) as a distinct line-drawing system; separate Besleney and Kabardian villages.
- **Abazins**: living between Circassian and Karachay neighbors, they may draw lines toward both.
- **Nogais**: steppe household layout and its own avoidance customs.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks**: stanitsas founded on a frontier line; fences and land claims may be told from the settler side.
- **Herding households of the high valleys**: the boundaries of seasonal pasture and the nature reserve press on them most.
- **Young urban women and men in Cherkessk**: dress, phone privacy and space may be negotiated differently from village norms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; family ethnography and frontier history are rich; measured personal-space and privacy data exist only at the national level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for frontier and census material; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for avoidance and threshold vocabulary; Abaza and Nogai for the smaller peoples' house customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land disputes between peoples are live and sometimes violent; the frontier zone limits field access; modesty and headscarf questions are politically charged. Property law and court records belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
