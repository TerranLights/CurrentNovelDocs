## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay and Circassian families**: whether their codes of restraint call for different ways of showing that one has understood another's feeling.
- **Men and women within each people**: lament and condolence roles may be divided, so empathy may be expressed in different settings.
- **Abazins**, often speakers of Abaza, Kabardian-Cherkess and Russian: crossing three languages daily may shape how they read others.
- **Nogais**: a smaller Turkic people whose view of the larger groups is seldom recorded.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks**: how the stanitsas understand Muslim neighbors' customs, and the reverse.
- **Born-in-exile Karachays and their grandchildren**: empathy for deportees may differ between those who lived it and those who inherited it.
- **Urban professionals** (doctors, teachers in Cherkessk): empathy as a trained skill against kinship empathy in the auls.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnographic material on fosterage, kunak ties and laments is likely rich, while measured empathy data for the republic is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for laments and tales; Abaza; Nogai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic tension, especially after the 1999 presidential election dispute, makes cross-group empathy a charged topic; frame questions neutrally.
- **Held for review:** none.
