# Karachay-Cherkessia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic for devotional terms. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: the 1828 defeat and the 1943 exile give two different templates of yielding.
- **Circassians**: the 1864 capitulation and expulsion shape public mourning, and differ between Besleney and Kabardian lineages.
- **Abazins**: subgroups that submitted, resettled or emigrated at different times in the nineteenth century.
- **Nogais**: an earlier incorporation into the empire, with steppe proverbs on fate.
- **Kuban Cossacks**: defeat in the Civil War and the Lienz handover, remembered by the revival groups.
- **Recovery and hospice users**: giving oneself over to treatment or a higher power, across peoples.
- **Athletes and their families**: the sporting refusal to yield as a public ideal to compare with religious resignation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; conquest history is well documented from the Russian side; local memory needs oral history; end-of-life data are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic terms; English for Lienz literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1942 occupation and charges of collaboration that preceded the deportation are painful and contested; the 1864 events are called genocide by many Circassians, and the term is politically charged in Russia. Never adopt outsiders' "fatalism" claims.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Russian State Library for "Хасаука 1828" and "присоединение Карачая к России".
2. Search AKAK's online scans for "покорность" with "абазины" and "бесленеевцы".
3. Search Bugay's bibliography for the volume on the Karachay deportation.
4. Search the press for "21 мая Черкесск день памяти" to see how the day is held.
5. Look for Fund "Vera" or regional health ministry pages on palliative care in the republic.
