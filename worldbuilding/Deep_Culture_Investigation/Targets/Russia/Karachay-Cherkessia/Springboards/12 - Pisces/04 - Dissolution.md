## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Abazins**: a small people under pressure from Kabardian-Cherkess and Russian, where language loss is the sharpest test.
- **Nogais**: a small Turkic people in a few villages, whose identity is spread across several Russian regions.
- **Karachays**: the people whose autonomy was abolished outright in 1943, so ending and return are a single story for them.
- **Circassians (Besleney and Kabardian speakers)**: a shared literary standard may be dissolving local dialects; separate the two.
- **Kuban Cossacks**: a host broken up in the 1920s and partly rebuilt since 1990.
- **Russians in Cherkessk**: the majority-language pole toward which others shift, worth asking how they see that pull.
- **Small communities that emigrated in the 1990s** (Greek and German families of the North Caucasus, *check* whether present in these lands): groups that left as a whole.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; administrative history and language statistics are good; mystical or ecstatic self-loss is likely thin and may be absent as a named category.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1992 split plans and the 1999 election dispute touch live interethnic tension; the 2018 language-law debate is politically charged; any separatism angle may be legally risky to discuss in the republic.
- **Held for review:** none.
