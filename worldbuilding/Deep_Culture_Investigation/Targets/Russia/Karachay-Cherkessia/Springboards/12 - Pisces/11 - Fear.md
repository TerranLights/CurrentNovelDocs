# Karachay-Cherkessia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: fear tied to the memory of a whole people's removal, which other groups did not share in the same way.
- **Circassians**: the Nart giants and the 1864 expulsion as two layers of dread; compare Besleney and Kabardian villages.
- **Abazins**: small villages where fear of gossip and shame may weigh heavily.
- **Nogais**: steppe dangers (storms, drought) differ from mountain ones.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks**: Russian folk frights and ghost legends in a mostly Muslim republic.
- **High-pasture herders**: predators, storms and mudflows as working fears.
- **Families touched by counter-terror operations**: fear of the security services as well as of armed groups.
- **Children and the old**: whose fears are taken seriously and whose are laughed off.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore of frights and monsters is rich; survey data are national; fear of the state is hard to ask about.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for surveys, warnings and stanitsa legends; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for epic monsters and child frights; Abaza and Nogai for their own fear vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** questions about security operations and insurgency can put respondents at risk; deportation and expulsion memories are traumatic; mental-health stigma limits disclosure.
- **Held for review:** none.
