## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland herding families against Cherkessk households:** learning by work on the pastures against learning by school and screen.
- **Circassian families of noble and commoner descent:** fosterage and manners teaching may have run differently by estate.
- **Karachays born in exile and those born after the return:** schooling in Russian and Central Asian classrooms against home-language schooling in the auls.
- **Abazins:** children who must learn Abaza, Circassian or Karachay, and Russian, with the thinnest stock of materials.
- **Nogais:** Ak-Nogai children with materials made largely in Dagestan, a link worth checking.
- **Cossack stanitsas:** a revived youth-training track of their own.
- **Families who add religious schooling:** madrasa learners beside the state curriculum.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnopedagogy writing and crafts likely rich; recent figures on home-language learning thin and politically charged.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Karachay-Balkar, Kabardian-Cherkess, Abaza, Nogai; Arabic for manuscripts and religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2018 change making native-language study optional is a law question (Libra) and a live grievance; exile schooling; state oversight of religious instruction.
- **Held for review:** none.
