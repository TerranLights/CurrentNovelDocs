# Karachay-Cherkessia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Karachay-Balkar, Kabardian-Cherkess, Abaza, Nogai. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay households in the upland auls against Karachay families in Cherkessk:** whether respect registers toward in-laws and elders hold in city flats.
- **Besleney-speaking and Kabardian-speaking Circassian villages:** the formulas of toast and blessing may differ by village tradition.
- **Abazins:** many speak three or more languages, so the question of who interprets for whom is sharp.
- **Nogais of Nogaysky district:** a separate oral-poetry line from the mountain peoples.
- **Cossack stanitsas and Cherkessk Russians:** forms of address and public speech in the stanitsa assembly against city usage.
- **Deaf residents:** the sign-language community as its own communicating group.
- **Elders schooled before 1943 or in Central Asian exile, and their grandchildren:** possible language brokering inside families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore and Circassian etiquette likely rich; everyday recorded talk thin outside linguistic fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Russian, Karachay-Balkar, Kabardian-Cherkess, Abaza, Nogai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** avoidance customs concern women's speech; record them as families describe them. Exile-era letters may involve living relatives. Religious bodies operate under state monitoring.
- **Held for review:** none.
