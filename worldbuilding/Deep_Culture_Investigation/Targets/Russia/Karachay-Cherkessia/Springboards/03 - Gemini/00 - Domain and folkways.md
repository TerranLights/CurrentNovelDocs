## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upland Karachay valleys** (Teberda, Uchkulan, Khurzuk, Kart-Dzhurt) against **lowland Karachay villages** (Malokarachayevsky, Prikubansky, Ust-Dzhegutinsky districts): distance from the capital may shape the daily round of talk and travel.
- **Circassians of Khabezsky and Adyge-Khablsky districts:** Besleney and Kabardian speech under one literary standard.
- **Abazins** (Psyzh, Inzhich-Chukun, Kubina, Elburgan): a small people with two dialects, Tapanta and Ashkharywa, living among several larger languages.
- **Nogais of Nogaysky district** (Erken-Shakhar, Erken-Yurt, Ikon-Khalk): steppe Turkic speech beside the Karachay language.
- **Russians of Cherkessk** against the **Cossack stanitsas** (Zelenchukskaya, Pregradnaya, Ispravnaya, Kardonikskaya): city standard against stanitsa speech.
- **Smaller groups** (Ossetians, Greeks, Armenians, Ukrainians; *check* census numbers): home languages beside Russian.
- **Diasporas** in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, as outside reference points.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: likely rich for Circassian etiquette, Nart folklore and census language tables; thin for Nogai and Abaza daily talk and neighbor life in numbers.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries "Russia" as the pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the former republic.
- **Languages needed:** the five above; Turkish and Ottoman Turkish (diaspora, outsiders); Arabic (Islamic teaching texts).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 deportation of the Karachays and their 1957 return; the 1864 Circassian exile; inter-ethnic politics around the republic's paired name; native-language schooling politics; some sources carry Russian "foreign agent" labels (note the label, not as a verdict).
- **Held for review:** none.
