## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay upland families** where several brothers may share stock and pasture, against **Cherkessk families** of two or three children.
- **Circassian families:** elder and younger rank marked in speech, as a measure of how far birth order reaches.
- **Abaza families:** small numbers, so ties may run across Circassian and Karachay kin by marriage.
- **Nogai families:** steppe kin terms with age-graded sibling words.
- **Russian and Cossack families:** an older large-family past and a lower-fertility present.
- **Families separated by deportation:** siblings scattered across Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and the republic.
- **Repatriate families:** siblings split between the republic and Turkey or Syria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; census and kinship-term data likely good; current sibling duties and rivalry thin, a fieldwork matter.
- **Languages needed:** all five of the republic's languages, with Abaza grammars for its kin terms; Kazakh or Kyrgyz for exile-era family records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sister-brother duties touch women's reputation, so record them as families describe them; deportation losses; inheritance and which son keeps the house are rule questions for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
