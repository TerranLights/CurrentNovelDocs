## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: reform-minded religious youth against elders who keep customary rites.
- **Cherkess**: revivers of *adyge khabze* against those who see the old code as outdated.
- **Abazins**: language activists, as a small people's reform agenda.
- **Nogais**: steppe herders turned settled villagers by Soviet policy; how that change is told now.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: the post-1991 Cossack revival as its own program of renewal.
- **Soviet-trained intelligentsia** against post-Soviet generations.
- **Mountain villagers resettled to the plain** and their descendants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imposed Soviet reforms; mixed for pre-1917 local reformers; thin and sensitive for present religious reform.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess; Arabic and Ottoman Turkish for jadid-era sources; Greek for Byzantine letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious reform is policed as "extremism" in Russia; collectivization and resettlement memory; detained activists. Legal and administrative reform belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
