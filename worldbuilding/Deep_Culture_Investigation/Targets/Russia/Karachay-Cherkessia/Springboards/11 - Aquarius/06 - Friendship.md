*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men**: age-mate bands, sport, army service and work away from home are the settings to check, set against the literature on men's unions.
- **Women**: whether, where and with whom friendships outside kin are kept, a question to ask women themselves.
- **Friends across peoples**, Karachay and Cherkess, Abaza and Russian, in Cherkessk schools and workplaces.
- **Karachays born in Central Asia**, whose oldest friends may be Kazakh or Kyrgyz.
- **Nogai and Abaza villagers** in mixed districts: friends from neighboring peoples.
- **Cossack descendants**, with their own comradeship words.
- **Diaspora returnees**, making friends in a homeland where they were strangers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal friendship customs in 19th-century and Soviet ethnography; thin for present friendship; no regional survey data known.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian-Cherkess and Karachay-Balkar for toasts and songs; Kazakh and Kyrgyz for exile-side memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the official "friendship of peoples" story can hide conflict; friendships between men and women are a guarded subject in some families.
- **Held for review:** none.
