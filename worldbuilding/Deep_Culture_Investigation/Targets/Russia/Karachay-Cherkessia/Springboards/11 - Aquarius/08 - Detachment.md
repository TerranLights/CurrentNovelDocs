# Karachay-Cherkessia: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic for religious terms. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes, in forms that are praised (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and forms that are condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays and Cherkess**: whether in-law avoidance and public reserve with one's own children still hold, by generation.
- **Abazins and Nogais**: whether their avoidance customs match their neighbors' or differ.
- **Women and men** at funerals: lamenting against composure, and whether Islamic teaching has moved the line.
- **Russians and Cossack descendants**: to compare display rules, without assuming a contrast.
- **Elders living alone or in care homes**, against those in large households.
- **Labor migrants and their families** left at home.
- **Professionals** (doctors, police, rescuers) who are trained to keep distance at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Soviet ethnography for avoidance customs; mixed for present practice; thin for psychology at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian-Cherkess and Karachay-Balkar for laments and proverbs; Arabic for religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grief tied to the deportation; isolation of elders as a source of family shame; turnout as a political subject. Avoid "stoic mountaineer" stereotypes.
- **Held for review:** none.
