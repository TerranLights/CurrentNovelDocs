## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays:** lineage pride through *тукъум*; check how memory of the deportation and return feeds collective pride.
- **Circassians:** pride tied to an old class order (princes, nobles, commoners); compare descendants of each layer.
- **Abazins:** a small people between two larger ones; ask how they mark a pride of their own.
- **Nogais:** a steppe past and a single district; pride in the Horde against present-day minority status.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** imperial and Soviet frames of pride; Cossack revival against non-Cossack Russians.
- **Muslim revival circles against secular families**, on whether ethnic pride is itself suspect.
- **Diaspora:** Circassian and Karachay communities in Turkey, for pride kept up in exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore and symbols are rich; survey data by people are thin and often only national.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for proverbs and songs; Abaza and Nogai for the smaller peoples.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic pride touches interethnic rivalry over offices and land; Circassian claims about the 19th-century expulsion are politically charged in Russia; family genealogies may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
