## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays:** felt and wool work tied to sheep pastoralism in the upper Kuban and Teberda; compare upland and resettled lowland villages for continuity of the craft after 1957.
- **Circassians:** gold embroidery, metalwork and saddlery, once bound to noble patronage; ask whether the end of the nobility changed who makes and why.
- **Abazins:** a literary language codified only in the 20th century, so written authorship is recent and small; separate written creators from oral singers.
- **Nogais:** bardic improvisation and felt from a steppe heritage, distinct in materials and forms from the mountain peoples.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks:** church art, embroidery and Soviet art-school training.
- **Diaspora makers** in Turkey and Jordan, against makers in the homeland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Museum and ethnographic material on crafts is rich; data on everyday improvising and on present-day creative careers is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; the four native languages for craft vocabulary; Turkish for diaspora theses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** objects taken to museums during the Caucasian War and the deportation years may carry claims of return; weapons as craft objects carry war memory.
- **Held for review:** none.
