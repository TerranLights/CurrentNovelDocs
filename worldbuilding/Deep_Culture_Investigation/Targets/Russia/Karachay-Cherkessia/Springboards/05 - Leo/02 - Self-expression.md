## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Circassians:** a written etiquette tradition (*Адыгэ хабзэ*) makes the restraint-and-display balance easy to study; compare villages with Cherkessk.
- **Karachays:** check how far mountain-village speech customs survived the deportation and resettlement.
- **Abazins:** a small people often bilingual in Circassian; ask whether expression in Abaza differs from expression in their second language.
- **Nogais:** improvised steppe verse gives a different channel of voice.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** a separate Orthodox and Soviet frame, with chastushki as a licensed outlet.
- **Generations and genders:** young women and men online against elders at the *хасэ* or feast table.
- **Muslim revivalists against secular and Soviet-shaped families**, on dress and music.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Etiquette and folklore studies are rich; survey data by people are rare and need checking.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian-Cherkess and Karachay-Balkar above all; Turkish for the diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression (ethnic claims, protests, religious dress) can carry legal and personal risk for informants; handle names with care; women's dress is a live dispute.
- **Held for review:** none.
