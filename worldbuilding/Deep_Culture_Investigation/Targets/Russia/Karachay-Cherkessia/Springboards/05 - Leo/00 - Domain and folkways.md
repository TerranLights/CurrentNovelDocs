## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays** of the upper Kuban and Teberda valleys, Karachayevsk, Uchkeken and the Malokarachayevsky district: mountain-pastoral festival calendar; split upland villages from lowland villages settled after the 1957 return.
- **Circassians (Cherkess)** of the Khabez and Adyge-Khabl districts and Cherkessk: dzhegu, horse culture and etiquette traditions; Besleney and Kabardian-speaking groups may differ.
- **Abazins** of the Abaza and Ust-Dzheguta areas: a separate language and Nart variant; Tapanta and Ashkharua dialect groups to keep apart.
- **Nogais** of the Nogai district (Erken-Shakhar and nearby villages): steppe Turkic heritage distinct from Karachay.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks** of Zelenchukskaya, Urup and Prikubansky districts: Orthodox calendar festivals and Cossack song.
- **Diasporas:** Circassians and Abazins in Turkey, Jordan and Syria; Karachays in Turkey and in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan (from the deportation years).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in Russian-language ethnography of folklore, dance and weddings; thin in republic-level statistics.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core lands of the former republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian first; Karachay-Balkar, Kabardian-Cherkess, Abaza and Nogai for primary sources; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora work; Ottoman Turkish for Evliya Çelebi's travel account of Circassia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 Karachay deportation and the 19th-century Circassian expulsion color every topic of display and celebration; inter-ethnic balance of offices is politically charged; bride abduction is sensitive and contested; alcohol at feasts is a religious question for many; any law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
