# Karachay-Cherkessia: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Karachay-Cherkessia, region Russia. **Language(s) for searching:** Russian, Karachay-Balkar, Kabardian-Cherkess, Abaza, Nogai; German and English for early travelers. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Circassian and Karachay brides and young wives:** when and before whom a young woman is shown or hidden; compare village and city practice today.
- **Young Muslim men:** visibility as risk under official watch, against visibility as status in the dance circle.
- **Abazins and Nogais:** census and media visibility for small peoples, against the two titular peoples.
- **Karachays in Central Asia** who stayed after the return: a community out of sight from the homeland.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** stanitsa and town Russians; check how they speak of their place on the republic's screens, stages and boards.
- **Elders and the rural old:** how far mountain villages see their elders, against those living alone in Cherkessk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Wedding and evil-eye folklore is rich; data on everyday visibility, loneliness or representation are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess; German or old English editions for Klaproth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reporting on preventive registers and on surveillance touches security services; protect informants; brides' and women's photographs need consent; evil-eye belief should be described as its holders describe it.
- **Held for review:** none.
