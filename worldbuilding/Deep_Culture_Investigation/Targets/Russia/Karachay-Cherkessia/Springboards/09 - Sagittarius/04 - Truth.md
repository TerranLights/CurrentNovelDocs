## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay and Circassian historians:** the shared past is told differently; compare citations, not just conclusions.
- **Abaza and Nogai narrators:** small peoples whose versions of disputed events may go unheard.
- **Cossack descendants:** their account of the conquest and the 1990s against the mountain peoples' accounts.
- **Young people online and elders at village gatherings:** different gatekeepers of what is true.
- **Scientists at Nizhny Arkhyz and Zelenchukskaya:** a community whose standard of proof differs from the clergy's and the bards'.
- **Clergy of the board against independent preachers:** rival claims to religious truth.
- **Diaspora historians in Turkey and Jordan:** accounts of 1864 and 1943 written outside Russian archives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Historical polemic is abundant; measured trust in science, news or clergy is thin at the republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Ossetian for one side of the Alan polemic; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Alan question, the 1943 charges and the genocide debate inflame relations between peoples; Memorial and RFE/RL outlets carry Russian legal designations, so access and citation need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
