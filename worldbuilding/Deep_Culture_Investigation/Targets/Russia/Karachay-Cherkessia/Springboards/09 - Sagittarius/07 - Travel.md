## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay herding families:** seasonal travel to the high pastures is part of work, not leisure; compare with town families.
- **Karachays with kin left in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan:** travel between two homes since 1957.
- **Circassians with kin in Turkey, Jordan and Syria:** visiting, marriage and return across the exile's borders.
- **Abazins:** a people whose own past migration story is little published.
- **Nogais:** memory of steppe mobility and of settlement.
- **Cossack descendants:** ancestors who arrived as settlers; how they tell the arrival.
- **Young labor migrants to Moscow and the north:** leaving for work, and whether returnees are admired or pitied.
- **Hosts in Dombay and Arkhyz:** guides, guest-house keepers and drivers whose work is receiving strangers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 19th-century emigration and alpine travel; mixed for present tourism and migration; thin for travelers' own words outside the elite.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish and Ottoman Turkish; Arabic; English and German for alpine writers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1860s exile and the 1943 deportation are trauma for many families; frontier zones near the Abkhazian border may limit travel and fieldwork.
- **Held for review:** none.
