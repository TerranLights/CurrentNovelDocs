## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay families of the exile generation:** schooling denied and then pursued, which may shape how degrees are prized.
- **Circassians of Khabez district:** the Circassian school tradition from Bersey to now.
- **Abazins:** a small literary language; schooling in it depends on a few teachers and books.
- **Nogais:** a minority within a minority; Nogai-language study may be thin.
- **Russian and Cossack families:** Russian-language schooling with Orthodox Sunday schools beside it.
- **Graduates of foreign Islamic universities:** learning brought from abroad, against home-trained clergy and secular academics.
- **Rural and urban pupils:** village schools in the high gorges against Cherkessk lyceums.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutional and language-policy history; mixed for attainment statistics; thin for Abaza and Nogai schooling and for families' own views.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Arabic; Turkish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** native-language policy is a live grievance; foreign religious study is a security topic, so returnees may not speak openly.
- **Held for review:** none.
