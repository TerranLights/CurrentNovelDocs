## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays by gorge** (Uchkulan, Teberda, upper Kuban, Malokarachay): the old village societies may keep different holy places and custom.
- **Karachays born in exile against those born after 1957:** two religious upbringings, one under Soviet bans in Central Asia.
- **Circassians of the Khabez and Adyge-Khabl districts:** Besleney and Kabardian-origin villages may differ in how custom and Islam sit together.
- **Abazins, Tapanta and Ashkharua villages:** two dialect groups with possibly separate old cults and Ottoman ties.
- **Nogais of the Nogai district:** a steppe Turkic tradition among mountain neighbors.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks** of Zelenchukskaya, Kardonikskaya and other stanitsas, against urban Russians of Cherkessk: Orthodox, Old Believer and Soviet-secular layers.
- **Smaller groups** (Armenians, Ukrainians, Greeks, Ossetians *check* each): minority faiths and churches.
- **Diasporas** in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan: how exile reshaped faith and memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on Alanian archaeology, Nart epic and post-Soviet Islam; thin on district-level survey data and on Nogai and Abaza religious life. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled**; these sheets are written for the core lands of the former republic.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish and Ottoman Turkish; Arabic; Byzantine Greek and Georgian for medieval sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official "extremism" labels on Muslims; deportation trauma; claims between peoples over the Alanian heritage; the 1999 republic election conflict; Circassian genocide recognition. Legal regulation of religion goes to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
