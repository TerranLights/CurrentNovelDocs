## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay activists of the return generation:** a conviction rooted in exile, which later generations inherit differently.
- **Circassian activists in Khabez and Cherkessk:** a cause tied to 1864 and the diaspora, kept up in small organizations.
- **Abaza and Nogai cultural leaders:** firmness about language and status within a republic run by larger peoples.
- **Young Salafi-leaning believers and Hanafi village imams:** two kinds of religious firmness, each calling the other weak or rigid.
- **Old Believers and other Cossack descendants:** fidelity to a rite or to a Cossack identity.
- **Officials and party members:** whether switching sides draws a sanction, and which.
- **Women activists:** the mothers and widows who lead rallies after killings and disappearances; their stand may be read differently from men's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Events are well covered in the press; the inner side of conviction (motives, cost) needs testimony and is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar; Kabardian-Cherkess; Abaza; Nogai; Turkish for diaspora activism.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1999 and 2004 left lasting grievances between peoples and families; religious firmness can be framed as extremism by officials. Court cases go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
