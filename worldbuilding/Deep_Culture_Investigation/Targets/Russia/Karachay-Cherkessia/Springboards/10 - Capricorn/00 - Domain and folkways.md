## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays, Greater against Lesser Karachay valleys:** a princely house in one, different settlement histories in the other; also exile-born against home-born generations after 1943-1957.
- **Circassians, Kabardian and Besleney communities:** old princely and noble ranks that may have left different traces.
- **Abazins, Tapanta and Shkarawa groups:** two histories of lordship and dependence to keep apart.
- **Ak-Nogai villages:** steppe-pastoral rank (mirzas, elders) against the mountain model.
- **Russians and Kuban Cossacks of the stanitsas:** a separate military-estate tradition of atamans and assemblies.
- **Small minorities (Ossetians, Ukrainians, Armenians, Greeks; *check* sizes):** urban and village enclaves with their own forms of address.
- **Diasporas in Turkey, the Levant and Central Asia:** emigrant and deportee lines that kept or changed the old codes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for 19th-century ethnography and Soviet folklore, thin for survey data at republic level and by people.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for most scholarship; Karachay-Balkar, Kabardino-Cherkess, Abaza and Nogai for folklore and press; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora and pre-Russian documents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 deportation, the 1864 Circassian expulsion and the 1999 election dispute are live wounds; ethnic shares of office are politically charged; some family genealogies are private.
- **Lands:** the tracker lists "Russia" as the pre-war lands carried, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the sign's others write for the republic's core lands.
- **Held for review:** none.
