## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Circassian families of the Khabez villages:** etiquette taught at home and at the dance floor; compare with urban families who learn it in ensembles.
- **Karachay herding families:** discipline tied to horses, flocks and shepherd camps; compare with town-raised children.
- **Abazins:** their dance and etiquette are often subsumed under Circassian forms; check what they keep apart.
- **Nogai villages of the plain:** fasting and upbringing in a steppe setting with its own words.
- **Cossack and Russian families:** cadet schools and Orthodox fasts as another discipline system.
- **Pious young men and secular peers:** prayer and fasting as chosen self-discipline, against sport or army discipline.
- **Girls and women:** what restraint in bearing and speech is asked of them, compared across generations and between town and village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for etiquette and dance; mixed for child-rearing; thin for measured data below Russia-wide level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza and Nogai dictionaries to fill the term table.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment and family violence; religious practice, where piety can be labeled "extremism"; hazing in sport and army.
- **Held for review:** none.
