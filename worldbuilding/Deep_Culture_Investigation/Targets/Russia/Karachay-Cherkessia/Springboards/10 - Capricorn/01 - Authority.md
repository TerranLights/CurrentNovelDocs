## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of the upper Kuban and Teberda valleys:** the house that ruled them, and how its memory frames elders and officials today.
- **Karachays resettled on the plains after 1957:** village authority rebuilt in new places; compare with returnees to old auls.
- **Circassians of Khabez district and around Cherkessk:** princely and noble lines met by Soviet office; who now speaks for a village.
- **Abazins:** whether old princely standing carried over into modern posts, a question to test village by village.
- **Nogais:** mirza authority in steppe camps, checked against the mountain chain of command.
- **Cossacks and Russians:** elected ataman and church against the muftiate and the elders.
- **Generations:** young men drawn to independent jamaats against the official muftiate; a split on religious authority.
- **Women:** the mother-in-law's household authority set beside men's public offices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; ethnography and press coverage are good, opinion data at republic level is thin and its reliability open to question.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardino-Cherkess for oral sources; Abaza and Nogai for village material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** counterinsurgency and "Wahhabi" labels, and the ethnic rotation of top posts; treat accounts from security services and from the accused as sides, not facts.
- **Held for review:** none.
