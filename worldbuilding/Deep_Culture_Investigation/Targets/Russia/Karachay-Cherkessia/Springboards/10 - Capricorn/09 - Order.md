## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays of the high valleys:** order tied to pasture rights and lineage quarters; compare with plains villages built after 1957.
- **Circassian villages:** the guest house and the dance floor as ordered public space; compare with Cherkessk apartment blocks.
- **Abazins:** their cosmology and village plans may be merged into Circassian accounts; pull them apart.
- **Nogais:** the move from steppe camps to fixed villages; check which household and camp order moved with them.
- **Russians and Cossacks:** the stanitsa grid and church square, a planned imperial order inside the republic.
- **Cherkessk residents of all peoples:** urban order where neighbors are mixed.
- **Those who lived through 1990-1992 and 2004:** order remembered through near break-up and crisis, by people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for archaeology and ethnography; mixed for crime data; thin for perceived safety at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardino-Cherkess for folk cosmology; Arabic for manuscripts; Abaza and Nogai for village material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990s and 2004 crises are partisan memory; insurgency and counterterror data are disputed; the Alan heritage is claimed by several peoples, so record each claim as a claim.
- **Held for review:** none.
