## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachay men and women:** the uzden code may name different duties for each; compare its texts with women's own accounts.
- **Circassian families still practicing fosterage or its memory:** a chosen tie with duties that cut across blood.
- **Abazins:** their village duties are often described through Circassian sources; their own folklore should be read first.
- **Nogais:** duties in a steppe household of herders; check how they differ in a sedentary village.
- **Cossack stanitsas:** service owed to the Host as a hereditary estate, with its own memorial calendar.
- **Families of deportees and their children:** duties to the exiled dead, which the younger generations may keep differently.
- **Emigrants in Turkey and Jordan:** duty to the homeland felt from abroad, and the strain of return.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography and folk texts; thin in measured data at republic level.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardino-Cherkess; Abaza for its own cycle; Turkish for diaspora; Ottoman Turkish for Evliya.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military duty since 2022 is a political subject; deportation memory is grief, not material; some funeral rites belong to families.
- **Held for review:** none.
