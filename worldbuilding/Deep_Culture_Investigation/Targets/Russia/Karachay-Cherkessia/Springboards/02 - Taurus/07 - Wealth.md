## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: livestock fortunes from the summer pastures against wealth from trade and public posts; compare older herding families and newer business families.
- **Circassians**: old princely and noble houses (pshi, work) whose wealth was broken in 1917-1930 *check*; whether name still counts beside money.
- **Abazins**: a small people whose wealthy families may be few and well known; check how far wealth follows kin.
- **Nogais**: herd-based wealth in the steppe-edge villages.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: stanitsa farm wealth and the post-Soviet farming firms.
- **Resort towns (Dombay, Arkhyz, Teberda)** against **farming districts**: new money from tourism.
- **Cherkessk elites tied to the republic's government** against **rural households**: how far wealth tracks public office (report sources, not charges).
- **Diaspora families in Turkey and Jordan** who send or invest money.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Income statistics are good; wealth (land, herds, cash, gold) is largely unrecorded, and informal income escapes the official series.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for tales and wedding talk; Greek or a translation for the Byzantine source.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption and clan politics; Soviet dekulakization and the fate of princely families; never tie wealth or poverty to a people as a whole. Taxation and property law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
