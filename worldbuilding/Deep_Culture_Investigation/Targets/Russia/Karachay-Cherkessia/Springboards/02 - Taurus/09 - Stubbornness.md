## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Circassians**: memory of the long resistance to 1864 and the recognition campaign; how far individual firmness is linked to that history.
- **Karachays**: the push for return and restoration after 1957; compare families active in the 1990s movement with others.
- **Abazins**: keeping a small language alive in school and at home against the pull of Russian and Circassian.
- **Nogais**: language retention and claims to their own district inside a larger republic.
- **Cossacks**: rehabilitation claims and refusals to accept the post-1990 settlement *check*.
- **Ossetians of Kosta-Khetagurovo**: a small community holding to its own language and memory.
- **Elders against youth**: who is called "set in their ways" and who "won't listen".
- **Followers of newer Islamic currents against older village practice**: firmness in doctrine on both sides, as each describes it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Collective holding-out (movements, resistance history, language) is well documented; personal stubbornness as a trait has little region-level data.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian-Cherkess and Karachay-Balkar for movement documents and tales; Turkish and Arabic for diaspora sources of the Circassian campaign.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1864 recognition is political in Russia; the 1990s movements touched on borders between peoples; feuds and blood revenge belong to Capricorn (spirit) or Libra (letter) and are only pointed to here; never cast a people as obstinate.
- **Held for review:** none.
