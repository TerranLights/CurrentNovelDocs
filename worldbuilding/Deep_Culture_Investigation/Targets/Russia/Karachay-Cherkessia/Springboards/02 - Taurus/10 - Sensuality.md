## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays**: dairy and mutton from the summer pastures and the pleasures of the kosh; compare herders and townspeople.
- **Circassians**: cheese, millet dishes and the dance gathering; compare the restraint of public touch with the expressiveness of music.
- **Abazins**: food and dance customs that may be folded into "Circassian" in sources; separate them.
- **Nogais**: steppe tea and broth customs against mountain dairy.
- **Russians and Cossacks**: the banya, Cossack songs, and drinking customs at stanitsa feasts.
- **Observant Muslims against secular residents**: which pleasures each permits, as they themselves explain.
- **Resort workers and visitors in Dombay and Arkhyz**: sensory pleasures sold to tourists against local everyday ones.
- **Elders and youth**: older home cooking against cafés, fast food and imported tastes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Food, music and dress are well documented; private pleasure, touch and comfort are thin in sources and need interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; German for Klaproth; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for song texts and food terms; Abaza and Nogai for the smaller units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** alcohol and religious observance; gender rules at dances; sexuality is Scorpio's and is not scouted here.
- **Held for review:** none.
