## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karachays against Kuban Cossacks**: the 1920s redistribution between auls and stanitsas left two different stories of whose land it was first.
- **Families of former Circassian and Abaza princely and noble lines** against **descendants of dependents**: ownership after the 1860s emancipation may still shape memory of land.
- **Karachay returnees to the old Klukhori district (Karachaevsk, Teberda)**: houses and land taken over while they were away.
- **Abazins and Nogais**: smaller peoples whose claims to districts and pastures are made against larger neighbors.
- **Residents of Arkhyz, Dombay and Teberda**: private plots beside state resort land.
- **Holders of Soviet-era land shares against private farmers (фермеры)**: two kinds of post-1990 owners.
- **Women and heirs who stay or leave**: who holds the house and plot in each people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Budget, land fund and 19th-century documents are rich; who owns what by people, gender or clan is thin and often disputed.
- **Languages needed:** Russian above all; Karachay-Balkar and Kabardian-Cherkess for proverbs and local memory; Georgian for Klukhori district records (1944-1957) *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land and pasture disputes between peoples are live and political; deportation property; 1990s calls for separate Circassian-Abaza, Karachay and Cossack units *check*. Property law, title and inheritance rules go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
