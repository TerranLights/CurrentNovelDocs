## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Karachay princely and uzden families against those of dependents:** whether old rank still decides who serves at a feast.
- **Cherkess (Kabardian and Besleney):** the most elaborate written etiquette of table service, worth testing against practice.
- **Abazins:** an aristocratic estate past of their own, with ties to both Circassian and Karachay houses.
- **Ak-Nogai:** service to khans and mirzas, a different chain of loyalty.
- **Cossacks and Russians:** service as a sworn estate duty to the tsar, then to the Soviet and Russian state.
- **Young men and women:** who serves elders at gatherings, and how that changes in Cherkessk.
- **Veterans and soldiers' families:** service across WWII, the Chechen wars and later conflicts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for etiquette and estate history; thin for modern service jobs and customer service.
- **Languages needed:** Russian; Kabardian-Cherkess and Karachay-Balkar for etiquette terms; Ottoman Turkish and Arabic for the Mamluk and Crimean layers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the estate past touches families' standing today; military service and mobilization since 2022 are politically charged; deportation-era service is painful memory.
