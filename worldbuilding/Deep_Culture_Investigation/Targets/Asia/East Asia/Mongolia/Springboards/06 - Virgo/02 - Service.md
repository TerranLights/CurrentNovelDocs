# Mongolia: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for Qing-era and earlier texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects; Russian and Persian-language translations for the empire period. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, household, customer, the public, the nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herding households at a distance from towns, who host passing travelers, against urban shops and hotels.
- Khalkha, Oirat and Buryat communities: variants of the hosting rites and tea offering.
- Bayan-Olgii Kazakhs and the Dukha: separate hospitality and obligation customs.
- Buddhist monasteries and lay helpers; shamanic communities (restricted knowledge).
- Conscripts, peacekeepers and the civil service; hired herders and domestic workers; hotel and restaurant staff.
- Elders and the young, by generation of socialist and post-socialist schooling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Hospitality and the pre-modern service categories are documented; present-day service work and domestic work are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic, with traditional script for older texts); Persian and Chinese sources in translation for the empire; Kazakh for Bayan-Olgii. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic service to spirits and monastic knowledge may be restricted; ask first. Conscription and peacekeeping accounts can touch on political topics.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (a category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Search the national library for "зочин хүндлэх ёс" (rite of honoring a guest) and "цай өргөх" to get the standard descriptions.
2. Look up the entries on banner service, *albat* and *shabi* in Atwood's encyclopedia, and note the Mongolian terms used.
3. Search gogo.mn and ikon.mn for "үйлчилгээний соёл" and sort the complaints by type of service.
4. Search for "нөхөр" in the de Rachewiltz glossary and note what it meant in service to a lord.
5. Look for the Mongolian Red Cross Society's reports on the 2009-2010 dzud.
