# Mongolia: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script and Tibetan for medical and Buddhist texts), Kazakh (Bayan-Olgii), Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Russian for socialist-era health writing. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herders far from clinics against Ulaanbaatar ger-district and apartment dwellers (air, water, access).
- Households that consult lamas, shamans, bone-setters or only clinics; Buddhist and Muslim families.
- Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan and Dukha communities: different healers and remedies.
- Gobi against Khangai and Khovsgol: climate, food and distance.
- Men and women; elders, children and mothers; miners and urban workers.
- Generations raised under the Soviet-style system and after it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official statistics and for the traditional medical canon; thin for everyday folk illness, minority healing and mental health.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; Tibetan and traditional Mongol script for the medical canon; Russian for older studies; Kazakh for Bayan-Olgii. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and tantric healing knowledge; alcohol, suicide and mental illness; patients' identities. Do not publish names.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (a category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Download the WHO Observatory review and the latest Health Indicators and note the leading causes of death and illness named.
2. Search for the three-humor vocabulary: "хий шар бадган" in medical and monastic sources.
3. Search the national library for "бариа засал" and "рашаан" and read what each reports.
4. Search for STEPS Mongolia survey tables on alcohol and tobacco by region.
5. Search Manba Datsan and the traditional medicine institute for publications and training lists.
