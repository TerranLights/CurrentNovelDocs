## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Monastic scholars against university scientists and Soviet-trained engineers.
- Herders with pasture and weather knowledge against agency forecasters.
- Ulaanbaatar schools and aimag schools; boarding-school children from herding families.
- Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan and Dukha communities, each with a different craft knowledge.
- Diviners, almanac readers and lamas who consult almanacs, against households that do not.
- Women and men in science and accounting jobs; mining engineers and analysts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official statistics and exam data are open; the culture's own sense of analysis is scattered across school, monastery and herding sources, and thinner for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; Tibetan and traditional Mongol script for monastic texts; Russian for Soviet science; Kazakh for Bayan-Olgii. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** divination and shamanic knowledge may be held by lineages; monastic debate materials are community property in practice.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (a category only).
