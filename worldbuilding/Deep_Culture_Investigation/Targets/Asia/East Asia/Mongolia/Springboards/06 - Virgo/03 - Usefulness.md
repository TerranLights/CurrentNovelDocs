# Mongolia: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for old texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Tibetan-language Buddhist texts through Mongolian translation. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose: a person's usefulness to family, community and nation, feeling useful or useless, being a burden, and the standing of the old, the sick and the disabled.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herding households (where children are hands from an early age) against ger-district and apartment households.
- Rural elders who remain in the herd camp against elders in Ulaanbaatar.
- Buddhist families, shamanic families, Muslim Kazakhs and secular households.
- Persons with disabilities by region, and by access to schooling.
- Girls and boys, mothers and fathers: who is expected to be useful in what way.
- Former collective-farm workers (a generation honored for labor) against youth in service work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: child labor and older-adult statistics are better covered than the household feeling of being useful or a burden, which is thin and in the national language.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; Kazakh for Bayan-Olgii; Tibetan or Mongolian for the Buddhist texts. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** elder neglect, suicide, child labor and disability are sensitive; read the sources with care and keep personal stories anonymous.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (a category only).
