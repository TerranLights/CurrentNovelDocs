# Mongolia: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise; hospitality, gifts and their obligations, giving that makes standing, and stinginess.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding households against urban households:** neighborly mutual aid against money gifts and remittances.
- **Khalkha, Oirat groups (Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Torguud, Altai Uriankhai), Buryats, Dariganga:** possible differences in guest customs and feasts.
- **Kazakhs:** Islamic almsgiving and its own hospitality code.
- **Buddhist laity and clergy; shamanic practitioners:** giving as merit or offering.
- **Dukha and Tuvans:** hospitality now mixed with tourism.
- **The rich and the poor;** Ulaanbaatar ger-district residents.
- **Migrants abroad and the families who receive their money.**
- **Women and men:** who gives and who receives at events.
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, Oroqen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for hospitality and disaster relief; mixed for charity and religious giving; thin for informal giving and rural mutual aid, which come from scattered ethnography. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian for older sources, Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line, English for scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious giving and monastic finances; poverty and aid dependence; gifts that bind or buy influence; handling of donations after disasters.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
