# Mongolia: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ulaanbaatar and provincial centers against steppe herders:** crowd anonymity against a landscape where each rider is seen from afar.
- **Ger-district migrants and registered city residents.**
- **Women, men, children, elders living alone.**
- **People with disabilities; street children; sexual minorities:** groups often described as unseen.
- **Khalkha, Oirat groups (Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Torguud, Altai Uriankhai), Buryats, Dariganga:** possibly different rules about hosting, greeting and eye contact.
- **Kazakhs:** a Muslim minority with its own gaze and visibility rules.
- **Dukha and Tuvans:** small groups made visible by tourism and film.
- **Monastics and lay Buddhists; shamanic practitioners.**
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, Oroqen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for rites and folk belief; mixed for representation and surveillance; thin for everyday gaze and eye-contact norms, which come from scattered ethnography. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian for socialist-era sources, Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line, English for anthropology.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children in street life; the harmful-gaze beliefs and the practices against them; shamanic knowledge; persecution of monks; sexual minorities; surveillance by the state; do not treat an advocacy report as neutral.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
