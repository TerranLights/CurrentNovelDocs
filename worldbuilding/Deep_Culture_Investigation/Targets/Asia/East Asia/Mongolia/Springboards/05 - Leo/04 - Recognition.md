# Mongolia: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title; recognition of identity and of wrongs; recognition withheld.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State and party honorees against community-honored people** (elders, herders, healers): formal against informal esteem.
- **Wrestlers, trainers, owners, jockeys:** who in a sporting team is credited.
- **Khalkha, Oirat groups, Buryats, Dariganga:** possible differences in how elders and guests are recognized.
- **Kazakhs and Tuvans:** minority honors and outside recognition.
- **Women and men:** whose achievements the press names (check obituary and award pages).
- **Victims of political repression and their families:** a group seeking acknowledgment.
- **Urban professionals, civil servants, herders.**
- **Buddhist clergy and shamanic practitioners:** titles within each.
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, Oroqen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for state honors and titles; mixed for everyday credit and workplace recognition; thin for minority-group honors apart from Kazakhs. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian for socialist-era sources, Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line, English for scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of repression and its victims; political honors as instruments of power; minority claims for recognition.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the President's Office or Montsame for "төрийн дээд шагнал" and note the orders and the stated reasons for the latest awards.
2. Search "Төрийн соёрхол" laureates and list fields and people over several decades.
3. NSO census: open the table of ethnic groups and note the categories.
4. Search Mongolian customs reference books for "хойморт" (the seat of honor) and "хадаг".
5. Find the Museum of Victims of Political Repression's own site or catalog and list its named exhibits.
