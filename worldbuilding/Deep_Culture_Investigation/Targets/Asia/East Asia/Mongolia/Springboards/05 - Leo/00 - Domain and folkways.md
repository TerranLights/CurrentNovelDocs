## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha herders by province versus Ulaanbaatar:** rural festival life against city entertainment, ger districts against apartment blocks.
- **Western Mongols (Dörvöd, Bayad, Torguud, Zakhchin, Olet, Myangad, Altai Uriankhai):** distinct dance, epic and singing styles; tests whether "Mongolian" forms are one thing.
- **Buryats and Dariganga, Khamnigan, Darkhad:** northern and eastern groups with their own festivals and ways of play.
- **Kazakhs (Bayan-Ölgii, Khovd):** eagle hunters, horse games, aitys; a Turkic Muslim tradition of display.
- **Tuvans and Dukha (Tsaatan) reindeer herders:** small groups with distinct festivals and music; tourism shapes how they are shown.
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, Oroqen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for Naadam, music and wrestling; mixed for romance, children and private pleasure; thin for minority groups other than Kazakhs. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian for socialist-era sources, Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and monastic ritual knowledge; sacred sites; the 1930s purges; tourist performance of small peoples' rites.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
