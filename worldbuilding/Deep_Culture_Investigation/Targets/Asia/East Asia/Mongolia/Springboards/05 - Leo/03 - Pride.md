# Mongolia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Tuvan; Chinese and Japanese for outside coverage. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance; group pride; wounded pride.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha majority against Oirat groups (Dörvöd, Bayad, Torguud, Zakhchin, Olet, Altai Uriankhai):** separate historical memories.
- **Buryats, Dariganga, Darkhad, Khamnigan:** regional pride and identity within the nation.
- **Kazakhs:** a minority with its own homeland pride across a border.
- **Dukha and Tuvans:** small peoples whose pride is mediated by outsiders' attention.
- **Herders and city dwellers:** pride in livestock and land against pride in schooling, jobs and cities.
- **Sports and arts figures, wrestlers and their fans.**
- **Elders against the post-1990 young;** men against women.
- **Buddhist clergy and shamanic practitioners:** pride in lineage and practice.
- **Mongols abroad (Korea, Japan, Europe, North America), if a diaspora unit is wanted.**
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for national and historical pride; mixed for individual pride and arrogance; thin for minority-group pride except Kazakhs. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian, Japanese and Chinese for outside sources, English for scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hostility toward neighbors (Chinese in particular) in surveys and ethnography; wartime memory; do not treat nationalist claims as findings.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
