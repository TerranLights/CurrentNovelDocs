## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha, Oirat groups (Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Torguud, Altai Uriankhai), Buryats, Dariganga:** each has its own noble and local-leader history.
- **Herding camps against provincial centers against Ulaanbaatar:** informal leaders by age and standing against elected and appointed officials.
- **Party leaders, civil servants, business leaders.**
- **Women and men:** formal office against informal community leadership.
- **Buddhist clergy and shamanic practitioners:** religious leaders and their followings.
- **Kazakhs, Tuvans, Dukha:** minority leaders and their elders.
- **Youth, students and activists;** socialist-era trained elites and the post-1990 generation.
- **Inner Mongolia, if carried:** Chakhar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, Oroqen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for pre-modern and political leadership; mixed for informal and women's leadership; thin for how followers describe good leaders in their own words. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Russian for socialist-era sources, Chinese for the Inner Mongolia line, English and Persian (in translation) for medieval accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** contested figures (Choibalsan, Tsedenbal, Demchugdongrub); party and corruption allegations; ongoing political disputes; religious succession.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
