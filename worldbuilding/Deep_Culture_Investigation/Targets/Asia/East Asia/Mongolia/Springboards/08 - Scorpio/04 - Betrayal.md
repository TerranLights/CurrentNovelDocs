# Mongolia: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Russian, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** breaking the trust of those who relied on one: turning against, deserting, deceiving or exposing people or a cause to which one owed loyalty.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Khalkha, Oirat and Inner Mongolian groups: each has a different account of who betrayed whom in the 17th to 20th centuries.
- Herding families against urban professionals: how kin loyalty and money disputes are framed.
- Buddhist, shamanic and Christian households on marital fidelity and oaths.
- Buryat and Dariganga groups, to compare oath and kinship customs.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: a Muslim and Turkic code of loyalty.
- Women and men, and older and younger generations, on infidelity and jealousy.
- Families of informers and of the informed-on after the 1930s and after 1990.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in chronicles and epics; mixed on modern private life; thin on survey data about infidelity and informing.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian for old texts; Russian and Mandarin for some files; Kazakh and Buryat for minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names of informers and the informed-on; closed secret-police files; separatism cases in Inner Mongolia; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
