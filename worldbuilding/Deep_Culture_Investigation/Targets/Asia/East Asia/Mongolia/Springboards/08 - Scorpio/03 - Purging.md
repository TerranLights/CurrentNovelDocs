# Mongolia: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Buryat, Oirat, Kazakh, Russian for Soviet-era papers, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lamas and laity against party members: who was targeted in the 1930s and who survived.
- Buryat and Dariganga families whose members were accused as spies or enemies.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: an Islamic purity tradition beside Mongol ones.
- Oirat groups (Torguud, Dörvöd, Zakhchin, Bayad) in the west.
- Herders in mining districts against Ulaanbaatar ger-district residents on pollution.
- Shamanic households, Buddhist households and secular families on cleansing rites.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): the 1960s purge and its survivors.
- Descendants of the repressed against descendants of those who carried it out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the 1930s repression since the 1990s; mixed on ritual cleansing; thin on household and bodily purging.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Russian for archives; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia; Kazakh and Buryat for minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names and lists of the repressed and of perpetrators; restricted or closed files; Inner Mongolian purge accounts are politically charged; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
