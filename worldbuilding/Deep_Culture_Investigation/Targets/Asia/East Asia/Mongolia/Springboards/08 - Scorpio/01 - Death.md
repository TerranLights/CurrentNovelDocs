# Mongolia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic), Classical Mongolian (traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means; grief and mourning are researched here, funeral practice elsewhere.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist lay families, shamanic households and secular urban families: three ways to talk about what follows death.
- Khalkha herders against Ulaanbaatar residents: where people die, who sees the body, and how it is spoken of.
- Buryat and Darkhad: spirits of the dead are central to their practice.
- Dukha (Tsaatan) reindeer herders and Tuvans: a different relation to place and to the dead.
- Oirat groups (Torguud, Dörvöd, Zakhchin, Bayad): separate epics and a distinct Buddhist history.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: Muslim teaching on death, memorial feasts and laments.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): Chinese-language scholarship and a different state context.
- The families of those killed in the 1930s repression.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good on epics, chronicles and spirit practice; official data on mortality is fairly open; private attitudes to dying and grief are thinly recorded.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian for old texts; French and Russian for some scholarship; Kazakh and Buryat for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burial sites and sacred mountains; the names of the repressed; suicide; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
