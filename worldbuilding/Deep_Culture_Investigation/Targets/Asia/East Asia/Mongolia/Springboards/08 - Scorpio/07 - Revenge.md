# Mongolia: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore honor or standing, or deter the next wrong; the alternatives are forgiveness, payment, apology and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Khalkha, Oirat and Buryat groups: separate memories of old feuds and different tales of avenging kin.
- Herders against Ulaanbaatar residents: how disputes over land, animals and insult are settled.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: a Turkic blood-debt idiom and a Muslim teaching on forgiveness.
- Buddhist, shamanic and secular households on revenge, karma and forgiveness.
- Men and women, and older and younger generations, on honor and shame.
- Teenagers and school communities on bullying and online payback.
- Families of the repressed and families of those who carried out the repression.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): grievance under another state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in chronicles, epics and curse-blessing folklore; mixed on modern violence and online revenge; thin on private feuding.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian for old texts; Kazakh and Buryat for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of killings and cursing; shamanic harm-doing is a restricted area; state punishment of wrongs belongs to Capricorn, not here; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
