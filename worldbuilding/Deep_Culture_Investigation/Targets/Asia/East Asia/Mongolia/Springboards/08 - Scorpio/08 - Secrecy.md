# Mongolia: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia, Russian for Soviet-era records. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is one of its facets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lamas, shamans and lay people: three holders of restricted knowledge and three rules for sharing it.
- Khalkha, Buryat, Oirat and Darkhad communities, each with its own guarded rites.
- Herders in gers against ger-district residents in fenced yards and apartment tenants.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: a Muslim idea of family confidence.
- Dukha and Tuvans: very small communities where little stays private.
- Gender and sexual minorities against the majority households around them.
- Older generations who lived under the socialist state and younger digital users.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): official secrecy and internal publication.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on the chronicles and on repression-era records; thin on household privacy and on what families keep from one another.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian script for old texts; Russian for Soviet-era files; Kazakh, Buryat and Tuvan for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic lineage knowledge, tantric teaching and sacred-site locations belong to their keepers and are not to be retold; names of informers and the repressed; LGBT respondents' safety; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
