# Mongolia: Death and transformation, domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic; traditional vertical script for older and Inner Mongolian material), Oirat dialects, Buryat, Kazakh (Bayan-Ölgii), Tuvan and Dukha, and Mandarin for Inner Mongolian sources. **Concept in one line:** how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden: death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance, secrets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Khalkha herders against Ulaanbaatar and Darkhan-Erdenet city dwellers: how funerals, money and secrecy differ with way of life.
- Buryat (east, Dornod and Khentii) and Dariganga: shamanic and household inheritance forms differ from Khalkha.
- Oirat groups (Torguud, Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Uriankhai) in the west: separate epics, a separate script tradition and funeral habits.
- Darkhad and Dukha (Tsaatan) reindeer herders in the north: spirit practice and the relation to the dead.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: Muslim burial, memorial feasts and a separate language.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (Chahar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur) as a candidate unit if the lands include them.
- Buddhist lamas and laity, shamanic practitioners, Christian converts, and Soviet-era secular urban families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on chronicles, epics and the 1930s repression; thinner on modern private life such as sex and secrecy; rural practice is documented unevenly.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic); older sources in traditional Mongolian script or Classical Mongolian; Russian for Soviet-era work; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia; Kazakh, Buryat and Tuvan for the minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and burial-site knowledge, remains and sacred places; names and records of the repressed; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
