# Mongolia: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down; loyalty, the trust given back, is researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herders in camp groups (khot ail) against Ulaanbaatar residents: who counts as someone you can rely on.
- Home-district (nutag) networks against professional and school networks.
- Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat and Dariganga groups: different kin and neighbor customs.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: a Muslim framework of oath and hospitality.
- Dukha and Tuvans: very small, tightly bound communities.
- Buddhist, shamanic and secular households on trust in specialists.
- Older and younger generations on institutions, banks and media.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): Mongol-Han trust and guanxi.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good survey series on institutional trust and corruption; thin on interpersonal trust in herding life; chronicles and folklore are rich.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian for old texts; Kazakh and Buryat for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survey microdata may need requests; trust in police and courts is researched under Capricorn, not here; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
