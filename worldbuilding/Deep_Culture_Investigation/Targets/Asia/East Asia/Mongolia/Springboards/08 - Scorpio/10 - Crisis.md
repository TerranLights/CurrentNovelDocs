*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Mobile herders against settled urban and soum-center families: exposure to dzud, cold and smog.
- Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat and Dariganga herding regions, each with a different hazard pattern.
- Western Altai (Bayan-Ölgii, Khovd) against the Gobi south and the northern forest-steppe.
- Kazakh herders and their Muslim neighbors in relief and emigration.
- Dukha and Tuvans in the taiga: disease, pasture and policy pressures.
- Ger-district settlers against apartment residents in Ulaanbaatar.
- Older people who lived through the 1930s and 1990s against younger households.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): the same hazards under another state's response.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on dzud, climate and the 1990s; mixed on the 1930s and personal crises; thin on household coping in private and on suicide and mental health.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Russian for Soviet-era work; Kazakh, Buryat and Tuvan for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and mental-health detail; closed archive files on the 1930s; herders' losses are personal; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
