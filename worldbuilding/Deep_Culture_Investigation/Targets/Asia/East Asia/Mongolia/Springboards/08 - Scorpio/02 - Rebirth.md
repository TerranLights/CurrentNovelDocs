# Mongolia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongol (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Mandarin for Inner Mongolia, English for the missionary and development literature. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist revivalists, new shamanic practitioners, Christian converts and the secular: four routes to a new life after 1990.
- Khalkha herders, Ulaanbaatar ger-district settlers and herders who moved after a dzud.
- Buryat and Darkhad communities where shamanic spirits are central.
- Oirat groups (Torguud, Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin): epic revival and a separate script.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: Islamic revival and a separate language.
- Dukha (Tsaatan) and Tuvans: reindeer herding and its revival.
- Inner Mongolian Mongols (if the lands include them): language and school revival under another state.
- Younger urban musicians and artists against older monastic and elder keepers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the post-1990 religious revival and heritage programs; mixed for personal conversion and recovery; thin for private accounts of addiction recovery.
- **Languages needed:** Khalkha Mongol; Classical Mongolian script for old texts; Kazakh, Buryat and Oirat for minorities; Mandarin for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic initiations and ceremonies; the recognition of incarnations is politically charged; Inner Mongolian language disputes; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
