# Mongolia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Tuvan, Buryat, Chinese, Russian. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha, Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Torguud, Buryat:** each with its own claim to the nation and to its own homeland.
- **Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii:** a Muslim minority with a kin-state elsewhere.
- **Tuvans, Dukha and Darkhad in the north-west and north:** small peoples at the edge.
- **Old Ulaanbaatar households against recent arrivals from the countryside:** insiders and newcomers.
- **Herders against townspeople;** aimag capitals against soums.
- **Inner Mongolia Mongols, Daur, Evenki, Oroqen,** against Han and Hui neighbors, and Mongols living in Han-majority towns.
- **Mongolian migrant workers and students abroad,** and those who returned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich on nationalism and state symbols; mixed on lived belonging; thin on small peoples and returnees. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Mongolian core and Inner Mongolia.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic, traditional script), Chinese, Kazakh, Russian; Tuvan *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ethnic-language policy in Inner Mongolia; discrimination against minorities; advocacy sources need cross-checking; migrants' legal status.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands. Citizenship law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.

## 5. First queries
1. Search NSO census tables for "төрсөн аймаг" and "дотоодын шилжих хөдөлгөөн" (birth aimag; internal migration).
2. Search a library catalog for Bulag, Kaplonski and Diener to see which cover which peoples.
3. Search the UNESCO site for the Naadam dossier and a soum Naadam schedule.
4. Search "нутгийн холбоо" with an aimag name (for example Увс or Говь-Алтай) to find a hometown association.
5. Search "Galsan Tschinag" and "Der blaue Himmel" in a catalog and read the publisher notes and reviews.
