*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families against ger-district and apartment families:** who is present, and where children live in term time.
- **Khalkha, Oirat groups and Buryat:** different lullabies, naming and haircut customs.
- **Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii:** a separate cradle tradition and Kazakh-medium schools.
- **Dukha and Darkhad of the north:** forest and reindeer childhood.
- **Boys and girls:** compare who herds, who stays in school, and who is raised to do what.
- **Inner Mongolia families:** Mongolian-medium against Chinese-medium schooling, and rural against urban.
- **Religious households:** Buddhist, shamanist, Muslim and Christian.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for statistics and education; mixed for folklore and ritual; thin for parents' own accounts of what nurture means. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Mongolian core and Inner Mongolia.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic, traditional script), Chinese, Kazakh, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Child welfare and discipline accounts; Inner Mongolian school-language policy; ritual knowledge held by elders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
