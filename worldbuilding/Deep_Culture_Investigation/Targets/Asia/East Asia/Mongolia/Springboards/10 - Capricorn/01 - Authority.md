# Mongolia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha Cyrillic; traditional script for old texts), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan; Chinese for the Mongol lands in China. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right (including how far it reaches into daily life).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Khalkha herders in different aimags; Ulaanbaatar office workers and ger-district households; former Party families against post-1990 entrants; monastic communities; Oirat peoples with their own princely memory; Buryats; Kazakhs, among whom elders hold a separate role; Dukha and Darkhad communities of the northern taiga; Inner Mongolian Mongols (banner heirs, Party members, herders); Hui and Han residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical authority and politics; mixed for everyday deference (mostly ethnographic); thin in published form for minorities; Inner Mongolian sources are politically managed.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian; Chinese; Kazakh; Buryat; Russian for socialist-era scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** repression of the 1930s and 1960s Inner Mongolian purges; present-day politics in Inner Mongolia; ritual knowledge held by shamans and lamas. The nation's exact post-war lands remain to be settled, and everything above is written for the core Mongol lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
