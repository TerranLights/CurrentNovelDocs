## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Khalkha herders and Ulaanbaatar households (distance from courts); Oirat peoples with their own 1640 code heritage; Kazakhs (biy tradition, Islam); Buryats and Darkhad (spirit-linked justice); Dukha reindeer herders; Buddhist families against shamanist and Muslim ones; descendants of purge victims and of those who carried it out; Inner Mongolian herders and Han settlers under Chinese courts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal texts and the purge history; mixed for everyday attitudes; thin in English for oral peacemaking among minorities. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the Mongolian core.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian, Chinese, Kazakh; traditional script for archive papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the purge memory involves living families; Inner Mongolian protest and rights material is political and often one-sided; shamanic and spirit-justice practice may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search legalinfo.mn for the rehabilitation (цагаатгах) laws and note dates.
2. Open the latest National Human Rights Commission annual report and list fair-trial findings.
3. Search library catalogs for "Халх журам" and "Их цааз" translations.
4. Search news.mn and ikon.mn for "мал хулгай" court stories.
5. Search "Kaplonski lama question trials" for sources on the 1937 court procedures.
