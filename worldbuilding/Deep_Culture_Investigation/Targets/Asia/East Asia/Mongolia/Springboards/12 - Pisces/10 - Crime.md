# Mongolia: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for chronicles); Kazakh (Cyrillic) for Bayan-Ölgii; Buryat and Oirat forms; Russian for Soviet-era and pre-1917 travel sources. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. **Law:** no law research here; what the law says and what it is for belong to the Libra and Capricorn sheets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding districts against Ulaanbaatar and the ger districts:** livestock theft against urban gangs and fraud.
- **Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Dukha and Darkhad:** local codes of shame and retaliation.
- **Officials and the powerful against ordinary households:** corruption as crime.
- **Youth and elders;** **monastic and lay;** **families of the repressed.**
- **Mining zones and border towns.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on official statistics and corruption surveys; thin on shame and informal sanction. The exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; Russian; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** records involve living victims and perpetrators; political-repression files touch living families; nationalist-group material must be read as discourse, never as a statement about any ethnic group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the crime tables in the latest NSO statistical yearbook and note the offense categories.
2. Search Mongolian press for "мал хулгай" with an aimag name, then for how thieves are described.
3. Look up IAAC and Asia Foundation corruption survey wording on what respondents call "corruption".
4. Compare гэм, нүгэл, ичгүүр and ёс бус in Tsevel's dictionary.
5. Search the library for Ossendowski and a Khovd history of Ja Lama and note the differences between the two accounts.
