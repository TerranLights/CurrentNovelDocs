# Mongolia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for chronicles and Qing-era documents); Kazakh (Cyrillic) for Bayan-Ölgii; Oirat, Buryat and Tuvan forms; Manchu, Chinese and Persian sources in translation for the pre-modern layer. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, an enemy, illness, death), including release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha and Oirat** (Dörvöd, Torguud, Zakhchin, Bayad): different histories of Qing conquest.
- **Buryat, Darkhad, Dukha, Tuvan;** **Kazakh Muslims** against Buddhist communities.
- **Herders who lost animals and migrated;** **former party members and officials;** **monastics and lay people.**
- **The dying and their families;** **men and women;** **generations.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on political history and war; thin on private surrender such as illness, letting go and quitting. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the header carries China as well as Mongolia); this sheet is written for the Mongolian core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; English; some Chinese and Manchu for Qing sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life records and 1930s survivor families; claims that a people is "fatalistic" are to be recorded as claims and tested, never adopted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
