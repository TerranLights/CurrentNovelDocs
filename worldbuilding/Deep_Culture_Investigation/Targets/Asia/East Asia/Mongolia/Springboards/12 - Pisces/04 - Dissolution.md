# Mongolia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for early texts); Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan and Dukha for minority material; Russian for Soviet-era sources; French for some ethnography. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the loss of the self's edges in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha herders against ex-collective workers against ger-district newcomers:** three experiences of one dissolution.
- **Buryat, Darkhad, Dukha and Tuvan:** shamanic trance in local forms.
- **Kazakh of Bayan-Ölgii:** emigration and a different religious frame.
- **Monks, shamans and the secular:** different routes to losing the self.
- **Marriage cohorts and generations;** **elders with failing memory.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on shamanism, collectivization and language shift; thin on dementia and on private experiences of self-loss. The exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; Russian; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic invocations and trance rites are restricted knowledge in some communities; alcohol and divorce touch living families; neo-shamanic books are not the communities' account.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
