# Mongolia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for religious and chronicle sources); Kazakh (Cyrillic) for Bayan-Ölgii; Buryat, Oirat, Tuvan and Dukha forms; English for agency and ethnographic literature. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it (worry and anxiety are its diffuse forms).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders and city dwellers:** dzud, plague and storms against smog, crowding and crime.
- **Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat, Darkhad, Dukha, Tuvan and Kazakh:** each with its own fear figures and customs.
- **Monastics, shamans, healers and doctors:** different diagnoses of fright.
- **Children, adults and the old; men and women; families of the repressed.**
- **Mining districts and areas near sacred sites.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on disaster and health agency material; thin on private anxiety and how fear is displayed. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the header carries China as well as Mongolia); this sheet is written for the Mongolian core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; English; French for one ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Great Repression accounts involve living families; spirit-owner beliefs and fright cures belong to communities; legend material is not evidence of any creature.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
