# Mongolia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for Qing-era records); Kazakh (Cyrillic) for Bayan-Ölgii; Buryat, Oirat, Tuvan and Dukha forms; Russian and Chinese for border and treaty material. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders in camps (*хот айл*), ger-district dwellers and apartment residents:** three spatial arrangements of privacy.
- **Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat, Dukha, Darkhad and Tuvan; Kazakh of Bayan-Ölgii:** language, religion and kin lines.
- **Border towns** (Altanbulag, Zamyn-Üüd, Tsagaannuur, Khankh) against the capital.
- **Daughters-in-law and in-laws; generations;** **women and men;** **mixed families** (Mongol with Russian, Chinese or Korean kin).
- **Monastic and lay;** **migrants returning from abroad.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on pasture, planning and border-trade reports; thin on personal and bodily boundaries. The exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; Russian; Chinese for treaty and border material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and national-identity talk touches live tensions; the Ikh Khorig and other sacred precincts are restricted; family-violence data involves survivors.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

1. Search the UNESCO World Heritage page for Burkhan Khaldun and read the zoning section.
