# Mongolia: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for older verse and chronicles); Kazakh (Cyrillic); Buryat and Oirat forms; Persian and Chinese chronicles in translation for the pre-modern layer. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders, ger-district residents and apartment dwellers:** different leisure and drink.
- **Men and women;** **youth;** **ex-collective workers and the post-1990 generation.**
- **Kazakh Muslims of Bayan-Ölgii** against Buddhist Khalkha and Oirat communities (to be tested, not assumed).
- **Miners, truckers and other mobile workers;** **monastics and lay Buddhists.**
- **Buryat and Dukha of the north,** where small-scale data are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on alcohol and mining; thin on private fantasy, games and reading. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the header carries China as well as Mongolia); this sheet is written for the Mongolian core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script for old verse; Kazakh; English for agencies; Persian chronicles in translation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction and treatment records involve living people; stereotypes about national drinking must be tested against sex, age and place, never assumed.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
