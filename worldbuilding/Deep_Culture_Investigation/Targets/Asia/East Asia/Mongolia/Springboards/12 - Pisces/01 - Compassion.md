# Mongolia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Khalkha Mongolian (Cyrillic; classical Mongol script for pre-modern sources); Kazakh (Cyrillic) for Bayan-Ölgii; Buryat and Oirat forms; Russian for Soviet-era welfare material. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist Khalkha herders against Ulaanbaatar ger-district residents:** different networks of neighbors, kin and charities.
- **Kazakh of Bayan-Ölgii:** alms and hospitality under an Islamic frame.
- **Buryat, Darkhad and Dukha of the north; Oirat peoples of the west:** their own care for the sick, animals and strangers.
- **Lamas, shamans, Christian converts and the secular:** different sources of the duty to help.
- **Elders against the post-1990 generation; women's and men's giving.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on charities, welfare and disaster records; thin on everyday attitudes toward pity; the later research must draw them from proverbs, fiction and ethnography. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (the header carries China as well as Mongolia); this sheet is written for the Mongolian core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical Mongol script; Kazakh; Russian for older welfare files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children and illness in crowdfunding posts; mission charities' conversion questions are contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Mongolian Google and Facebook for "тусламж хэрэгтэй" and "хандив" posts, and note the illnesses and amounts named.
2. Open the NSO and ministry pages for Хүүхдийн мөнгө and social-welfare benefits.
3. In a Mongolian dictionary, compare нигүүлсэл, энэрэл, өрөвдөх and хөөрхий.
4. Search the dzud appeals of 2009-10 on the IFRC site, then the Mongolian Red Cross.
5. Look up Damdinsüren's text in a library catalog and read the first pages for the pity vocabulary.
