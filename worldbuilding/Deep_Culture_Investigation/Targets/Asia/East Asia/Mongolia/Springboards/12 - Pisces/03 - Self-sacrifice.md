## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mothers, fathers, eldest children and the elderly:** who is described as giving.
- **Migrants and the households that stayed behind** (Korea, Czechia, Kazakhstan, Ulaanbaatar from the countryside).
- **Khalkha herders, Buryat, Oirat, Darkhad and Dukha:** offerings and vows differ; **Kazakh Muslims.**
- **Buddhist monks and nuns, shamans, soldiers and veterans, political-persecution families.**
- **Rural and urban households; generations.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on state honors, wars and memorials; thin on private household sacrifice, where the research must lean on songs, interviews and time-use data. The exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; classical script; Kazakh; Russian; English for the ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** persecution-era sacrifices and soldiers' deaths involve living families; shamanic offering is part of restricted ritual knowledge.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the presidential and ministry sites for Алдарт эх and Эхийн алдар honors.
2. Search the National Library catalog for *Эрдэнийн товч* and read the oath and loyalty passages for vocabulary.
3. Look for NSO and UN Women time-use tables, split by sex.
4. Search Mongolian press for "1990 оны өлсгөлөн" (the 1990 hunger strike) and note which accounts are first-person.
5. Query "Mongolian migrant workers Korea remittances" in a library database for ethnographies.
