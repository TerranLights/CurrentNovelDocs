# Mongolia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan; Chinese for Inner Mongolia; Russian for older studies. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?", across personal, family, ethnic, regional, national and religious identity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha, Oirat (Dörvöd, Torguud, Bayad, Zakhchin), Buryat, Dariganga, Barga:** own dialects, dress and descent stories.
- **Kazakh, Tuvan, Dukha:** Turkic-language groups with their own self-names.
- **Herders and city residents:** the homeland versus the ger district.
- **Lamas, shamans, Muslims, Christians:** religious identity layered on ethnic.
- **Inner Mongolian Mongols (Chahar, Khorchin, Ordos, Daur, Evenki):** identity under the Chinese classification.
- **Mixed Mongol-Han families and Hui and Han residents:** hybrid and edge cases.
- **Diaspora generations:** first against later.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in chronicles and symbols; mixed for surveys; thin for private family genealogy.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (both scripts), Chinese, Russian, Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ultranationalism and anti-neighbor sentiment; Inner Mongolian identity under state policy; restricted lineage knowledge in religious groups; the exact lands of the target are still to be settled (the header carries Mongolia and China), so this sheet is written for the Mongolian core and the Mongolic peoples of the Chinese side.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
