# Mongolia: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Oirat, Buryat, Chinese for Inner Mongolia. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others (speaking up, saying no, standing one's ground, claiming credit, complaining, asking).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha herders against Ulaanbaatar office workers:** the same words in different settings.
- **Oirat and Buryat communities:** separate oral genres and dialect terms for speech.
- **Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii:** the aitys tradition and Kazakh-language norms.
- **Women and men, elders and youth:** who may speak first and last.
- **Inner Mongolian Mongols:** speech inside Chinese institutions.
- **Mongolian migrants abroad (South Korea, Czechia):** assertion in a second-language workplace.
- **Lamas and shamans:** religious norms on speech, humility and pride.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern texts and oral genres; mixed for modern surveys; thin for conversation records and complaint data.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (both scripts), Kazakh for aitys, Chinese for Inner Mongolia; Russian for older scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political protest accounts; Inner Mongolia media is censored; the exact lands of the target are still to be settled (the header carries Mongolia and China), so this sheet is written for the Mongolian core with Mongolic and other pointers from the Chinese side.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. In the Mongolian proverb collections, list every entry containing үг, хэл or дуу and sort by praise or warning.
2. Search "Naadam бөхийн цол" and "засуул" in video archives to find who announces and what they say.
3. Search the State Great Khural site for plenary transcripts and note the forms of address.
4. Search Kazakh-language media for "айтыс Баян-Өлгий" and list recorded contests.
5. Pull the Sant Maral or World Values items on voice and trust for the Mongolian wave.
