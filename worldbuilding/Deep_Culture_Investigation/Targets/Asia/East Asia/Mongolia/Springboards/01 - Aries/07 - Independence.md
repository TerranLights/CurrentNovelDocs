## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pastoral herders and ger-district households:** self-provisioning in different forms.
- **Gobi, steppe and mountain herders:** how risk and distance shape reliance on others.
- **Kazakh, Dukha and Buryat households:** differing household economies.
- **Miners and migrant workers abroad:** wage and remittance dependence.
- **Elders, pensioners and welfare recipients:** the state as a source of help.
- **Women-headed households:** independence by necessity or choice.
- **Inner Mongolian herders and Xinjiang Oirat Mongols:** state resettlement and subsidy frames.
- **Monastic communities:** an older model of shared support.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on household economics and state dependence; mixed on attitudes; thin on emotional independence.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (both scripts), Chinese, Russian; Kazakh for Bayan-Ölgii.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relations with the two neighbors and with the Chinese state are politically live; Inner Mongolian resettlement is sensitive; the exact lands of the target are still to be settled (the header carries Mongolia and China), so this sheet is written for the Mongolian core with Mongolic pointers from the Chinese side.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
