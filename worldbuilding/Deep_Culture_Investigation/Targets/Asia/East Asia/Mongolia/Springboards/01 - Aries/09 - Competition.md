## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha wrestlers, trainers and child riders, against Oirat and Buryat contest traditions:** differing games and rules.
- **Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii:** kokpar, baiga, aitys and eagle contests.
- **Tuvan and Dukha communities:** smaller contests and reindeer culture.
- **Herders and urban professionals:** prizes for herds against prizes for scores and sales.
- **Women, men, children:** who may enter which contest.
- **Athletes abroad (sumo, judo):** competition away from home.
- **Inner Mongolian and Daur groups:** contests under the Chinese state.
- **Lamas and monasteries:** a tradition of debate as contest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on sport and games; mixed on school and business rivalry; thin on envy and sibling rivalry.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (both scripts), Kazakh, Chinese, Japanese for sumo; Russian for older sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** match-fixing, doping or race-safety controversies, if any, need careful sourcing; the exact lands of the target are still to be settled (the header carries Mongolia and China), so this sheet is written for the Mongolian core with Mongolic pointers from the Chinese side.
