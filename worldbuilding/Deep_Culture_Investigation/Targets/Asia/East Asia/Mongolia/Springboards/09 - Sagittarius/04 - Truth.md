# Mongolia: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Buryat, Oirat, Kazakh, Tuvan; Russian and Chinese for older and outside accounts; English. **Concept in one line:** what is so, how a culture decides it, whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Khalkha, Buryat, Oirat, Kazakh and Tuvan communities and their versions of the past; monks, shamans, doctors and scientists as sources of truth; the generation raised on socialist schooling and those after; Ulaanbaatar's social-media users against rural audiences who rely on radio; families of the repressed; Inner Mongolia comparisons, where the nation's lands and its textbooks meet another state's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on chronicles and on memory politics in English; thinner on how ordinary people check a claim today. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script for old texts); Russian, Chinese, Persian for outsiders' accounts (translations exist); Kazakh and Buryat for minority press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims over the Mongol past are contested with neighbors; 1930s memory is raw; press ownership is a live dispute; lineage knowledge held by elders and healers.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
