# Mongolia: Domain and folkways, belief and philosophy (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, in Cyrillic; the traditional vertical script for older and revived texts), Oirat dialects, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan (Dukha), English, Russian and Chinese for older scholarship. **Concept in one line:** what is true, where is meaning found: faith, philosophy, higher learning and long journeys, and the religious and freedom ways that come with them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Khalkha core against Oirat peoples (Dörvöd, Bayad, Torguud, Zakhchin, Olet, Myangad and others) of the west; Buryats of the northeast; Darkhad and Dukha of Khövsgöl; Tuvans and Uriankhai; Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii and Khovd; Muslim Khoton in Uvs; urban Ulaanbaatar and ger-district newcomers against herders; Gobi, Altai, Khangai and Khentii homelands; Christian converts; lamas against shamans as specialists; older people who lived through the closures against the young. Whether the lands of Inner Mongolia come into the target is open.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Buddhism and shamanism in English-language ethnography; mixed on Islam and Christianity; thin on how ordinary people talk about meaning outside faith. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic essential); Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat, Tuvan for regional work; Russian for Soviet-era sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and clan ritual knowledge is held within lineages; some sites are not to be visited or photographed; the memory of closure and killings of clergy is raw; Chinggis Khan's cult is politically live.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
