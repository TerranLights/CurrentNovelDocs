## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Herder families and urban families; boarding-school children and day students; Khalkha, Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan, Dukha and Oirat communities and their languages of instruction; monastic and secular students; women and men in higher education (a pattern to check, not assume); graduates of Soviet-bloc universities, post-1990 graduates and those schooled abroad; teachers and university staff. Whether the lands of Inner Mongolia come into the target is open.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on official statistics and donor reports; mixed on monastic and minority education; thin on how ordinary people rank the educated. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script); Russian; English; Kazakh and Buryat for minority schooling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** teachers' pay and corruption claims in admissions are live disputes; monastic lineage knowledge is held by teachers; school data on poor families should be read with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
