# Mongolia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, Cyrillic; traditional script for older texts), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat, Tuvan; Russian and Korean for mission and older sources; English. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth (a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally) is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against change; whether the word fits secular and political certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Mainstream Khalkha Buddhists and the central monastery against smaller or newer lineages; shamanic organizations and their critics, and Buryat, Darkhad and Dukha practitioners; evangelical, Pentecostal, Latter-day Saint and Jehovah's Witness communities; Kazakh Muslims and Khoton Muslims; ultranationalist groups against mainstream nationalism; socialist-era orthodoxy against its post-1990 critics; urban youth and rural elders. This sheet expects thin data; the concept may fit only some of these. Whether the lands of Inner Mongolia come into the target is open.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Few studies apply the word "fundamentalism" to Mongolia; most data is about related themes (authenticity, new churches, nationalism). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian; Kazakh, Buryat, Korean and Russian for different strands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations of "extremism" and "sect" can be political; mission reports are advocacy; some small groups are wary of outsiders; violence is rare and must not be overstated.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
