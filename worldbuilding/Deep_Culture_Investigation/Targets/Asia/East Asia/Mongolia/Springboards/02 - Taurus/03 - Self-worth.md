# Mongolia: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, Cyrillic), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Chinese (Inner Mongolia), Russian for older studies. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha** herders against **Oirat** (Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin) and **Buryat** groups.
- **Kazakhs** of Bayan-Ölgii: a separate honor vocabulary.
- **Dukha** and **Darkhad**: small groups whose worth is spoken of by others as well as by themselves.
- **Ulaanbaatar ger-district migrants** against long-time urban residents.
- **Men and women** in herding, mining and city jobs.
- **Monks, shamans and wrestlers**: holders of titles.
- **Inner Mongolian** Mongols, if included.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Mixed. Rich on honor and titles (Naadam, state orders) and on elite texts; thin on private self-esteem; clinical data exists but is sensitive. The exact lands the nation carries are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian, Kazakh, Buryat, Russian, Chinese; German for Finke.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Suicide and mental-health data need careful handling. The standing of Inner Mongolian Mongols is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
