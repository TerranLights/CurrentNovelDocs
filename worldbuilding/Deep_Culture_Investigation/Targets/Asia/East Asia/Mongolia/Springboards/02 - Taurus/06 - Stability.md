# Mongolia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, Cyrillic; traditional script for old chronicles), Kazakh, Buryat, Oirat dialects, Chinese, Russian. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mobile herders** against **aimag-center** and **Ulaanbaatar** residents.
- **Households that kept herds** across generations against **those that left** after zud or 1990.
- **Khalkha** with the **Oirat**, who recall a separate old state, and the **Buryat**.
- **Kazakhs** of Bayan-Ölgii, with their own winter and summer camps and a pull toward Kazakhstan.
- **Dukha** and **Darkhad**: small groups under strain.
- **Monastic** communities rebuilt after the purges.
- **Mining-boom** towns against settled farm-areas of the north.
- **Inner Mongolian** herders, if included.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Mixed. Rich on migration, prices and politics; mixed for continuity of household routine; scholars disagree about long-run stability, so check the model each source assumes. The exact lands the nation carries are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian (Cyrillic, with traditional script for chronicles), Kazakh, Russian, Chinese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** The 1930s purges and religious suppression carry family grief; ask carefully. Inner Mongolian settlement policy is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
