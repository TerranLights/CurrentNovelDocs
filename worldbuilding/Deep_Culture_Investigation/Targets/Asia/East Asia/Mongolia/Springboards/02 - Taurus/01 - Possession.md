# Mongolia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Khalkha, Cyrillic), Buryat, Oirat dialects, Kazakh, Tuvan. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and what they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nomadic herding households** against **settled** aimag-center and **Ulaanbaatar apartment** residents: what can be carried and what is stored.
- **Ger-district** households with fenced plots.
- **Buddhist laity and monks** against **Darkhad and Daur shamanic** families: objects with spirits.
- **Kazakhs** of Bayan-Ölgii: chests and felts of their own.
- **Dukha** reindeer herders and **Oirat** (Dörvöd, Torguud) herders of the west and Altai.
- **Dzud-hit** herders against herders spared.
- **Inner Mongolian** Mongols, if included.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Mixed; rich on ger life and museums, thin on urban clutter and hoarding. The exact lands the nation carries are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Mongolian lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian; Kazakh and Russian for Bayan-Ölgii and older studies; Chinese for Inner Mongolia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Spirit vessels and shamanic objects may be private. Objects of the dead need care: ask who may speak of them.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Mongolian dictionary for эд зүйл, эд өлгийн зүйл and дурсгал and note their sense ranges.
2. Read Humphrey's ger essay and Empson's chapters on things for the vocabulary scholars use.
3. Search NSO crime tables for мал хулгай by aimag.
4. Search the National Museum's catalog for "эд өлгийн" entries and Xiongnu grave goods.
5. Search "ongon" with Pedersen and Buyandelger for objects as bearers of spirit.
