## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Khalkha of the central aimags against Oirat groups of the west (Dörvöd, Bayad, Torguud, Zakhchin): dialect and epic tradition.
- Buryats of the northeast and Dariganga of the southeast: distinct varieties.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii: a Turkic-speaking school and media system.
- Tuvans, Dukha and Darkhad of Khövsgöl: small languages, taiga travel.
- Urban Ulaanbaatar ger districts against rural bag and sum.
- Southern Mongol groups (Chahar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur), Hui and Han neighbors, in a Chinese-language schooling setting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Khalkha language and folklore; mixed for minorities; thin in English for neighbor customs.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; traditional script for old sources; Kazakh; Russian for Soviet-era works; Chinese for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and ritual speech; Inner Mongolian language politics is contested and some sources are restricted. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
