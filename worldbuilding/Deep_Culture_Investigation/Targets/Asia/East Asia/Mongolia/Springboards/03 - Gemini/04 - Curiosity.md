*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Scholars, students and urban professionals against herders: different stances on asking and exploring.
- Buddhist lamas, shamans and diviners, as holders of restricted knowledge.
- Kazakh eagle hunters and Bayan-Ölgii residents, who meet many visiting questioners.
- Dukha and Darkhad of Khövsgöl, host communities for ethnotourism.
- Khalkha, Oirat and Buryat speakers, whose proverbs on asking may differ.
- Mongolian speakers in the south, where outside questioners work under different limits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for foreign expeditions, museums and film; thin for everyday curiosity and nosiness.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; traditional script; Kazakh; Russian for Soviet-era science; Chinese for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and monastic knowledge; consent in tourism and research; fieldwork limits in the southern lands. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
