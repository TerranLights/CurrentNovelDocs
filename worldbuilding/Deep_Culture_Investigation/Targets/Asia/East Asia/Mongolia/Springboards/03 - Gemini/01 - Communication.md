# Mongolia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic; traditional script), Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan, Oirat varieties; Chinese for the southern lands. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what a culture believes good communication is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herders of the Khangai and Gobi against Ulaanbaatar office and ger-district residents: distance, phone coverage, setting of talk.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii (aitys, Kazakh press) and Buryats: distinct speech traditions.
- Buddhist clergy and monastic debate against secular speakers.
- Young messenger-app users against elders using older formulas of address.
- Mongolian speakers in the southern lands, where Chinese is the other language of public life.
- Deaf people and Mongolian Sign Language users.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal custom and telecom; mixed for ordinary conversation; thin in English for informal talk and silence.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; traditional script for old sources; Kazakh for Ölgii; Chinese for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual and shamanic speech; politically sensitive speech in the southern lands. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
