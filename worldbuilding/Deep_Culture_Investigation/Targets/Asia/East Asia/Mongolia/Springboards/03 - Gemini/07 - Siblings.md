# Mongolia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan, Russian, Chinese. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Herding families (animals divided among sons; the youngest staying) against city families.
- Khalkha, Oirat, Buryat and Darkhad customs on heirs and seniority.
- Kazakhs of Bayan-Ölgii, with their own youngest-son and elder-brother rules.
- Families of migrant workers abroad, where older siblings may carry the home.
- Girls and boys in rural school and herding, against the urban middle class.
- Mongolian families in the south under different family-planning rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on kinship and succession in older sources; thin on modern sibling relations.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian Cyrillic; traditional script; Kazakh; Chinese for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family violence; inheritance disputes. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
