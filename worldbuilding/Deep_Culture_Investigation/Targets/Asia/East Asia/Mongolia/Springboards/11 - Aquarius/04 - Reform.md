# Mongolia: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic and traditional script), Oirat, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan, Chinese for Inner Mongolia, Russian for Soviet-era material. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha reformers** of the capital against **Oirat and Buryat** voices; **Buddhist clergy** against **shamanic practitioners**.
- **Herders** against **city and ger-district residents**; those who gained from privatization against those who lost.
- **Mining-area communities** and **provincial towns** against Ulaanbaatar.
- **Kazakhs**, **Tuvan Dukha**, **Khoton**: how reforms reached them.
- **Inner Mongolia**: banners under different reforms (pastoral, farming, urban); Mongol, Han and Hui accounts; Daur and Ewenki.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1990s and for party history; mixed for earlier reform; thin for minority voices.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian, Russian for Soviet-era sources, Chinese for Inner Mongolia; Kazakh and Tuvan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1930s purges; the Inner Mongolia campaigns of the 1960s and later language and schooling disputes; ongoing political quarrels over corruption and mining; some ritual and clan matters. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the core Mongolian lands plus Inner Mongolia.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
