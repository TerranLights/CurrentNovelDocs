# Mongolia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Mongolia, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mongolian (Cyrillic, traditional script), Oirat dialects, Buryat, Kazakh, Tuvan, Chinese. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the culture weighs the group against the person, draws "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khalkha** against the **Oirat** peoples (Dörvöd, Bayad, Zakhchin, Torguud, Myangad, Uriankhai): older distinct identities and histories.
- **Buryat** and **Darkhad**; **Tuvan Dukha**; **Kazakhs** (Abak Kerei and other tribal sections) and **Khoton**.
- **Urban** and **rural**; ger-district residents and apartment dwellers; elders and the post-1990 generation.
- **Party and union members** against non-members; nationalist street groups.
- **Inner Mongolia**: Chahar, Ordos, Khorchin, Barga, Daur, Ewenki, with Han and Hui neighbors; different categories (minzu).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on clan and empire history; mixed on present-day voluntary groups; thin for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian; Kazakh, Buryat, Tuvan; Chinese; Russian for older studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** purge-era records and memories of collective punishment; nationalist and anti-Chinese groups (violence and hate); Inner Mongolia's political history and campaigns. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the core Mongolian lands plus Inner Mongolia.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. In the 2020 census tables, list the ethnic groups (ястан) and their provinces.

4. Read the Secret History's passages on anda and the tribe lists in Rashid al-Din.
5. Look up inclusive and exclusive "we" in a Mongolian grammar.
