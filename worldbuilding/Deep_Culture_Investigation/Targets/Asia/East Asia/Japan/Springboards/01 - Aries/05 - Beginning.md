# Japan: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan and other Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** (own origin stories; the settler founding story told about them) and **Ryukyuans** (reversion in 1972).
- **Hokkaido settler families** against **old villages** of Honshu and Kyushu, where houses and shrines claim long continuity.
- **Shrine and temple families** against **salaried urban households** on how the New Year and April are kept.
- **Disaster-area communities** (Tohoku, Kobe, Noto, Kumamoto) with different recoveries.
- **I-turn and U-turn migrants** against long-time residents of depopulating areas.
- **Zainichi and Nikkei families** with their own arrival stories, and **new immigrants**.
- **Older and younger cohorts** on divorce, job change and starting over.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Japanese on rituals, statistics and press coverage; mixed for Ainu and Ryukyuan origin material (some in translation); thin for informal starts such as vanishing.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese and Chinese-style texts for *Kojiki* and chronicles; Ainu and Okinawan for primary material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** imperial ritual and the founding-day debate; Ainu sacred stories (some are restricted, so ask who may share them); the vanishing and suicide topics; disaster grief.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands. The post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheet is written for the core lands.
