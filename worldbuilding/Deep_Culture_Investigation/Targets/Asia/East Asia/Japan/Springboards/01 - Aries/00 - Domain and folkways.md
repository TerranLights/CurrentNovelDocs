# Japan: Domain and folkways, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (nihongo, 日本語); Ainu (Ainu itak, アイヌ語); Ryukyuan languages, above all Okinawan (Uchinaaguchi, ウチナーグチ); Korean for the Zainichi community. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here, and how it asserts itself: the self, body, appearance, beginnings, initiative, and the naming ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** of Hokkaido (and diaspora in the Kantō region): own language, naming and body marks; Ainu living in cities may differ from those in Hokkaido's Ainu-majority villages.
- **Ryukyuans (Okinawa, Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama, Yonaguni):** distinct languages and naming; also separate Amami from Okinawa Island.
- **Hokkaido settlers' descendants** against the older core: the frontier question.
- **Regions of Honshu and Kyushu:** Tōhoku, Kantō, Kansai, Chūgoku, Shikoku, Kyushu; dialects of the first person differ.
- **Zainichi Koreans and Chinese-descended residents:** the alias (*tsūmei*) question; generational split.
- **Burakumin descendants:** a stigmatized-status group; handle with care (see flags).
- **Newer residents:** Nikkei Brazilians and Peruvians, Filipinos, Vietnamese, and *hafu* people of mixed parentage.
- **Class and trade lines:** temple and shrine families, hereditary performance houses, artisans, farm families, salaried urban workers.
- **Gender and sexual minorities** and **disabled people's** self-advocacy groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the mainland majority in Japanese; mixed for Ainu and Ryukyuans (some memoir and museum sources, less survey data); thin in English for smaller minorities and for older people's own words.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical Japanese for Heian and Edo texts); Ainu and Okinawan for primary material; Korean for Zainichi sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burakumin status and family-register exposure (a past scandal over published place lists, *check*); Ainu human remains and sacred knowledge; Ryukyuan *yuta* and *noro* ritual knowledge; do not treat any single group as one voice.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands. The post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheet covers the core lands of Honshu, Hokkaido, Shikoku, Kyushu and the Ryukyus.
