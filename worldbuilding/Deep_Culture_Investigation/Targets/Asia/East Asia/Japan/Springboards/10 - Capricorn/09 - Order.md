# Japan: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Uchinaaguchi and other Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** and **Ryukyuan** peoples: different pictures of cosmic and village order.
- **Neighborhood associations** in old villages against **anonymous apartment districts.**
- **Festival towns** (Kishiwada, Nada, Gion) against **everyday salaryman Tokyo.**
- **Day-laborer quarters** (Kotobuki, San'ya, Kamagasaki) and **the unhoused.**
- **Disaster regions** (Kobe, Tohoku, Kumamoto): how local order was rebuilt.
- **Youth subcultures:** bōsōzoku, street-fashion, Halloween crowds.
- **Okinawa** with U.S. bases against the mainland: disorder attributed to others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on crime statistics, disaster, workplace and household order; mixed on felt order; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan own-words.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for the *Kojiki* and Edo texts; Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for the minority tracks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of the 1923 vigilante killings; homeless people's privacy; sacred groves and rites with restricted access; claims that a nation is "orderly" are stereotypes to test, not assume.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
