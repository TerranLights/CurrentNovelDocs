# Japan: Law, the spirit of it (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Uchinaaguchi and other Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of law only; the written letter belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** and **Ryukyuan/Okinawan** communities: own dispute-settling traditions and a contested relation to the state's law.
- **Villages and neighborhoods** with strong informal sanction (*murahachibu* history) against **city anonymity.**
- **Police, prosecutors, bar and bench:** their own sense of what law is for.
- **Yakuza-linked communities** and the groups that police them.
- **Zainichi Koreans, Chinese residents, Nikkei and technical interns:** rules made without their say.
- **Okinawa** against **the mainland** on base-related law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on legal sociology, court surveys, policing and informal enforcement; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan own-terms.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese and kambun for medieval and Edo documents; Ainu for *charanke* sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** detention and confession cases involving named people; yakuza reporting carries risk; wartime and base-related legal disputes are politically charged. **Law note:** this sheet takes spirit only; the written text of statutes belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
