# Japan: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Uchinaaguchi and other Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

English keywords: Japanese obligation, giri ninjō, filial piety, eldercare, family altar, neighborhood association, volunteering, conscription, karōshi, Ainu ancestral rites.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** and **Ryukyuan** peoples: duties to ancestors and to kin groups are organized differently from the Yamato household model.
- **Eldest sons, daughters-in-law and unmarried daughters:** who traditionally carried eldercare and how that has moved.
- **Farming and fishing villages** against **city salaried households:** communal duties against workplace duty.
- **Showa-born** against **Heisei and Reiwa-born** adults.
- **Tohoku disaster zones** and **Kobe 1995:** where volunteer duty was tested.
- **Immigrant and Zainichi households,** and Nikkei returnees: duties split between Japan and another homeland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on care, work and neighborhood ties; mixed on duty as moral language; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan perspectives outside published works.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for Edo texts; Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for primary work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime sacrifice and its glorification; karōshi bereavement; Ainu remains and ritual knowledge; family details in caregiving cases.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
