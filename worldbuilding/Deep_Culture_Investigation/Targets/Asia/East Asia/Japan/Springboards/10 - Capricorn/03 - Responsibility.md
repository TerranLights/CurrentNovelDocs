# Japan: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Uchinaaguchi and other Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** and **Ryukyuan** peoples: the state's responsibility toward them is a live public question.
- **Pollution and disaster communities:** Minamata, Niigata, Tohoku, Fukushima, Kobe, Okawa.
- **Parents against company employees against officials:** each is blamed differently.
- **Women and men:** unequal sharing of household load.
- **Hikikomori and "lost generation" households,** and those labeled non-regular workers.
- **Zainichi Koreans and Chinese,** and Korean victims of 1923: addressees of collective apology.
- **Prefectures:** compare local government answerability after disasters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corporate and disaster blame, war responsibility, and school practice; mixed on personal responsibility as moral language; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan own-word concepts.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for Edo records; Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for the minority tracks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war responsibility, comfort women and the 1923 massacres are heavily contested; pollution victims' health data; individual blame cases involving suicide.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
