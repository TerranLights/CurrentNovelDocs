# Japan: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan and others; *check*); Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** postwar growth-era cohort, the employment-ice-age cohort, the "satori" cohort, today's children.
- **Work and place:** regular and non-regular workers, farmers and fishers, day laborers in yoseba districts, depopulating towns against big cities.
- **Disaster areas:** Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Kobe (1995), the Tōhoku coast (2011), the Noto area (2024), each with its own hope-making.
- **Faiths and practices:** shrine-goers and temple parishioners, Pure Land and Nichiren followers, new religions, Christians.
- **Peoples:** Ainu, Ryukyuan peoples, Zainichi Koreans and Chinese, Nikkei returnees, newer migrants.
- **Despair and support:** people who have used crisis lines, family members of those who died by suicide, and volunteers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, religion and disaster response; thin for private hope and for minority communities in English; "Japan lacks hope" claims in the press are claims to test.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for Pure Land texts); Ainu; Ryukyuan; Korean; Portuguese for Nikkei.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and bereavement (follow safe-reporting norms); atomic-bomb survivors' testimony; disaster victims; the Ryukyu independence debate and North Korea repatriation are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
