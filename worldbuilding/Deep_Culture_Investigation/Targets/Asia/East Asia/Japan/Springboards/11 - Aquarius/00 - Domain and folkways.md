# Japan: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia (the four main islands, Hokkaido's Ainu lands, the Ryukyu/Okinawa chain). **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu (*check* orthography); Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan/Uchinaaguchi, Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama; *check*); Korean for the Zainichi community. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what do they hope for (friends, groups, hopes, humanitarian ideals).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages and towns by region:** Tōhoku, Kansai merchant towns (近江商人), mountain villages, depopulated "marginal" settlements (限界集落), against Tokyo and Osaka.
- **Hokkaido settler lands (屯田兵) against Ainu communities:** two ways of forming a community on the same ground.
- **Okinawa and the Amami, Miyako and Yaeyama islands:** mutual-aid and kin systems of their own.
- **Burakumin communities; Zainichi Koreans (Mindan, Chongryon); Nikkei Brazilians and Peruvians; later migrants and Chinese-heritage communities:** groups built by exclusion or migration.
- **Religious communities:** Shinto parish groups (氏子, 宮座), Buddhist parishioners (檀家), new religions, Kakure Kirishitan groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and folklore, and in Japanese-language scholarship; thin in English for minorities and small islands.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (including old orthography for Edo texts); Ainu; Ryukyuan; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ainu and Ryukyuan ritual knowledge, Kakure Kirishitan practice, buraku identity records (not to be traced to individuals), survivor testimony.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
