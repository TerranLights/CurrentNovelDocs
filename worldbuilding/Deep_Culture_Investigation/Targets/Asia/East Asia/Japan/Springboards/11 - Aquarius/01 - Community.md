# Japan: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan and others; *check*); Korean for Zainichi communities. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest, cause), who recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Neighborhood and place types:** inner-city shopping streets, Edo-descended old quarters, danchi and new towns, suburbs, farming and fishing villages, and mountain hamlets (limit cases of emptied places).
- **Regions:** Tōhoku coast (after 2011), Kansai against Kantō, Kyoto's old merchant quarters, Hokkaido settlements, Okinawa's villages and the outlying islands.
- **Peoples:** Ainu, Ryukyuan peoples, Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei returnees, newer migrant and technical-trainee workers; each makes its own kind of community.
- **Faith communities:** Shinto parish groups, Buddhist parish families, new-religion congregations, Kakure Kirishitan groups.
- **Age:** elders in aging estates and villages against young people in cities and online.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, rural sociology and disaster research; mixed for urban neighborhoods; thin in English for minorities and remote islands.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (modern and older orthography); Ainu; Ryukyuan; Korean; Portuguese for Brazilian-Japanese communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** buraku area lists and family registers (do not trace individuals); the dead of disasters and kodokushi cases need discretion; Ainu and Ryukyuan sacred sites and rites.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
