# Japan: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan and others; *check*). **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sexes and generations:** men and women on showing feelings; elders against the young; the generation that grew up with phones and group chats.
- **Occupations:** doctors, nurses, journalists, scientists, civil servants, firefighters, police, monks, against service and sales staff.
- **Settings:** big-city anonymity against small-town closeness; one-person households against multigenerational homes.
- **Faiths:** Zen and other Buddhist schools, Shinto, Christian communities, new religions.
- **Regions:** Okinawa and the southern islands against the mainland, Kansai and Kantō, Tōhoku.
- **Peoples:** Ainu, Ryukyuan peoples, Zainichi Koreans, newer migrants, each with their own display rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for psychology, religion and literature; mixed for everyday behavior; foreign press descriptions of "Japanese calm" are claims to test, not findings.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for Buddhist and Noh texts); Ainu; Ryukyuan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mourning rites, isolation deaths, Ainu and Ryukyuan ritual practice, private mental-health data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
