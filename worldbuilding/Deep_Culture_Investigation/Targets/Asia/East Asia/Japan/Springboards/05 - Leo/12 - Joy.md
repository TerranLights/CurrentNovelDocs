# Japan: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and some classical); Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean and Portuguese for resident communities. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it, including joy beside grief.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Festival towns** (Tokushima, Aomori, Kyoto, Hakata, Sendai) against **places without major festivals.**
- **Okinawa and the Ryukyu islands** (kachāshī, eisā) against **mainland** regions.
- **Ainu** gatherings and dance (rimse), as Ainu people describe them.
- **Elders, adults and children** in what is called enjoyable.
- **Women and men,** **salaried workers and farmers or fishers.**
- **Zainichi Korean, Nikkei Brazilian and other migrant communities.**
- **Urban singles, families, idol and game fans** (joy found in fandom).
- **Religious communities** (temple and shrine festivals; new religions).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for well-being surveys, festival registers, aesthetic writing and psychology; mixed on how people describe joy in everyday life; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan joy vocabularies in English.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu, Okinawan, Korean, Portuguese for those units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ainu ritual songs and dances (not all are open); depression and suicide data; ecstatic outbreaks in historical records.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
