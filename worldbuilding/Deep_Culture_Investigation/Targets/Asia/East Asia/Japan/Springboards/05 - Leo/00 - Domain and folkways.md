# Japan: Creativity and self-expression, domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia (core Japanese lands: Honshu, Hokkaido, Shikoku, Kyushu, the Ryukyu chain). **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; the Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan and its sisters); Korean and Portuguese for the largest resident minorities. **Concept in one line:** how people shine, play and take pleasure: how much standing out is wanted, how the culture groups play, pride, recognition, honor and joy, and what it makes of pleasure, romance, children, fame, celebration, gambling and sport.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kansai (Osaka, Kyoto)** against **Tokyo and Kanto:** comedy (お笑い) and festival styles are often discussed as different traditions.
- **Tōhoku, Kyushu, Sea of Japan coast:** festival cycles (nebuta, Yamakasa, danjiri) differ by region.
- **Okinawa and the Ryukyu islands (Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama):** separate music, dance and courtship traditions, with their own languages.
- **Ainu of Hokkaido and elsewhere:** own song, dance and rite traditions, and their own contemporary revival.
- **Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei Brazilians and Peruvians, Chinese residents and recent migrants:** festivals and sport as community display.
- **Generations and sexes:** idol fans, otaku, women's groups, youth subcultures (yankī, bōsōzoku); stock images need testing, not assuming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, media, sport and fandom; mixed for pride and showing off (folk sources need care); thin in English for Ryukyuan and Ainu everyday play.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (kanji and kana); Ainu and Okinawan for minority sheets; Korean and Portuguese for the resident minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ainu ritual knowledge held by communities; burakumin and Zainichi identities (never compile lists of people); fan and creator privacy.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Open the latest 社会生活基本調査 leisure tables on e-Stat and note which categories (hobbies, sports, entertainment) the Japanese statisticians themselves use.
2. Search the 文化庁 heritage database for 重要無形民俗文化財 by prefecture to find festival-dense regions.
3. Read Kuki's 『「いき」の構造』 together with one modern commentary, to see which native terms to add to the term list.
4. Search CiNii (ci.nii.ac.jp) for 遊び 文化 and 出る杭 to find Japanese scholarship.
