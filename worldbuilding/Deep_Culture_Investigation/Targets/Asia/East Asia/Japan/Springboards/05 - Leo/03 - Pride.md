# Japan: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan and other Ryukyuan languages (*check*); Korean (Zainichi sources). **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance; including group, craft, family and movement pride.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Craft and trade communities** (shokunin) against **white-collar workers.**
- **Prefectures and home towns:** kenmin-sei claims need testing, not repeating.
- **Okinawans and other Ryukyuan peoples** (Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama) with their own pride vocabularies.
- **Ainu** in Hokkaido and Tokyo.
- **Burakumin** communities and liberation movements.
- **Zainichi Koreans, Chinese, Nikkei Brazilians:** pride in origin against pride in the country of residence.
- **Generations:** postwar, bubble, "lost" cohorts; **women and men**; **LGBTQ** communities.
- **Alumni and school communities** (high-school baseball, university cheer squads).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national-level surveys and on scholarly debate; mixed on personal pride (which may be recorded through modesty norms; check question wording); thin on minorities' own pride vocabularies.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu and Okinawan for those units; Korean for Zainichi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** flag and anthem politics; Okinawan status debates; burakumin identification (never compile lists of persons or neighborhoods); Zainichi names.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Japanese National Character survey item list and note which items concern self-regard, pride or superiority.
2. Search CiNii for 自己肯定感 日本 and 自己高揚 日本人 and read the first review article from each.
3. Read the 水平社宣言 and a short commentary for the pride-movement vocabulary.
4. Search Okinawan newspapers for ウチナーンチュ 誇り to compile everyday usage.
5. Look up 能ある鷹は爪を隠す and 出る杭 in a proverb dictionary (ことわざ辞典) to see which sayings cover pride and modesty.
