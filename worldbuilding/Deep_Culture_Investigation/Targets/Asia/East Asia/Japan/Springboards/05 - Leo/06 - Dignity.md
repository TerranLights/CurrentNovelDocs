# Japan: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard"). Law is Libra's and Capricorn's; rights texts are not researched here.*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; dignity in poverty, work, illness, old age and death.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Day laborers and the homeless** of Kamagasaki and Sanya against **salaried workers** and **the self-employed.**
- **Elders in care homes and at home,** and **their carers** (family, aides, foreign trainees).
- **Patients:** terminal, dementia, and victims of pollution diseases.
- **Disabled people** and their movements.
- **Ainu and Ryukyuan peoples:** ancestral remains, respect for elders and ancestors.
- **Burakumin, Zainichi Koreans, and other residents.**
- **Women and men** in how bearing and composure are expected.
- **Rural villages** against **cities** in funeral and burial practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on end-of-life and care surveys, and on literary and film depictions; mixed for everyday bearing; thin in English for minority dignity vocabularies.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu, Okinawan, Korean for those units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths, patients and victims (names withheld); Ainu and Ryukyuan ancestral remains and ritual knowledge; killings at a care home; burakumin identification (never compile lists).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
