# Japan: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom (including being watched, unseen or anonymous by choice).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elders living alone** in cities and in depopulated villages, against **those in neighborhood associations.**
- **Young adults:** hikikomori, online anonymous accounts, idol fans.
- **Women and men,** and **salaried workers** against the **self-employed.**
- **Ainu and Ryukyuan peoples:** media visibility, school visibility, mainland attention.
- **Mixed-heritage people (hāfu), Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei Brazilians, other foreign residents:** visibly different against invisible by naming.
- **Burakumin and LGBTQ people:** disclosure choices.
- **Disabled people** in public space.
- **Urban against rural** neighborhoods in how people look and are looked at.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for solitary death, loneliness and shut-in surveys, and for theater and ritual presentation; mixed for the gaze of everyday life (essays more than data); thin on minority visibility in English.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu, Okinawan, Korean for those units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths and the bereaved; identification of burakumin and Zainichi people (never compile lists); minors' and fans' online data; outing risks.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
