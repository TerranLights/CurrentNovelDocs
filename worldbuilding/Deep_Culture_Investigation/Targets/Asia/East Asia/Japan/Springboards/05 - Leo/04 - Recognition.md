# Japan: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (kanji and kana); Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title; including recognition withheld and wrongs acknowledged.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Workplace types:** large firms (seniority, seiseki) against small firms, start-ups and craft workshops.
- **Schools and school levels:** ranked schools, club activities (bukatsu), and the honors of each.
- **Women and men:** household and office credit (naijo no kō).
- **Ainu, Ryukyuan peoples, burakumin, Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei:** recognition of standing as a collective question.
- **Local honor systems** by prefecture; **Okinawa** with its own prefectural honors.
- **Artists and craftspeople** inside lineage systems (iemoto) against outsiders.
- **Care and farm workers, and foreign technical trainees:** whose work is acknowledged.
- **Patients and disaster victims:** recognition by certification.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honors lists, surveys and public statements; mixed for everyday credit and praise (idiom and fiction help); thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan recognition practices in English.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu, Okinawan, Korean for minority sheets.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war-related statements and their politics; victims' names (Minamata, leprosy); burakumin identification (never compile lists); persons honored posthumously and families' wishes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Open the 賞勲局 pages and note the categories of decorations and the stated criteria.
2. Search CiNii for 承認欲求 and 承認論 and note the earliest Japanese uses and the philosophers cited.
3. Read one e-Stat or JPC new-employee survey table that asks about evaluation and praise.
4. Search news archives for 縁の下の力持ち 表彰 and 内助の功 to see who is credited and where.
5. Compare three prefectures' 県民栄誉賞 lists to see what regions honor.
