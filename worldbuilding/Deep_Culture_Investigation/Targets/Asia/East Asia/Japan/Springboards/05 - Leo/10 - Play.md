# Japan: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean and Portuguese for resident communities. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, pretending, joking, toying, and digital play.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City, suburban and rural children,** and children with and without cram-school evenings (juku).
- **Girls and boys:** toys, games, who plays where.
- **Kansai** comic play against **Tokyo** forms (*check*).
- **Okinawa, Hokkaido and Tōhoku** folk games and toys by region.
- **Ainu** children's play and music.
- **Zainichi, Nikkei and other migrant children:** games brought from elsewhere.
- **Adult players:** card and board-game circles, pachinko halls, otaku and game communities.
- **Elders:** gateball (*check*) and other late-life games.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the games industry, early-childhood policy and folklore of children's rhymes; mixed for adults' everyday play; thin for minority children's play in English.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu, Okinawan, Korean for those units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minors; Ainu songs and ritual-linked games (do not treat as toys); gambling harm.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Tada's 『遊びと日本人』 table of contents and a review to list the senses of asobi it separates.
2. Compile five regional variants of one rhyme or game (for example janken or kagome-kagome) from the 柳田 and Machida collections.
3. Read the play passages of the 幼稚園教育要領 and note the verbs used.
4. Pull the latest 社会生活基本調査 tables on games and child-play, by age group.
5. Search the CESA game white paper for participation and age tables.
