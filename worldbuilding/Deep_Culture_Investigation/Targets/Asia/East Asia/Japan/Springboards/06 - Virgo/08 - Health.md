# Japan: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu and Ryukyuan for the island peoples. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Urban and rural populations, including island and mountain communities with doctor shortages.
- Okinawa (longevity talk, war trauma, ユタ healing) against the main islands.
- Hokkaido and the Ainu, with their own plant medicine and health conditions.
- Pollution-affected communities: Minamata, Niigata, Toyama, Yokkaichi, Fukushima.
- Hiroshima and Nagasaki survivors and their families.
- Elders in care homes, at home, and living alone.
- Women (caregivers, birth, menopause) and men (checkups, work and sleep).
- Users of kanpō and new-religion healers against hospital-only patients.
- Foreign residents, technical interns and Zainichi Koreans, on access to care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, clinical studies and medical history; mixed for religious and folk healing; thin and sensitive for Ainu and Ryukyuan healing.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for the old texts; Ainu and Ryukyuan *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pollution and radiation victims' testimony; leprosy-isolation survivors; mental illness and suicide; solitary death; Ainu and shaman healing knowledge held by communities.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. e-Stat and MHLW: 国民生活基礎調査 and 患者調査 for the health picture by prefecture and age.
2. Search 苦海浄土 and 水俣病 原田正純 for the best-known case of illness told as harm.
3. Search 脚気 論争 and 石塚左玄 食養 for how the causes of illness were argued.
4. Search 新宗教 病気直し (Hardacre, Davis) for religious healing.
5. Ainu and Okinawan dictionary entries for "sick", "medicine" and "heal" before any claim.
