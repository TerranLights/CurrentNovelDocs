# Japan: Work and service, domain and folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia (Honshu, Hokkaido, Shikoku, Kyushu, the Ryukyu chain and outer islands; the header records no change to the pre-war lands). **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (kanji and kana); Ainu and the Ryukyuan languages for the island peoples. **Concept in one line:** the domain question: what is good work and a well-kept life? Where do work, service, daily routine, health and cleanliness sit in a life, and how does the culture group them?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kansai merchant towns (Osaka, Ōmi) and Tokyo's salaried workforce: different trade traditions and firm cultures.
- Tōhoku and Hokuriku snow country: seasonal migrant labor (出稼ぎ), farming, hunting (マタギ).
- Hokkaido: settler farmers and the Ainu, whose own work and healing words differ.
- Okinawa and the Ryukyus: yuimaaru, moai, Ryukyuan healing and shamans (ユタ).
- Fishing and diving communities (海女, fishery co-ops) and sake-brewing guilds (杜氏), as trade cultures.
- Burakumin communities, whose trades were once classed as polluting (sensitive; see flags).
- Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei returnees and technical-intern workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, labor history, media and folklore; mixed for Ainu and Ryukyuan material; much of it in Japanese only.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (including classical for Edo sources); Ainu and Ryukyuan for the island peoples (*check* with specialists).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burakumin and pollution ideas; Ainu knowledge held by communities and past research ethics; Okinawan memory of the war and base labor; migrant-worker abuse reports. Karōshi involves bereaved families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
