## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kansai (Osaka merchants, abacus and ledgers) and Tokyo (state and university science).
- Rural and fishing communities with folk weather and navigation knowledge, against urban technical workers.
- Women and men on the science and humanities tracks.
- Artisans and engineers (tacit skill, the artisan's "kan") against managers and statisticians.
- Ainu communities (place-name and land knowledge) and Okinawa (the Kumemura tradition).
- Generations: Edo-born scholars, Meiji adopters, postwar firm culture, the post-Fukushima public.
- Foreign-born researchers and students in Japan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for education, science policy, quality management and Edo mathematics; mixed for folk analysis; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan reckoning.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for wasan and Edo thought; Ainu and Ryukyuan *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Fukushima blame and data; official statistics scandals; claims about "Japanese logic" and the national-stereotype risk (name variation, not one voice); Ainu knowledge held by communities.
