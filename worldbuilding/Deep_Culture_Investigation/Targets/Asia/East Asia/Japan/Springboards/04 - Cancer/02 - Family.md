# Japan: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Ainu and Okinawan (*check*); Korean for Zainichi communities; Portuguese for Nikkei returnee families. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Region:** the eastern and western halves of the main island, Tohoku and Kyushu villages, Shikoku, with their house and branch customs.
- **Rural farm families, merchant houses, warrior-descended lines, white-collar urban families:** different reasons to keep or drop the ie.
- **Okinawa and the Amami islands:** munchu and ancestor-tablet succession.
- **Ainu families,** with their own kin terms and lineage marks.
- **Zainichi Korean and Chinese families:** surname, clan seat and ancestral rites.
- **Nikkei returnee, newly arrived migrant and international marriage families.**
- **Single-parent, childless, same-sex-couple and other chosen-family households** (their own words first).
- **Burakumin families** (sensitive).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and scholarship, very rich for the ie; mixed for chosen kin; thin in English for Ainu and Ryukyuan kin systems.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for pre-modern registers; handwriting often needed); Ainu, Okinawan and Korean for the minorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies can expose burakumin descent (do not trace or publish it); family records are private; Ainu lineage knowledge belongs to Ainu families. Surname and family-registry law belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. In SSJDA, open the NFRJ and JGSS codebooks and list their kin-contact and obligation questions.
2. Search CiNii for 本家 分家 同族 and 米村千代 家 存続 to get the main scholarly line.
3. In the ISM 国民性調査 tables, find the family items and see how wording shifted over decades.
4. Search 発言小町 for 義母 / 嫁 and for 実家 墓 to sample family quarrels in their own words.
5. Search the Okinawa Prefectural Library for 門中 and トートーメー to see the island's account against the mainland's.
