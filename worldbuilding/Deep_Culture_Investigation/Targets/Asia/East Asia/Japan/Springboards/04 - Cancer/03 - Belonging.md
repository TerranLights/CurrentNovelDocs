# Japan: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu and Okinawan (*check*); Korean for Zainichi communities; Portuguese for Nikkei returnees. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native-place groups:** Kanto, Kansai, Tohoku and Kyushu locals, with Okinawa's own Uchinanchu identity.
- **Ainu and Ryukyuan peoples,** each with a distinct account of belonging.
- **Zainichi Korean and Chinese, Nikkei returnees, recent migrants, mixed-heritage people:** the border of "Japanese".
- **Company employees, freelancers, non-regular workers, the unemployed.**
- **Students, school non-attenders and hikikomori.**
- **Rural residents against city dwellers; old-established families against newcomers** in a village or neighborhood.
- **Burakumin communities** (sensitive).
- **Religious groups:** shrine and temple parishes, new religions, Christian churches, Muslim communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and public discussion; mixed for lived experience; thin in English for Ainu and Ryukyuan voices. Nihonjinron generalizations need caution.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu and Okinawan; Korean for Zainichi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burakumin identity and community; hate-speech material aimed at Zainichi and other groups (document, do not circulate); Ainu belonging is for Ainu to define; school bullying and suicide cases. Nationality and citizenship law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Cabinet Office loneliness and isolation survey tables and note its categories of groups and relationships.
2. Search the Ministry of Education's non-attendance reports (不登校 統計) and the Cabinet Office 居場所 items together.
3. Search CiNii for 世間 阿部謹也 and 村八分 to start the shunning and seken literature.
4. Search the Ryukyu Shimpo and Okinawa Times for ウチナーンチュ アイデンティティ and 郷友会.
5. Search city 多文化共生 plans (for example Hamamatsu or Kawasaki) for how local governments name newcomers.
