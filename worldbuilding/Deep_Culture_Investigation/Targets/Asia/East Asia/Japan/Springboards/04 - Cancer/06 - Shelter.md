# Japan: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan and Ainu (*check*); Korean, Portuguese and English for migrant and refugee sources. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yoseba districts of Tokyo, Osaka and Yokohama:** aging day laborers and aid groups.
- **Women, youth and families who are not visible:** net-cafe lodgers and people living in cars.
- **Disaster-hit regions:** Tohoku, Kobe, Kumamoto, Noto and nuclear evacuees, with different shelter experiences.
- **Okinawa,** where caves and typhoon shelters have their own histories.
- **Ainu and Ryukyuan peoples** and their seasonal and ceremonial dwellings.
- **Refugee and asylum-seeking communities and foreign workers in company housing.**
- **Temples, shrines, pilgrimage routes and religious groups** that give lodging.
- **Rural villages with empty houses against crowded cities.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official homeless counts and disasters; thin for hidden homelessness and refugee housing; the pre-modern refuge material is scholarly but patchy.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for medieval and Edo sources); Okinawan; English and other languages for refugees.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** locations of shelters for people fleeing violence are kept secret; Battle of Okinawa testimony; homeless people's privacy and dignity; asylum cases. Law on tenancy, asylum and detention belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
