# Japan: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan and Ainu (*check*); Korean; Portuguese for Nikkei families. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Region:** depopulating villages and dying mining towns against dense cities; Tohoku and Fukushima evacuation zones; Okinawa and the island frontier.
- **Children in care, foster families and adoptive families;** care leavers.
- **Elders living alone,** in cities and in marginal villages; men and women.
- **Postwar populations:** returnees from China, mixed-heritage children, war orphans (diminishing in number).
- **Zainichi Korean families split across the Sea of Japan.**
- **Nikkei families in which children were left in Brazil or in Japan.**
- **Ainu families** (little data on abandonment; ask first and treat respectfully).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official child-welfare data and for historical studies; mixed for vanished adults and left-alone elders; thin in English for Ainu and Ryukyuan material. The ubasute and mabiki literature is contested; treat legend and evidence separately.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for the tales; handwriting for local records); Korean; Portuguese; English for occupation-era sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** identity of hatch-surrendered children and their mothers; case files of abused children; lonely deaths; the North Korea return project; the Hanshin and Tohoku evacuees. Child-custody and registry law belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the latest children-in-care survey and list the stated reasons for admission.
2. Search CiNii for 江戸 捨て子 and 子返し to open the Edo-period literature.
3. Search NDL Digital for こうのとりのゆりかご 検証報告 (hatch verification reports).
4. Search the Tokyo Medical Examiner's reports for 孤独死 and NHK Archives for 無縁社会.
5. Search Aozora Bunko for 大和物語 姨捨 and the library catalog for 楢山節考 to read the legend and the novel side by side.
