# Japan: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the culture understands the sibling bond: who counts, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tohoku (ane-katoku customs) against western Japan (youngest-heir customs)** against Kanto: heirs differ by region *check*.
- **Okinawa** (onarigami, tōtōmē, monchū) and **the other Ryukyu islands.**
- **Ainu:** their own kin terms and sibling roles; urban against Hokkaido.
- **Farm and fishing households** against **salaried and urban households.**
- **Only children and small families** against **large families**; older birth cohorts against younger.
- **Zainichi and newcomer households:** siblings as translators and carers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for heirs, household registers and survey data; mixed for the emotional side of siblinghood; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan kin terms in English.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical and old documents for registers and tales); Ainu and Ryukyuan for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Okinawan tōtōmē rules about daughters touch contested gender questions; young-carer and disability material needs care; inheritance law belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
