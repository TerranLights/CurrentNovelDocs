# Japan: The hidden and transcendence, domain and folkways (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia (the main islands, Hokkaido, Kyushu, Shikoku and the Ryukyu chain; the Ainu homeland in Hokkaido is part of the lands). **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ryukyuan languages (Okinawan *Uchinaaguchi*, Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama; *check*); Ainu; Korean for Zainichi communities. **Concept in one line:** how does the culture meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape?

## 2. Where to look first
| Pointer | What it is | What it would give | Reach | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ISM Survey of the Japanese National Character (統計数理研究所 日本人の国民性調査) | Repeated opinion survey since 1953 | Long-run items on religion, belief and outlook; shows whether belief in the unseen shifts by cohort | open web, library | *check* |
| NHK Survey of Japanese Values (NHK 日本人の意識調査) | Five-yearly attitude survey since 1973 | Items on the afterlife, religion and luck to open how the unseen is held and by whom | library, partial web | *check* |
| MHLW Patient Survey (患者調査) and Comprehensive Survey of Living Conditions (国民生活基礎調査) | National health statistics | Counts of treated mental illness and self-rated distress by prefecture; asks who seeks help | open web (e-Stat) | *check* |
| Annual White Paper on Suicide Countermeasures (自殺対策白書) and National Police Agency suicide tables | Official suicide data | Place, age and cause by prefecture; opens how society names its despair | open web | *check* |
| Nichibunken Yōkai and Strange Tale database (怪異・妖怪伝承データベース) | Folklore database of reported uncanny tales | Which spirits and tales belong to which district; a base for regional comparison | open web | *check* |
| Yanagita Kunio, *Tōno monogatari* (1910), and his folklore studies | Founding collection of Tōhoku tales | A folklorist's starting map of ghost, spirit and boundary tales, to be read critically | library, open web (Aozora Bunko) | *check* |
| *Nihon ryōiki* (c. 822) and *Konjaku monogatari shū* (12th century) | Medieval Buddhist tale collections | Pre-modern account of karma, miracle, ghosts and rescue; shows older frames for suffering | library, open web | *check* |
| Kamo no Chōmei, *Hōjōki* (1212), and the *Tannishō* | Classical writing on impermanence and the sinner's refuge | Early voices on suffering, withdrawal and surrender | open web (Aozora Bunko) | *check* |
| Ishimure Michiko, *Kugai jōdo* (1969) | Book on Minamata disease patients | A first-hand account of sickness, injustice and community grief in Kyushu | library | *check* |
| Carmen Blacker, *The Catalpa Bow* (1975), and Margaret Lock's medical anthropology | English-language ethnography of Japanese shamanism and healing | Outsider baselines on mediums, mountain ascetics and illness concepts | library | *check* |

| Hansen's disease sanatoria (e.g., Nagashima Aiseien) and the National Hansen's Disease Museum, Tokyo | Institutions and museum | Survivors' testimony on confinement and its end in 1996 | in person, open web | *check* |

| Chiri Yukie, *Ainu shin'yōshū* (1923), and the National Ainu Museum Upopoy | Ainu god-songs and a national museum | Ainu own forms for spirits and sacred beings | library, in person | *check* |
| Omoro sōshi (16th to 17th century) and Okinawan ethnography of yuta | Ryukyuan sacred songs and folk-medium studies | Ryukyuan routes into spirit, soul and healing; distinct from the mainland | library | *check* |
| Inochi no Denwa (いのちの電話) and other crisis lines | Telephone counseling charity | What callers raise and how helpers speak; a view of present distress | open web (reports) | *check* |

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tōhoku** (Osorezan and its *itako*, the post-2011 coast): a district of spirit-mediumship and recent mass bereavement.
- **Okinawa, Amami, Miyako and Yaeyama:** Ryukyuan soul, medium and priestess practices that differ from the mainland.
- **Ainu of Hokkaido:** their own spirit-world and rites, with their own sources.
- **Kyoto and Nara core, and mountain-ascetic districts** (Dewa Sanzan, Kōyasan, Kumano): the old temple and *shugendō* centers.
- **Shikoku and the San'in coast:** possession lore (*inugami*, fox) and the henro pilgrimage.
- **Nagasaki and Amakusa hidden Christians (*kakure kirishitan*):** a secret religion with its own prayers.
- **Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei returnees and recent immigrants:** shamanic and other rites carried in.
- **Urban commuters against rural and island elders:** different help-seeking and different belief.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for yōkai, ghost tales, folk religion, pilgrimage and psychiatric history; mixed for Ryukyuan and Ainu in print outside local libraries; thin in open web for private spirit and healing practice.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan and Ainu terms with local editions; English baseline works are plentiful but mostly outsider.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ryukyuan *utaki* and Ainu ritual knowledge are partly restricted; leprosy and psychiatric confinement involve survivors' privacy; Aum Shinrikyō and other new religions are contested; suicide sites and burakumin matters need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
