# Japan: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan; Ainu. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher, including release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hiroshima, Nagasaki and Okinawa:** war-dead communities with different memories of defeat.
- **Tōhoku coast:** disaster survivors, with the *tendenko* tradition.
- **Pure Land believers against Zen and Nichiren followers:** different doctrines about handing over the self.
- **Ainu of Hokkaido:** a people whose land was taken and whose memory of yielding is their own.
- **Okinawan households:** *nankuru naisa* talk and the long refusal of base politics.
- **Elderly and hospice patients against younger relatives:** where acceptance of death differs by generation.
- **Workers by sector:** salaried staff, freelancers and part-timers against resignation.
- **Veterans' families and repatriates from Manchuria and Siberia:** returnees' words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory, Zen and Pure Land texts, and end-of-life statistics; mixed for ordinary acceptance talk (*shikata ga nai*), which is a phrase more than a topic; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan voices outside memoirs.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for death poems, Dōgen, Shinran); Okinawan and Ainu for minority terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hibakusha, repatriates and war-dead families; Okinawan base politics and Ainu dispossession are live disputes; suicide framed as acceptance.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
