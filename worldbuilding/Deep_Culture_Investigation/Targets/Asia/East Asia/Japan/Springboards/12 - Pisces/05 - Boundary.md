# Japan: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan and Ryukyuan; Ainu; Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural villages against large cities:** neighborhood-association duties against apartment anonymity.
- **Okinawa and the Ryukyus:** "Okinawan against mainlander" lines and local *shima* units.
- **Ainu of Hokkaido:** territory and its redrawing, and the word *shamo* for outsiders.
- **Burakumin communities and Zainichi Koreans:** groups for whom the line was drawn from outside and then contested.
- **Kyoto, Tokyo and Osaka residents:** claimed differences in distance and visiting habits, a stereotype to test.
- **Families by generation:** parents and adult children in the 8050 pattern, and in-law households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for neighborhood, family and nation-boundary scholarship and statistics; mixed for personal space and touch, which come mainly from small studies; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan perspectives in print outside specialist work.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for Heian and Edo records; Okinawan and Ainu for minority terms; Korean for Zainichi sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burakumin lineage and name-tracing is a protected, painful subject; territorial disputes are politically charged and have opposing national narratives; harassment cases involve victims' privacy.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
