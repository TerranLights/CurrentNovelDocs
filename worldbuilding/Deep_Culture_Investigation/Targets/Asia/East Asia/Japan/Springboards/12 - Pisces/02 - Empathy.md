# Japan: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Okinawan and other Ryukyuan; Ainu; Japanese Sign Language for Deaf communities. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kansai against Kantō speakers:** their popular reputations for talk style are a stereotype to test, not assume.
- **Tōhoku and Kyushu dialect regions:** different habits of directness and silence.
- **Okinawa and Ainu communities:** other words for the heart, kin and the felt tie.
- **Generations:** older and "Heisei-born" Japanese, and school-age children raised under the moral-education subject.
- **Deaf and hard-of-hearing communities:** a communicating group with its own norms.
- **Workplace types:** service staff, nurses, office workers and farmers.
- **Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei Brazilians and recent foreign residents:** cross-cultural reading and misreading.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for psychology scales, communication studies and service and nursing manuals; mixed for everyday talk of empathy as such, because the English word is a recent import; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan usage in print.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Okinawan and Ainu for minority terms; JSL for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hikikomori and disability material involve families' privacy; "reading the air" talk intersects with school bullying (*ijime*).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
