# Japan: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Okinawan (Uchinaaguchi) and other Ryukyuan; Ainu; Korean (Zainichi). **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

| Japanese | 助け合い / 支え合い (tasukeai / sasaeai) | helping each other | mutual aid, the community side of care |

| Okinawan | ちむぐりさ (chimugurisa) | heart aching for another | *check*; also いちゃりばちょーでー (*ichariba chōdē*) and 命どぅ宝 (*nuchi du takara*) *check* |

English keywords: Japanese compassion, *jihi*, Jizō, volunteer movement, welfare commissioners, kodomo shokudō, self-responsibility, charity in Japan.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural districts against metropolitan areas:** neighbor help and welfare commissioners against anonymous city life.
- **Okinawa and the Ryukyus:** distinct mutual-aid forms (*yuimāru*, *moai*).
- **Ainu of Hokkaido:** their own kin-based care and the history of state aid.
- **Buddhist, Shinto, Christian and new-religion groups** (e.g., Sōka Gakkai, Risshō Kōsei-kai, Nagasaki Catholics): different charity sources.
- **Burakumin and Zainichi Koreans:** groups for whom pity has been both given and refused.
- **Disaster-hit coasts of Tōhoku:** neighborhoods shaped by large volunteer inflow.
- **Elderly caregivers and workers in care professions:** the strain side of compassion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering, giving and welfare statistics; mixed for how pity is felt, since it is rarely asked directly; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan words in print outside regional libraries.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for pre-modern items; Okinawan and Ainu for minority terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** welfare stigma (生活保護 recipients), burakumin identity and care-related deaths are painful topics; keep survivors' and family voices first.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
