# Japan: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan; Ainu. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Office workers against farmers, fishers and freelancers:** drinking and leave habits by work.
- **Tokyo, Osaka and Fukuoka nightlife districts against rural areas:** distinct escape spaces and costs.
- **Okinawa and Amami:** island spirits and moai drinking; also as destination for mainland escapers.
- **Hokkaido and Ainu communities:** their own drinking and recovery stories, to be heard in their voices first.
- **Onsen towns** (Beppu, Kusatsu, Noboribetsu): spa retreat as a regional industry.
- **Generations:** older salaried men, young single adults, otaku and fan communities.
- **Women and men:** different spaces for drink, gambling and fandom.
- **Zainichi Koreans in the pachinko trade and other immigrant business groups:** a sensitive link to approach through the communities' own histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gambling, alcohol, game, comic and travel markets, and for hikikomori; mixed for private drinking and mental escape; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan material.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for Man'yōshū, Kenkō and Saikaku); Okinawan and Ainu terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction and withdrawal involve families and anonymity; yonigeya and evaporation touch debt and domestic violence; pachinko and ethnic business history is contested; Ainu ritual liquor use is ceremonial knowledge.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
