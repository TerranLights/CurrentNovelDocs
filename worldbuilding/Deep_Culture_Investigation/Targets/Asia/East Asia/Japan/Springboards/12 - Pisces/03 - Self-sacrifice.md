# Japan: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (modern and classical); Okinawan and Ryukyuan; Ainu; Korean for Zainichi sources. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Okinawa:** the 1945 battle shaped its memory differently from the mainland.
- **Hiroshima, Nagasaki and other hibakusha communities:** suffering carried for others and for peace.
- **Aizu (Byakkotai site), Kagoshima and Chiran:** places that keep old and wartime sacrifice stories.
- **Tōhoku coast after 2011:** disaster-duty sacrifice.
- **Women and men within families:** who is expected to give care, and who leaves work.
- **Ainu communities:** their own heroic songs and different memory of state wars.
- **Zainichi Koreans and Taiwanese-descended residents:** colonial-era soldiers enshrined or excluded, a source of dispute.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for karōshi, caregiving statistics, war memory and literature; mixed for private family sacrifice, which appears mostly in memoir and drama; thin for Ainu and Ryukyuan sources outside local publications.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (including classical for pre-modern items); Okinawan and Ainu terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yasukuni, kamikaze memory and Okinawan mass deaths are politically contested, with survivors still alive; sokushinbutsu and suicide material need respect; Ainu and Ryukyuan ritual details may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
