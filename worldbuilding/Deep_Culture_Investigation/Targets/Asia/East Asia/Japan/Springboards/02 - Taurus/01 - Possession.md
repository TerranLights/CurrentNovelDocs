# Japan: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (kanji and kana); Ainu; Okinawan and other Ryukyuan languages (*check*); Korean for the Zainichi. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Ainu communities (Hokkaido; Tokyo): treasure objects and objects held abroad. Ryukyuans (Okinawa, Amami, Yaeyama, Miyako): family tombs and altars. Old merchant and farm households with storehouses (蔵 *kura*) against small urban apartments. Elderly people living alone, and their heirs. Temple and shrine households. Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei returnees, and recent migrant workers: what they carried, and what they left. Children and school age.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on decluttering, gifts and lost property; mixed on hoarding and on the dead's things; thin on minority households' belongings.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese, with classical Japanese for old tales and scrolls; Ainu and Okinawan for treasure and tomb terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ainu remains and sacred objects (follow the communities' own guidance); hoarding and solitary-death material is stigmatized; private estate cases.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
