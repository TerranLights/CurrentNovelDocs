# Japan: The domain, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (kanji, kana); Ainu (Latin and katakana); Ryukyuan languages, above all Okinawan (*check*); Korean for the Zainichi. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how is it held: possessions, money, resources, values, self-worth, with the food and dress folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Ainu (Hokkaido, Sakhalin-descended and Tokyo communities): treasure, hunting and fishing economies. Ryukyuans and the Okinawan, Amami and Yaeyama peoples: moai and the kingdom's trade past. Tohoku, Kansai (Osaka, Kyoto), Kanto, Kyushu: regional sayings on spending and food. Buraku communities (sensitive; own organizations' work): leather and work-based wealth. Zainichi Koreans, Nikkei returnees from South America, Chinese residents, Ogasawara islanders. Urban salaried households against farm, fishing and mountain households; religious sects and shrine parishes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official surveys and consumer history; mixed for Ainu and Ryukyuan material; thin in English for minority money habits.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical Japanese for Edo and earlier texts); Ainu, Okinawan, Korean for minority material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ainu ceremonial objects and remains; Buraku discrimination (use their organizations' own publications); respect Ainu guidance on sacred things.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Cabinet Office "life of the people" survey item on "mental or material richness" and trace it across years.
2. Search Aozora Bunko for 貧窮問答歌, 方丈記 and 日本永代蔵 and read each for what they call wealth.
3. Search 模合 沖縄 and 頼母子講 for rotating credit and compare the regions.
4. Pull MAFF's 郷土料理 pages for Hokkaido, Okinawa and Kansai to mark regional staples.
