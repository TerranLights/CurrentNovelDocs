# Japan: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (kanji and kana; classical Japanese for old texts); Ainu; Okinawan (*check*); Korean for the Zainichi. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Ainu (Hokkaido; Tokyo): identity, hiding and pride. Ryukyuans (Okinawa, Amami, Yaeyama): language shame and reclaimed identity. Buraku communities. Zainichi Koreans, with and without a use-name. Women across homemaker and career lives. Salaried workers, the 就職氷河期 (job-ice-age) cohort, and day laborers in Sanya, Kotobuki and Kamagasaki. Older adults, rural and urban. Students inside and outside the exam ranking.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on surveys and psychology; mixed for minority and class voices; the English literature leans on national-character claims, which need checking.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Ainu and Okinawan for community material; Korean for Zainichi writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and hikikomori data; Buraku and Ainu identification (use their own organizations); do not treat "shame culture" as settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the self-satisfaction items from the Cabinet Office youth survey and the national school questionnaire, and compare their wording.
2. Search 自己肯定感 低い 理由 日本 and read the first scholarly and press sources.
3. Read Fukuzawa, 『学問のすゝめ』 chapter 1, and 『歎異抄』 on worth, for the two classical grounds.
4. Search 方言札 沖縄 and アイヌ 生活実態調査 差別 to see how minority worth is recorded.
