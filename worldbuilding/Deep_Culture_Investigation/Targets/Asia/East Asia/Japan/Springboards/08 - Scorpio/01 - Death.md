# Japan: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu and Okinawan terms *check*. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means (grief and mourning here; funeral practice is in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ainu** and **Ryukyuan** peoples: different souls, afterworlds and ritual specialists.
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki survivors (hibakusha) and Okinawan war survivors:** mass death as lived memory.
- **Tohoku coast communities (2011), Kobe (1995):** disaster bereaved.
- **Older and younger generations; urban single households and rural families:** who is likely to die alone and who is attended.
- **Hidden Christians, Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren, new religions:** differing teachings on the dead.
- **Zainichi Koreans:** their own account of death and the dead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official statistics, end-of-life surveys and classical texts; mixed for lived belief, which surveys capture only partly; thin in English for Ainu and Ryukyuan accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (modern and classical); Ainu and Okinawan *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hibakusha and war survivors' testimony; suicide and bereaved families; Ainu and Ryukyuan ritual knowledge held by specialists; Buraku history tied to death and defilement. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
