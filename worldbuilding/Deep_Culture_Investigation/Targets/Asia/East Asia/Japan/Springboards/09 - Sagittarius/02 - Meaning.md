# Japan: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Okinawan. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it, the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japanese | 生きがい (ikigai); 生き甲斐; 生きる意味; 人生の意味 | what makes life worth living; the meaning of life | Look at the word's own history and its modern senses, and at its later reshaping abroad |
| Japanese | やりがい (yarigai); 働きがい; 天職 (tenshoku); 使命 (shimei); 志 (kokorozashi) | worth in a task; worth in work; vocation; mission; aspiration | Work as a source; やりがい搾取 is a critic's term *check* |
| Japanese | 居場所 (ibasho); 絆 (kizuna); 縁 (en); 恩 (on) | a place where one belongs; bonds; ties; indebtedness | Belonging and obligation as sources |
| Japanese | 無常 (mujō); もののあはれ (mono no aware); 一期一会 (ichigo ichie); 諦め (akirame) | impermanence; pathos of things; once-in-a-lifetime meeting; resignation | Old frames for suffering and transience |
| Japanese | 自分探し (jibun sagashi); 自己実現 (jiko jitsugen); 生きづらさ (ikizurasa) | searching for oneself; self-actualization; difficulty of living | Modern vocabulary of drift and strain |
| Japanese | 終活 (shūkatsu); 遺言; 生き様 (ikizama); 孤独死 (kodokushi) | end-of-life planning; a will; one's way of living; solitary death | Legacy and the end of life |
| Ainu | アイヌ・ネノ・アン・アイヌ (Ainu neno an Ainu); カムイ (kamuy) | "a human who is humanlike" (a phrase from Ainu movement usage, check); god | *check* form; meaning drawn from Ainu own teaching |
| Okinawan | 命どぅ宝 (nuchi du takara); ゆいまーる (yuimāru) | life is the treasure; mutual help | *check*; both are widely used slogans to examine as phrases |

English keywords: ikigai, purpose in life, meaning at work, kodokushi, hikikomori, karoshi, ibasho, end-of-life planning, hibakusha testimony, Okinawa longevity, wabi-sabi, calling.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Salaried workers, women (including 専業主婦 and working mothers), freelancers and the irregularly employed**: different routes to worth.
- **Elders in depopulating regions** against **city dwellers**; Tohoku after 2011.
- **Okinawa and Amami** (longevity, war memory) and **Hiroshima and Nagasaki** (survivors).
- **Ainu**, **Zainichi Koreans**, **Nikkei returnees**, **Burakumin descendants** *check*: identity and belonging as sources.
- **Young people** (hikikomori, NEET, student life) against **seniors**.
- **Religious practitioners** and **nonreligious respondents**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on official surveys and white papers, on essays and fiction, and on the word 生きがい; mixed on regional comparison; thin in accessible form for Ryukyuan and Ainu own-voice. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core islands and Ryukyu/Okinawa.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for pre-modern texts; Ainu and Okinawan helpful.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, overwork death and hibakusha testimony (consent and care); family bereavement; Ainu and Ryukyuan restricted knowledge. Treat "ikigai" Western diagrams as a lead on reception, not as Japanese sources.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Find 生きがい items in Cabinet Office surveys and the 高齢社会白書, then check the wording and years.
2. Search Aozora Bunko for 生きがい and 生甲斐 to date and contextualize the word.
3. Read Kamiya Mieko's 生きがいについて beside a recent 過労死等防止対策白書 to see how the question is posed then and now.
4. Open Hiroshima, Nagasaki and Okinawa testimony pages and note how each frames witness as purpose.
5. Search NHK 「プロフェッショナル」 and 人生案内 for how work and "worth" are narrated.
