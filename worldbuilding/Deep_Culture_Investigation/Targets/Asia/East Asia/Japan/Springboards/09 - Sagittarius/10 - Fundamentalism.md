# Japan: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Korean for Zainichi and Unification Church sources; Ainu and Okinawan for any local currents. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth (usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally) is complete and binding, with a movement to restore and defend it; whether the word fits any group in Japan is itself a question to research, not assume.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New-religion bodies** separately: Aum successors, Unification Church, Happy Science, Sōka Gakkai/Nichiren Shōshū, Tenrikyō, Ōmoto, Jehovah's Witnesses; they differ in doctrine and in how they are viewed.
- **Strict Protestant and Catholic communities** against the mainstream churches.
- **Hidden Christian holdouts** in Nagasaki and Goto against Catholics.
- **Nationalist-religious networks** (State Shinto heritage, shrine politics): a political rather than doctrinal question.
- **Zainichi Korean** members and Korean-origin movements.
- **Second-generation members** and **leavers** against active members.
- **Ainu and Ryukyuan** religious revival (ritual revival is not to be treated as fundamentalism without Ainu and Ryukyuan voices).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Aum and post-2022 Unification Church material and on new-religion scholarship; rich in pre-modern orthodoxy records; thin on groups that refuse outsiders' attention; no known Ainu or Ryukyuan material. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core islands and Ryukyu/Okinawa.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; Korean for Unification Church and Zainichi sources; classical Japanese for pre-modern.
