# Japan: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (including classical and *kanbun* for early sources); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between states and peoples, in business and labor, and in daily social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Okinawa and the Ryukyu chain (a former kingdom with its own foreign relations, and base-hosting communities); Ainu communities and organizations; Kansai, Kanto and other regional speech styles (stereotypes of indirectness are claims to test with regional sources); Tsushima and other border islands; office, shop-floor and farm settings; younger against older speakers of *keigo*; foreign residents and Nikkei returnees who interpret for their families; diplomats and trade negotiators against local officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state diplomacy and archives; mixed on daily tact, where much is self-help and comparative claims; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan voices in the older period outside outsider records. Treat comparative negotiation studies as claims, not as findings.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese and *kanbun* for medieval and Edo; Ainu and Ryukyuan for Indigenous terms; English for the negotiation literature. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** postwar relations with neighbors and wartime redress are contested; cite each government's own documents; Ainu ritual protocols.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
