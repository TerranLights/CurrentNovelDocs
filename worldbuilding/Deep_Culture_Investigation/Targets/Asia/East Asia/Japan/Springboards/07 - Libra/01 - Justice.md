# Japan: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese; Ainu; Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered (retributive, restorative, procedural, corrective, distributive, historical, divine, self-help and group justice).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Ainu (Hokkaido and elsewhere); Okinawans and other Ryukyuans; communities hit by pollution (Minamata in Kumamoto and Kagoshima, Niigata, Yokkaichi, Toyama); Hiroshima and Nagasaki survivors; Fukushima evacuees; Zainichi Koreans and other foreign residents; Burakumin; people held in custody and their families; regional contrasts of Tohoku, Kansai and Kyushu in attitudes to punishment (to measure, not to assume).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on courts, crime and public opinion; mixed on restorative practice; thin on Ainu and Ryukyuan custom of redress outside outsider records.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese (classical for Edo and earlier); Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for Indigenous terms. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this is written for the core islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims' names; Burakumin records; Ainu remains and ritual knowledge; wartime redress is contested, so cite each side's own sources.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- Law here takes the letter only; the spirit of the law is Capricorn's.
