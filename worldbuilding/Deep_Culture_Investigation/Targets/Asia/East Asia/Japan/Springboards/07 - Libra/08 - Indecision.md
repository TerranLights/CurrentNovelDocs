# Japan: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (classical for medieval and Edo sources); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others; with its virtuous form, deliberation, and the culture's rules for who decides and how fast.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Okinawa (decades-long base decisions; former kingdom's own council); Ainu communities and their councils; Fukushima evacuees deciding whether to return; large firms against small and family firms; Tokyo and Kansai cities against farming regions; university graduates in the shūkatsu cycle against those outside it; older people making end-of-life choices against younger adults; local assemblies and the Diet.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on politics and policy delay; thin on private indecision, which surveys rarely ask; thin on Indigenous decision-making outside community sources. The popular claim that decisions in Japan are slow or consensual is a claim to test, not a finding.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese and *kanbun* for Shōtoku and medieval texts; Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for Indigenous terms. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life and family-decision records; Ainu council protocols.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
