# Japan: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Japan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Japanese (classical for early sources); Ainu; Ryukyuan languages; Korean (Zainichi sources). **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own or someone else's rights, truth or justice; with its principled neighbor, pacifism and nonviolence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Okinawa (a prefecture with both a peace-memory tradition and a protest tradition); Hiroshima and Nagasaki survivors; Ainu; Burakumin; Zainichi Koreans and other foreign residents; school pupils and teachers; salaried workers in large firms against small firms and unions; women reporting violence; Tohoku disaster survivors; religious minorities (Christians in Nagasaki, Nichiren-derived groups); rural villages against cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on reported bullying, harassment and consultation counts; the unreported is by definition thin; mixed on Indigenous and minority silence. "Harmony" is the best-known outsider frame, so test it against variation (conflict studies, protest histories) instead of using it.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese; classical Japanese for medieval sayings and Edo texts; Ainu and Uchinaaguchi for Indigenous terms. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the core islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases and survivor identities; Ainu, Burakumin and Zainichi identity concealment (use community-authored sources first); hibakusha testimony (consent and context).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
