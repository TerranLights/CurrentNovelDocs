## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Jeju islanders with their own origin narratives against mainland lineages; clans that trace to different founders; refugees from the North and their descendants (separate arrivals of the 1950s and the 1990s onward); farm and fishing villages with different first-of-year rites; shamanic practitioners, Buddhists, Protestants, Catholics and non-religious families; the three generations since the war.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ritual and calendar materials; mixed for failure and starting over; thin for the North outside official histories. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; classical Chinese for the oldest sources; Jejueo.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic narratives and rites belong to practitioners and need permission; founding myths carry political weight.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up 새출발, 재기 and 건국신화 in the encyclopedia.
2. Read the Samguk Yusa passages on founding, in a library translation.
3. Pull the National Folk Museum's yearly-customs entries for the new year.
4. Find Jeju origin-narrative studies in RISS.
5. Search the credit-recovery body's pages for second-chance programs.
