# Korea: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul; hanja for clan and genealogy records); Jeju-eo; northern usage for the North. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Lineage-seat families with clan associations against families with no such ties. Eldest sons' households against younger siblings'. Rural kin networks and urban apartment families. Single-parent, single-person and divorced households. Adoptive and foster families, including overseas adoptees returning. Marriage-migrant households. Jeju kin and neighbor networks. Northern families and families split across the border. Christian, Buddhist and nonreligious families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South in panels and surveys; mixed on feelings of obligation; thin for the North, mostly defector and state sources. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; hanja for genealogies; English for scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Adoption and separated-family records are personal; many clan books are private. Family-law sources go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the KGSS question lists for 가족 and 친척.
2. Pull household-type tables for 1인 가구 and 한부모 from KOSIS.
3. Search RISS for 가족주의 and 문중.
4. Look up adoption and separated-family archive catalogs.
5. Skim Kendall's and the Janellis' indexes for kin vocabulary.
