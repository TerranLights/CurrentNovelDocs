# Korea: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul; hanja for genealogies and older records); Jeju-eo for Jeju-do; northern usage (문화어) for sources from the North. **Concept in one line:** home and family: what a home is, who counts as family, and how houses, kin ties, meals, memory and child-rearing are arranged.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Clan-seat families with lineage halls against families without them. Rural villages against apartment-complex suburbs and single-room city housing. Jeju households, which have their own house and neighbor terms. Northern households in the neighborhood-unit system, and defector families resettled in the South. Single-person and elder-only households. Migrant and marriage-migrant families. Christian, Buddhist and nonreligious homes, since rites differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South (census, panels, folklore archives); mixed for village life; thin and externally filtered for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; hanja for genealogies; Jeju-eo glossaries; English for ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Defector and relatives' safety; state-made northern sources need critical reading. Family law is left to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search KOSIS for 가구 유형 and 주택 점유 형태.
2. Open the folk-culture encyclopedia and search 가옥, 족보 and 혼례.
3. Search RISS for 가족 인식 and 촌수.
4. Read the Janelli and Kendall ethnographies for the vocabulary.
5. Search KINU reports for 북한 가정 and 인민반.
