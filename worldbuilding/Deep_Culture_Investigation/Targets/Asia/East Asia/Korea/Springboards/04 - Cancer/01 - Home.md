# Korea: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jeju-eo for Jeju-do; northern usage for sources from the North. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Hanok owners, apartment dwellers, villa and officetel dwellers, and semi-basement tenants. Owners against jeonse and monthly-rent households. Rural villages and Jeju households with stone walls and lanes. People displaced by war or redevelopment. Northern households, where assignment of housing runs through the state. Single young renters in one-room flats, and elders living alone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South on tenure and cost; mixed on the feeling of home; thin and state-shaped for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; English for the outside studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Evictions and homelessness involve vulnerable people; separated-family records involve relatives in the North.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the latest housing-survey tables and read the questions on satisfaction and tenure.
2. Search RISS and DBpia for 집의 의미 and 주거 의식.
3. Search the Seoul history museum for 재개발 and 달동네 exhibitions.
4. Search oral-history archives for 피난 and 실향.
5. Read the Gelezeau and Cho Se-hui texts for terms to follow.
