# Korea: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jeju-eo; English for adoptee scholarship and memoirs. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Adoptees sent abroad and domestic adoptees. Children raised in institutions against family care. Unwed mothers. Elders living alone in villages and in cities. Goose fathers and their families. Families split by the 1950s war and division. Depopulated rural counties and mining towns against growing cities. Jeju households and the 4.3 period. Migrant and marriage-migrant mothers who left or were left. Northern families who fled or crossed a border.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South on adoption and care statistics and on adoptee writing in English; mixed on elders and on men who leave; thin and state-filtered for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; English for adoptee sources; some French and Scandinavian languages for host-country adoptee archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Adoption and child-welfare files are restricted; stories of abandoned babies and lonely deaths are painful; do not name private people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull child-welfare tables for children entering protective care and reasons.
2. Search news and commission pages for 해외입양 진상조사.
3. Search RISS for 유기 and 고독사 studies.
4. Read the Trenka and Hubinette books for vocabulary.
5. Search press archives for 기러기 아빠 and 지방소멸.
