# Korea: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jejueo for island sources. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct, decide for or speak for others, and others' acceptance of it; includes how far authority reaches into daily life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State and household authority:** compare how each is described, then how they link.
- **Jeju-do:** a distinct local history of state power and its own address terms.
- **Generations** before and after the democratic transition.
- **Women and men,** given the role of the household head in the older family system.
- **Congregations, lineage associations (문중) and shaman-using households:** non-state authorities.
- **The northern state's own account** against defector and outside accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the South; thin and state-shaped for the North. The permission-share measure belongs to Libra's letter-of-the-law work, so point there and do not research statutes here.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jejueo for some oral sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** North Korean sources are propaganda or defector testimony; tag by origin. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up 권위 and 윗사람 in the Standard Korean Language Dictionary; list compounds.
2. Search Gallup Korea's archive for institutional-trust questions across years.
3. Search RISS for "권위주의 문화" with 가정, 학교, 직장 to split by setting.
4. Search news for 교권 and 군대 문화 to find how each setting names its own authority.
