## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Apartment dwellers and those in detached or rural homes.**
- **Seoul-area and other cities,** against villages.
- **Jeju-do,** with its own shrines and myths of order.
- **Generations,** by their experience of drills, curfews and street protest.
- **Religious practitioners:** shamanic, Buddhist and Christian views of order.
- **Foreign residents and migrant workers:** how newcomers learn local rules.
- **The North:** neighborhood-unit order, to be compared with the South by source, not assumed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in the South for statistics and municipal data; mixed for the home and the cosmic sense of order; thin for the North beyond official and defector sources.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jejueo for shrine and myth sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and shrine knowledge may be restricted to practitioners; consult published works first. Protest policing is politically charged. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up 질서, 치안 and 정리정돈 in the dictionary and compare senses.
2. Search the national police and victim-survey tables for perceived safety by region.
3. Search the Seoul open-data portal for noise and trash complaints by district.
4. Search for 풍수 and 본풀이 in RISS and library catalogs to map the cosmic side.
