# Korea: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jejueo for island sources. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. **This sheet takes the spirit of the law only;** the letter (statutes, courts as written) belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rich and poor litigants,** as the public perceives equality before law.
- **Jeju-do,** with its history of state violence and later redress.
- **Rural villages and cities:** village compacts and neighbor settlement against courts.
- **Generations:** those who lived under military rule and those who did not.
- **Religious groups,** especially those that discourage suing.
- **Migrant workers and marriage migrants:** their view of the system from outside.
- **The North,** where the party's role defines the law's use.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in the South on legal opinion and elite scholarship; thin on everyday practice outside courts; the North mostly outside investigations and defector accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jejueo for local mediation traditions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cases involving named living people; use published reporting only. Statute text is Libra's, not this sheet's. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the dictionary and RISS for 법감정 and 법의식 to find the working definitions.
2. Search the Supreme Court and a polling firm for trust-in-courts items.
3. Search for 선처 and 전관예우 in news archives to see how discretion is judged.
4. Search for 향약 to trace village rules.
