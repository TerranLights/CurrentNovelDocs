## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Speakers of the Seoul standard against Gyeongsang, Jeolla, Chungcheong, Gangwon and Jeju speech. Northern speakers (Pyongan, Hamgyong) and defectors who learned another standard. Apartment-complex dwellers against rural village residents. Older and younger generations, since literacy and schooling histories differ. Deaf signers. Migrant-worker and marriage-migrant households. Christian, Buddhist and nonreligious congregations as talk settings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South (surveys, corpora, dialect records); mixed for village and neighbor life; thin and externally sourced for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; hanja for pre-1900 records; Jeju-eo glossaries; some English for secondary scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Northern media and schooling sources are state-made and need critical reading; defector interviews carry safety risks for relatives.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
