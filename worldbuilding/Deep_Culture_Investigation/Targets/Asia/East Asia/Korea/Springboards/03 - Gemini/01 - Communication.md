*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); hanja for Joseon-era letters; the northern standard for North Korean sources. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what a culture holds good communication to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Speakers by age cohort, since address and speech-level habits shift across generations. Regional speakers (Gyeongsang, Jeolla, Chungcheong, Jeju) whose directness and politeness markers may differ. Men and women. Workplace ranks against peer groups. Rural elders against urban young. Northern speakers and defectors adapting to the southern standard. Deaf signers and interpreters. Christian church members and clergy for sermon speech.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South (surveys, corpora, journals); mixed for gesture, silence and family talk; thin for the North beyond official language policy. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; some hanja for old letters; English for comparative scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Popular "nunchi" and "indirectness" explainers are often written for foreigners; read them as claims to test. Messaging data raise privacy limits.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
