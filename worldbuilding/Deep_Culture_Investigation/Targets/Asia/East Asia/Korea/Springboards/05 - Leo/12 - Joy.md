# Korea: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jeju-eo; northern usage for the North. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

English keywords: Korean joy, happiness, heung and sinmyeong, festival, collective celebration, life satisfaction, laughter, fandom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Village farm-music troupes and apartment-block neighbors. Fan communities and gamers against older adults at traditional events. Churchgoers (including lively services), temple communities and ritual (gut) participants. Honam and Gyeongsang regions as candidate units, not assumed differences. Jeju islanders. Job-seeking young adults who use the "Hell Joseon" phrase against settled households. Elders asking what their 낙 is. Marriage migrants. Northern residents and defectors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South on life satisfaction, festivals and fandom; mixed on private, everyday joy; thin and staged for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; scholarly writing on 흥 and 신명 is largely in Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Shamanic rituals are the practitioners' own; ask before using. Happiness-and-suicide data are touchy.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the quality-of-life indicator tables for life satisfaction by age and region.
2. Search RISS and KCI for 흥, 신명 and 신바람 with 한국인.
3. List festivals on the tourism listings by season and region.
4. Look at the Gugak Center's catalog for farmers' music and pansori.
5. Search news for 소확행 and 헬조선 by year.
