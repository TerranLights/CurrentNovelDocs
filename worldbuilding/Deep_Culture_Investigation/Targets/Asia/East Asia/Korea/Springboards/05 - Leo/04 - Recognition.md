# Korea: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul; hanja for titles and honors); Jeju-eo; northern usage for the North. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Large-firm salaried workers against small shops and the self-employed. Holders of elite credentials against those without them. Arts and entertainment people against athletes and scientists. Women and men in workplaces, where credit may fall differently. Jeju divers and their island ranks. Veterans and families of the honored dead. Survivors awaiting acknowledgment of wrongs. Teachers and students. Northern honored titleholders and ordinary residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for the South on prizes, lists and credentials; mixed on felt recognition; thin and state-shaped for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; hanja for honors lists and orders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Acknowledgment of wartime and colonial-era wrongs is political and unfinished; read the sides. Honors tied to the military are contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Standard Korean Dictionary for 인정, 칭찬 and 알아주다.
2. List the main honors and prizes and note who awards them.
3. Pull workplace-survey items on praise and respect.
4. Search RISS for 인정 욕구 and 학벌 사회.
5. Find the commission reports and read their acknowledgment sections.
