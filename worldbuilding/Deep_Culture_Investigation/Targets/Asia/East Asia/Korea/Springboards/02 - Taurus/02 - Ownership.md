# Korea: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul); Jeju-eo for island commons. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, exclude others from, give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on. Also the left-right axis: how much of what is produced stays private and how much goes to the state.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Homeowners against jeonse and monthly-rent tenants, a division visible in housing data. Capital-area residents against provincial ones. Clan-village (jipseongchon) households against newer apartment communities. Jeju village fishing associations. Farmers and landlords, in the postwar land-reform lands. Northern households, whose holdings sit under a different ownership order. Family-business owners against salaried staff.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich for housing and public finance (South); mixed for common holdings; thin for the North. The left-right figure is gathered at Stage 2, not here. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; classical Chinese (hanmun) for older deeds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Postwar land redistribution and confiscation are contested politically. No law: the letter of property law goes to Libra, its spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
