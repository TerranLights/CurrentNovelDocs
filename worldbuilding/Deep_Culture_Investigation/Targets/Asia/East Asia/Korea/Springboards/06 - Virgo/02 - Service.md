## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Men, who face conscription, against women, who do not in the South; Protestant, Catholic and Buddhist communities, each with its own serving practices; chaebol and shop staff against civil servants; Jeju and island villages, where neighbors exchange help; the North, where service is state-framed; household helpers, including Korean Chinese (조선족) women; veterans and their families; students meeting school volunteer-hour rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the South on volunteering, conscription and service work; mixed on household help; thin on the North beyond slogans and defector accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; the church and military registers use their own vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conscription abuse cases and camp-town service histories are painful; defector testimony needs source protection; military records are partly restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core Korean lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search KOSIS social survey tables for 자원봉사 and note region splits.
