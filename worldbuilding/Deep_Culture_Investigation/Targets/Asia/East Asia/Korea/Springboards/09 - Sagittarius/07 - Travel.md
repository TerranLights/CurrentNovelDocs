## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Older generations who traveled little against younger backpackers and working-holiday travelers; families with a member abroad (the "goose family" pattern, to be checked); Protestant and Catholic pilgrim groups against Buddhist temple-stay visitors; Jeju-do, with its island crossings and divers who worked away; people displaced by the 1950 war and their descendants; recent arrivals from the north, whose journeys were forced and hidden; the north's permitted travel for officials, athletes and tourists, as reported.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the south (tourism and immigration statistics, travel writing); mixed for Jeju-do; thin and restricted for the north. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; classical Chinese for the older diaries, with modern translations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** defection routes and brokers are dangerous to identify; separated-family testimony is intimate.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
