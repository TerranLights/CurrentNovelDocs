## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Capital-area students against provincial ones; families who pay for cram schools against those who cannot; graduates of the top universities against vocational and junior-college graduates; women and men in higher education; Protestant mission-school lineages against public ones; Jeju-do and its own school history; rural village schools; recent arrivals from the north placed in southern classrooms; northern students, as described by those who left; adult returners and lifelong-learning students.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the south (official statistics, research institutes, scholarship); mixed for Jeju-do; thin and state-presented for the north. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; English covers a few historical and ethnographic books only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** student stress and self-harm are heavy topics; test-related cases name real students; exam content is copyrighted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. KESS: school and university counts by region and year.

4. Pai Chai and Ewha archives for founding documents.
5. Ministry of Unification portal: 북한 교육 overview.
