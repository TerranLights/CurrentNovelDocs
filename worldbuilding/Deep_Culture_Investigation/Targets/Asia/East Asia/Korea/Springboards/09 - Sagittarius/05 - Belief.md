# Korea: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul; Jeju-eo for Jeju-do). **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Practicing shamans and their clients; Protestants who reject shamanic belief and Protestants who blend it; Buddhists whose temples keep mountain-spirit shrines; Confucian lineage households with ancestral rites; the unaffiliated who still read saju; Jeju-do, with its village gods; rural against urban; generations; scientists and engineers; northern citizens under an officially materialist state, as described by those who left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the south and Jeju-do in folklore archives; thin on lived belief in the north. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jeju-eo glossaries for island sources; Japanese for colonial-era fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some practitioners guard rites; "superstition" can be an insult, so record who uses the word; colonial-period ethnographers had their own aims.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. National Folk Museum catalog: 민간신앙 and 금기.

4. Handbook of Korean Oral Literature: search 귀신 by region.
5. Jeju folklore collections: 당신화 (village-shrine myths), for the island set.
