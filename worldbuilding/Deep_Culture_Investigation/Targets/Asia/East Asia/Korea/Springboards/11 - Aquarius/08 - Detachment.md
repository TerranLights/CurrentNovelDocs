# Korea: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul, hanja for Buddhist terms); Jejueo for the island. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; valued in some forms, condemned in others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist, Christian, Confucian-lineage and unaffiliated outlooks.
- Men and women, since display rules and the "anger illness" label may differ.
- Generations: the endurance-minded old, and the young who talk of giving up or living alone.
- Rural villages, big-city apartments and workplaces with emotional-labor jobs.
- Jeju survivors' families, whose silence has its own history.
- North and South: distance as caution against distance as choice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; much psychology and survey material for the South, almost none for the North beyond defectors. Exact lands of the nation are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; hanja for Buddhist texts; English for most psychology.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and mental-health data; trauma testimony; stereotypes about Korean "reserve" must be treated as claims to test, not findings.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up 초연, 무심 and 거리두기 in the Standard Korean Dictionary.
2. Search Statistics Korea for living-alone and time-alone tables.
3. Pull an overview of han and hwabyeong in the psychology literature.
4. Read the Templestay and Beopjeong materials.
5. Search press archives for noise-dispute coverage.
