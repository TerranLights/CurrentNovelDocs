## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- South, North and Jeju: different state forms of association; Jeju has its own language and labor-exchange vocabulary.
- Regions (Honam, Yeongnam, Gangwon, Chungcheong, Seoul area): regional ties may weigh differently.
- Generations: elders, the 1980s protest generation, today's youth.
- Rural villages, big-city apartment complexes, and newcomer groups (defectors, migrant workers, marriage migrants).
- Christian, Buddhist, Confucian-lineage and unaffiliated communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the South (surveys, ethnography, media); thin and mediated for the North (defector accounts, state publications). Exact lands of the nation are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jejueo for the island; English for much scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** North Korean sources can be state propaganda or defector testimony; both need careful reading. Jeju 4.3 and Gwangju memory are painful and contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
