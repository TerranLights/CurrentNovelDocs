# Korea: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (south, north, Jeju). **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Village and city households; apartment neighbors against old quarters.
- Jeju villages with labor-exchange words and fishing associations.
- Adult children, elders, and those without family support.
- Church and temple congregations with their own giving rules.
- Northern lands: neighborhood units and market traders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the south (surveys, folklore, forums); mixed for Jeju; thin for the north.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jeju speech for local terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gift money touches graft rules in some settings (see Libra's law section); defector testimony is risky.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search KLoSA documentation for transfer items.
2. Search 경조사비 설문 and 축의금 for amounts and norms.
3. Search 품앗이 두레 수눌음 in folklore and Jeju studies.
4. Search 가는 말이 고우면 and related proverbs in the dictionary.
5. Read the Giving Korea report's motive tables.
