*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (south, north and Jeju usage). **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Honam (Gwangju) and Yeongnam, on how past state violence is remembered.
- Jeju-do, for the 4.3 events and their redress.
- Victims' families, survivors and perpetrators' descendants.
- Northern lands and exiles from them, with separate penal systems.
- Older and younger cohorts; Christian, Buddhist and nonreligious households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for southern courts and commissions; thin and secondhand for the north.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Hanja for Joseon texts; Jeju speech for local testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** living victims, contested memory of 4.3, 5.18 and wartime wrongs; defector safety. Spirit of the law and obedience belong to Capricorn; only the written law is taken here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search KICJ for 범죄피해조사 and 법의식 (legal consciousness) survey reports.
2. Search 과거사 진상규명 보고서 for each commission's final report.
3. Open the Criminal Act and the citizen-trial act (국민참여재판) in the statute center.
4. Search 억울하다 with 사법 불신 in Korean newspapers.
5. Pull the KINU white paper's penal chapters.
