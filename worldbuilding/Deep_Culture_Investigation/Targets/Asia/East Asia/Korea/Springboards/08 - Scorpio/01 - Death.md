# Korea: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul and romanization); Jeju language for Jeju-do sources. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means (grief and mourning here; funeral practice is in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jeju-do:** own reaper myths and memory of the 1948 killings.
- **Honam and Gwangju:** memory of 1980 deaths.
- **Buddhist, Catholic, Protestant and Confucian-ritual households:** different pictures of what follows death.
- **Shaman clients and non-clients.**
- **Older villagers against young city dwellers living alone.**
- **North Korea:** famine dead and state-led mourning.
- **Bereaved families of disasters, war and suicide.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in the South (surveys, fiction, film, medicine); thin and filtered for the North. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Korean; Jeju language for local myth; Hanja for older texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, child death and disaster survivors; shamans' restricted knowledge; defector testimony.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search KOSIS for "생명표" and read the tables.
2. Search the Folk Museum encyclopedia for "저승" and "귀신".
3. Search library catalogs for "바리공주" and "차사본풀이".
4. Search KLoSA documentation for end-of-life items.
5. Search news archives for "웰다잉" and "고독사".
