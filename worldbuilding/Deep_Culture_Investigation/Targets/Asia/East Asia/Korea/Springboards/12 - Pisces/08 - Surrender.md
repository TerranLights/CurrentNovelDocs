*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Korea, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Korean (hangul). **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher: God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death; release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Christian, Buddhist and shaman-led households: each names whom one yields to.
- Hospice patients and families against the general public.
- Unmarried mothers and adopted people, who hold a separate vocabulary of giving up.
- Veterans and those who lived through the 1950 war, for surrender and capture.
- Jeju islanders against mainlanders, where the 1948 history shapes wartime memory.
- Generations: elders who say "that is fate" against youth who say "never give up" or "quit".
- Northern-origin arrivals, who have decided to leave one order for another.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on end-of-life and adoption, thin on informal letting go. The legal rules on withdrawing treatment belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here.
- **Languages needed:** Korean essential; Sino-Korean terms for older sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** adoption testimony, suicide and end-of-life cases, and war memory. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; start with the southern core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
