## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist meditators, Pentecostal and charismatic Protestants, and shaman-led ritual circles: three kinds of letting go.
- Divorced, never-married and widowed people, and shrinking-district residents against metropolitan ones.
- Jeju speakers against standard-Korean speakers, for language loss.
- Families split by the northern division, and northern-origin arrivals adapting to the south.
- Older rural people against young city fans, for crowds.
- Dementia caregivers, who speak of losing a person while the person lives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on divorce, population shrinkage and funerals; mixed on mystical experience, which sits in ethnography and sermons; thin on the north.
- **Languages needed:** Korean essential; Classical Chinese terms for the doctrine; Jejueo for island speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dementia and care accounts; cult-like religious groups; suicide in connection with a breakup or debt. Colonial-era assimilation policy is a separate history to approach carefully. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; start with the southern core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
