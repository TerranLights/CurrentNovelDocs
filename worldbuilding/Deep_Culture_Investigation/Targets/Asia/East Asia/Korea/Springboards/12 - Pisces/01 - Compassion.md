*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist, Catholic, Protestant and unaffiliated households: each has its own vocabulary of mercy.
- Jeju villages, where exchange labor and the women's diving cooperatives are a separate tradition.
- Rural elders against urban young adults, especially on donation habits.
- Northern-origin arrivals, as givers and as receivers.
- Animal-rescue circles, which draw on different moral language from human charity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on giving and volunteering; mixed on the felt side (pity against respect), which lives in psychology journals and fiction.
- **Languages needed:** Korean essential; English abstracts cover only part.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of recipients may be stigmatizing; church fundraising can be contested. Mercy toward offenders and the poor-relief rules belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; start with the southern core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
