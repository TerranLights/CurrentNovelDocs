## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Victims, offenders and police: each speaks of crime differently.
- Seoul and other big cities against small towns and villages, where reporting and shame differ.
- Jeju islanders, with their own lore of wrongdoing and the state's 1948 violence.
- Chaebol-level and ordinary-worker offenders, as public attitudes separate white-collar from street crime.
- Protestant, Catholic, Buddhist and nonreligious households on sin, guilt and redemption.
- Northern-origin arrivals, who bring the north's categories of crime.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on statistics and media; mixed on moral attitudes toward offenders; the north is known only through defector and outside sources.
- **Languages needed:** Korean essential.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victim and minor data; sexual-crime cases; state violence; naming of private suspects. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; start with the southern core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
