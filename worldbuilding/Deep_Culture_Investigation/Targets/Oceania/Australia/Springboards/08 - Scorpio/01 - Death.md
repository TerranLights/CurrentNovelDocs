# Australia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages (Western Desert, Yolngu Matha, Noongar and others); Greek, Italian, Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese, Maori. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means (grief and mourning are researched here; funeral practice is in death ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal peoples by region (Yolngu, Western Desert, Noongar, Koori, Murri): beliefs about the dead and naming differ.
- Torres Strait Islanders: the dead and ancestral presence in a separate tradition.
- Anglo-Celtic Protestants, Irish Catholics and the secular: heaven, purgatory or nothing.
- Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese and Pacific communities: ancestors and prayers for the dead.
- Muslim, Jewish and Buddhist communities: views of death and afterlife.
- Remote, rural and urban Australia: where death happens and who is near.
- Veterans and war-bereaved families against civilians.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, newspapers and war death; mixed for Aboriginal belief (partial, mostly mid-century ethnography); thin for private fear of death.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal languages and community languages for primary sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names and images of the dead; deaths in custody; suicide (handle with care). Follow AIATSIS protocols.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
