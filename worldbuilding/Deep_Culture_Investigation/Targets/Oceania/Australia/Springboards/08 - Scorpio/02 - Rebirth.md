# Australia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages (Tiwi, Kaurna, Yolngu Matha and others); Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Maori. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Pentecostal, Catholic, Orthodox and secular Australians: sudden against gradual change.
- Aboriginal peoples by region: Tiwi, Yolngu, Kaurna, Palawa (Tasmania), Koori and Noongar revivals differ.
- Aboriginal Christians (Elcho, Ngukurr, Hermannsburg) and Aboriginal traditionalists.
- Stolen Generations families and communities.
- Convict-descended and migrant families (Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese).
- Fire-affected regions (Victoria, New South Wales, Queensland) against cyclone and flood regions.
- Urban recovery services against remote communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religion statistics, disaster recovery and addiction treatment; mixed for language revival and Aboriginal ceremonial renewal; thin for lived accounts outside memoirs.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal languages and community languages for primary sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Aboriginal ceremonial detail, Stolen Generations testimony and ex-cult survivors' accounts; follow AIATSIS protocols.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Census religion tables for two censuses and compare "No religion" and Pentecostal counts by state.
2. Search the Victorian Bushfires Royal Commission for "recovery" and read the town chapters.
3. In the AIATSIS Languages report, list the revival programs named, then locate each community's own site.
4. Search the NDSHS tables for recovery or treatment measures by region.
5. Search Museums Victoria for migrant "new life" oral histories.
