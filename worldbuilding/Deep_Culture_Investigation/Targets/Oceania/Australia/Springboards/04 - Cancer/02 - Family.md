# Australia: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian), Aboriginal English, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, main migrant languages. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aboriginal peoples with different systems:** Yolŋu (moiety and clan), Warlpiri and Arrernte (section and subsection systems), southeastern Koori and Murri families after dispossession, Noongar, palawa.
- **Torres Strait Islanders:** island kinship and child-giving.
- **Migrant communities:** Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese, Turkish, Samoan, Tongan, Māori; first against second generation.
- **Older settler stocks:** Anglo-Irish families, convict descendants, pastoral dynasties against urban working households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and published memoirs; mixed for kin systems, because community accounts and old ethnographies differ; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal languages for kin terms; Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Arabic, Samoan and Tongan for communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names and images of the deceased; sacred and gender-restricted knowledge in ethnographies (the Strehlow holdings, for example); family records of removed children; follow community access rules.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ABS: "family composition" and "multi-family household" by state and by Indigenous status.
2. AuSSA data catalogue: family items, then HILDA family modules.
3. AIATSIS catalog: "kinship" with one language name (Yolŋu, Warlpiri, Arrernte).
4. Trove: "family reunion" in a regional paper for one decade.
5. Search library catalogs: "transnational families Australia" and read the top two studies' bibliographies.
