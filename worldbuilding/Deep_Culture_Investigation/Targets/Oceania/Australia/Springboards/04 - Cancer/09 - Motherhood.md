# Australia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian), Aboriginal English, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, main migrant languages. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aboriginal peoples:** desert, Arnhem Land and southeastern mothers; the place of grandmothers, aunties and women's business in each.
- **Torres Strait Islander mothers**, including those who give a child to kin.
- **Migrant mothers:** Chinese, Greek, Italian, Vietnamese, Lebanese, Pacific and Māori, by generation.
- **Single mothers; mothers in paid work; stay-at-home mothers; refugee mothers.**
- **Region:** rural and remote mothers far from maternity care, against capital cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, advice and media; mixed for Indigenous mothering, where restricted women's knowledge is not for general use; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal languages for mother terms; Chinese, Greek, Italian, Vietnamese and Arabic for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** women's business and birth knowledge held by Aboriginal women; names and images of the dead; maternal loss and perinatal grief. Reproductive law is not researched here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. AIHW: the latest *Australia's mothers and babies* summary by state and Indigenous status.

3. WGEA and Grattan: key findings on mothers' employment.
4. Library catalog: Kaberry and Bell, then the first chapter headings.
