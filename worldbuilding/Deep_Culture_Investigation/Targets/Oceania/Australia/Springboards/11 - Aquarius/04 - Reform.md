*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Aboriginal peoples (as subjects and authors of reform; Yolŋu, Aṉangu, Noongar, Wiradjuri, Koori), and Torres Strait Islanders; Tasmania (Franklin and green politics) against Queensland (Fitzgerald); Sydney and Melbourne reform cultures; Labor and trade-union reformers against free-market reformers; church-based reformers (Catholic, Protestant, Quaker); Chinese, Arabic-speaking and other diasporas with their own homeland reform traditions; rural and regional communities that bore the cost of reforms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inquiries, parliament, archives and media; mixed for Aboriginal reform in their own voices; thin for migrant reform societies after about 1920.
- **Languages needed:** English; Yolŋu Matha and other Aboriginal languages for community texts; Chinese and Arabic for diaspora reform papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Stolen Generations and abuse testimony; deceased persons' names and images; closed-door inquiry evidence.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
