# Australia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian); Aboriginal languages and Kriol; Torres Strait languages; migrant languages. **Concept in one line:** how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Moiety and clan systems of Arnhem Land (Yolŋu), skin-name systems in the Central Desert (Warlpiri, Arrernte), and urban Aboriginal groups that form by place and not descent; Torres Strait Islander clans and island communities; union and Labor communities against employer and rural Liberal-National ones; Anglo-Celtic Protestant and Catholic traditions; ethnic clubs (Greek, Italian, Croatian, Lebanese, Chinese); sports tribes (Australian rules, rugby league states of origin); outlaw motorcycle clubs; Pacific Islander church and village groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, war and sport; mixed for Aboriginal social organization (older ethnography, recent community voices); thin for closed groups.
- **Languages needed:** English; Warlpiri and Yolŋu Matha materials; Arabic, Greek, Italian and Chinese for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial and totemic detail; gang material; ethnic profiling coverage; Cronulla accounts are contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Trove: "scab" and "shearers' strike" 1891 newspaper reports.
2. AIATSIS catalogue: "moiety" with "Yolngu" or "skin names" with "Warlpiri".
3. Gammage, *The Broken Years*: read the letters on comrades and desertion.
4. Australian Election Study: party-identification tables by state.
5. Hansard: the 2008 apology debate.
