# Australia: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Warlpiri, Pitjantjatjara, Yolŋu Matha and Torres Strait Creole through community-led sources; Cantonese and Mandarin, Vietnamese, Greek, Italian and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in remote communities, towns and cities, where food supply, income control and housing differ.
- Pastoral, farming and mining-region families, and fire-prone coastal and bush-fringe households.
- Cyclone coasts of Queensland and the Top End, flood regions, and drought-exposed districts.
- Older women, single parents, renters and casual or gig workers.
- Retirees with and without home ownership.
- Post-war European arrivals, refugee groups and recent skilled migrants, for the networks they relied on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, disasters and welfare; mixed for remote and Aboriginal contexts, where official and community accounts may differ; thin on private saving habits.
- **Languages needed:** English; Kriol and Aboriginal English; community languages; Aboriginal languages only through community-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of violence and family safety; disaster trauma; welfare control programs, which are political; restricted knowledge in seasonal and food teaching.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ABS General Social Survey: find the items on ability to raise emergency funds and on financial stress, by group.
2. Read the *Retirement Income Review*'s summary and note who it says is most exposed.
3. Read survivor sections of the Black Saturday and 2020 disaster inquiries for the words used about safety and leaving.
4. Trove: "susso" and "sustenance" in 1930-1935; note how recipients and officials speak of it.
5. A remote-store or income-management evaluation: note what community members say.
