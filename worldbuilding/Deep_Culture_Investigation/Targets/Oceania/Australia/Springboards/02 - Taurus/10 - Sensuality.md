# Australia: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Yolŋu Matha, Pitjantjatjara, Torres Strait Creole and other Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages through community-led sources; Cantonese and Mandarin, Vietnamese, Greek, Italian and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure; sex itself is Scorpio's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coastal and inland people, for beach and swimming life against bush and desert comfort.
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities by region, for music, feasting, foods and bodily practice as the people share them.
- Wine, coffee and craft-beer districts (Barossa, Hunter, Yarra Valley, Margaret River; Melbourne and Sydney cafés) against pub and barbecue cultures.
- Post-war Italian, Greek, Chinese, Vietnamese and Lebanese communities, for food and gathering customs.
- Older and younger Australians, and men and women, for drinking and body norms.
- People with sensory differences, and older or disabled people, whose comfort is often less documented.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health surveys, food and music media; mixed for touch and private comfort; thin for Aboriginal sensory practice, where public accounts are limited and some practices are restricted.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; community languages for migrant food and festival sources; Aboriginal terms via community-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial smoke, song and dance that are not for outsiders; alcohol-restriction policies in some communities, which are political; nude-beach and body-norm material; images of the dead in archives.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Trove: "six o'clock swill" and "temperance" for the dates and arguments around public drinking.
2. AIHW National Drug Strategy Household Survey: read the alcohol and tobacco tables by age.

4. NFSA *Sounds of Australia* list: note the recordings chosen and why.
5. Read the Garma Festival's program text and one Bangarra program note for how Aboriginal performers describe their own art.
