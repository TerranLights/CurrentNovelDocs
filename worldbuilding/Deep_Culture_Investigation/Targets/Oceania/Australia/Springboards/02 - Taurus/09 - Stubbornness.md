# Australia: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Māori; Cantonese and Mandarin, Vietnamese, Greek, Italian and Arabic; Aboriginal languages only through community-led sources. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities in remote, town and urban settings, where long campaigns and refusals are told by community.
- Labor-history regions: mining towns (Broken Hill), pastoral districts, waterfront cities.
- Bush and country people against city people, for how firmness is praised or mocked.
- Political parties and generations, for who counts a refusal as principle.
- Migrant communities by language and arrival period, for how obstinacy is judged.
- Families and neighbors in deadlock, handled through mediation services.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for strikes, protest history and public apology debates; mixed for everyday stubbornness, which is thinly measured; thin for how migrant communities discuss it.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; community languages; Aboriginal and Māori terms only via community-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** frontier violence and its memory, told by living descendants; contested heroes (Ned Kelly); ongoing disputes, which are political.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Trove: "refuses to sell" and "holdout" for local newspapers across decades.

3. Read the Jerilderie Letter and an account of Wave Hill; note the words of refusal.
4. Hansard and press for February 2008 and for 1997: compare how refusal and apology are framed.
5. Search the Raising Children Network for strong-willed children and list the terms used.
