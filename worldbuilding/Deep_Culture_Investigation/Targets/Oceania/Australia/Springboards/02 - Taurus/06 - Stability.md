# Australia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Warlpiri and other Aboriginal languages through community-led sources; Māori; Cantonese and Mandarin, Vietnamese, Greek, Italian and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal groups by country: those living on their homelands, those in towns and those who have returned to country, and the Torres Strait Islander home islands against the mainland diaspora.
- Farming and pastoral families against mining and fly-in fly-out workers, and against city renters.
- Older settler families in country towns against newcomers on a tree change.
- Retirees on the move (grey nomads) against those who stay in place.
- Children in stable homes and in out-of-home care, for placement stability.
- Post-war European and later Asian and Middle Eastern arrivals, for homeland ties and settling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mobility, family and economic data; mixed for continuity as practiced, where Aboriginal and community sources should lead; thin for emotional stability and daily routine.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; community languages; Aboriginal and Māori terms only via community-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-protection data and Stolen Generations histories; ceremonial and place knowledge; outside-coined labels such as "walkabout", which are not the people's own terms.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ABS Census population mobility tables: list the measures (one year, five years) and who is shown moving most and least.
2. Search Trove for "jubilee" and "centenary" of farms, towns and clubs; list what is celebrated.
3. Read an ABARES or Family Business Australia succession report for the stated reasons farms pass on or are sold.
4. Read a short Indigenous-authored account of continuity (Steffensen or a community media piece).
5. List the dated yearly events in one state calendar (show, race, cup, memorial) and how each is described.
