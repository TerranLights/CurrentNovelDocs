# Australia: Domain and folkways, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages (Yolŋu Matha, Pitjantjatjara, Warlpiri, Arrernte, Noongar, Meriam Mir, Kala Lagaw Ya, Yumplatok); the main community languages (Mandarin and Cantonese, Vietnamese, Arabic, Greek, Italian). **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how is it held (possessions, money, resources, values, self-worth), plus the food ways, dress ways and wealth ways of the lands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal peoples by country and language group: desert (Western Desert, Warlpiri, Arrernte), Arnhem Land (Yolŋu), the southwest (Noongar), the southeast coast and Victoria, Tasmanian Aboriginal communities, and urban Aboriginal people: sharing and place may be worded differently in each.
- Torres Strait Islanders (Meriam, Kaurareg and western, central and eastern islands) and the mainland Torres Strait diaspora.
- Australian South Sea Islander descendants in Queensland; Pacific and Maori communities.
- Pastoral and farming families against mining towns and fly-in-fly-out workers (Pilbara, Bowen Basin), against coastal city dwellers.
- State differences: Western Australia's mining wealth, Queensland, Tasmania, South Australia's wine districts, the Northern Territory.
- Older Anglo-Celtic settler lines, post-war European arrivals (Greek, Italian, Maltese, German), Chinese-Australians of the goldfield era and of recent decades, and Vietnamese, Lebanese and other later communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, press and archives; mixed for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander views, where sources are community-held and often restricted; thin on food and dress folkways beyond brand and cookbook histories.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Kriol and Aboriginal English for the north; community languages for migrant press; the Aboriginal language terms only through community-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** items in AIATSIS and museums held for community access only, including images and names of the dead; sacred and gendered knowledge; contested histories of land and labor, with the legal side held for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
