# Australia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages (Yolŋu Matha, Pitjantjatjara, Meriam Mir and others); Chinese, Arabic, Greek, Vietnamese, Italian. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe each other, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal peoples by kinship system (Pintupi and other Western Desert peoples, Yolŋu, Warlpiri, Noongar, Koori): classificatory siblings against household siblings.
- Torres Strait Islanders, for island adoption customs.
- Families touched by removal: Stolen Generations, Forgotten Australians, former child migrants.
- Migrant families by wave: Greek, Italian, Lebanese, Vietnamese, Chinese, with their rank terms.
- Farm and pastoral families against suburban small families.
- Larger families (some religious communities) against only-child households (to check).
- Twins and multiples.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography, carers and migration; mixed for Indigenous kinship (mostly outsider-written, older); thin for adult sibling closeness and estrangement.
- **Languages needed:** English; Yolŋu Matha or other languages for kin terms; Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese for rank terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kin terms involve avoidance rules and names of the dead; records of removed children are painful and access-controlled; siblings found through tracing are private matters; follow each service's rules.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ABS and AIFS: family size and number of siblings by decade and by state.
2. AIATSIS catalog: "kinship" plus Yolŋu, Pintupi, Warlpiri, Noongar; list the studies.
3. Find and Connect: search for stories of separated siblings, and note which are told by survivors.
4. SDAC carers tables: adult children caring for a parent, by sex and place.
5. AgriFutures farm-succession reports: note sibling roles and gender.
