# Australia: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (with Australian slang); Pitjantjatjara, Yolŋu Matha, Kriol; Chinese, Arabic, Italian, Greek. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: a due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else (the letter of the law enters only where a statute defines a fair deal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Desert and Top End Aboriginal peoples (sharing and kin duty) against settler wage-and-award fairness.
- Union and employer sides; farm and city (Brett's divide); mining towns.
- Women and men on housework; the young and old on housing.
- Chinese, Italian, Greek and Arab communities: *furbo*, *zhàn piányi*-type words for gain.
- Pacific Islander workers, past and present (Queensland cane; seasonal-worker schemes).
- States with different consumer and succession laws; cities against remote towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on wages, consumer law, sport and inquiries; mixed on everyday talk; thin on migrant and Aboriginal fairness in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** English with slang; Pitjantjatjara and Kriol for terms; Chinese, Arabic, Italian and Greek for migrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Aboriginal rules of distribution tied to ceremony or kin are not for open use; follow the AIATSIS code (*check*). No stereotype: "fair go" is a phrase to test per group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
