# Australia: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian usage); Aboriginal English and Aboriginal languages; Chinese, Greek, Arabic, Italian, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting off a choice, or leaving it to others; with the culture's rules for who decides and how fast.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal communities that decide by consensus (Anangu, Yolŋu, Noongar, Daly River peoples) against state bodies with vote rules; Torres Strait Islander councils.
- Federal parliament against states and councils; party rooms against voters.
- Quaker, Uniting, Catholic and Orthodox decision styles.
- Migrant households where elders or the family decide: Chinese, Greek, Arab, Italian, Vietnamese.
- Young adults and the old on putting choices off; urban and rural voters.
- Planning-heavy cities (Sydney, Melbourne) against smaller ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for voting, referendums, inquiries and approvals; mixed for personal and family indecision; thin for Aboriginal and migrant decision-making in people's own words.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal English and Ngangikurungkurr terms; Chinese, Greek, Arabic, Italian, Vietnamese for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life choices and live native title disputes; group decision rules tied to ceremony; follow the AIATSIS code (*check*). Do not read slow consensus as weakness; the sheet gives no verdict.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
