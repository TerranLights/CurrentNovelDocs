# Australia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Aboriginal and Torres Strait languages; Mandarin, Greek, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal peoples by region and language, and Torres Strait Islanders, whose names for themselves differ; urban against remote.
- Descendants of removed children, who reconstruct identity from records.
- Anglo-Celtic families of long standing, convict descendants, and later migrant groups (Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese, Pacific Islander, Māori) by generation.
- State and city loyalties (New South Wales, Queensland, Victoria, Western Australia, Tasmania, the Territory); rural against capital-city residents.
- Religious and non-religious people; people of mixed descent; recent refugees.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census, attitude surveys and literature; mixed for Aboriginal self-identification (rich in memoir, charged in public dispute); thin for small and new communities.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal-language sources through community centers; Chinese, Greek and Vietnamese for ethnic press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disputed claims of Aboriginal descent; deceased persons' names and images; records of removed children; discussion of who may speak for a group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
