*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal homelands and outstation residents against town and urban Aboriginal households; Torres Strait Islanders.
- Rural, outback and farming families against suburban households; grey nomads and the self-funded retired against Age Pension recipients.
- Migrant families with obligations to kin (Chinese, Greek, Lebanese, Vietnamese, Samoan, Tongan), by generation.
- People who rent against owner-occupiers; young adults in capital cities against regional towns.
- People with disability and older residents in care or at home.
- The Northern Territory, Western Australia and Tasmania, where distance or separation claims shape the self-image.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for welfare, housing and retirement; mixed for Aboriginal self-sufficiency (strong policy research, uneven on household life); thin for family obligation in small migrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal-language sources via community centers; Chinese, Greek, Arabic, Vietnamese and Pacific languages for family-obligation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** welfare and rations records naming individuals; the politics of compulsory income management; cultural material on obligation that belongs to the community.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
