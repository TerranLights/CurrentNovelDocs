# Australia: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania (the mainland, Tasmania and the Torres Strait; the tracker records no change to its pre-war lands). **Language(s) for searching:** Australian English; Aboriginal English and Kriol; Aboriginal languages of the main language regions (Warlpiri, Pitjantjatjara, Arrernte, Yolŋu Matha); Torres Strait languages (Meriam Mir, Yumplatok); the largest community languages (Mandarin, Greek, Vietnamese). **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, beginnings, naming.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aboriginal peoples by language region (desert, Top End, south-east, Kimberley, Tasmania's palawa), and Torres Strait Islanders, for personhood and naming set in country and kin.
- Urban against remote communities, for how the same people live two kinds of self.
- Beach cities (Sydney, the Gold Coast, Perth) against Melbourne and the inland bush.
- Post-war migrant communities: Greek, Italian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Lebanese, Pacific Islander, Māori; name order and name-day habits differ.
- Old families (convict, pastoral) against recent arrivals; gender and age.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the census, names, body-image and beach culture; rich but access-controlled for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander naming; thinner for religion-linked naming in small migrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal-language dictionaries via AIATSIS and community language centers; Mandarin, Greek and Vietnamese for diaspora naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names and images of the dead (many communities avoid them; archives carry warnings); restricted ceremonial knowledge; moiety and skin-name rules belong to the peoples; removed-children histories.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Census ancestry and Indigenous-status tables, latest year, with the language-spoken-at-home table.

5. Wierzbicka 1986 and the Cultural Scripts Australian English page, for the language side.
