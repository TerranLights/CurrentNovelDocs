## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Young men and women; teenagers and Schoolies cohorts; older drivers.
- Rural and remote communities (long roads, farm machinery, quad bikes) against capital cities.
- Surf and coastal communities against inland towns; the Top End against the south.
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities, whose risk vocabulary and harms differ by region.
- Migrant communities by generation, handled from data and never by stereotype.
- Workers in mining, fishing and trades; investors, small-business owners and punters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for road, drowning, substance and gambling statistics and for campaign material; mixed for Aboriginal accounts; thin for private risk-taking and for non-English-speaking communities' own terms.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal languages via community centers; Chinese, Greek, Arabic and Vietnamese for diaspora vocabularies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths and drowning data; volatile-substance and alcohol harm in named communities; gambling harm; Uluru is sacred ground to Anangu.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
