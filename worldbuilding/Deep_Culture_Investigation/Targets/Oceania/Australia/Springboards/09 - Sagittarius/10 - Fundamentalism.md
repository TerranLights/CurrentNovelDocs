# Australia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Greek, Italian, Arabic, Chinese, Vietnamese; Yolngu Matha for the Arnhem Land accounts. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; the word itself is contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Within each faith: strict against moderate (conservative Sydney Anglican and Pentecostal against liberal Anglican and Uniting; traditional Catholic against mainstream Catholic).
- Closed groups (Brethren; ultra-Orthodox Jewish communities; some Orthodox traditionalists) against open evangelical churches.
- Muslim communities by origin (Lebanese, Turkish, Bosnian, and others) and by view.
- Aboriginal Christian revival communities in Arnhem Land and elsewhere against traditional-law communities.
- Rural, regional and outer-suburban strict congregations against the cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official threat assessments and academic work on extremism; mixed for closed groups; thin for ordinary members' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** English; Arabic, Greek, Italian, Chinese and Vietnamese for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fundamentalism" is a contested label and an insult in some uses; high-control group and abuse cases; terrorism material; leavers' safety. Name no group as fundamentalist unless the source does.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read two or three ASIO annual threat assessments for the categories and vocabulary.
2. Look up "wowser" in the Australian National Dictionary and read Dunstan's *Wowsers* summaries.
3. Search state education-department sites for home-education registration counts.
4. Read the ABS classification of religious groups for how small denominations are coded.
5. Search Trove for "Exclusive Brethren" and "Orange Order" in one decade.
