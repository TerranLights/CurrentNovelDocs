# Australia: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian and Aboriginal English), Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, Samoan, Greek, Italian, Arabic, Chinese, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose: a person contributing and being needed, or a thing or piece of knowledge with practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aboriginal peoples** (Warlpiri, Anangu, Koori, Murri, Noongar): elders' roles, practical bush skill, children's learning.
- **Torres Strait Islanders** and **Pacific communities**: extended-family and church roles.
- **Bush and coast against the city:** the shed and the farm against the apartment.
- **Older men** (Men's Sheds) against older women and carers.
- **Migrant elders** (Greek, Italian, Lebanese, Chinese, Vietnamese): children as translators, grandparents' child care.
- **People with disability** and those on welfare, against employed workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, inquiries and the Men's Shed literature; thin for private feelings of uselessness. No scope file exists; post-war lands are unrecorded, so the core mainland and Tasmania are assumed.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous, Pacific and migrant language terms with community help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eugenic and institutional histories, inquiry testimony, and the deaths of Elders; Elder knowledge is community-held. Pre-war sources need a post-war check. The legal side of "terra nullius" belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search "Men's Shed" with "purpose" or "isolation" for the Golding studies and AMSA material.
2. Read the Willing to Work and aged-care inquiry summaries for how usefulness is spoken of.
3. Look for explorers' words ("useless", "barren") in Sturt and Eyre, then Reynolds.
4. Watch or search *Bush Mechanics* and *Mother and Son* for two very different pictures of the useful person.
