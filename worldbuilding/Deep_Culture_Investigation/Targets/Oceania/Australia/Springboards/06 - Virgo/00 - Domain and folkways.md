# Australia: Domain and folkways, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Australia, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Australian English, Aboriginal English, Kriol), Auslan, the main migrant languages, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages. **Concept in one line:** what is good work and a well-kept life, covering work, service, daily routine, health, cleanliness, chores, craft, rest and the work ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aboriginal peoples**: desert (Anangu, Warlpiri), Arnhem Land (Yolngu), Noongar, Koori and Murri; hunter-gatherer rounds against station, mission and CDEP work.
- **Torres Strait Islanders** and **Pacific Islander** (Australian South Sea Islander) descendants: sea work, pearling and cane.
- **State and region**: Queensland sugar and mining, Victoria's trades halls, South Australia's free-settler heritage, Western Australia's mining, Tasmania, the Northern Territory, the outback against the coastal suburb.
- **Migrant waves**: British and Irish, Chinese goldfields, Italian and Greek postwar, Lebanese, Vietnamese, recent arrivals.
- **Occupational worlds**: shearers and drovers, tradies, miners, nurses, farmers, public servants, volunteers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler society (statistics, Trove, union archives); thinner and less public for Aboriginal ways, where the researcher should follow community-held sources. No scope file exists; the post-war lands are unrecorded, so this sheet is written for the core Australian mainland and Tasmania.
- **Languages needed:** English; Aboriginal English and Kriol terms; migrant languages for oral histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing, men's and women's business and the work of ngangkari are restricted knowledge, not to be extracted; some archives, names and images of deceased Aboriginal people carry protocols. Pre-war sources may need a post-war continuity check.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Trove for "eight hours" and "working man's paradise" to see how work and rest were argued over.
2. Pull the ABS time-use tables on paid work, housework and volunteering by sex.
3. Read Ward and McQueen on the bush legend, then check Obituaries Australia for the phrases used to praise a working life.
4. Search for NACCHO and RFDS histories to see how health is grouped with place and community.
