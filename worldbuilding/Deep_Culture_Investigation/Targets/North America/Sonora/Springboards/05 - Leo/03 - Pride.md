# Sonora: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages of the desert borderlands. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the Sonoran Desert core.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Pride of place: Sonoran ranching towns, the Baja border cities, Arizona retirement and desert communities, Tijuana's migrants.
- Indigenous peoples (Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac, O'odham, Cucapá, Kumeyaay, Apache), each with its own sources of pride and its own history of attack on it.
- Mexican American and Chicano communities against Mexico-born residents.
- Working-class pride in mining, farming, fishing, assembly work.
- LGBTQ communities and Pride organizers; church-based and secular groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Survey items on pride are national, rarely regional; regional pride is best found in press, slogans and community archives.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous terms through community guidance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous histories of loss and removal; identity-related material about movements.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Mexican and U.S. national pride items from ISSP and Latinobarómetro, then look for state breakdowns.
2. Search *"orgullosamente sonorense"* and *"orgullo de ser"* with each major city name.
3. Request tribal statements and cultural-center pages on heritage pride.
4. Read Ramos and a recent reply, then ask what local sources test the claim.
