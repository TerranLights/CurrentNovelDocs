## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sonoran *norteño* ranch and town culture against Baja California's border and port cities, and Arizona's Anglo, Mexican American and retirement communities.
- Indigenous peoples with their own festival and games traditions: Yoeme (Yaqui), Mayo (Yoreme), Comcaac (Seri), O'odham, Cucapá and Kumeyaay, and Apache nations; compare as separate peoples, not as one.
- Recent arrivals from Mexico's interior and elsewhere against families rooted for generations.
- Urban against rural; church-centered against secular celebration; men's and women's sport.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Sport and festivals are well documented; courtship and private play are thin. U.S. side is richer in surveys, Mexican side in local press and folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language terms only through each people's own dictionaries and teachers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial dance, song and masks held by Indigenous communities may be restricted; seek what peoples have shared publicly, and never ask for the rest.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
