# Sonora: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages of the desert borderlands. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the Sonoran Desert core.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Farmworkers, miners, maquiladora and domestic workers against professionals, ranchers and retirees.
- Migrants in transit and in shelters; deported residents; long-settled households.
- Indigenous peoples (Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac, O'odham, Cucapá, Kumeyaay, Apache), each with its own sense of the dignity of a people and a place.
- Old people living alone, in families or in care homes; disabled people.
- Urban against rural; church-based charities against secular and state services.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Surveys exist at national scale; felt dignity is thin outside testimony, oral history and organizational reports.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous terms via community guidance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school survivors' testimony; migrants' deaths and abuse; end-of-life accounts; identify no individuals.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
