*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages of the desert borderlands. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the Sonoran Desert core.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Children of border cities, ranch towns and desert villages; Arizona suburbs against Sonoran colonias.
- Indigenous peoples (Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac, O'odham, Cucapá, Kumeyaay, Apache), who hold their own games and teach them in their own schools.
- Boys and girls; school-age children against teenagers and adults at play.
- Class: scheduled childhoods against street and farm childhoods; families of working children.
- Migrant and transit children in shelters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Time-use and industry data are well documented; street play and local rhymes are thin and need fieldwork or older collections.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous games need the peoples' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children, so no identifying data; games with ceremonial meaning held by Indigenous communities; shelter children.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
