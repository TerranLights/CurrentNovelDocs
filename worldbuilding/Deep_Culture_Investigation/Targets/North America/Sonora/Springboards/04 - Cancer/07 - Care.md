# Sonora: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, O'odham, Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Daughters, wives, sons, grandchildren and paid helpers as carers; Mexican and U.S. care systems for older people; rural and city care; migrants caring from afar for kin at home; carers of the disabled and of the dying; folk healers against clinics; the Native nations with their own healing and clinics; retirees without nearby kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for time use, surveys and agency reports; mixed for folk healing; thin for carers' feelings.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Native terms as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing knowledge and ceremonies belong to their people; health records and abuse cases are private. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ENASIC and ENUT care tables for Sonora and caregiving tables for Arizona.
2. Read the MHAS documentation and list its caring items.
3. Read Trotter and Chavira beside current clinic leaflets.
4. Attend, or find accounts of, the Magdalena pilgrimage and the vows offered there.
5. Read a month of obituaries for the words used about carers.
