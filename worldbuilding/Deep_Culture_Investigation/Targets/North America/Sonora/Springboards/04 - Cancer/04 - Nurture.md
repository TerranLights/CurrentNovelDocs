# Sonora: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, O'odham, Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Mothers, grandmothers and older siblings as carers; urban professional against ranch, farm and mining-town families; Spanish-dominant against English-dominant households; the Native nations, each with its own teaching of the young; migrant families with children split across the border; households using paid carers; adoptive and foster homes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for time use, schooling and health guidance; mixed for parents' own theories; thin for Native teaching, much of it held by the nations.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Native languages as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school history and child-protection cases need care; teachings are the nations' to publish. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ENUT child-care hours for Sonora and the matching ATUS items for Arizona.
2. Read Rogoff, then search the interview literature on Mexican-heritage parents.
3. Collect pediatric and IMSS feeding and sleep leaflets from both sides.
4. Ask CONAFE and the tribal education offices what they publish on teaching the young.
5. Search regional radio and school archives for lullabies and Día del Niño programs.
