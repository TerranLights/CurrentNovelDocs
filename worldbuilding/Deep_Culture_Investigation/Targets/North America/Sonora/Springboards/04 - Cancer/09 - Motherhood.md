# Sonora: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, O'odham, Yoeme, Mayo, Comcaac. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it, including the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Biological, adoptive, foster and step mothers; grandmothers, aunts and *comadres* who mother; single, teen and older mothers; working mothers against those at home; mothers in ranch, city and mining-town settings; migrant mothers sending money or left behind; Native mothers within each nation's own teaching; Latter-day Saint and Protestant mothers against Catholic ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health, labor and household data; mixed for ideals of the mother; thin for mothers' own words outside surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Native terms as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal death, loss of children and birth ceremonies belong to their families and nations. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the latest INEGI Mother's Day sheet for Sonora and the ENSANUT birth items.
2. Read Hill Collins and Stevens, and list which claims need local testing.
3. Collect the songs and radio dedications aired around May 10 in Hermosillo and Tucson.
4. Watch two Golden Age mother films and note the mother's roles, to compare.
5. Ask the midwifery and tribal women's offices what they publish.
