# Sonora: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, O'odham, Yoeme, Comcaac. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means: dwelling, household base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Owners and renters; city, ranch and valley-town homes; self-builders against tract-estate buyers; border-straddling families with a house on each side; retirees and seasonal residents against lifelong residents; reservation and off-reservation Native households; Comcaac and Yoeme villages with their own home territories; new migrants in shared housing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tenure and housing quality; mixed for home as feeling; thin for the home of seasonal and unrecorded residents.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Native terms only as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a people's ties to its territory are stated in its own publications; use those. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the ENVI and CONEVAL tables for Sonora and the ACS tenure tables for southern Arizona.
2. Read Ríos's memoir beside one Zepeda collection to see how home is written on each side.
3. Search newspaper archives for "desalojo", "eviction" and house-blessing notices.
4. Compare ten real-estate listings on each side of the border for the words that sell a home.
5. Write to the tribal housing offices about what they publish.
