## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sonoran-born Spanish speakers on the Mexican side, and Arizona-born English and bilingual speakers: different labels and different census questions.
- Yoeme in the Yaqui villages, Yoeme in Arizona, Yoreme, O'odham (two main divisions), Comcaac, Cucapá, Pima: each its own named people and its own relation to the border.
- Descendants of Chinese, Lebanese, Mormon, Basque and Anglo settlers: separate heritage societies.
- Urban against ranching and mining towns; old families against recent migrants from the south.
- Veterans, miners and cross-border commuters: occupational identities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census categories and borderlands literature; mixed for Spanish-language primary introductions; thin for first-person Comcaac and Cucapá voices in print.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages for primary sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** impostor or false-claim disputes over Native identity and enrollment are charged topics; tribal membership rules belong to the tribes. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
