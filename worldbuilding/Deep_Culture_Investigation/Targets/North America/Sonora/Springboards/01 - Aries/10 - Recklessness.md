## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Young men, young women and older drivers: separate groups for risk behavior.
- Border-town youth, farm workers, miners, ranchers and professionals.
- Mexican-side and U.S.-side driving cultures, taken separately; resort visitors against residents.
- Indigenous communities (Yoeme, Yoreme, O'odham, Comcaac, Cucapá, Apache): each its own cautionary tales and concept of risk.
- Gamblers against investors against thrill-seekers: three kinds of recklessness.
- Migrants whose risks are imposed, set apart from chosen risks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for road, health and rescue statistics; mixed for informal risk culture; thin for first-person accounts of "recklessness" as a word.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug-trade violence and migrant deaths are live and dangerous to approach; traffic and drug-use statistics are not portraits of a people. Fatalism and religious sayings belong to believers, so do not mock them. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
