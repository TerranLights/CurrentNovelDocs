## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Hermosillo against Ciudad Obregon and Navojoa; Tucson against Phoenix; Nogales on each side of the line.
- Baseball towns against charro towns against football towns.
- Mexican-side and U.S.-side school and university systems.
- Yoeme, Yoreme, O'odham, Comcaac, Cucapá: traditional contests and games, taken separately.
- Grape, wheat and cattle producers against urban professionals: the contests of livelihood.
- Girls and boys, men and women in sport.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for baseball, school and rankings data; mixed for envy beliefs; thin for traditional Indigenous contests in print.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling stories involve organized crime in places; evil-eye and healing beliefs belong to practitioners; use only what is published. Match-fixing and doping accounts need sourcing. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search league and club histories for rivalries by town, not by nation.
2. Pull SEP and Arizona assessment data for the core counties and municipalities.
3. Search envy proverbs and *mal de ojo* in regional folklore collections.
4. Search press archives for fair, rodeo and charreada contest reports.
5. Ask tribal cultural centers whether traditional games are published for outsiders.
