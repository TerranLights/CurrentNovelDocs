**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, Yoeme, O'odham, Yoreme. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled, for persons and for groups.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families that keep adult children at home against households that send them out: compare by region and class.
- Yoeme pueblos and Yoreme communities, which have their own traditional authorities; O'odham districts; Comcaac; Cucapá: collective autonomy as each describes it.
- Ejido members and private ranchers on the Mexican side; ranchers on the Arizona side.
- Migrants and border-crossing households split between two systems.
- Mormon colonies, Mennonites and homeschooling networks: separate claims over schooling and family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household surveys and for Yaqui history; mixed for Mayo and Seri collective self-rule in print; thin for what O'odham and Comcaac themselves say about "autonomy" as a word.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Yoeme and O'odham for primary materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous governance and ceremony may be restricted by the peoples; use what they publish. Legal status of tribes, ejidos and sovereignty is law: point to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit). Water and land conflicts are live disputes. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
