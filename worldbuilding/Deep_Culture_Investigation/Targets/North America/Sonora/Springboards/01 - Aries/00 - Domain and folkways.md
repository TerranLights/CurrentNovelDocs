## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Urban Hermosillo, Cajeme and Tucson residents against rural ranch and mining towns: different working bodies and dress.
- Yoeme (Yaqui) in Sonora and in Arizona (Pascua Yaqui Tribe), Yoreme (Mayo), O'odham, Comcaac, Cucapá: each names itself and its persons its own way.
- Mormon colonies of the northern Sierra and Arizona, Mennonite and Anglo ranching families, Chinese-descended families of Sonoran towns: separate naming and dress customs.
- Recent migrants from southern Mexico and from the United States interior; border-crossing families split between two civil registries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Yaqui and O'odham (decades of ethnography); mixed for Spanish-speaking ranch and city cultures; thin for the Seri on naming beyond ethnobotany.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Yoeme, O'odham and Comcaac for primary materials (translations exist for some).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ceremonial and naming knowledge may be restricted by the peoples; use only what they have published or released. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands (Sonora and southern Arizona).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
