## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Households that live on wages, remittances, retirement income or farming: independence means different things by income source.
- Irrigated-valley farmers, desert ranchers, mining towns and cities.
- Yoeme, Yoreme, O'odham, Comcaac, Cucapá: food sovereignty and reciprocity as each describes it.
- Latter-day Saints colonies, retirees and snowbirds, off-grid dwellers.
- Multigenerational families against single-household independent living.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income, transfer and household statistics; mixed for food sovereignty; thin for first-person accounts of "independence" as a word.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** secession and regional-independence talk (a Sonoran state of the 1850s and later fancies) is a historical topic to handle through histories only; welfare and immigrant-status questions can be sensitive; Indigenous food and seed knowledge may be restricted. Sovereignty status is law: Libra and Capricorn. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

1. Pull ENIGH and BEA transfer tables for Sonora and southern Arizona side by side.

3. Search both extension services and the food banks' reports for "self-sufficiency".
4. Read the nonprofit publications of Native Seeds/SEARCH and Tohono O'odham Community Action.
5. Search *mantenido* and *salir adelante* in interviews and proverb collections.
