## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Indigenous peoples whose beginnings are told as emergence from this land (Yoeme, O'odham, Yoreme, Comcaac, Cucapá) against settler founding stories of Spanish presidios, the Gadsden-era border and mining towns.
- Migrants arriving from southern Mexico and Central America, and retirees arriving from the north.
- Border-split families: the same relatives telling different founding days.
- Longtime ranch and mining families against recent arrivals in Tucson and Hermosillo.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler founding histories and migration data; mixed for recovery and second-chance culture; thin for origin accounts told by Indigenous peoples in their own voice.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous languages for primary origin texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin and emergence stories and seasonal ceremonies (including Yaqui Lenten rites) may be restricted or not for outsiders; use only what the peoples have published. Migrants' narratives need care over safety. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet follows the core lands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
