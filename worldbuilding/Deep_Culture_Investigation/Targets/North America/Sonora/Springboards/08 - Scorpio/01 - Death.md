# Sonora: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, and the nations' own languages through their programs. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means; grief and mourning are here, funeral practice is in the domain sheet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Sonoran Catholic families and border Mexican Americans; Santa Muerte devotees; evangelical converts who reject the *ofrenda*; Latter-day Saint communities; O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac and Apache nations with their own teachings; retirees in Arizona's age-restricted cities; migrants and the families of the dead at the border; farmworkers and miners; women and men. Compare them because the dead are present, owed prayer, owed work, or simply gone, depending on the community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Mexican death culture and statistics; mixed on Native teachings; thin on private dying. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Native languages through the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native teachings about death and the dead are often restricted; use only what each nation publishes. Migrant deaths and grieving families need respect; no names.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull INEGI death tables for Sonora and CDC WONDER for Arizona.

3. Read Chesnut on Santa Muerte and search for Sonoran shrines.
4. Search the nations' cultural offices for public statements on death.
5. Search the GSS and WVS afterlife items for the region.
