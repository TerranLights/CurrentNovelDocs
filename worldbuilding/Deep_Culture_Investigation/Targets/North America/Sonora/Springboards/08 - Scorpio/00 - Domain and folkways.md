# Sonora: Domain and folkways, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, and the Native languages of the Sonoran Desert lands (O'odham, Yaqui/Yoeme, Mayo/Yoreme, Comcaac/Seri, Yuman languages). **Domain in one line:** how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden: death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance, secrets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Border-city Mexican American and Sonoran families (kin across the line); Anglo Arizona retirees and ranchers; O'odham (Tohono and Akimel); Yaqui and Mayo; the Comcaac; Yuman peoples of the Colorado delta; Apache; Latter-day Saint colonies; Mennonite and other Chihuahuan farm communities; Chinese Mexican communities of Mexicali; recent migrants. Compare them because death rites, inheritance and secrecy differ by faith, kin network and land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on border death and Mexican death culture; mixed on Native ritual; thin on sex ways at village level. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Native-language work through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native ceremony and burial knowledge is restricted by many nations; use only what they publish. Disappearance and cartel material is dangerous; use families' and journalists' public accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Lomnitz's chapters and then search "Sonora" plus *velorio* and *panteón* in El Colegio de Sonora's publications.
2. Pull the medical examiner's and Colibrí Center's public reports on border remains.
3. Search INEGI's mortality tables for Sonora municipalities.
4. Search the nations' own sites for what they publish on burial and mourning.
5. Search "tandas Sonora" and "ejido agua Sonora" for pooling and commons.
