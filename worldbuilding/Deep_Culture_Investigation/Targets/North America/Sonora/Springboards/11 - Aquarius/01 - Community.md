# Sonora: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham, Seri for the Indigenous nations. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a place, faith, trade or origin, recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and how that bond feels.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Border cities (Tucson, Nogales, San Luis Río Colorado, Mexicali), mid-size cities (Hermosillo, Yuma), and ranch or ejido towns.
- The Ocho Pueblos Yaquis, Mayo towns, the O'odham districts, the Comcaac villages: each its own community form.
- Renters and owners; planned retirement communities against old barrios.
- Mormon-descended colonies in northern Mexico against other rural towns.
- Recently arrived migrants from the south of Mexico against families long in place.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. surveys; mixed for Mexican state data; thin for small towns and Indigenous communities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages for first-hand accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial and village-governance knowledge may be restricted; use what each nation publishes. Places hit by violence and disappearances call for care.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. INEGI: ENUT and ENCUCI tables for Sonora; compare with the Arizona supplement.
2. Search the Arizona Historical Society for "barrio" and "neighborhood association".
3. Search "3x1 para migrantes Sonora" for hometown-association projects.
4. Read each Indigenous nation's own site for its description of community and governance.
