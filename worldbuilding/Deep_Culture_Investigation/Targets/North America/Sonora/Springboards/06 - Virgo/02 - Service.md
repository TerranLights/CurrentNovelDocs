## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Yaqui pueblos, where ceremonial duties are held within the community; O'odham villages and church life; Mayo communities; Comcaac; Catholic parish and festival communities against Latter-day Saint colonies with callings and missions; migrant-aid volunteers against the migrants they serve; hotel, resort and casino workers; households that employ domestic help against those that do not; military families around the bases; border-town service economies against farm valleys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering, military and border aid; mixed for domestic service; thin for Indigenous ceremonial duty in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages for duty and ceremony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yaqui and O'odham ceremonial offices and rites may be closed; ask the community. Migrant testimony needs care (safety, consent). Law-related service questions go to Libra and Capricorn. Exact lands of the nation are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search "servicio social" with Universidad de Sonora and Colegio de Sonora for how students describe it.
2. Pull the Arizona volunteering rates from the Census supplement.
3. Read the Kino Border Initiative's and No More Deaths' published reports for volunteer accounts.
4. Search Spicer on Yaqui offices, then ask which parts are public.
5. Query dictionaries of Yaqui, O'odham and Seri for "serve" and "help".
