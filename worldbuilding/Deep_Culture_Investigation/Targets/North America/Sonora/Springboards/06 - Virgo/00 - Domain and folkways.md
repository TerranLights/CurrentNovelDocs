## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Yoeme (Yaqui) of the eight river pueblos and the Pascua Yaqui in Tucson; Yoreme (Mayo); Tohono and Akimel O'odham; Comcaac (Seri) of the coast; Guarijío; Cucapá of the Colorado delta; Apache and Yavapai communities; ranching and irrigated-valley Sonorans; border factory towns (Nogales, Agua Prieta, San Luis Río Colorado); copper towns (Cananea, Nacozari, Bisbee, Morenci); Latter-day Saint colonists and their descendants; long-settled Tucson Mexican American families against recent arrivals; retirees from elsewhere; descendants of Chinese, Lebanese and other immigrants. Candidate units, to be settled at Stage 0.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and mining and farm labor history; mixed for household and cleanliness ways; thin for Indigenous daily routine outside ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing knowledge and ceremonial calendars of Indigenous peoples may be restricted; ask the nation, not the archive. Medical research with Indigenous communities has its own ethics history (tribal review). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Law:** labor law belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
