*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham, Seri and Guarijío communities facing imposed improvement and running their own renewal; irrigated-valley farmers against dryland ranchers; border-colonia self-builders against planned suburbs; first-generation college students; Latter-day Saint colonies and their self-reliance programs; recovery and Pentecostal congregations; retirees who arrived to remake the desert; those who left for work abroad and returned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for agriculture, water and education history, thin for personal aspiration outside surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages for renewal terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school and land-seizure history is painful for living communities; survivors speak first. Language programs and ceremonies are the nations' own. Law excluded. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search "superación" and "salir adelante" with Sonora in press, schools and radio.
2. Read Voss and Almada Bay for the language of progress in the state.
3. Pull ESRU-EMOVI on beliefs about getting ahead.
4. Search "Phoenix Indian School" and INI materials, then ask the nations how they want it told.
5. Query Barrio Viejo oral histories for residents' words for the clearance.
