# Sonora: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (northern Mexican usage) and English; Indigenous languages for community sources. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own or someone else's rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Women and men, whose pressures to stay quiet may differ.
- Household and workplace settings, and small-town against city.
- Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham and Comcaac communities, with their own ways of settling disputes.
- Church members against nonreligious people.
- Newcomers and undocumented residents, who may have more to lose by speaking.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed; reporting surveys exist, while the meaning of silence must come from fieldwork and stories.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extortion, abuse and cartel pressure are dangerous topics; use published surveys and press only, never individual people. Tribal dispute settlement may be restricted. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ENVIPE and ENVE state tables on reasons for not reporting.
2. Search library catalogs for Díaz-Guerrero.
3. Search "mal arreglo" and "calladita" with "Sonora".
4. Read the Chavez fast and Bisbee episodes for confrontation against silence.
5. Ask the state human-rights commission what it publishes.
