# Sonora: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (northern Mexican usage), English, plus O'odham, Yaqui/Yoeme, Mayo/Yoreme and Comcaac for community sources. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others, and the steadiness that results in a person, household, society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Irrigated farm valleys against dryland ranching and desert towns.
- Farmers, miners, cities and tribal nations, which hold different shares in water decisions.
- Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham and Comcaac communities, whose teachings on balance may be their own.
- Healers and clinic-trained people: how each frames health.
- Salaried workers against day laborers and shift workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; water and time use are rich; folk and Indigenous senses of balance are thin in print and best from the communities' own statements.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yaqui and Comcaac ceremonial knowledge and O'odham teachings may be restricted. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the ENUT and ATUS tables on paid work, care and rest.
2. Read the Colorado River compact documents and the groundwater act for the written balance among users.
3. Search "equilibrio" with "Sonora" in El Colegio de Sonora's publications.
4. Look at the Trotter and Chavira book for the hot-cold framework.
5. Ask tribal cultural offices for public statements.
