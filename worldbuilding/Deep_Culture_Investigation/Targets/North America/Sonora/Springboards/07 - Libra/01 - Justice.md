# Sonora: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (northern Mexican usage), English, Yaqui/Yoeme, Mayo/Yoreme, O'odham, Comcaac (Seri). **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered. Libra takes the letter of the law only: what statutes, constitutions and courts say as written.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Rural ranching and farming Sonora against the Hermosillo and Tucson metros.
- Yaqui (Yoeme), Mayo (Yoreme), Tohono O'odham, Comcaac, Guarijío and Pima communities, each with its own authorities.
- Mexican-heritage and Anglo-heritage Arizonans, and long-settled families against recent arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on written law and court structure; mixed on how people feel about courts; thin on tribal court practice in print.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous languages for community-held material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal justice processes and some Yaqui and Comcaac knowledge may be closed to outsiders. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the constitutional articles and procedure code.

4. Request tribal court codes from the tribal court offices.
5. Search *Arizona Daily Star* and *El Imparcial* archives for "justicia" and "injustice" with a recent notable case.
