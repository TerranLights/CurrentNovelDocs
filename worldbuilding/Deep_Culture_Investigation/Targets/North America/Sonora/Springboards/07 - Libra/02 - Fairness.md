# Sonora: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (northern Mexican usage), English, plus Indigenous languages of the Sonoran Desert. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Ejido* members, private ranchers, orchard and vegetable-field laborers.
- Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham and Comcaac communities, where rules of sharing may be their own.
- Maquiladora workers against professionals in Hermosillo and Phoenix.
- Newcomers against families settled for generations.
- Generations: elders and young adults.
- Mixed-heritage households that carry two sets of habits about bills, inheritance and favors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; opinion data is rich at the national level and thin at the state level; everyday practice such as splitting bills must come from fieldwork and fiction.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** distribution customs of Indigenous communities may be closed to outsiders. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the ENADIS and ENCIG state tables for Sonora.
2. Search the Rocky Mountain Poll and Arizona pulse archives for "fair" items.
3. Search regional newspapers for "no se vale", "injusto" and "unfair" beside water, pay and prices.
4. Look for studies in *Región y Sociedad* on shares in *ejidos* and cooperatives.
5. List *compadrazgo* and favor words for a later pass under Partnership.
