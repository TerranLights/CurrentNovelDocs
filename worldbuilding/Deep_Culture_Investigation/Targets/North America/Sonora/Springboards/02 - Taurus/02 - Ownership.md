# Sonora: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources for O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo and Seri lands. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, exclude others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous nations as collective owners:** Tohono O'odham, Akimel O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac, Cocopah, Apache: territory split by the border or by reservation lines.
- **Ejidatarios, small private owners and large ranch or agribusiness holders:** valleys of the Yaqui and Mayo rivers against sierra ranches against Hermosillo's coast.
- **Anglo and Mexican-heritage ranch families in southern Arizona;** and **foreign second-home owners** on the Sonoran coast.
- **Latter-day Saint colonists** in sierra settlements, a separate land-founding story.
- **Renters, squatters and mobile-home owners** in border cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal tenure (registries and censuses, on each side separately); thin for informal holdings and for what owners say about their claims. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous-language sources for territory teachings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred sites and territory knowledge held by nations; land disputes still live; use only what nations publish.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** the legal rules of land and water (agrarian law, concessions) go to Libra and Capricorn; here only records and talk of owning.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Sonora's *ejido* and community land shares from the Registro Agrario Nacional; compare Arizona's public-land shares.
2. Search REPDA and Arizona water-right records for who holds irrigation water in the Yaqui valley and the Colorado basin.
3. Read the Sheridan and Spicer indexes for "land", "title", "boundary".
4. Search the Hermosillo dailies for *invasión de terrenos* and *propiedad*.
5. Look up each nation's own published statements on territory.
