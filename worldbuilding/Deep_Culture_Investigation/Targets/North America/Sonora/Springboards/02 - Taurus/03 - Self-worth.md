# Sonora: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources for O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo and Seri speakers. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous peoples:** O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac, Apache: each has its own account of the damage of boarding schools and of renewal.
- **Class and occupation:** miners, ranch hands, farmworkers, maquila workers, professionals and retirees.
- **Color and ancestry:** darker-skinned and lighter-skinned families, mestizo and Indigenous identities, as people speak of them.
- **Generation of migration:** recent arrivals, long-settled Tucsonenses, and Sonoran families split across the border.
- **Men and women:** who is expected to provide, and what happens to worth when that fails.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Survey scales exist but are not designed for the region; most rich material is memoir, fiction, song and interview. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and mental-health data (handle with care); boarding-school testimony (use what survivors and nations publish); color and caste talk.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Pointers not to use as voices for all:** the national-character essays are one writer's thesis, not the culture's.

## 5. First queries
1. Look for ENADIS and ENBIARE Sonora tables.
2. Search the Arizona YRBS and CDC WONDER for county gaps.
3. Search the dailies for *dignidad*, *humillación*, *orgullo* with "Sonora".
4. Read the Heard Museum and Interior report pages on boarding schools.
5. Find one ethnography of mine, ranch or farm labor and read what it says about workers' pride.
