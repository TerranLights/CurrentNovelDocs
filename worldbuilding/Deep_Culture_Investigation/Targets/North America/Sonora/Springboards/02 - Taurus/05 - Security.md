## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Salaried against informal workers:** public-post holders (*de base*), maquiladora workers, day laborers, farmworkers in the grape and vegetable harvests.
- **Households with relatives across the border** against those without: remittances and dual-country safety nets.
- **Retirees (snowbirds) in gated communities** against long-resident owners and renters.
- **Indigenous nations:** O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac: food security and water access differ by community.
- **Border towns against desert ranches:** differing exposure to crime, heat and flood.
- **Women and men:** fear of crime and who provides security.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys on both sides, but they do not match across the border; mixed for informal practices; thin for how security is spoken of at home. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence and cartel-related reporting (risk to sources; use official data and published reporting only); migrant deaths; domestic abuse.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** the legal content of insurance, pension or border rules goes to Libra and Capricorn.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ENSU and ENVIPE tables for Hermosillo, Nogales and Ciudad Obregón.
2. Compare ENIF savings and insurance items with the Arizona SHED and FINRA figures.
3. Search the county heat-death reports for the vulnerable groups they name.
4. Read the Alianza Hispano-Americana finding aid.
5. Search "guardadito", "tanda" and "plaza de base" in regional publications.
