# Sonora: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources for O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo and Seri. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous nations:** O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac: continuity in place against removal and return, as each nation tells it.
- **Old ranch and farm families against newcomers and snowbirds:** length of residence differs sharply.
- **Seasonal and migratory workers** against year-round residents: harvest and tourism cycles.
- **Mining towns against farm valleys against border cities:** boom-and-bust against steady settlement.
- **Mexican-heritage Tucsonenses** against recent arrivals; **sierra settlements** against the coast.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for moves, job tenure and prices; thin for emotional and routine stability; mixed for family continuity. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** histories of forced removal and deportation (use survivors' and nations' own accounts); political instability; family breakups.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Note:** continuity of seasonal ceremony belongs to nations; use what they publish.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull census residence-five-years-ago tables for Sonora municipalities and Arizona counties.
2. Compare ENOE and BLS job-tenure figures.
3. Search *de toda la vida*, *pueblo de siempre* and "pioneer family" in regional newspapers.
4. Read the Hu-DeHart and Sheridan chapters on removal, return and boom-and-bust.
5. Search the Arizona Memory Project for "centennial", "century" and "family ranch".
