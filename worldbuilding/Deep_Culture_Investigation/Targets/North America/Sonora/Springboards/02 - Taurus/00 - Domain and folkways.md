# Sonora: Possessions and value, with food, dress and wealth ways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish and English; Indigenous languages by people (O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Seri). **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how it is held; plus the folkways of food, dress and wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous peoples:** Tohono O'odham and Akimel O'odham, Yaqui (Yoeme), Mayo (Yoreme), Comcaac (Seri), Pima Bajo, Guarijío, Cocopah, Yavapai, Apache nations: each holds goods, land and water by its own rules.
- **Ranching and farming lands against mining towns against the border cities:** the Yaqui and Mayo valleys, the sierra, copper districts, Nogales and Mexicali-side towns.
- **Mexican-heritage Arizonans (Tucsonenses) against recent arrivals against Anglo retirees (snowbirds):** differing class and tenure.
- **Latter-day Saint colonists in the sierra, Chinese-descended families, Mennonites and Lebanese-descended merchants:** distinct histories of trade and property.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for spending and finance (INEGI and U.S. federal surveys, which split at the border); mixed for Indigenous holdings; thin for what people say about worth, outside memoir and fieldwork. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources for dictionaries and teachings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial goods and sacred objects (Yaqui and O'odham ritual knowledge, regalia), grave goods, and repatriation cases; use only what nations publish themselves.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** land tenure law (*ejido*, water rights) belongs to Libra and Capricorn; point there.

## 5. First queries
1. ENIGH Sonora tables: spending on food, dress and goods; compare Arizona's Consumer Expenditure Survey sample.
2. Search *Región y Sociedad* and *Journal of the Southwest* for "propiedad", "patrimonio", "ganadería", "wealth".
3. Search the Arizona State Museum and the Tohono O'odham Nation's published materials for material-culture and food pages.
4. Search the dailies' archives for *tianguis*, *paca*, swap meet, price rises.
5. Read the Spicer and Sheridan indexes for "property", "land", "cattle", "wages".
