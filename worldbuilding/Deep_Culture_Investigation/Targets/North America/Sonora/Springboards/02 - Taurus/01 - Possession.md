# Sonora: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, and O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo and Seri for Indigenous sources. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and what they keep, use, lend, lose and give away.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *pertenencias, tiliches, triques, cachivaches* | belongings; odds and ends | the last three are informal |
| Spanish | *apartar, es suyo, a sus órdenes* | to set aside; courtesy phrases about objects | see how guests and hosts speak of things |

| Spanish | *herencia, ajuar, recuerdo, reliquia* | inheritance; trousseau; keepsake; relic | family keepsakes |
| Spanish | *segundas, pacas, yonke* | used goods stores, bales, junkyard | secondhand trade near the border |
| English | keepsake, heirloom, swap meet, storage unit, repatriation | | Arizona usage |
| O'odham | *himdag* with object words | way of life, with sacred or personal objects | ask speakers' own glossaries; *check* |

English keywords: material culture, extended self, inalienable possessions, gift exchange, lost and found, curation of belongings.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous nations:** O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac, Cocopah, Apache: separate rules for sacred, family and shared objects, and different histories of seizure and return.
- **Ranch families against town renters against migrant households:** what each keeps, stores and sends.
- **Border pairs (Nogales and its Arizona twin):** goods cross daily; compare what each side keeps.
- **Retired snowbirds against long-settled Tucsonenses:** heirlooms and downsizing differ by origin.
- **Women and men in a family:** who guards the family's things and memories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Census items on goods are rich and comparable by place; talk about attachment is thin outside memoir, fiction and interviews. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language glossaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred objects, grave goods and burial customs (do not use outside what nations publish); repatriation disputes; hidden possessions (guns, cash, debts).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Sonora and Arizona census tables on goods a dwelling has; note the differing questions.
2. Search ENVIPE Sonora for stolen-goods categories.
3. Search "pertenencias" and "tiliches" in the Hermosillo and Tucson dailies.
4. Read the Arizona State Museum repatriation pages for the nations' stated positions.
5. Look up the Saxton dictionary entries for "have", "own", "give".
