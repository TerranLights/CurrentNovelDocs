# Sonora: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources for Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham, Apache and Seri. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Indigenous nations** with distinct histories of resistance and accommodation: Yaqui, Mayo, Tohono O'odham, Apache, Comcaac.
- **Organized labor** (miners, farmworkers) against employers, and **ranchers** against agencies.
- **Neighborhood and homeowner holdouts** against developers.
- **Generations:** elders "set in their ways" against youth who refuse differently.
- **Men and women:** who is called stubborn and who is called determined.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin for everyday stubbornness; rich for named conflicts, strikes and blockades, which are told in opposing ways. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** live land and water disputes, protests with arrests, sacred-site conflicts; use what nations publish themselves.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** the legal side of blockades and rights goes to Libra and Capricorn.

## 5. First queries
1. Search *terco*, *necio* and *no dar el brazo a torcer* in the dailies and in regional proverb collections.
2. Read two accounts of the Yaqui blockades, one from Yaqui authorities and one from the press.
3. Compare BLS and Mexican strike-length data for mining.
4. Read the Hu-DeHart chapters on holding out and return.
5. Search the Tohono O'odham and Apache nations' own statements on refusal and sacred land.
