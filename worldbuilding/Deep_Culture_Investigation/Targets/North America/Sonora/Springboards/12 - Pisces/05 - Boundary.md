# Sonora: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; O'odham, Yaqui, Comcaac and Cucapa sources for territory. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Border-city residents (Nogales, San Luis Rio Colorado, Sonoyta) against people inland.
- The Tohono O'odham Nation and other Indigenous communities whose territory crosses a line.
- Ranchers against urban homeowners.
- Gated-community residents against barrio and village residents.
- Mexican and Mexican American households on family privacy, by generation.
- Families of mixed citizenship, split across the line.
- Conservation groups, Border Patrol families and migrant aid volunteers, each drawing the line differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on border statistics and narrative; mixed on personal-space norms; thin on how Sonoran and Arizonan households draw privacy lines. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the border itself may become internal. If the old international line is dissolved, ask how it is remembered and what replaced it.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous sources through tribal publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** migrant routes, smuggling and shelter locations should not be extracted; sacred sites and boundaries of Indigenous territory may be restricted; sexual-boundary and abuse material needs care.
- **Held for review:** none.
