*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Rural Sonoran towns against Hermosillo, Cajeme and the border cities: help through kin and *compadrazgo* against institutions.
- Mexican American parishes in Tucson against Anglo and newcomer congregations.
- O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo and Comcaac communities, where sharing is organized by the peoples' own institutions.
- Latter-day Saint wards, with their own welfare system.
- Migrants, shelter volunteers and neighbors who object to shelters.
- Retirees and seasonal residents, whose giving is often at a distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the border-aid movement and on giving statistics; mixed on everyday kin help; thin on Indigenous concepts of compassion in print. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Indigenous-language sources only as the peoples publish them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** aid to undocumented people raises safety questions for those named; do not extract names. Do not treat the border groups as one voice; their critics are part of the picture.
- **Held for review:** none.
