## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Yaqui (Yoeme) and Mayo (Yoreme): own governance, offices and ritual calendar.
- Seri (Comcaac), Guarijío, Pima Bajo, Opata descendants, Cucapá: small peoples with separate arrangements.
- O'odham (Tohono and Akimel) and Pascua Yaqui, if Arizona lands are carried.
- Ranch and farm families of the sierra and the Yaqui and Mayo valleys.
- Mining towns (Cananea, Nacozari, Arizona copper towns) against irrigated-farm towns (Ciudad Obregón).
- Border and maquiladora cities (Nogales, Agua Prieta, San Luis Río Colorado) against the capital, Hermosillo.
- Retirees and newcomers in Arizona; Mormon colonies on both sides; Chinese-Mexican families; recent migrants from southern Mexico.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Sonora's political and labor history and on Yaqui ethnohistory; mixed for survey data by state; thin for Seri and Guarijío authority beyond a few ethnographies. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Yaqui and O'odham for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yaqui ritual and governance knowledge shared only on the community's terms; sacred-calendar material; living political rivalries.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
