## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Yaqui, Mayo, Seri and O'odham families, each in their own terms.
- Evangelical, Catholic and Latter-day Saint households.
- Ranch and farm households against the capital's middle class.
- Border maquiladora families, many with shift-working parents.
- Athletes and trainees in sports systems against ordinary schoolchildren.
- Arizona retirees and long-settled Arizona families, if Arizona lands are carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Surveys on child discipline are national; school data are strong on the Arizona side and thin for Sonora; Native boarding-school sources are rich in the U.S. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse in boarding schools and recovery houses; survivors' testimony comes first and on their terms; children's data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Find the ENIM or MICS Mexico tables on discipline and check what each item asks.
2. Search Arizona Department of Education data for discipline by district and race.
3. Search press and human-rights reports for "anexos" with "Hermosillo" or "Sonora".
4. Look for "Phoenix Indian School" survivor testimony in a university archive.
5. Search sports-press profiles of Sonoran baseball players for how they describe training.
