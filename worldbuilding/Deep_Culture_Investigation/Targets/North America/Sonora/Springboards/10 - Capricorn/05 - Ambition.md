*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish; English; Yaqui, Mayo, O'odham for community sources. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Valley farm families against fishing and ranching families.
- Mining-town workers, factory workers and professionals in Hermosillo.
- Yaqui, Mayo, Seri and O'odham youth, whose ambitions may be stated in community terms.
- Migrants and return migrants; border-crossing daily workers.
- Arizona boosters and newcomers against long-settled families, if Arizona lands are carried.
- Women and men, whose ambitions may be judged differently (to check).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Mobility and migration data are national, with some state cuts; political ambition is well covered in Sonoran history; community-level aspiration data are thin. The Opportunity Atlas is not used (held under the source rule). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** living political families; migration status; Native youth interviews need community permission.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; the Opportunity Atlas.

## 5. First queries
1. Open EMOVI and note which items touch hard work, luck and connections; check for state breakdowns.
2. Read a chapter of Aguilar Camín on the rise of the Sonoran leaders and list the family and regional ties he stresses.
3. Search CONAPO for Sonora's migration index and the destinations.
4. Search press for Ciudad Obregón wheat-farm families and farm-to-business histories.
5. Collect five corridos about rising or leaving and note the endings.
