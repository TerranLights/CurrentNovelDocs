*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Yaqui and Mayo ceremonial societies and village offices against non-Native towns.
- Migrant households with members across the border against non-migrant households.
- Eldest-child and eldest-daughter obligations in rural and urban homes (to check, not assume).
- Latter-day Saint colonies, Catholic towns and Protestant congregations.
- O'odham communities, if Arizona lands are carried.
- Fishing communities on the Gulf of California coast; farm families of the valleys.
- Military and veteran families on the U.S. side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Surveys on care and remittances exist by state; duty in ritual life is mostly ethnography, rich for the Yaqui and thin for others. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Yaqui and Mayo for ceremonial vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial offices and rites are described only as communities share them; no photographing of ceremonies; vows are personal.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Banco de México remittance data for Sonora and note the series' dates.
2. Search INEGI for ENASIC tables, "Sonora", and compare their wording of care duties.
3. Search for press accounts of the Magdalena de Kino pilgrimage, listing what vows and sponsors the reporters name.
4. Read the Yaqui ceremonial chapters in Spicer for the offices' duties, then look for the community's own accounts.
5. Search "Servicio Militar Nacional Sonora" for current rules and local stories.
