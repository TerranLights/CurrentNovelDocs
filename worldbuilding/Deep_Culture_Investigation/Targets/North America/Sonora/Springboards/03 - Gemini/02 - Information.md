## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Tucson, Phoenix and Hermosillo urbanites against ranch, desert and border-town residents; those with broadband and those without (reservation and ejido communities); Spanish-language, English-language and bilingual news users; Yaqui, O'odham, Mayo and Seri communities with their own radio and information rules; farmworkers and mining families; younger people on messaging apps against older radio listeners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for access and trust surveys; mixed for local media content; thin for informal channels (rumor, church, shop talk) except where scholars have sampled them.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native knowledge is restricted by the nations; follow their data rules. Reporter safety in Mexico is a live matter; keep contacts private. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Reuters Institute country pages and note which Sonora-relevant items exist.
2. Map county and municipal internet access from the FCC, ACS and INEGI.
3. List local newspapers, radio stations and tribal stations; sample one week of each.
4. Search library catalogs for "corrido" plus "Sonora" and "periodismo" plus "Sonora."
5. Read the Native Nations Institute pages for the data rules each nation sets.
