## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Sonoran, Arizonan and Baja Californian Spanish speakers (rural ranch, mining, port, city); Anglo and Mexican-heritage Arizonans; the O'odham, Yaqui (Yoeme), Mayo (Yoreme), Seri (Comcaac), Kumeyaay, Cucapá, Pai and Apache peoples, each separately; Mennonite and Mormon colonists in Chihuahua and Sonora; Chinese-heritage families in Mexicali; recent migrants from southern Mexico speaking Mixtec or Zapotec; Deaf signers; cross-border commuters against those who stay on one side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Spanish and English varieties and border studies; mixed for Indigenous languages (uneven by nation); thin for small private speech habits.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English; Indigenous languages by nation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native stories, songs and ceremonial speech belong to their peoples; use only what each nation shares. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
