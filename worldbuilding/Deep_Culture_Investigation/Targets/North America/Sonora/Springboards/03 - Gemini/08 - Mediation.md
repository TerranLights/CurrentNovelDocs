# Sonora: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English; Native languages through each nation's own programs. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping sides settle a conflict without imposing a decision, and the go-between more broadly: brokers, interpreters, intercessors.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Catholic, Protestant, Mormon and Mennonite communities (intercession against direct approach); ranch and mining towns against city dwellers (compadre or priest against paid professional); farmworkers and growers; Yaqui, O'odham, Mayo, Seri and Apache peoples, each with its own methods for settling disputes; migrants who need coyotes and fixers against citizens who need none; women as kin-keepers against men as formal brokers; water users upstream and downstream.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for labor and water histories; thin for everyday go-between practice.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peacemaking and council methods belong to the nations; migrant smuggling is dangerous to study. Legal rules of mediation go to Libra and Capricorn. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read one history each of Cananea and Bisbee for named intermediaries.
2. Search the IBWC and CILA sites for Colorado River and Rio Sonora minutes.
3. Ask a tribal justice or family-services office what peacemaking it shares publicly.
4. Search "compadre" plus "conciliación" plus "Sonora" in thesis catalogs.
5. Read local columns on "no te metas" and "metiche."
