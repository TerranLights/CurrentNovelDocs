# Sonora: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, and the Native languages of the Sonoran Desert lands (O'odham, Yaqui/Yoeme, Mayo/Yoreme, Comcaac). **Concept in one line:** going far from home (long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage, migration) and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Border-city families who cross daily; migrants from other Mexican states in transit, and returned migrants; Yaqui and Mayo families with histories of exile; O'odham with kin on both sides of the line; ranchers and miners who rarely leave; winter visitors from the north; students who move to Phoenix or Tucson; tourists at Puerto Peñasco; farmworkers on the seasonal round. Compare them because the road is need for some and pleasure for others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on crossings, remittances and tourism; mixed on Native journeys; thin on how ordinary people value travel itself. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Native terms only via the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** O'odham pilgrimage and trail knowledge is partly restricted. Migrant deaths and smuggling are dangerous; use published accounts and aid groups' public reports.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull EMIF Norte, remittance and tourism tables for Sonora and Arizona.
2. Read Urrea and Steinbeck, then the Anza Trail's pages.
3. Search the Bracero History Archive for Sonoran and Arizona workers.
4. Search the Frontera Collection for corridos of leaving.
5. Ask the nations' own pages how they describe exile, return and the trail.
