# Sonora: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Sonora, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish, English, and the Native languages of the Sonoran Desert lands (O'odham, Yaqui/Yoeme, Mayo/Yoreme, Comcaac). **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true (about God, the world, people, right and wrong, religious or secular) and what warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Catholics (official and folk), Pentecostals and evangelicals, Latter-day Saints, the unaffiliated and humanists; curanderos and their clients; O'odham, Yaqui, Mayo, Comcaac and Yuman peoples, each on its own terms, including Native Christians and traditionalists; border-city Mexican American families; ranchers and miners; students; newcomers and retirees from elsewhere in the U.S. Compare them because the warrant for belief (church, elder, experience, evidence) differs by group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on survey items and folk-belief collections; mixed on Native belief; thin on hidden unbelief. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the rest is written for the Sonoran Desert core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English; Native-language terms only via the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native belief is often restricted; use only what each nation publishes. Folk healing and unbelief in religious families need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Griffith for the regional map of belief, then the Southwest Folklore Center's catalog.
2. Pull belief and moral-acceptability items for Arizona and, where possible, Sonora.
3. Search *leyendas* and *creencias* with "Sonora" in the state culture agencies' catalogs.
4. Search ENADIS for belief-based discrimination in Sonora.
5. Ask the nations' own programs what they publish on stories and belief.
