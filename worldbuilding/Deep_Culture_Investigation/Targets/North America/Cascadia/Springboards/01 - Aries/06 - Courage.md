# Cascadia: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Lands: Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, with Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Tagalog, French and the Indigenous languages of the coast as needed. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Japanese | 勇気 yūki; 我慢 gaman | courage; enduring | gaman is a term to test, not assume |
| Indigenous languages | no terms written | Lushootseed, Haida, Tlingit, Nuu-chah-nulth, Chinook Jargon and others | find terms through each nation's language program and published dictionaries |

English keywords: moral courage, whistleblower, bystander intervention, hero award, firefighter, smokejumper, search and rescue, dangerous occupations, veteran, draft resister, Medal of Bravery.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Washington and Oregon rural and logging communities against Puget Sound and Portland: working-class physical courage against middle-class ideals.
- California by region (Bay Area, Los Angeles, Central Valley farmworking towns, the north coast), and Baja California Sur against Baja California: differing vocabularies and heroes.
- British Columbia, including its Vietnam-era arrivals, against the American lands.
- Mexican-heritage, Japanese American, Chinese, Filipino, Korean and Vietnamese communities: different words, different histories of service and resistance.
- Indigenous nations by language family and legal setting (Washington tribes, BC First Nations, Alaska Panhandle peoples, Baja peoples): warrior traditions as each nation itself describes them.
- Military families, first responders and fishing and logging crews, separated from civilians.
- Men and women; young and old: whether women's endurance is counted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for awards, enlistment, occupational deaths, press and veterans' accounts; mixed for vocabulary and everyday belief; likely thin for Baja and for non-English community sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog and others for community sources; Indigenous languages only through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ceremonial and warrior knowledge may be restricted; use only what a nation publishes. Veterans' trauma and line-of-duty deaths are painful topics; narcocorrido material touches present-day violence.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Carnegie Hero Fund search by Washington, Oregon and California; Canadian bravery decoration lists by BC recipient.
2. Densho and Veterans History Project searches on "courage", "afraid", "gaman" and "brave" for West Coast speakers.
3. BLS fatal-injury tables and NFPA volunteer-firefighter counts, by state.
4. Search the regional newspapers' archives for obituaries and for coverage of one eruption, one wildfire and the 2017 Portland MAX incident *check*.
5. Ask each Indigenous nation's language or culture department what it publishes on courage, rather than assuming terms.
