# Cascadia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Lands carried: Washington, Oregon, California, British Columbia, Baja California, Baja California Sur, and the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, plus the Indigenous peoples of those lands. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French (small, BC); Indigenous languages by unit (below). **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare
- The eleven scope units: Left Coast, BC coast, Far West, El Norte, Sudcalifornia, and the six Indigenous culture areas: each answers "who are you?" from a different founding.
- Settler-born against immigrant generations (Mexican, Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Armenian, Iranian), because the question changes by generation.
- Coast against interior (Far West); border against peninsula (Baja California against Sur).
- Indigenous peoples by nation or band, not as one group: house, clan and village introductions differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian settler and immigrant identity; mixed for Baja California Sur and the Panhandle; thin and community-held for Indigenous identity.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages for self-names and introductions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan, house and lineage knowledge is the nations' to share; false Native-identity claims and enrollment are the nations' own matters; census categories on race are contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the census identity questions (race, ethnic origin, autoadscripción) across the three countries, by era.
2. Search the Environics and Angus Reid archives for "regional identity" and "primary identity", BC and the West.
3. Search library catalogs for "Pacific Northwest identity", "California identity" and "Baja California identidad".
4. Search the museums' sites above for oral histories on "who we are".
5. Ask each Indigenous nation's own cultural department how it shares introductions, before searching further.
