# Cascadia: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Lands: Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean (large immigrant communities); Indigenous languages by nation. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast and BC coast core against Far West interior and El Norte (Southern and Baja California): regional politeness and speaking style.
- Baja California Sur (Sudcalifornia) against Baja California: separate unit proposed in the scope.
- Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and northern Dene peoples: each has its own speaking-order customs.
- Immigrant communities (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Mexican): code-switching between home and workplace norms.
- Gender, class, and newcomers against old families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English-language U.S. and Canadian sources; mixed for Baja (Spanish, thinner online); thin and uneven for Indigenous speaking customs.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages and community-held accounts for the peoples above.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feast and speaker protocols may be restricted knowledge; use only what the nations themselves publish or approve.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. National and state surveys: search GLOBE and Statistics Canada, INEGI and U.S. Census survey catalogs for "assertiveness", "voice" and "complaint".
2. Public comment at city councils in Seattle, Vancouver, Los Angeles and Tijuana: search each clerk's archive.
3. Search "Seattle Freeze" and "Canadian nice" in newspaper archives.
4. Search *igualado* and *dejado* in the Real Academia Española dictionary and Mexican corpora.
5. Contact tribal and First Nations cultural offices (e.g. First Peoples' Cultural Council, *check*) about what may be consulted.
