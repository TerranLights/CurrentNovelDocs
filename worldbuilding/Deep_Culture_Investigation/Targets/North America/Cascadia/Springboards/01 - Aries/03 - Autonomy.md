# Cascadia: Autonomy (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia (post-war), region North America: Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin); Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "my choice", "live and let live", "mind your own business", "don't tread on me", "self-determination" | everyday and political idioms | Also "free-range parenting", "helicopter parent" |
| Spanish (Bajas) | *autonomía*, *libre albedrío*, *cada quien su vida*, *libre determinación*, *usos y costumbres* | autonomy, free will, each to their own life, self-determination, Indigenous self-government | *Ejido*, *asamblea* for collective rule |
| Chinese | 自主 zìzhǔ; 自由 zìyóu | self-direction; freedom | Pair with family-decision terms (e.g. 孝 xiào, filial duty) *check* |
| Indigenous languages | Lushootseed, Lingít (Tlingit), Haida, Nisga'a, Kumeyaay, Kumiai | words for self-rule, deciding, being one's own | Terms not known to me; take them from each nation's language program or dictionary |

English keywords: autonomy, self-rule, independence, consent, paternalism, family decision-making, young adults living with parents, homeschooling, homeowners' association, tribal self-government.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler units:** Left Coast, British Columbia coast, Far West (interior ranching, water and federal-land disputes), El Norte (southern California, Baja California), Sudcalifornia.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene peoples: collective autonomy, kept separate from individual autonomy.
- **Cross-cutting:** age (youth, elders), religion, gender, migrants (Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec in the Bajas; Chinese Canadian and Asian American families), urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for individual autonomy in English and for U.S. surveys; mixed for the Bajas and British Columbia; thin for Native-nation voices outside their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese for Asian-heritage families; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted Native knowledge; family-control and estrangement material; medical and bodily-autonomy topics. Law is not scouted here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. World Values Survey: pull the freedom-of-choice item for the three countries; check for state and province splits.
2. Census, Statistics Canada, INEGI: young adults living with parents, by state, province and entidad.
3. Search each Native nation's own site for "self-government" and "self-determination"; list the terms they use, then ask their language programs for equivalents.
4. Search regional press archives for "helicopter parent", "free-range", "homeowners association" and *usos y costumbres*.
5. Search the Blackhall study's citations for follow-up work in California and the Bajas.
