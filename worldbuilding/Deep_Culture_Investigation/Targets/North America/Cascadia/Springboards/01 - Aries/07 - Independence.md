# Cascadia: Independence (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, Southeast Alaska, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Indigenous languages (see below), some French in British Columbia. **Concept in one line:** not depending on others; self-reliance and self-sufficiency.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, British Columbia coast, Far West (interior and ranch country), Southern California and the Bajas: separate settler cultures with different relations to federal land and help.
- Rural and urban; class; generation; immigrant households with several earners.
- Tribal and First Nations groups, modern-treaty nations, and Bajas communities (Kumiai, Pa ipai, Cucapá, migrant Mixtec and Triqui): food sovereignty, stated by each nation itself.
- Southeast Alaska Native communities and Panhandle settlers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states and British Columbia (statistics, surveys); mixed to thin for the Bajas and for Indigenous nations' own statements.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages for food-sovereignty work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** each nation's teachings and food knowledge are theirs to share; use only what nations publish.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI tables on young adults living with parents, by state or province.
2. Search "self-reliance" and "independent living" in each region's newspaper archives and state or provincial histories.
3. Read Limerick and White for the frontier-versus-federal-dependence thread.
4. Collect the Jefferson and Cascadia independence groups' own pages and pamphlets.
5. Search each tribal and First Nation government's site for "food sovereignty."
