# Cascadia: Competition (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, British Columbia, the Alaska Panhandle, the Bajas). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French (British Columbia, minor); Indigenous languages of the lands (see Flags). **Concept in one line:** striving against others for a prize, a rank or a result.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (S1) and British Columbia coast (S2):** the core; compare for the school, tech and city-rivalry forms.
- **Far West (S3):** rural fairs, rodeo, timber and ranching rivalries against the cities.
- **El Norte (S4) and Sudcalifornia (S5):** Spanish-language terms and charrería; cross-border Baja life.
- **Indigenous units (I1 to I6):** compare by nation, not as one voice; contests are described by each people themselves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport, school and business (U.S. and Canada); mixed for Baja and rural interior; thin for Indigenous understandings in published form.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French; Indigenous languages (Coast Salish, Haida, Tlingit, Tsimshian, Kumiai and others) via each nation's own resources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge belongs to its nation; ask for permission and use published or shared material only. Riot, doping and cheating cases name living people: use public record only.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. World Values Survey: the competition item, filtered to the United States, Canada and Mexico; note whether state or province splits exist.
2. Search each regional high-school athletic association for sportsmanship codes and rivalry-game pages.
3. Search "rivalry" plus each daily newspaper's archive for the named rivalry games and the Cascadia Cup.
4. Search INEGI and Baja state sources for "competitividad" and "charrería".
5. Ask each Indigenous unit's language program for terms for "race", "contest" and "game", and for what the nation has chosen to publish.
