# Cascadia: Domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Lands: Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur (post-war nation, not the old USA, Canada or Mexico). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages by people (below); Cantonese, Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean for large settled communities. **Domain in one line:** what a self is here, and how it asserts itself (self, body, appearance, beginnings, initiative; naming ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast (western WA and OR, coastal California to Monterey); BC coast; Far West interior; El Norte (Southern California and Baja California); Sudcalifornia: the settler units, separated by founding and institutions.
- Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin edge and Dene peoples: where naming and personhood are the peoples' own and not the settlers'.
- Immigrant communities (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Mexican and Central American, Pacific Islander): naming, anglicizing and reclaiming of names.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names, body and survey data in the U.S. and BC lobes; mixed for the Bajas; thin and restricted for Indigenous naming (kept inside the peoples' own institutions).
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages through the peoples' own language programs; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean for immigrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hereditary names, crests and regalia are owned and carry rank; seek the peoples' own publications and permissions. Do not treat naming as a stereotype of any people; name the variation.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
