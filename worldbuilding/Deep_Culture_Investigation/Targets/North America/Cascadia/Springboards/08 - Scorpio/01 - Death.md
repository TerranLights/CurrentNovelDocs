# Cascadia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinuk Wawa and other Indigenous languages; the main immigrant languages. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means; grief and mourning included.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

English keywords: death attitudes, end of life, good death, grief, mourning, bereavement, afterlife belief, hospice, medical aid in dying, overdose deaths, ancestors' remains.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Left Coast settler culture and the British Columbia coast: secular and spiritual-but-not-religious, and the "death positive" scene.
- Far West interior and rural communities: older religious and ranching practice.
- El Norte (southern California, the Bajas) and Mexican American families: Catholic custom and the *Día de Muertos* tradition.
- Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Japanese and Korean heritage communities: Buddhist, Catholic and ancestral practice.
- Indigenous units (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): each separate, since their teachings differ.
- Migrant Mixtec, Triqui and Zapotec communities (San Quintín): separate customs of the dead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for deaths, medical aid in dying and belief surveys; mixed for practice and meaning; thin for Indigenous views and for the Bajas' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinuk Wawa and Indigenous languages through the nations; the immigrant languages above.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings on death and the dead, and the return of ancestors' remains, are the nations' to share or withhold; take only what they publish. Mass death, suicide and the loss of children call for care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull county death-by-cause tables (CDC WONDER) and INEGI's municipal tables for the Bajas; note place of death.
2. Pull the medical aid in dying reports for Oregon, Washington, California and Canada, and the BC overdose data.
3. Search the GSS and Pew afterlife items by state or region.
4. Search "obituary" and "esquela" samples in one city per unit, listing the words used for death.
5. Search each Indigenous nation's own site and language program for what it publishes on death and mourning.
