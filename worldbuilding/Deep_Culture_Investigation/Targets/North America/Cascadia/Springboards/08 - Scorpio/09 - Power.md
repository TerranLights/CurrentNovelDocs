# Cascadia: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (British Columbia), Chinese, Tagalog and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will; and how a culture judges who holds it, how it is held and how it is used.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and the British Columbia coast:** city politics, tech and port economies, civic reform.
- **The Far West (interior, Central Valley, Mojave):** growers, mines, dams and federal land.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican political practice, border politics, local strongmen, cartels as a power question.
- **The Panhandle's settlers:** company-town and corporation-land politics.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples:** hereditary and elected leadership, kin-based rank, relations with the state.
- **Cross-cutting groups:** union members against employers, newcomers against old families, rural against urban, farm labor against growers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the U.S. side (campaign money, surveys, histories); mixed in British Columbia; thin and uneven for the Bajas and the Panhandle; thin for Indigenous views outside published work.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French, Chinese and Tagalog for specific communities; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous leadership, rank and ceremonial knowledge is restricted or the nations' own to share; ask the nations first. Cartel power in the Bajas is a risk for any field work. Abuse-of-power cases involve living people and victims.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search OpenSecrets and the state and provincial disclosure bodies for money in politics, then compare the three U.S. states and British Columbia.
2. Pull the efficacy and strong-leader items from the surveys above, split by state or province where possible.
3. Search INEGI's ENCIG for Baja California and Baja California Sur.
4. Search library catalogs for "California" or "Pacific Northwest" with "railroad," "water," "machine" and "corporate power."
5. Search tribal and First Nations governments' own sites and language programs for leadership terms.
