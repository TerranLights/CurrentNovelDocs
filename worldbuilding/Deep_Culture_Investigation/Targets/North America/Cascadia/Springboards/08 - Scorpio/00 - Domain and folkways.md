# Cascadia: Domain and folkways, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, and the Indigenous languages of each unit. **Concept in one line:** how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden: death ways, sex ways, shared resources, inheritance, secrets, and how people are believed to change.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and the Panhandle's settler population:** founding-era Yankee and Russian-era records.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian institutions, separate statistics.
- **Far West:** inland, rural, resource-based; commons and shared water.
- **El Norte** (southern California, Baja California): Spanish-language and border funeral and migrant-death material.
- **Sudcalifornia** (Baja California Sur): small, cooperative fishing economy; thin published record likely.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene peoples: separate each.
- **Immigrant communities by origin** (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Mexican, Oaxacan): their own funeral and ancestor customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian surveys and for funeral industry data; mixed for Mexico's Baja states; thin for Indigenous peoples in open sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names and mourning customs of the dead, rites, and human remains are often restricted by the peoples; use only what communities publish or share. Migrant deaths, the disappeared and sexual violence data are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the CDC, BC Vital Statistics and INEGI death, birth and marriage tables for the territory, by county or state.
2. Search funeral-industry and cremation statistics for Washington, Oregon, California and British Columbia.
3. Search local newspapers for obituary and "celebration of life" notices, one outlet per unit.
4. Query INEGI and ENIF for Baja California informal saving and *tandas*.
5. Look up each Indigenous community's own language-program pages for what they share on death and mourning, before any outside scholarship.
