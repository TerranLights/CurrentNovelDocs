# Cascadia: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Indigenous languages of the lands, and the main immigrant languages. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden (privacy is one facet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast, Far West, British Columbia coast:** urban anonymity against small-town visibility; privacy online; coming out.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Spanish-speaking Baja and California):** *intimidad* and *chisme* register, family confidences, border-crossing secrets.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples:** knowledge owned by a family, house or society; each nation sets its own limits on what is told.
- **Immigrant communities:** hidden status, silence after removal or exile.
- **Faith and fraternal groups:** confession, lodges, recovery fellowships.
- **Generations:** reticence of the old against the oversharing of the young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English-language attitudes surveys, memoirs, noir and DNA-test stories; mixed for Baja California and BC breakouts; thin where Indigenous knowledge is restricted by design.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Cantonese or Japanese for some archives; Indigenous languages only through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted and sacred knowledge (ceremonial societies, sacred-site locations); research only what a nation publishes and never retell what it keeps; abuse, outing and immigration status in living memory; sealed adoption records and DNA surprises involve living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Bok for the frame, then list the facets that apply.
2. Pull Pew, Gallup and the Canadian Privacy Commissioner surveys for state and province breakouts.
3. Search "secreto a voces", "intimidad" and "chisme" in Baja California press, radio and oral-history collections.
4. Ask the Northwest Coast, Plateau and California nations' cultural centers what they publish on protocols for sharing knowledge.
5. Search the GLBT Historical Society, Densho and Hanford public histories for memoirs of kept silence.
