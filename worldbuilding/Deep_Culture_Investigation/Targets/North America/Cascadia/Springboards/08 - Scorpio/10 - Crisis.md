# Cascadia: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; Southeast Alaska; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), plus Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast cities against the Far West interior: different hazards (the Big One, against fire and drought).
- British Columbia coast and interior: floods, fires and the heat dome fall unevenly.
- Baja California and Baja California Sur: hurricanes and the Mexican civil-protection tradition; distinct from the U.S. lobes.
- Coastal and interior Indigenous peoples (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Subarctic Dene): their own disaster oral histories, and distance from services.
- Immigrant and farmworker communities (Mixtec and Triqui in San Quintín; Chinese, Filipino and Latin American communities along the coast): help-seeking differs.
- Rural and urban; rich and poor; generations; men and women; people living through the overdose and housing emergencies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and preparedness (official and academic); mixed for personal and family crisis (memoirs, crisis lines, clinical studies); thin for Indigenous and migrant views outside the literature.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish for the Bajas; Chinese for some communities; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous oral histories belong to the nations that tell them: ask before citing. Crisis-line and overdose data involve grief; mental-health crises are often kept hidden.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
