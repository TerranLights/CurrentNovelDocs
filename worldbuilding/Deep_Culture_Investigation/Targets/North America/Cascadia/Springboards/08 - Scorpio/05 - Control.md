*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler cultures:** Left Coast, British Columbia coast, Far West interior, El Norte (Southern California, Baja California), Baja California Sur: different histories with water, fire and family.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Northwest Coast (including Tlingit and Haida), Plateau, California, Yuman and Kumiai, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene: control of land, water and fire.
- **Immigrant and farm-labor communities:** Mexican and Mexican-American, East and Southeast Asian, Pacific Islander, Russian and Ukrainian, Central American: family control against each community's own history.
- **Cross-cutting:** gender, generation, religion, class, disability.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for water, fire, partner-violence surveys and parenting research; thin below the nation for attitudes; thin for Baja California Sur and the Panhandle.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; the Asian languages above; Indigenous languages only through their communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coercive control in families (survivors' accounts need care); Indigenous fire and land knowledge may be restricted, so use only what peoples publish; Celilo Falls and the missions are painful histories, told in the peoples' own voices.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the WVS control item and the state and provincial cuts of NISVS, ENDIREH and the Canadian survey, to see how thin they are.

4. Search digitized newspapers for "mandilón" and "ni modo" in Baja and Californian Spanish.
5. Find church parenting and marriage manuals for the household side.
