# Cascadia: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish (Southern California, Baja), Canadian English and French (British Columbia), plus Indigenous-language programs and the main immigrant languages of the coast. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **S1 Left Coast and S2 British Columbia coast:** wellness, detox and decluttering culture against callout politics; compare across the border.
- **S3 Far West:** interior and rural; church discipline, recall campaigns, distrust of coastal cleansing.
- **S4 El Norte and S5 Sudcalifornia:** limpia, temazcal and curanderismo; the deportation and repatriation layers; compare by immigrant generation.
- **I1 to I6 Indigenous units:** cleansing practices and forced purging of languages differ by nation; separate by nation, not by culture area.
- **Religious groups:** Catholic confession, evangelical revivalism, high-control congregations that shun, Buddhist and other Asian-heritage temples (Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean).
- **Descendants of the expelled:** Chinese, Japanese and Mexican communities, whose memory of expulsion shapes the concept.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions, sites, expulsions and blacklists; mixed for folk practice; thin for family-level shunning and for the purged themselves.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; Indigenous languages only through the nations' own programs; Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean for immigrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ceremonial cleansing and sweat-lodge practice (research only what the nations publish); tribal disenrollment disputes (their own voices only); boarding-school records and survivor testimony; eating disorders (a silence); deportation and encampment-sweep subjects.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Moral Foundations data for Washington, Oregon and California, with British Columbia comparators where they exist.
2. List EPA Superfund and Canadian contaminated-site entries for the Cascadian counties and districts.
3. Search Densho and the TRC reports for "expulsion", "removal" and "cleansing" language, noting what survivors call it.
4. Look up *limpia* and *temazcal* in Southern California and Baja *botánica* and *yerbería* listings and *curanderismo* studies.
5. Search regional newspaper archives for "clean house", "recall" and "blacklist" to find named purge episodes.
