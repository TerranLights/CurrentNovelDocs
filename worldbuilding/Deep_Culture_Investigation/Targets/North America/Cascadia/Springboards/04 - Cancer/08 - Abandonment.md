# Cascadia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Chinese, Tagalog and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with, and the fear and experience of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast vs. British Columbia coast:** two foundings, two child-welfare systems.
- **Far West interior:** towns emptied by mining and timber.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** migration, deportation, transnational families.
- **Panhandle settlers:** small isolated towns.
- **Indigenous peoples,** each separately: the history of removed children.
- **Within each:** mothers and fathers, rural and urban, elders alone, LGBTQ youth, recent migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for child welfare; mixed for the Baja peninsula; thin for beliefs and feelings (only stories, songs and interviews).
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog; Indigenous languages through each nation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous children's removal and any people's own knowledge are theirs to share; ask first. Survivor testimony is traumatic.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull AFCARS and NCANDS tables for the three states, the California dashboard and British Columbia's oversight reports.
2. Search INEGI and Baja California's DIF, then El Colegio de la Frontera Norte, for deportation and family separation.
3. Search section 1's words in each region's newspapers; note which carry the concept.
4. Ask three or four Indigenous nations' cultural offices how they wish it approached.
