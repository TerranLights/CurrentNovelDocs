# Cascadia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; the Baja Californias). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (minority), Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it, including the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast cities versus the Far West interior: leave, work and farm life.
- El Norte and Sudcalifornia: Mexican and Mexican-American mothers; Baja California Sur apart.
- British Columbia coast and Panhandle settlers: Canadian benefits, Alaska setting.
- Immigrant mothers: Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, and migrant Mixtec, Triqui and Zapotec families.
- Religious groups: Latter-day Saints, Catholics, evangelicals, secular.
- The six Indigenous units, each apart.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian statistics; mixed for the Bajas; thin for Indigenous peoples and small immigrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish; Chinese; Indigenous languages through each nation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings on women and birth held as restricted; sterilization and child-removal histories (use only what is public); bereaved mothers and the disappeared.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
