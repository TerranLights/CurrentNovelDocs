# Cascadia: Domain and folkways, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family; with Fischer's folkways of building, family and child-rearing.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler regional cultures:** Left Coast, BC coast, Far West, El Norte and Sudcalifornia (the Scope's units): different founding households and house traditions.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples, each with its own house and kin systems.
- **Later-arriving communities:** Mexican-heritage, East and Southeast Asian, Pacific Islander, Black, Slavic and Scandinavian-heritage communities, and refugees.
- **Urban against rural; renters against owners; recent arrivals against old families.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household and housing counts in all three countries; mixed for the Bajas' qualitative material; thin and scattered for Indigenous family teachings in print.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog and Vietnamese for immigrant sources; Indigenous languages via the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous family and ceremonial knowledge is the nations' to share; family separation by boarding schools and child removal is painful, so start with the survivors' own words; domestic violence sits behind the home's walls.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI household-type and multigenerational tables for the lands; compare by unit.
2. Search each Indigenous nation's own site for "house", "family", "kinship" and language-program pages; note what they publish.
3. Search HRAF and D-PLACE for the Northwest Coast, California, Plateau and Yuman peoples' household and descent codes.
4. Scan *Sunset* and local housing-history sources for house types by period.
5. Search "parenting" and "child-rearing" with Rogoff and Whiting names, then the national parenting-survey series.
