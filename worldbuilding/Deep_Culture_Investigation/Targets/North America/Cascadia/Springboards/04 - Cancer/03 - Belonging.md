# Cascadia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Spanish; Cantonese; Tagalog; Japanese; Indigenous languages by nation. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-timers and newcomers**, by metro against interior and rural towns.
- **The settler regional cultures** in the scope: founding populations may differ in what makes one local.
- **Indigenous nations by culture area** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Dene): belonging as each states it, on reservation or reserve and in the city.
- **Immigrant communities:** long-settled Chinese, Japanese and Filipino communities; Mexican American communities; Oaxacan and other Indigenous migrants; recent refugees.
- **Border residents** with ties on both sides.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for belonging, loneliness and school-connection surveys in the U.S. states and British Columbia; mixed for Baja California; thin for Indigenous nations' own understanding (it sits in their own materials). Question wording differs across the border.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese-language and Japanese-language community media; Indigenous languages only for published material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous membership and kinship belong to each nation; take only what it publishes or shares. Oral histories of exclusion may carry permission terms.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Community-belonging items for BC health regions; then CHIS and the state youth surveys.
2. Press and forums for "Seattle Freeze", "transplant" and "native Oregonian": map the folk vocabulary.
3. HistoryLink, Oregon Encyclopedia and BC Archives for "newcomers", "old families", "pioneers".
4. *fuereño*, *choyero* and *arraigo* in Baja California sources and COLEF.
5. What two or three Indigenous nations and urban Native centers publish about who belongs.
