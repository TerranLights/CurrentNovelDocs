# Cascadia: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, the Baja peninsula). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (some Canadian material), Cantonese and Mandarin; Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler regional cultures:** Left Coast, Far West, British Columbia coast, El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Baja), since the lands cross three national care systems.
- **Immigrant and heritage communities:** Mexican-, Chinese-, Filipino-, Japanese-, Southeast Asian-heritage and Pacific Islander households; migrant carers against the families they serve.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples; separate on and off reserve, urban, and by tribal or First Nation.
- **Other splits:** rural against metropolitan; class; gender; chosen-family care among LGBTQ elders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for measured care in the U.S., Canada and Mexico, but split across three statistical systems that define caregiving differently. Mixed for ethnic breakdowns. Thin and uneven for Indigenous concepts of care outside health-authority materials. Thin for Baja outside INEGI.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; French for some federal Canadian material; Chinese for community sources; Indigenous languages only through their owners.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing knowledge and ceremonies belong to their communities; use only what nations publish themselves. Elder abuse and end-of-life care are sensitive for interviews. Care-worker immigration status.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the three national time-use and caregiver surveys (ATUS, GSS, ENUT) and note how each defines care.
2. Search state and provincial program pages (California IHSS, Washington and Oregon long-term care, British Columbia home support) for who is paid to care.
3. Search the Seniors Advocate and the First Nations Health Authority for elder-care reports.
4. Query university libraries (UW, UBC, UC, UABC) for "caregiver" and "cuidadora" oral histories and theses.
5. Search Baja California news for "cuidados" and "trabajadoras del hogar".
