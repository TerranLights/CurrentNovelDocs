# Cascadia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French and Chinese for British Columbia and the Bay Area; Indigenous languages per unit. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area): the 2010s-20s encampment disputes.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian policy and the Downtown Eastside.
- **Far West** (Central Valley, interior): farmworker housing, small-town shelters.
- **El Norte** (southern California): Spanish-language church and migrant networks.
- **Sudcalifornia** (Baja California Sur): tourism, seasonal labor, hurricanes.
- **Northwest Coast peoples:** plank-house tradition, urban Indigenous homelessness.
- **Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples:** shelter traditions in each; reservation and rancheria housing.
- **Migrants and displaced people** (San Quintin Mixtec, Triqui; Tijuana arrivals) and faith-based against public shelters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and BC counts and press; mixed for opinion and ritual; thin for the Bajas outside Tijuana and for Indigenous shelter ideas.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; French and Chinese for a few sources; Indigenous languages, mostly through community programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous sacred or ceremonial matters (winter ceremonial season, longhouse practice) may be restricted; ask nations' own cultural offices. Migrant-shelter details and shelter residents are privacy and safety risks. Avoid one voice per city.
- **Held for review:** none beyond South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull HUD's AHAR and Housing Inventory Count for the Cascadian Continuums of Care and the Metro Vancouver count.
2. Read the street newspapers (*Real Change*, *Street Roots*, *Megaphone*) for how unhoused people name shelter.
3. Search PPIC and local polls for "encampments" and "homeless shelter" attitudes in each unit.
4. Search the Tijuana migrant shelters and *Las Posadas* in the Bajas in Spanish.
5. Ask the nations' cultural offices and museums for public material on traditional houses.
