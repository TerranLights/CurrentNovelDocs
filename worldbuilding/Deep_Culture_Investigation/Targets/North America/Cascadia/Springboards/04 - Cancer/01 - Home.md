# Cascadia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Chinese, Japanese, Tlingit, Haida, Chinuk Wawa and the Coast Salish, Californian and Yuman languages. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and the Panhandle's settlers:** the Yankee and Pacific-city home and its suburbs.
- **British Columbia coast:** British-colonial founding and Pacific Rim immigration; a Canadian housing system.
- **Far West (Central Valley, Sierra, interior):** farm, ranch and mining-town homes; Dust Bowl and farmworker housing.
- **El Norte (Southern California) and the Bajas:** Spanish-language and border homes, rancho and mission inheritance, multigenerational households.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Dene): the house as building, kin group or seasonal round; separate by nation.
- **Immigrant communities and owners against renters:** Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Mexican households; the unhoused.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing counts and time use (U.S., Canada, Mexico); mixed for meaning and feeling; thin for Indigenous groups outside what the nations publish.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; for communities, Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog; Indigenous-language material only as the nations share it.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous house knowledge, house-front and pole stories and ceremonies belong to the families and nations; take only what is shared publicly and with permission. Internment, removal, eviction and displacement are painful memories for living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS and Statistics Canada tenure, crowding and years-in-home tables for the coastal and interior counties and the province; INEGI for the Bajas.
2. Search *Sunset* magazine archives and regional house-pattern books for the Western house ideal.
3. Query Mapping Inequality, covenants projects and Densho for homes lost and excluded.
4. Search nation, museum and cultural-center sites (Burke, Museum of Anthropology, U'mista) for what Indigenous peoples publicly say of the house.
5. Search El Colef and UABC for border and Baja housing and self-built homes.
