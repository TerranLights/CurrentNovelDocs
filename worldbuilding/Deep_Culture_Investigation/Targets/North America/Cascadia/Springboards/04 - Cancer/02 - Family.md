# Cascadia: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese; Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settler (S1)** and **British Columbia coast (S2)**: different founding populations and household patterns to test.
- **Far West interior and Central Valley (S3)**: farm and ranch families.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia**: Spanish-speaking families, ritual kin, families split by the border.
- **Panhandle settlers**: fishing and cannery families.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples**: each people's own kin system and house or clan units; compare nation by nation, never as one voice.
- **Cross-cutting:** Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Mexican and Oaxacan (Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec) heritage communities; Catholic, Latter-day Saints and evangelical groups; LGBTQ chosen families; foster-care alumni; urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household and family counts (census sources in three countries); mixed for practice and values; thin for ritual kin and Indigenous kin systems outside the nations' own publications.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; Chinese for Chinese-heritage communities; Indigenous languages for kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan and house teachings, names, songs and crests belong to their owners; use what the nations publish. Boarding-school and removal histories involve living trauma; use survivors' own words and consent norms. Family separation at the border.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS household and family tables for Washington, Oregon and California, and the Statistics Canada and INEGI equivalents for British Columbia and the Bajas.
2. Search each Indigenous group's own cultural institutions and tribal websites for published kin, house and clan teachings, noting what they ask outsiders not to use.
3. Search the Asian American and Chinese Canadian museums and archives for family histories and reunion records.
4. Search COLEF and Mexican Migration Project materials for border families and *compadrazgo* in Baja California.
5. Search the boarding-school reports for family-separation passages by state and province.
