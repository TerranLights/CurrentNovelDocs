# Cascadia: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, plus Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | crime, criminal, crook, convict, ex-con, thug, snitch, bad apple | the wrong and the wrongdoer | slang varies by city and generation |
| Spanish (Baja) | *delito*, *crimen*, *delincuente*, *ratero*, *transa* | formal wrong; grave crime; wrongdoer; thief; cheat | register and region vary |
| Spanish (Baja) | *narco*, *sicario*, *levantón*, *cobro de piso*, *mordida*, *impunidad*, *cifra negra* | cartel; hired killer; abduction; extortion; bribe; impunity; unreported crime | the last two are also survey terms |
| Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin) | 罪, 犯罪 (*zeoi*/*zui*, *faan zeoi*/*fanzui*) | sin or crime; to commit a crime | for Chinese Canadian and Chinese American communities |
| Chinuk Wawa, Lushootseed, Lingít, Haida, Kumiai | terms only as each nation publishes them | wrongdoing, harm, repair | use the nations' own dictionaries and teachings |

English keywords: fear of crime, victimization, moral panic, social bandit, stigma, redemption, reentry, vigilance committee, true crime, missing and murdered Indigenous women.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast cities (Seattle, Portland, the Bay Area): urban safety debates, neighborhood apps, drug-policy argument.
- Far West interior (Central Valley, Inland Empire, interior British Columbia): rural and small-town views; poaching, cannabis, meth.
- British Columbia coast: Canadian framing, a different survey and press system.
- El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Los Angeles to Tijuana to the Cape): border towns, cartel-era talk, *nota roja* readers, outlaw ballads.
- Indigenous units (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): their own words for harm and repair.
- Migrant communities: Mixtec, Triqui and Zapotec farmworkers; Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese and Korean communities; recent arrivals.
- Faith groups, and class, race, generation and gender.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for victimization and recorded crime, with a gap between states, provinces and Mexico (different definitions); thin for attitudes by unit; mixed for the Indigenous units.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Cantonese or Mandarin for some Chinese-language media; Indigenous languages for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** missing and murdered Indigenous women, disappeared persons, family grief, gang and cartel material (researcher and source safety); a people's restricted knowledge; stereotypes about who looks like a criminal are to be recorded, never adopted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
