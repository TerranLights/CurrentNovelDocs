# Cascadia: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog; Indigenous languages by dictionary query. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler regional cultures (S1 Left Coast, S2 British Columbia coast, S3 Far West, El Norte lands of southern California and Baja California, Baja California Sur):** separate because their founding layers and institutions differ.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene peoples): separate by nation; each has its own words and exemplars.
- **Hispanic, Mexican and Mexican-American communities;** Chinese and Filipino heritage communities; Black communities of the port cities; each as its own check.
- **Religious and secular groups** (evangelical, mainline, Catholic, Buddhist, none): worth separating given the debate over empathy in some churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for English-language scales, school programs, the health sector and the TRC; mixed for Spanish-speaking Baja and for Asian-heritage communities; thin for Indigenous understandings of the concept in published form.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Cantonese, Mandarin and Tagalog next; Indigenous languages through dictionaries and community-approved sources only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential-school and boarding-school testimony (survivors' words, use as published and with care); Indigenous restricted knowledge; trauma and crisis-line material; patient privacy; migrant communities at risk.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up "empathy" and "empatía" in a large English and a Spanish dictionary, plus the Chinese and Tagalog terms above, for senses and idioms.
2. Search the Interpersonal Reactivity Index and Konrath-style studies for U.S. state or Canadian province breakouts; note which of the three countries has data.
3. Pull the Washington, Oregon and California social-emotional learning standards and BC's school curriculum; compare how each defines empathy.
4. Search the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's reports and Vancouver events for "empathy" and "witness".
5. Search newspapers and Google Trends for "empath" and "tone-deaf" by place and decade.
