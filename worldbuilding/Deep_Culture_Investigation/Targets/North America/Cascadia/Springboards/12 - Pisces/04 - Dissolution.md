# Cascadia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (Canadian sources), Chinese, and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the loss of the self's edges in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (Bay Area, Puget Sound, Willamette):** counterculture and contemplative centers; the heaviest "Nones" and psychedelic activity.
- **Far West (interior, Central Valley):** possibly different church and family patterns.
- **El Norte (Southern California) and Sudcalifornia:** Spanish-language family and church life; border migration.
- **British Columbia coast and interior:** Canadian multiculturalism against the U.S. melting-pot talk; Chinese-heritage communities.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples, each separately, since assimilation was imposed on them differently.
- **Immigrant communities:** Chinese, Filipino, Japanese and Mixtec, Triqui and Zapotec, for assimilation and language shift.
- **Ecstatic against restrained congregations, and generations** (festival and rave cohorts, retirees).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. states and Canada (surveys, trials, closures, divorce); mixed for Baja (INEGI counts, little on mystical experience); thin for Indigenous understandings, which sit in community sources, not surveys.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, French, Chinese; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted ceremonial knowledge; residential and boarding-school survivors' testimony; cult survivors; dementia and bereavement; psychedelic use, which is legal only in part.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI divorce and language-shift tables for the target's states, province and municipalities.
2. Search the GSS and NSDUH for the West region's mystical-experience and hallucinogen items.
3. Search "ego death" and "None Zone" in library catalogs; read the Pacific Northwest religion volume first.
4. Contact the language and heritage bodies (First Peoples' Cultural Council, Alaska Native Language Archive, INALI) for the peoples' own terms.
5. Search the residential and boarding-school records for assimilation, survivors' accounts first.
