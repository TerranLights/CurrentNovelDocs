# Cascadia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and British Columbia coast settlers**, apart: different founders, different military and caregiving institutions.
- **Far West interior** (farm, ranch and mining towns): rescue crews, wildland firefighters, extended families.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia**: Mexican-heritage families, remittances, Catholic vows, the mother's role.
- **Asian-heritage communities** (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean): parents' sacrifice, the immigrant bargain; separate by group and generation.
- **Latter-day Saint, Catholic, evangelical, Buddhist and secular groups**: religious self-denial.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): each nation's own teaching on giving for the people, plus veterans' service.
- **Military and first-responder families**; **migrant farmworkers** (Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec, San Quintín).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on caregiving, donation and remittances; mixed by state and province; thin for the Panhandle and Baja California Sur.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages only through their own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ceremonial vows and fasts (restricted knowledge, record only what the nation publishes); suicide framed as sacrifice; bereaved families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

1. Pull the caregiving, time-use and donation tables for Washington, Oregon, California and British Columbia; INEGI for the Bajas.
