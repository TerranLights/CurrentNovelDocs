# Cascadia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Japanese and Chinese for immigrant communities; Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (WA, OR, CA settlers):** recovery culture, medical aid in dying, decluttering and "manifesting" ideas all live here; separate urban from rural.
- **British Columbia coast:** a different legal and health regime (Canada) and different public language on dying.
- **Far West, El Norte, Sudcalifornia:** interior and Baja Spanish-speaking Catholic communities; compare Mexican Baja with California Latino communities, and recent arrivals with long-settled families.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples:** each has its own memory of treaty, removal and war; separate by nation, never as one "Native view."
- **Cross-cutting groups:** Japanese and Chinese American and Canadian communities (internment memory); veterans; people in recovery; religious communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on medical aid in dying, hospice and recovery (public reports); mixed on everyday giving in, quitting and letting go (surveys, forums, press); thin for Baja and for most Indigenous units.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Japanese for some archives; Indigenous languages for each unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and assisted dying; a people's restricted or ceremonial knowledge (ask the nation first); grief, abuse and addiction accounts held in confidence; do not label any people "fatalistic."
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Oregon, Washington, California and Health Canada annual reports and note what each reports on reasons and place.
2. Search the Spanish terms (*ni modo*, *entregarse*, *tirar la toalla*) in Baja California press and INEGI-linked health pages.
3. Find hospice and recovery-fellowship directories by county and province; map density.
4. Search each Indigenous nation's own cultural center, language program and museum for treaty-era and war memory.
5. Search local oral-history archives for "giving up" and "letting go" in the English and Spanish above.
