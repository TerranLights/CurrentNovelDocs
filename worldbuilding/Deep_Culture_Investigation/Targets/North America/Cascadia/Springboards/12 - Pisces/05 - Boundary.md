# Cascadia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (British Columbia), plus Indigenous languages (Coast Salish, Tlingit, Kumeyaay and Kumiai, Cucapá, others). **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (Seattle, Portland, Bay Area) and **British Columbia coast**: dense cities, therapy talk, neighbor norms; separate to test whether the border at 49 degrees matters.
- **Far West** (Central Valley, interior, Okie and farm-labor towns): open land and fences against crowded labor housing.
- **El Norte** (Southern California) and **Sudcalifornia** (Baja California Sur): Spanish-language family boundaries; the line as a place people live beside.
- **Border residents** (San Diego and Tijuana; Blaine and Surrey) against those inland: daily crossers against distant residents.
- **Immigrant communities** (Mexican, Asian, Pacific Islander, others) and their children: family privacy across generations.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Coast Salish and Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman (Kumeyaay and Kumiai, Cucapá, split by the Mexican line), Great Basin, Subarctic Dene; each states its own territory and relations.
- **Generations and genders:** online privacy, bodily boundaries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Rich in English for U.S. and Canadian cities and the border; mixed for Baja California Sur; thin for Indigenous views in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French for some British Columbia sources; Indigenous languages for territory words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge of territory and kin relations (ask the nation); survivors' accounts of abuse; deaths at the border.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
