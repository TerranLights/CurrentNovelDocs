# Cascadia: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Spanish (the Bajas and large communities elsewhere); Chinese and Tagalog in diaspora communities; Indigenous languages through the communities' own outlets. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and British Columbia coast:** city leisure, cannabis and craft-drink culture; the opioid crisis lands hard on the downtown cores.
- **Far West interior and rural counties:** rural despair, mining and farm labor, long drives and small-town bars.
- **El Norte (southern California, Baja California):** the border towns' nightlife tradition, cross-border escape in both directions.
- **Baja California Sur:** the resort and retirement economy; locals who serve escape versus those who seek it.
- **Panhandle:** fishing and cannery seasonal life; isolation.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Dene peoples): separate each, and separate use inside ceremony from use outside it.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Mexican, Vietnamese, Hmong, others): their own leisure and gambling customs, and the old panics aimed at them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. states and British Columbia on use and deaths; mixed for the Bajas; thin and uneven for Indigenous nations and the Panhandle at substate scale; thin for media-as-escape beyond industry reports.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish at minimum; Chinese and Tagalog for diaspora outlets; Indigenous languages only through the communities' invitation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial substance use belongs to the people who hold it; take it only as they publish it. Anexos involve coercion and abuse allegations; use survivors' own published accounts with care. Overdose data names real deaths.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
