# Cascadia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Canadian French, Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Japanese, plus the Indigenous languages of its lands. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety are its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settler culture** (Puget Sound to the Bay Area): hazard-heavy region; compare quake and tsunami talk with crime talk.
- **Southern California and Baja California** (El Norte side): border, migration and *la migra* fear; Spanish-language fright traditions.
- **Far West interior** (Central Valley, Sierra, Columbia basin, BC interior): wildfire, isolation, rural-urban fears.
- **British Columbia coast and the Panhandle's settlers:** a different national survey system and different institutions.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Japanese, Mexican, Mixtec and Triqui): own words and folk cures; fear of deportation.
- **Indigenous peoples** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): own fear and warning traditions.
- **Gender, age and religion** cut across all of these.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and emergency materials (English, Spanish); mixed for folk fear; thin by region for Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages for any native-language work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous stories of dangerous beings or places may be restricted or seasonal; ask the nation. Deportation fear: protect informants. Anxiety data is health data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
