# Cascadia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; Southeast Alaska; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, French (British Columbia), Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what a culture believes good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Left Coast (Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area) against the interior Far West and El Norte, since the units follow Woodard's lines.
- The British Columbia coast against the interior.
- Baja California against Baja California Sur (mainland migrants against old ranching families).
- Northwest Coast, Plateau, California and Yuman peoples; Indigenous nations of the Bajas.
- Immigrant generations: first-generation speakers, children who broker, and later generations.
- Deaf and DeafBlind communities as language communities.
- Migrant Indigenous Mexican communities against Spanish-speaking mestizo neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English and Spanish in the U.S. states; mixed for British Columbia and the Bajas; thin and scattered for Indigenous norms of talk and silence, and for Southeast Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish at minimum; Chinese for Vancouver and the Bay Area; each Indigenous nation's own materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous speech protocols, stories and names (some held by seasons, families or clans); the years of the residential and boarding schools; immigration status in families; Deaf communities' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI language tables for the target's counties and districts to see which languages to cover.
2. Search each Indigenous language program's site for its entries on speaking, listening and silence, and ask before using anything restricted.
3. Read Stivers and colleagues, then search for studies of conversation in Spanish, Chinese and English in the region's cities.
4. Search ethnic-press archives and advice columns for "how to talk to" a partner, child or coworker.
5. Search Deaf and DeafBlind community histories for Seattle, Fremont and Riverside.
