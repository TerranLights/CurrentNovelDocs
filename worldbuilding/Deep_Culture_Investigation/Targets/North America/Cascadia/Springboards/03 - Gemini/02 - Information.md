# Cascadia: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur, with their Indigenous nations). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese and Russian for immigrant communities; Indigenous languages as each nation publishes its own. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how a culture gathers, passes on, trusts, withholds and uses it: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settlers and the BC coast:** tech and media centers (Seattle, Portland, the Bay Area, Vancouver) against the Canadian public-broadcaster tradition.
- **Far West interior and rural areas:** news deserts and broadband gaps; distrust of coastal media varies.
- **El Norte, the Bajas and Southern California:** Spanish-language media, WhatsApp networks and Mexican press conditions.
- **Indigenous units:** each nation's own rules on what knowledge is shared, and its data governance.
- **Immigrant communities:** Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, Russian, Hmong and Mexican and Central American; language and generation set the sources.
- **Farmworker and migrant communities:** word of mouth, community radio, and wariness of giving information to officials.
- **Cross-cutting:** age (platform), party (trust in media), religion, urban and rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian media use, trust and access (national surveys, county data); mixed for the Bajas (thin regional samples); thin and unevenly public for Indigenous nations and many immigrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog and others for community press; Indigenous languages as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge may be owned and restricted; use only what a nation publishes itself, and follow OCAP and CARE. Residential-school and missing-persons records are painful and may be limited. Do not read survey results as one voice for a people. Mexican reporting risks are real; do not contact sources without care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
