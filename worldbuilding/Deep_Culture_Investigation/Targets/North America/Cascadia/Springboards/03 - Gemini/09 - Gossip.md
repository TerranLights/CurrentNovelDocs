*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (post-war: Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, the two Bajas). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French, Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor is researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast:** big-city, tech-era and online talk.
- **BC coast:** Canadian press norms; immigrant communities with own-language media.
- **Far West:** small towns, farm and ranch communities.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Spanish-language gossip culture, border families, Mexican media.
- **Panhandle settlers:** small fishing and government towns.
- **Indigenous peoples** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): separate each; their norms of speech about others are theirs to describe.
- **Also split:** gender, age, workplace, neighborhood, online, religious communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for media, online gossip and youth surveys; thin for everyday talk in any one community.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, French; Indigenous languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fear of cartels or officials silencing talk in the Bajas; whisper networks and anonymous reports naming people; Indigenous knowledge about speech that may be restricted; minors' data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Gluckman and Merry; list the facets to test per unit.
2. Pull tightness-looseness scores and the youth surveys' rumor and bullying items; find INEGI and Statistics Canada equivalents.
3. Search the folklore archives for "rumor", "legend" and "gossip" with place names.
4. Search 1942-43 newspapers and Densho for rumor-driven episodes.
5. Ask regional language programs, by unit, for the peoples' own words and norms.
