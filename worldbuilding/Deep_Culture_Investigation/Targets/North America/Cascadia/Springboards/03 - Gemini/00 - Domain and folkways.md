# Cascadia: Domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; Southeast Alaska; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (Canada); Chinuk Wawa and other Indigenous languages; Chinese languages, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean. **Domain in one line:** communication, siblings and neighbors, short journeys and early learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home. Fischer's folkways for the sign: speech ways and learning ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (with the Panhandle settlers): Puget Sound, Willamette Valley, Bay Area, North Coast; variation in neighbor ties, accent and public talk.
- **British Columbia coast:** English, French and immigrant-language mix; Canadian schooling.
- **Far West:** interior towns and ranch country; interior and coastal speech.
- **El Norte** and **Sudcalifornia:** Spanish and English, code-switching, cross-border daily travel.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): separate by language family; each nation's language program is its own source.
- Later layers inside units (immigrant and Indigenous-migrant languages) as variation to check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the census, dialect and survey items in English and Spanish; mixed for Baja California Sur and the interior; thin for speech ways in daily life, which needs ethnography, oral history and media.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, French; Indigenous languages for primary sources, with the nations' permission.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous stories, oratory and language programs belong to their nations; take only what they publish, and ask. Boarding-school history: the nations' own accounts first.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ACS home-language and journey-to-work tables for Washington, Oregon and California; the Statistics Canada equivalents for British Columbia; INEGI for the Bajas.
2. Dialect atlases and regional dictionaries: "West Coast English", "California vowel shift", "Canadian raising", "español de Baja California".
3. The Indigenous language programs and agencies named above, for what each nation publishes about its language.
4. "Neighbors" and "neighborliness" surveys by city or province; "Seattle Freeze".
5. Oral-history and school-history collections by region, to find where speech and learning ways were recorded.
