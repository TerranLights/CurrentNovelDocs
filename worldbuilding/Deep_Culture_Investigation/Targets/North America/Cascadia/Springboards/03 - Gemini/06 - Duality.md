# Cascadia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia (post-war: Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California, Baja California Sur), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French (Canadian); Indigenous languages (Kumeyaay/Kumiai, Cucapá, Lushootseed, Halkomelem, Chinook Jargon, Tlingit, others by unit); immigrant heritage languages (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Tagalog). **Concept in one line:** how a culture lives with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how it treats people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **S4 El Norte (Southern California and Baja California) and S5 Sudcalifornia:** border twin cities and the strongest Spanish-English two-ness; Sudcalifornia is also the retiree and snowbird end.
- **S1 Left Coast and S2 British Columbia coast:** the Pacific immigration communities (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Filipino, Vietnamese) and Canada's official-bilingual setting.
- **S3 Far West:** farm-labor communities who follow harvests between countries; interior two-ness may differ sharply from the coast.
- **I1 to I6 Indigenous units (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin edge, Dene):** borders that split nations (Coast Salish; Cucapá and Kumeyaay), and each people's own view of paired worlds.
- **Generation:** first, 1.5, second; mixed-race people; religious traditions that read pairs as opposed or as complementary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census and school counts, border studies and Spanish-English literature; mixed for Pacific immigrant communities; thin and unequal for Indigenous views of duality.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and English at minimum; Chinese, Japanese and Korean for community sources; Indigenous languages for any native-theory claim.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Two-Spirit meanings, masks, twin and trickster stories and masked dances belong to their communities; use only what they share publicly, and let each nation define its own terms. Hidden immigration status and closeted lives call for care with living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
