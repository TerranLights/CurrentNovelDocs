# Cascadia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, French (British Columbia), and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settler culture** (WA, OR, coastal CA) and **British Columbia coast:** differing founding lines (Yankee and upland Southern; British colonial).
- **Far West interior:** farm and ranch families, Dust Bowl migrants; sibling work on the land.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican family systems split by a border; sibling migration chains.
- **Asian-heritage communities** (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese and others): eldest-child and caregiver roles in immigrant households; separate each.
- **Indigenous peoples by culture area** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman and Kumeyaay, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): kinship may widen who counts as a sibling; some Northwest Coast nations are matrilineal (check which).
- **Panhandle settlers** and Alaska Natives: fishing families, corporations.
- Within each: rural and urban, gender, religion, generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for household counts and migration oral histories; thin for sibling feeling and obligation in survey form; thin for Baja California Sur; scattered for Indigenous kinship.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages for kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school, foster-care and Sixties Scoop separations, told by survivors and nations first; Indigenous kinship knowledge a people may restrict; sibling violence; estrangement; private people in reunion and DNA-match stories.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Census, Statistics Canada and INEGI tables on household makeup and children ever born for each land.

3. Search Densho and the Bracero History Archive for "brother" and "sister".
4. Check each Indigenous nation's language program for sibling and cousin terms, and whether it publishes them.
5. Search Spanish-, Chinese- and Native-run news outlets for sibling stories ("hermanos", "兄弟姐妹").
