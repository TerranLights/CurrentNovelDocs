# Cascadia: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (British Columbia), Chinese, Korean, and Indigenous languages (Chinuk Wawa, Lushootseed, Halkomelem, Tlingit, Kumeyaay and others by unit). **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and the British Columbia coast:** the "Seattle Freeze" lore against Vancouver high-rise isolation.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican family and *compadre* networks; cross-border ties.
- **Far West:** rural and small-town ties; Okie descendants; farmworker families.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman and Dene peoples:** kin networks, trade and visiting, each nation separately.
- **Immigrant layers:** Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Korean and Vietnamese communities, where association ties differ by origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canada (surveys); mixed for the Bajas; thin and uneven for Indigenous units.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, then Chinese and others by community.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous protocols and kin knowledge belong to the nations; use only what they publish. Loneliness data touches health.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Surgeon General's advisory: read its bibliography for Pacific-region studies.

3. Search Statistics Canada and the Vancouver Foundation for "social connections British Columbia".
4. Search INEGI and El Colef for "redes sociales" and "compadrazgo Baja California".
5. Ask the canoe-journey host nations and Xwi7xwa Library what Indigenous connection material is open.
