# Cascadia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia (post-war), region North America. Lands carried: Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Japanese, Tagalog; Indigenous languages (Lushootseed, Halkomelem, Kwak'wala, Nuu-chah-nulth, Haida, Tlingit, Sahaptin, Nez Perce, Kumeyaay/Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Mixtec, Zapotec; *check* each). **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Left Coast and Panhandle settlers (Yankee thrift, consumer culture); British Columbia coast; Far West interior (rural sheds, ranch and orchard households); El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Mexican and border households, *tianguis*, *pacas*); Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples (giving and keeping as each describes it); Chinese, Japanese and Filipino descendants and newer immigrants; the unhoused and the very rich, as ends of the range.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for consumption and household surveys; mixed for belief and feeling; thin for Baja California Sur and the Indigenous units.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Japanese and Tagalog for diaspora sources; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** potlatch, regalia and objects of the dead are restricted or ceremonial for some peoples; use only what nations publish themselves. Sacred objects and repatriation can be contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the household-goods and spending tables for each state, province and Baja state (CE/ACS, SHS, INEGI).

3. Search the Spanish terms (*tiliches*, *pacas*, *apartar*) in Baja news and the Chinese and Japanese terms in diaspora media.
4. Look up the UCLA CELF study and one Buy Nothing or decluttering source per metro (Seattle, Portland, Vancouver, Bay Area, Los Angeles, Tijuana).
5. Ask each Indigenous cultural center for its language program's words for "own" and "give".
