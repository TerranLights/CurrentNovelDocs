# Cascadia: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia (post-war), region North America: Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin); Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "self-esteem", "self-respect", "know your worth", "you're worth it", "good-for-nothing", "impostor syndrome", "doormat" | worth as feeling, as claim, as insult | Keep apart from "net worth" and "worth five million" |
| Spanish (Bajas) | *autoestima*, *amor propio*, *dignidad*, *valer*, *sentirse menos*, *hacer menos*, *el qué dirán* | self-esteem, self-love, dignity, to be worth, to feel less, to belittle, what people will say | *Me vale* and *vale madre* use the same verb for indifference |
| Chinese | 自尊 zìzūn; 面子 miànzi; 尊严 zūnyán | self-esteem; face; dignity | Pair with 丢脸 diūliǎn (losing face) *check* |
| Indigenous languages | Lushootseed, Lingít, Haida, Nisga'a, Kumeyaay, Kumiai | words for standing, being honored, being lowered | Terms not known to me; take them from each nation's language program or dictionary |

English keywords: self-worth, dignity, honor, face, shame, status anxiety, residential school, deaths of despair, job loss and identity, "participation trophy", "deserving poor".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler units:** Left Coast (including mill and fishing towns); British Columbia coast; Far West (ranching and farm interior); El Norte (southern California, Baja California); Sudcalifornia (ranch and port towns).
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, where standing is held through named honors; Plateau; California; Yuman peoples; Great Basin edge; Dene peoples. Each nation's own account is kept apart, including what residential schools did and what renewal restores.
- **Cross-cutting:** class and the tech-against-trades divide; gender, especially men's worth tied to providing; race and colorism; immigrant generation (Chinese, Mexican, Filipino, Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec families); age; urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. English-language surveys and self-help material; mixed for British Columbia and the Bajas; thin and mostly self-published for Native-nation voices.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese for Asian-heritage families; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivor testimony on schools and abuse; suicide and self-harm data; restricted Native knowledge about names and honors; class shame. Law is not scouted here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
