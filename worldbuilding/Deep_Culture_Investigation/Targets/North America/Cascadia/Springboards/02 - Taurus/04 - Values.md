# Cascadia: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur, with their Indigenous nations). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese and Russian for immigrant communities; Indigenous languages as each nation publishes its own. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settlers (U.S. lobe and Panhandle):** the Yankee-founded civic tradition, separate from the upland farm settlement.
- **British Columbia coast and interior:** British-colonial founding against Woodard's Left Coast reading.
- **Far West interior (Central Valley, Sierra, Columbia basin, interior BC):** environment-defined, often differs politically from the coast.
- **El Norte, Southern California and the Bajas:** Spanish and Mexican heritage; border and migrant communities.
- **Baja California Sur:** its own unit in Stage 0; check its distinctness.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene peoples; each states its own values.
- **Immigrant communities:** Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, Russian, Hmong, Armenian, Mexican and Central American; by generation.
- **Religious groups:** the unaffiliated, evangelical, Catholic, Latter-day Saint, Adventist, Orthodox, Jewish, Muslim, Buddhist.
- **Cross-cutting:** urban and rural, generation, class, party.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian settlers (state and metro surveys); mixed for Mexico's Bajas (national surveys, thin regional samples); thin and unevenly public for Indigenous nations; thin for many immigrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and others for community press; Indigenous languages as each nation shares them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings may be restricted; use only what a nation publishes itself and respect its protocols. Do not read survey results as one voice for a people. Religious and political values are contested; record the variation.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the World Values Survey items for the three countries; check whether any wave has regional breakdowns for the West Coast, British Columbia or the Bajas.
2. Query PRRI, Pew and the state polls for Washington, Oregon and California values and religion items; list the regional splits they report.
3. Collect the published "values" or "guiding principles" pages of the Indigenous nations in each unit, in their own words and spellings.
4. Search *valores* with Baja California and Baja California Sur in INEGI and the local press (Zeta Tijuana); note Mexico's civics course materials.
5. Read the Woodard and Fischer sections on each regional founding as hypotheses to test, then pick Chinese-language and Spanish-language community media for the immigrant comparison.
