# Cascadia: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, and the Indigenous languages of its lands. **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **S1 Left Coast:** coffee, craft beer, farm-to-table, the counterculture and wellness trades.
- **S2 BC coast:** a Canadian setting with its own food, cannabis and spa markets.
- **S3 Far West:** interior ranch, orchard and farm foodways; hot springs; rural tastes.
- **S4 El Norte:** Southern California and Baja: Mexican and Asian-immigrant cuisines, beach and dance culture.
- **S5 Sudcalifornia:** a smaller Spanish-language setting, with the tourist economy of Los Cabos beside older ranch life.
- **Indigenous units:** the Northwest Coast peoples (feast and food traditions), the California peoples and the Yuman peoples (sweat baths and plant use, as the nations themselves describe them), and the Plateau peoples, each on its own terms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in English for food, drink, spending and wellness; mixed in Spanish for Baja; thin for Indigenous concepts of pleasure, and for the private side.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual pleasure (Scorpio's, so scout only its edges); Indigenous ceremonial uses of sweat baths, plants and smoke, which are sacred to many nations and may be restricted; the sale of white sage and of sweat-bath "experiences" is contested; addiction and eating disorders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (their food, scent and wellness traditions) set aside under the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ATUS, BRFSS sleep and household-spending tables, one column per unit.
2. Search each wine, coffee and craft-beer region's trade body for how it describes itself, by unit.
3. Search Spanish: "sobremesa", "antojitos" and "disfrutar" with Tijuana, Ensenada, La Paz and Los Cabos press.
4. Read the anthropology of the senses (Howes and Classen), then search "sensory" with Pacific Northwest and California.
5. Contact Indigenous language and cultural programs about words and practices, with consent, before using anything.
