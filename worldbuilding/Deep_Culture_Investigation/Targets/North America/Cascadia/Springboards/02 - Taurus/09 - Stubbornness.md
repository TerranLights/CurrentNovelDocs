# Cascadia: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Southeast Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, French (British Columbia); the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **S1 Left Coast and S2 British Columbia coast:** different founding institutions (Yankee and British colonial), so compare how each frames dissent, consensus and compromise.
- **S3 Far West:** ranching, mining and farming interior; holdout and land-sale stories sit here.
- **S4 El Norte and S5 Sudcalifornia:** Spanish-language usage and a different relation to the state and to land.
- **I1 to I6 Indigenous units:** separate each people; firmness over land and fishing may be told in its own terms. Never one voice.
- Within units: age, gender, class, party, and the large Asian-heritage communities of the coastal cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in English news, labor and protest records; mixed for Spanish usage in Baja; thin and uneven for Indigenous words, which are often held by communities.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Cantonese and Mandarin helpful; Indigenous languages through community partners.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous accounts of land, fishing and protest belong to the people who tell them; ask before quoting. Recent standoffs (Malheur, Wet'suwet'en) are politically charged; record the framings, not a verdict.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search each unit's main language dictionary for the terms in section 1; note which words carry praise and which blame.

5. Contact one Indigenous language program or cultural office and ask what is shareable.
