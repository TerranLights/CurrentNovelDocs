# Cascadia: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, Southeast Alaska, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, some French (British Columbia), Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin; large communities in the Vancouver, Seattle and Bay Area), Indigenous languages (see below). **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |

| Indigenous languages | no terms given | ask each nation's language program for words on safety, provision and food stores | do not guess terms |

English keywords: financial security, food security, housing insecurity, precarity, wildfire insurance, earthquake preparedness, fear of crime, gated community; French *sécurité d'emploi* (B.C.).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, British Columbia coast, Far West (interior, ranch and timber country), Southern California, the Bajas: different exposure to quakes, fire, crime and layoffs.
- Renters and owners; gig and salaried workers; age; urban and rural; fishing, logging, tech and tourism workers.
- Mexican American, Chinese, Japanese American and other immigrant communities with their own mutual-aid forms; migrant Mixtec and Triqui workers in the Bajas.
- Tribal and First Nations groups and Southeast Alaska Native communities: food and provision, as each nation states it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states and British Columbia (surveys); mixed for the Bajas (strong crime-perception surveys, thinner on savings); thin for Indigenous nations' own statements.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Indigenous languages for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** each nation's food and provision knowledge is theirs to share; use only what nations publish. Crime material in the Bajas: published surveys only.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Federal Reserve, FINRA, USDA and Statistics Canada tables for WA, OR, CA and B.C., and the INEGI tables for the two Bajas.
2. Search each region's newspaper archives for "layoffs", "foreclosure", "insurance" and "emergency fund" in a few decades.
3. Collect insurer-retreat and earthquake-preparedness material from state and provincial agencies.
4. Search mutual-aid and benevolent-society records (*mutualistas*, Chinese and Japanese American associations, credit unions).
5. Ask tribal and First Nation governments' sites for "food security" and "food sovereignty."
