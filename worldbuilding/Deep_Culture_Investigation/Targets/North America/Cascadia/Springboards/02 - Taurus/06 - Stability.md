# Cascadia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Lands: Washington, Oregon, California, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur, and the Southeast Alaska Panhandle. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (small BC minority), Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin), Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

English keywords: residential mobility, job tenure, place attachment, rootedness, century farm, heritage business, displacement, gentrification, boom and bust, cost of housing, migrant farmwork, seasonal round.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (coastal cities and metros): housing churn and newcomers against old families.
- **Far West** (interior, ranch and resource country): land held over generations and boom-bust trades.
- **El Norte** (Hispanic southern California): family continuity and migration ties.
- **British Columbia coast:** a separate national frame for statistics.
- **Sudcalifornia** (Baja): border cities against the peninsula's small towns; migrants on both sides.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene peoples): each separately; continuity in place is told differently by each.
- **Other cuts:** migrant farmworkers; renters against owners; displaced neighborhoods; recent arrivals and refugees; Asian-heritage and other long-settled immigrant families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian measures; mixed for Mexican local history; thin for Indigenous perspectives in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French and Chinese for some communities; Indigenous languages through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings on land and continuity may be restricted or belong to the nations; use only what a nation publishes. Displacement stories are painful for living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI mobility tables for the unit areas.
2. Search each language's terms above in local newspapers and obituary pages.
3. Look up the century-farm programs and historical societies in WA, OR, CA and BC.
4. Search the Indigenous language and cultural organizations for what each publishes on place and continuity.
5. Find tightness-looseness and state-personality studies and note which units they split.
