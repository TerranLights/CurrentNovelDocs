# Cascadia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French (British Columbia); Cantonese, Mandarin, Tagalog, Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages by nation. **Concept in one line:** how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast, Far West, El Norte and the British Columbia coast:** different founding groups and union histories.
- **Urban and rural:** the Puget Sound and Bay Area against timber, farm and mining towns.
- **Baja Californians:** ejido, cooperative and border-city groups.
- **Immigrant communities:** Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Mexican, Korean and Vietnamese associations.
- **Indigenous nations:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Subarctic Dene and Yuman peoples, each separate, each in its own voice.
- **Generations and classes:** union and non-union, joiners and non-joiners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states and British Columbia (labor, sports, polling); mixed for Baja (INEGI, El Colef, local press); thin for Indigenous accounts in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog, Japanese and Korean for associations and press; French for francophone groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous house, clan and ceremonial knowledge may be restricted; seek permission through the nation. Gang and cult material can name living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
