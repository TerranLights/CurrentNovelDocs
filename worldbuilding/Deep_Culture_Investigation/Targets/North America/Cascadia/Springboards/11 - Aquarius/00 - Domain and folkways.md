# Cascadia: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America. Pre-war lands: Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (BC), Chinese varieties, Tagalog, Japanese, and Indigenous languages (Chinuk Wawa, Lushootseed, Haida, Tlingit, Kumiai and others). **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, associations, humanitarian ideals, the future, and the customs of settling, joining and hosting.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Settler regional cultures:** the Left Coast, the BC coast, the interior Far West, the Southern California and Baja borderlands, the Panhandle's towns; their founders differed.
- **City against countryside:** Seattle, Portland, Vancouver, the Bay Area, Los Angeles against logging, farm, fishing and desert towns.
- **Indigenous peoples, one by one:** coast feast peoples, Plateau, California and Baja peoples (Kumiai, Pa ipai, Cucapá).
- **Immigrant communities:** Mexican and Central American, Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Russian, Scandinavian; each has its own associations.
- **Group types:** congregations, unions, co-ops, intentional communities, tech workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian surveys and archives; mixed for the Bajas; thin and uneven for Indigenous communities.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; French, Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog for specific communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous feasts, ceremonies and clan knowledge may be restricted; use only what each nation publishes. Immigration-status and sanctuary records are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
