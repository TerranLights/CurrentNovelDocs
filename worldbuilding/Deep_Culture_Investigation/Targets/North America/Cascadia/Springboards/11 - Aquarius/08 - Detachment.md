# Cascadia: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Spanish (the Bajas, and Spanish-speaking communities across California, Washington and Oregon); Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean for large migrant communities; Indigenous languages only where a people chooses to share. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms valued (calm, objectivity, equanimity), others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast (Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area): the reputed "freeze" and tech-era distance.
- British Columbia coast: an English-speaking reserve alongside large Chinese-Canadian communities.
- Far West interior: small-town familiarity against urban anonymity.
- El Norte and Sudcalifornia: Spanish-language norms of warmth and reserve; the Bajas as their own case.
- Panhandle settlers: remoteness and solitude as a way of life.
- Indigenous peoples (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin edge, Dene): non-interference as some describe it, which outsiders may misread as distance.
- Migrant and refugee communities (Mexican, Filipino, Vietnamese, Chinese, Korean): other display rules.
- Generation, gender, class and occupation (medicine, journalism, tech, the military) within each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for living alone, time use and professional codes; mixed for loneliness and affect; thin for regional emotional style, and for the Bajas especially.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings on conduct and silence; use only what a people publishes or shares; do not adopt outsiders' accounts as findings. Men's isolation and suicide data need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull one-person households and time-alone figures for each state, province and Baja municipio, to see whether regional contrast exists at all.
2. Search newspaper archives for "Seattle Freeze" and its equivalents, recording claims as claims.
3. Search Spanish-language sources for desapego, frialdad and calor humano, with "Baja California" and "Tijuana".
4. Find the professional codes on objectivity and boundaries (journalism, medicine, therapy) and how each names distance.
5. Ask Indigenous nations' own cultural centers and publications what they say about non-interference and silence.
