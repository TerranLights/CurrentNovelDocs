# Cascadia: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (pre-war lands: Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese for large immigrant communities; Indigenous languages through the peoples' own programs. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

English keywords: nonconformity, deviance, labeling, tightness-looseness, subculture, counterculture, intentional community, dress code, "weird", moral panic.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (settler core):** the region's self-image as "weird" versus its measured data; compare city and suburb.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian norms and a long dissenting-church history; Vancouver's scenes against Seattle's.
- **Far West (interior):** the test of the "refuge for misfits" idea against interior towns; rural against urban.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican norms in the borderlands, Chicano youth cultures, the Baja scenes.
- **Indigenous peoples (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene):** each people's own place for the different; do not merge.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canada (surveys, press, archives, scholarship); mixed for Baja; thin and variable for Indigenous peoples and immigrant communities, where the question may be put differently.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese for the large Chinese Canadian and Chinese American communities; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gender and sexual nonconformity (use people's own accounts); Indigenous teachings on the different (some may be restricted; ask each people); living individuals.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull state and provincial figures from tightness-looseness, regional-personality and religion surveys for the three U.S. states, British Columbia, and the Baja states.
2. Search the alternative weeklies and independent-label archives for how scenes name themselves and the "weird" slogans.
3. Search the Pacific Northwest and California special collections for counterculture, commune and dissenting-church holdings.
4. Look up the Baja California scenes (rock, punk, lowriders, Pride) in Spanish-language press and local university work.
5. Ask each Indigenous nation's language or cultural program how to approach the topic, and what may not be recorded.
