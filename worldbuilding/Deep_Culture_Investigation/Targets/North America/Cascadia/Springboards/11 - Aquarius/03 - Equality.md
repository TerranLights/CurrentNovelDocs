# Cascadia: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Korean, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages of its lands (Tlingit, Haida, Halkomelem, Nuu-chah-nulth, Lushootseed, Chinuk Wawa, Yurok, Kumeyaay/Kumiai). **Concept in one line:** people of equal worth and standing, treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and the BC coast** (Yankee and British-colonial founding; to be tested, not assumed).
- **Far West** and interior BC (environment, farm and mining labor).
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia** (Mexican civil society, class and color).
- **Panhandle settlers.**
- **Northwest Coast peoples:** ranked and redistributive societies, by their own account.
- **Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples:** compare each separately.
- **Cross-cutting:** immigrant generations (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Mexican, Vietnamese), tech workers, farmworkers, domestic workers, religious congregations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states and Canada (surveys by state); mixed for Baja California; thin for Baja California Sur and the Panhandle; thin and uneven for Indigenous views.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for community press; Indigenous languages for the peoples' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge on rank, houses and ceremony may be restricted; use only what the nations publish. Slurs and prejudice are described, never repeated.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
