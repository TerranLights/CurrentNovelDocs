# Cascadia: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Spanish; Chinese for a large immigrant community; the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settlers** (Puget Sound to Monterey): Yankee reform lineage, Progressive-era direct democracy.
- **British Columbia coast:** British colonial founding; reform through commissions and assemblies.
- **Far West** (interior and Central Valley): resource and farm economies; reform as something done to them.
- **El Norte** (Southern California, northern Baja): Mexican reform tradition crossing a border.
- **Sudcalifornia:** peninsular, thinly settled; likely different attitudes.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples, as targets of reform and as its authors.
- **Cuts inside units:** farmworkers and growers; urban reformers and rural objectors; Chinese-, Japanese-, Filipino- and Mexican-heritage communities; generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states and British Columbia; mixed for Baja California; thin for Baja California Sur.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese for some press; Indigenous languages for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivor testimony on boarding and residential schools; Indigenous nations' restricted knowledge; the safety of activists and journalists in Mexico; ballot measures are law, so use only their campaigns and results.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Ballotpedia and the three state offices: list the major reform measures by state, then see which passed by county.
2. WVS and ISSP: pull the reform-or-revolution item for the United States, Canada and Mexico; check for state or provincial breakdowns.
3. Search each regional archive for "reform association", "good government" and "recall" with a date range.
4. Search the Bancroft, Reuther and Mexican university catalogs for farmworker and reform-movement papers.
5. Ask each Indigenous nation's language and cultural program what it will share on change and renewal.
