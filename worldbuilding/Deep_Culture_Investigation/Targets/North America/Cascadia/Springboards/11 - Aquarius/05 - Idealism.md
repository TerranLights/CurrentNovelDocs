# Cascadia: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin) for the large immigrant communities; Indigenous languages through the nations' own language programs. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and Far West:** coastal communes and cities against interior and rural places; idealism may sit differently in each.
- **British Columbia coast:** the Finnish and Doukhobor colonies, and its own countercultural history.
- **Baja California and Baja California Sur:** Mexican ideals and political language.
- **Chinese, Filipino, Mexican and other immigrant communities:** ideals brought and ideals learned.
- **Religious groups:** evangelical, Mormon, Quaker, Buddhist, secular; idealism may be framed differently.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene; each nation's own ideals.
- **Age cohorts and the tech sector:** the young against the old; mission talk in tech.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler English-language sources and the colonies' histories; mixed for Baja and immigrant-language sources; thin for Indigenous ideals, which outsiders have often romanticized.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese if immigrant communities are included.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted Indigenous knowledge; accounts of cult survivors (Brother XII, Peoples Temple, Synanon).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the World Values Survey postmaterialism items for the three U.S. states, BC and Mexico.
2. Search the Foundation for Intentional Community directory by state and province.
3. Read the colony histories for Aurora, Kaweah, Home, Sointula; look for the colonists' own diaries.
4. Search regional newspaper archives for "idealist", "sellout" and "make a difference" across eras.
5. Ask the Native nations' cultural programs what, if anything, they publish on right living.
