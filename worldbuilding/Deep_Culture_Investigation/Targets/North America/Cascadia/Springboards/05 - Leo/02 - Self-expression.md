# Cascadia: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Korean, Vietnamese, Tagalog, and the Indigenous languages of the Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman and Dene peoples. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (Puget Sound, Portland, Bay Area) against **Far West** (interior, Central Valley): the "weird" and tech cities against farm and mining towns.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia** (Southern California, the Bajas): Spanish-language expression, the *qué dirán*, border style; lowrider and Chicano traditions.
- **British Columbia coast** against interior: a separate national setting from the U.S. units.
- **Panhandle settlers** against its Native peoples.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): dress, song and ceremony, and their suppression and revival; no single voice.
- Within every unit: gender, generation, class, immigrant communities (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Korean, Vietnamese, Mexican, Oaxacan), faith, LGBTQ people, urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for the U.S. and Canadian coast (surveys, archives, media); mixed for the Bajas and the Panhandle; thin for Indigenous units except through their own programs and the Canadian inquiry record.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, Tagalog; Indigenous languages for the Native units.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial dress, regalia, song and dance belong to families and nations, and some knowledge is restricted; ask the nations' own cultural offices. Boarding and residential school material is painful and sometimes restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the World Values Survey self-expression items; note which waves allow state or province cuts, and the Rentfrow state data.
2. Search the Online Archive of California and BC Archives for "zine", "student newspaper", "dress code", "Free Speech Movement".
3. Search INEGI and Statistics Canada for identity and discrimination surveys.
4. Search school-district dress-code and hair policies in Seattle, Portland, San Francisco, Vancouver and Tijuana.
5. Contact tribal cultural and language programs before touching dress, regalia or ceremony.
