# Cascadia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, and the Indigenous languages of the eleven units. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and Far West settlers:** separate civic pride (city, state, "weird") from rural and trades pride.
- **British Columbia coast:** pride defined against its country and its neighbors; test, do not assume.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican and Chicano pride traditions; Baja's own regional pride phrases.
- **Immigrant-origin communities (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Mexican and others):** pride in origin against pride in the new country.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, northern Dene): pride as revival and as restricted knowledge; keep each people separate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for U.S. national pride and Pride histories; thin at state and sub-state level; thin to unknown for Indigenous and Baja concepts of pride.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages through dictionaries and archives, with translators.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous crest, name and song rights, and ceremonial display belong to families and nations; do not extract without permission. Pride in contested heritage (pioneer against dispossession) needs care. Wounded pride and supremacist group pride are real topics; record variation, no stereotypes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Gallup and World Values Survey national-pride items for the U.S. and Canada, and the INEGI items for Mexico; note what state or province cuts exist.
2. Search the California, Washington and Oregon state and city archives and the three Pride organizations' histories for "pride" in local civic and movement contexts.
3. Search FirstVoices, the Alaska Native Language Archive and the Berkeley collections for the unit languages' words for "proud", "boast" and "honor", and record only attested entries.
4. Search Baja California and Baja California Sur newspapers for *orgullo*, *orgulloso* with the state and city names, and for *presumido* and *mamón*.
