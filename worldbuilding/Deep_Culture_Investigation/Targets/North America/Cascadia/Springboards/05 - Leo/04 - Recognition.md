# Cascadia: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Mexican Spanish, Mandarin and Cantonese, Japanese, Tagalog, and Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, BC coast and Panhandle settlers: civic honors and renaming debates may differ by city.
- Far West (interior, Central Valley, Okanagan): rural and farm labor often unsung.
- El Norte and Baja California settlers: Mexican credential and title customs.
- Baja California Sur: separate framework, small and distinct.
- Northwest Coast peoples: honors given by witnessing and naming.
- Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples: each its own standard of worth.
- Immigrant communities (Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Mexican and Mixtec, Vietnamese, others): credentials and redress.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on formal honors, apologies and surveys (English); mixed on everyday practice and on Mexican Baja; thin to unknown on Indigenous concepts in published sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog for community presses; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** potlatch and memorial honors belong to the peoples holding them; names, regalia and crests are restricted knowledge; go through each nation's own heritage body. Redress histories involve living survivors.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Order of Canada and Order of British Columbia lists, and the California Hall of Fame inductees, for exemplar names by unit.
2. Search "reconocimiento" and "premio estatal" with Tijuana, Mexicali and La Paz.
3. Read the Truth and Reconciliation final report's BC sections and the California and BC apology texts for the vocabulary of acknowledgment.
4. Search Gallup and Statistics Canada for recognition items by state and province; note the finest level available.
5. Ask Sealaska Heritage Institute and BC and Washington tribal language programs how to approach honoring terms.
