# Cascadia: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, plus Indigenous languages of the lands (Salish, Wakashan, Dene, Yuman, Uto-Aztecan and others). **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Settler regions: the Left Coast cities, the British Columbia coast, the interior Far West, El Norte and Sudcalifornia in Baja (the Scope units). Compare because games, climate and spaces for play differ.
- Indigenous peoples of the Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Great Basin, Yuman lands and Subarctic Dene lands, each separate: their games, tricksters and revivals are their own.
- Urban and rural children; boys and girls; income groups (free play against paid activities).
- Immigrant-heritage communities (East Asian, Pacific Islander, Mexican, Filipino, Vietnamese): their play traditions and new games.
- Surf, skate and snow communities as a separate vertigo-play group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the settler regions (time use, school policy, industry, local press); mixed for Baja; thin and scattered for Indigenous play, and mostly historical or by community.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages only through speakers or community-approved sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native games carry ceremonial or gambling-song meaning; use only what communities publish. Early collectors' accounts (including the 1907 survey) can misdescribe; read critically.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ATUS (state or West-region estimates), Statistics Canada and ENUT tables for games, sports and play with children.
2. Search each state's, British Columbia's and Baja's school recess and physical-education rules.
3. Search Online Archive of California and Royal BC Museum for "games", "toys", "playground" and "recess".
4. Search Indigenous nations' own sites and cultural centers for "stick game", "hand game", "slahal" and "games" (their own publications first).
5. Search regional press for skate-park, playground and youth-sports disputes, and the games industry's regional history.
