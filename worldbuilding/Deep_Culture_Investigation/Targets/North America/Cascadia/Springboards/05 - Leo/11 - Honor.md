# Cascadia: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages (Tlingit, Haida, Kumeyaay, Lushootseed, Nisga'a and others). **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, British Columbia coast and Far West settler cultures: different founders (Yankee, British, upland Southern, Okie).
- Baja Californians, and Mexican-heritage Californians: *honra* lines with different histories.
- Military families and veterans against civilians.
- Chinese, Filipino, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese communities, separately.
- Black communities of the port cities: street and church codes.
- Indigenous units: Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman and Subarctic Dene peoples, separately, as each describes it.
- Rural against urban; first generation against later ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on honor psychology and the U.S. West's military life; thin on Baja, the Panhandle and interior BC.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Chinese varieties and Tagalog for diaspora communities; Indigenous languages through community programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous honoring ceremonies and regalia are community-governed; ask, and use only what the nations share. Honor used to justify violence in families needs careful, non-sensational sourcing.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search "honor states" with the three states and British Columbia; read the original studies' lists.
2. Search *honra* and *palabra de honor* in Baja California newspapers and COLEF publications.
3. Search Densho for "honor" and "shame" in oral histories.
4. Search Sealaska Heritage Institute and U'mista for public teaching on honoring.
5. Search *corridos* by place (Tijuana, Mexicali, Central Valley) in the Frontera Collection.
