# Cascadia: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Canadian French, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, and each Indigenous unit's languages. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Stage 0 units, with the Left Coast and the Far West separated: city and interior may differ.
- Coast Salish, Tlingit, Haida and other Northwest Coast nations, whose accounts of witnessing need their own sources.
- Mexican American and Baja communities.
- Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Korean and Vietnamese communities.
- Recent arrivals and their children versus long-settled families.
- Women and men; older people living alone; the unhoused; farm and service workers.
- Native women and girls, where visibility and danger meet.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on media representation, census coverage and surveys; thin on everyday acknowledgment and on Indigenous and Baja views in print.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; Chinese and others for diaspora communities; Indigenous languages through community programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ceremonial witnessing (a nation's own knowledge; use only what it publishes). MMIWG testimony needs care. Disaster-death records name individuals; use aggregates.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the MMIWG report's contents and the 2020 Post-Enumeration Survey results for Washington, Oregon and California.
2. Search the UCLA, Annenberg and GLAAD reports for Pacific coast findings.
3. Search the museums' sites for exhibits on being overlooked.
4. Search each Indigenous nation's language program for the word for witness, and ask before using it.
5. Search the Seattle Times and Vancouver Sun archives for "Seattle Freeze".
