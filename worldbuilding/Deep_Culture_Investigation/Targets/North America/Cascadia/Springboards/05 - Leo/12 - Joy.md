# Cascadia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Chinuk Wawa and the Indigenous languages of each unit. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

English keywords: joy, happiness, well-being, life satisfaction, celebration, collective effervescence, fiesta, potlatch feast (see Flags), laughter, smiling norms, toxic positivity, joy and grief.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **S1 Left Coast** and **S2 British Columbia coast**: a shared outdoors-and-gloom setting; check whether joy talk differs across the border.
- **S3 Far West**: farm, ranch and Okie-descended communities against the coastal cities.
- **S4 El Norte** and **S5 Sudcalifornia**: Spanish-language fiesta and music traditions, on both sides of the Mexican border.
- **Immigrant communities**: Chinese, Mexican and others, separated by generation and by language.
- **I1 Northwest Coast, I2 Plateau, I3 California, I4 Yuman and Kumiai, I5 Great Basin edge, I6 Dene peoples**: each on its own terms; do not merge.
- **Religious groups**: Pentecostal, Catholic, secular and others (Azusa Street Revival, Los Angeles, 1906, is a known start for Pentecostal joy; *check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and festivals in the U.S. and Canada; mixed for the Bajas (state-level INEGI data, thinner press access); thin and indirect for joy as Indigenous nations themselves frame it.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Chinese and other immigrant languages; Indigenous languages through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonies, feasts and songs may be a nation's restricted knowledge (potlatch and winter dances, for example); seek the nation's own public statements and permission before using anything beyond them. Joy beside grief (funerals, memorials) is delicate.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Pacific-state and British Columbia life-satisfaction figures from the GSS, BRFSS and Statistics Canada, and the two Bajas' from BIARE.
2. Search the regional dictionaries and slang sources for "stoked", "hella" and *chido*; note who uses each.
3. List each unit's major festival and read its program and press coverage.
4. Search each Indigenous nation's own site for celebration, feast and language-program pages on joy words.
5. Search archives for "fiesta", "celebration" and "joy" with place names, for the history layer.
