# Cascadia: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Japanese, Korean, French (BC), and Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** and **British Columbia coast** against **Far West**: dignity at work in cities against farm and mining country.
- **El Norte** and **Sudcalifornia**: Spanish-language *dignidad* and *vida digna*; Mexico's service standards.
- **Migrant farm workers** (Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec in San Quintín; Mexican and Filipino farmworkers in California): work and treatment.
- **Asian Canadian and Asian American communities**: Chinese, Filipino, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese; "face" against dignity.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Dene), each separately: residential-school and boarding-school survivors; each nation's own teachings on respect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey and health reporting (U.S., Canada); mixed for the Bajas; thin or restricted for Indigenous teachings.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for diaspora sources; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony (residential and boarding schools, internment) needs care and survivors-first sourcing; Indigenous teachings may be restricted knowledge; end-of-life accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Oregon, Washington, California and Health Canada assisted-dying reports for how end-of-life dignity is framed.
2. Search ENADIS and Statistics Canada for state and province tables on respect and discrimination.
3. Search "trato digno" with Baja California and the state human-rights commission.
4. Search Densho and the TRC reports for "dignity" and "respect" in testimony.
5. Ask the major Indigenous language programs and cultural centers what they publish on respect.
