## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coast Salish, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, Kwakwaka'wakw, Nuu-chah-nulth: each with its own feast customs; never merge.
- Plateau and southern Oregon and California Native nations.
- Baja Indigenous peoples and Oaxacan Indigenous migrants in Baja California.
- Mexican American and Baja Mexican households (kin giving, *padrinos*, remittances).
- Tech-wealth donors against rural and small-town givers.
- Tithing churches, Catholic, Jewish and Latter-day Saint givers against secular mutual-aid networks.
- Asian and Pacific Islander immigrant communities.
- British Columbia against the U.S. states; urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian giving statistics; mixed for Mexican informal giving; thin and scattered for Indigenous feast practice outside nation-held material.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Indigenous languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feast protocols, names and privileges belong to the nations and families; some knowledge is restricted. Prefer what the nations publish; read colonial ethnography critically.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull IRS giving tables for the three states and the Statistics Canada survey for British Columbia; note what they cannot see.
2. Search each Northwest Coast nation's cultural center for "potlatch", "giveaway" and "feast".
3. Search Baja California for *cooperacha*, *clubes de oriundos*, *mayordomía* and remittances by state.
4. Search Buy Nothing and mutual-aid groups in Seattle, Portland and the Bay Area.
5. Search regional newspapers for "tipping" and "tip fatigue".
