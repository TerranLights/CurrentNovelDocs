# Cascadia: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Spanish (Baja, El Norte, California); Chinese, Vietnamese and Tagalog for the large immigrant communities; the Indigenous languages of the lands (see Flags). **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (Pacific California, Oregon, Washington):** the Hollywood, San Francisco and Seattle art and tech worlds.
- **British Columbia coast:** Vancouver's film and arts scene, under Canadian funding.
- **Far West (the interior):** likely different from the cities.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican and Chicano making-do traditions and Baja's craft and cooking.
- **Northwest Coast peoples:** art tied to houses, crests and feasts.
- **Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples:** each separate.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean and others), and class, gender and age within all of these.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for the United States and Canada (surveys, grant records, art criticism, tech-industry press); mixed for Baja; mixed to thin for the Indigenous peoples, where much is held by the nations themselves.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Chinese, Vietnamese and Tagalog for immigrant arts; Indigenous languages through the nations' language programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** designs, crests and regalia carry owners and protocols; use only what the nations share publicly. Some ceremonial objects and songs are not for outside study. Ask before quoting any maker.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the arts satellite account for the three states, then the Statistics Canada and INEGI equivalents.
2. Search the NEA participation survey for state or regional tables.
3. Read the grant lists and jury language of the California Arts Council, BC Arts Council and Secretaría de Cultura for how each names creativity.
4. Search the Indigenous cultural centers' and museums' public pages for their own statements on art and design ownership.
5. Search guild statements on AI and authorship, then local arts press from Vancouver, Seattle, Los Angeles and Tijuana.
