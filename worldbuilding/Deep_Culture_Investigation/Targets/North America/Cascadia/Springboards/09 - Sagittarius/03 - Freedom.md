# Cascadia: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also Chinese, Tagalog, Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese, Russian and French; Indigenous languages as each nation publishes them. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or the state; and freedom as a belief and political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast core (Puget Sound, Portland, Bay Area):** civil-liberties and libertarian traditions side by side.
- **Far West interior (eastern Washington and Oregon, Central Valley, interior BC):** public-land access, anti-government strands, religious liberty.
- **El Norte (Southern California, Baja):** Mexican and Mexican-American usage; border crossing.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian rights-and-freedoms framing against the U.S. one.
- **Indigenous nations:** sovereignty, fishing and land rights (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Subarctic Dene, Yuman, Great Basin peoples, Kumiai and neighbors).
- **Communities remembering freedom lost:** Japanese, Chinese, Black, and Filipino groups.
- **Newcomers and refugees:** Vietnamese, Russian-speaking, Chinese, Latin American.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English and U.S. polling, and for internment and protest archives; mixed for British Columbia and the Panhandle (small samples); thin for Baja California Sur and for Indigenous usage.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for diaspora press; Indigenous languages via the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings are the nations' to share; use only what they publish. Internment and exclusion testimony: follow archive terms. The pandemic and "Freedom Convoy" debates are live and partisan: record, do not judge.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull WVS, Gallup and AmericasBarometer items on freedom by country; note what splits to state or province.
2. Search the Free Speech Movement, IWW free-speech fights and Magonismo collections for how each used the word.
3. Search Densho and the Nikkei National Museum for "freedom" in internment testimony.
4. Search each Indigenous nation's own site for "freedom" and "sovereignty."
5. Compare "libertad" and "libertinaje" in Baja California press and school texts.
