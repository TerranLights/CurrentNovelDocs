# Cascadia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, the Indigenous languages of the eleven units, and immigrant congregations' languages. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settlers:** secular cities against evangelical and Catholic pockets; test by county.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian frame, so separate from U.S. data; large Chinese congregations.
- **Far West interior:** Central Valley, Redding, Okanagan, Columbia Plateau; Pentecostal and evangelical strands, measured not assumed.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** Mexican Catholic practice, evangelical growth, patron-saint fiestas.
- **Immigrant congregations:** Korean, Filipino, Chinese, Vietnamese, Latino, Slavic, Armenian, Muslim, Jewish; each its own faith life.
- **Indigenous units:** mission and residential-school history beside traditional practice; each nation separate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. units; mixed for British Columbia and the Bajas (thin regional cuts); thin and sensitive for Indigenous units.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; immigrant languages; Indigenous languages through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial and spiritual knowledge belongs to each people and may be closed. Abuse and residential-school material is painful; take survivors' and nations' own accounts first.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Pew and Gallup state and province cuts, then INEGI's religion tables for the two Bajas.
