# Cascadia: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; French; Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese; Indigenous languages through each nation's own programs. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast:** Yankee reform and the counterculture's self-fulfillment tradition.
- **Panhandle settlers:** small fishing, timber and tourism towns.
- **British Columbia coast:** a Canadian setting, with large Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Filipino communities.
- **Far West:** farm and interior towns, with Okie, Mexican and Hmong layers.
- **El Norte** and **Sudcalifornia:** Spanish-language and Catholic strands; migrant labor, fishing, tourism.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples, each separate.
- **Cross-cutting:** class, religiosity and the unaffiliated, generation, immigrants, veterans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. states; mixed for British Columbia (slices of national surveys); thin to mixed for the Bajas; Indigenous meaning is mostly in the nations' own words, thin in public data.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean for community press; each Indigenous language through its own program.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and despair data; Indigenous ceremony and vision quests, where some knowledge is restricted and outsiders who copy it draw sanction; cult and psychedelic material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

5. Sample a year of obituaries from one Left Coast, one Far West and one Baja paper.
