*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Settler units by state, province and the Baja peninsula: systems and funding differ.
- First-generation students against families with long college ties.
- Tech hubs, farm towns, rural and coastal communities: access and the meaning of college differ.
- Mexican-heritage and Baja communities: Mexican titles and system against U.S. schooling.
- Asian-heritage, Pacific Islander and other immigrant communities: heritage-language Saturday schools (from data, not assumption).
- Indigenous peoples by nation (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): boarding and residential school histories, language programs and tribal colleges differ nation by nation.
- Religious schooling, homeschooling and unschooling communities; party lines in attitudes to higher education.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian attainment and attitude surveys; mixed for Baja California (INEGI solid, culture-level sources thinner); thin and nation-specific for Indigenous education.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish; Indigenous languages through the nations' own materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding and residential school trauma; teaching and language material a nation restricts; debt and dropout silences. Ask before using community material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull attainment by county, province and *municipio* from the ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI to fix the units.
2. Find Gallup and Pew data on confidence in higher education, split by state and party.
3. Search the nations' own language and education departments for what each shares publicly.
4. Search *educación*, *bien educado* and *persona preparada* in Baja California press.
5. Search student papers and school-board archives for "first-gen", "street smart" and "ivory tower".
