# Cascadia: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), French, Tagalog, Chinook Jargon, Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it, and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Settler units: Left Coast (with the Panhandle's settlers), British Columbia coast, Far West interior, El Norte (Hispanic and Mexican-American communities), Sudcalifornia (Baja). Indigenous units: Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples, because oral-history standing and its relation to science and settler records may differ by people. Also separate language communities, religion, urban and rural, and generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trust in news and science, mostly state or national; mixed for language communities and Baja; thin for Indigenous peoples' own accounts, much of which the nations hold.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish; Chinese and French for the Canadian coast; Indigenous language programs for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' statements, burial searches, and oral histories a people controls; ask each nation's office before citing. Baja journalism carries risks for sources.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Reuters, Pew and Gallup trust tables; note what is cut by state or province.
2. Read the TRC's British Columbia material and the California and federal boarding-school reports; list the nations named.
3. Search each Indigenous unit's tribal or First Nations site for a heritage or language office and its rules on sharing oral history.
4. Query the Online Archive of California and BC archives for "oral history" plus "truth".
5. Compare vaccine-exemption and local-news maps by county.
