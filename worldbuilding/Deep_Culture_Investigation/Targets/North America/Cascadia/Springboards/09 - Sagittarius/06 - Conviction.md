# Cascadia: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French, Cantonese and Mandarin, Japanese, Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content (not a criminal conviction).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settler culture** (Seattle, Portland, Bay Area, Los Angeles): the heaviest protest and movement history; compare cities against each other.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian party discipline and a different protest history (Greenpeace's origin, draft-resister arrivals).
- **Far West interior:** ranching, mining and farm-labor politics; the farm-labor boycotts happened here.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Baja):** Mexican party blocs, Mexican Catholic and Protestant conscience traditions.
- **Indigenous peoples** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): stands for fishing, land and sovereignty; each nation separately, not one voice.
- **Within units:** generation, party, religion (born-in against convert), peace churches, Asian American and Latino communities, activists against the disengaged.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. coast (surveys, archives, oral histories); mixed for British Columbia; thin for Baja California and for the Indigenous units' own words on this concept.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; some Chinese and Japanese for community press; Indigenous languages through each nation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge and ceremony are the nation's to release; ask before quoting. Accounts of arson and sabotage, and of clinic protests, are contested: describe, do not endorse. Interviews with people who left family over a cause need consent.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. A Spanish and an English dictionary entry for each term above, then the idiom lists: what words stand for steadfastness and for the turncoat.
2. Densho and the UFW archive: search "refused", "resister", "fast", "boycott", and list the people and places.
3. WVS, ANES and Statistics Canada: pull petition, boycott and demonstration items for the three states, British Columbia and Baja California; note the sample sizes.
4. Each Indigenous nation's language program and tribal newspaper: ask for words and stories of standing firm; record only what is offered publicly.
5. Regional newspaper archives: search tree-sit, fish-in, hunger strike, sanctuary; build a place-and-date list.
