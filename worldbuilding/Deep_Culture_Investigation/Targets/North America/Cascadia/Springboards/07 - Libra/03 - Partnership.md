# Cascadia: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; the Bajas). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also French, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages by nation. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: couple, work and business, and between peoples. **Libra reading:** the letter of the law only.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (U.S. lobe) and the BC coast:** one culture, two legal families.
- **Far West interior:** rural, religious and family-firm pockets.
- **El Norte lobe (southern California and the Bajas):** Spanish-language, civil-law side; compare Baja California with Baja California Sur.
- **Panhandle settlers:** small, Russian-era roots.
- **Indigenous nations:** tribes of Washington, Oregon, California and Southeast Alaska; BC First Nations and modern-treaty nations; Baja peoples (Kumiai, Pa ipai, Cucapá). Each has its own legal standing.
- **Immigrant communities:** Chinese, Filipino, Mexican, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes and cases; mixed for Baja; thin for tribal and First Nations law.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French; community languages above.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous kinship and marriage rules may be restricted; use only what a nation publishes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the marriage, domestic-partnership and partnership statutes of Washington, Oregon, California and BC; note how each defines "spouse" and "partner".
2. Read Baja California's civil code on marriage and concubinato, in Spanish.
3. Search the case-law databases for unmarried cohabitants' property and partners who fall out.
4. List treaty, compact and co-management texts in the territory; read their wording of partnership.
5. Request each major tribal and First Nations government's published family code.
