# Cascadia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean; Tlingit, Kwak'wala, Kumeyaay. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northwest Coast peoples** (Tlingit, Haida, Kwakwaka'wakw, Coast Salish): feasting and giveaway systems with their own rules.
- **California, Plateau and Baja Indigenous peoples** (Kumeyaay, Yurok, Pa ipai): separate systems of obligation; do not merge with the coast.
- **Left Coast, British Columbia coast and Far West settlers:** rural exchange of labor against urban anonymity.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** *compadrazgo*, *tandas*, remittance households, border kin.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Mixtec, Zapotec): benevolent and hometown associations, rotating credit.
- **Religious groups:** offerings, tithing, vows to saints.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and the potlatch; mixed for everyday reciprocity (rarely recorded directly); thin for small immigrant groups and the Bajas' Indigenous communities.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Japanese and Korean for immigrant sources; Indigenous languages for the nations' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feast protocols, names and regalia may belong to families or houses; the potlatch was banned, so the written record comes from the ban's side. Ask before using community material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
