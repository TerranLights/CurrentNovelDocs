# Cascadia: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (Canadian sources), plus Indigenous languages of its lands. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and Far West settlers:** different state law, a split in views on affirmative action and redistribution.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian law and wage rules, and a different inheritance regime.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Baja):** Mexican law, profit-sharing, a separate vocabulary of unfairness.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Great Basin, Yuman and Subarctic Dene peoples:** each with its own distribution rules, which may follow standing or role as well as shares.
- **Within units:** class, gender, generation, rural and urban, immigrant and native-born.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian attitudes and law; mixed for Baja; thin for Indigenous peoples in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; French; Indigenous languages for primary sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous distribution customs and ceremonies; ask each nation before using anything not published by it.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
