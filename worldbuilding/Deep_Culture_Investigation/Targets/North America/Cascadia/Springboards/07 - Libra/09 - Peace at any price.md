# Cascadia: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, plus Tagalog, Cantonese, Mandarin, Japanese, Tlingit and Haida for communities in its lands. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even at the cost of rights, truth or justice.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | "keep the peace", "don't rock the boat", "go along to get along" | idioms of giving way | also "pushover", "people-pleaser", "enabler" |
| Spanish | *llevar la fiesta en paz* | keep things peaceful | Bajas and Californian Spanish |
| Spanish | *cobro de piso*, *ley del silencio* | protection payment; law of silence | Tijuana, Mexicali |
| Tagalog | *pakikisama* | going along with the group | Filipino communities |
| Japanese | *gaman*, *shikata ga nai* | endurance; it can't be helped | Japanese American usage; check origin of each |
| Chinese | 忍 (*ren*), 和 (*he*) | endurance; harmony | Cantonese and Mandarin forms differ |
| Tlingit, Haida, Kumiai | unknown | peacemaking words | ask community language programs; do not guess |

English keywords: appeasement; self-help on people-pleasing (a genre to sample); conflict avoidance; negative and positive peace; nonviolence; pacifism; conscientious objection; nondisclosure agreement; silence and abuse; whistleblower; mediation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast settlers, British Columbia coast, Far West interior (ranching, mining, farm towns): different founders, different norms of speaking up.
- Quakers, Mennonites, Doukhobors against mainstream churches: principled peace against giving way.
- Japanese Americans and Japanese Canadians (incarceration era and after); Chinese and Filipino communities, each with its own vocabulary.
- Mexican Americans in California against Baja residents; migrant farmworkers (Mixtec, Triqui, Zapotec) against long-settled families.
- Coast Salish, Tlingit, Haida, Yurok, Kumeyaay and other Indigenous nations, each separately.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on reporting rates, settlements and Japanese American and pacifist history; thin on informal family norms, which sit in memoirs, oral history and advice genres.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Japanese, Chinese and Tagalog for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous peacemaking practices may be restricted knowledge; ask the nation before recording. Abuse testimony and extortion reporting need care for living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Densho and the JACL for "gaman", "cooperation" and "resistance" to see how that community frames giving way.
2. Pull the World Values Survey and Statistics Canada victimization data for the three countries' lands; note what splits by state or province.
3. Search the three states' NDA statutes and the EEOC's regional data (letter of the law only).
4. Search ENVIPE and Zeta for unreported crime and "cobro de piso" in Baja California.
5. Search the regional history journals for "pacifism", "conscientious objector" and "Doukhobor".
