# Cascadia: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin) for the large Chinese-heritage communities; Indigenous languages of the Northwest Coast, Plateau, California and Baja peoples. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others, and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (Pacific Northwest and Bay Area):** wellness and outdoor-recreation culture; restoration politics.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian federalism and leave rules differ from the U.S. side.
- **Far West and interior (farm, mining, ranching):** balance of land use and water; long seasonal hours.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia (southern California, the Bajas):** Mexican folk medicine, two-culture living, Mexican labor law.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Dene peoples:** each with its own teaching; do not merge them.
- **Chinese-heritage communities:** yin-yang and the mean in health and food.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for time use, hours, election returns and wellness (U.S., Canada, Mexico); mixed for folk medicine; thin and uneven for Indigenous teachings, which are sparsely published.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese for the Chinese-heritage press; Indigenous languages for the nations' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous teachings of balance and ceremony to restore it are the nations' to share; use only what they publish and say so. *Curandero* practice may be private.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the time-use surveys (U.S., Canada, Mexico) and note which can be cut to Washington, Oregon, California, British Columbia and the Bajas.
2. Search "work-life balance" and *equilibrio* with each state or province's name in local newspapers and public radio.
3. Find what each nation says about balance: start with the Nuu-chah-nulth Tribal Council and the Lower Elwha Klallam and Yurok tribes' own sites.
4. Search library catalogs for "hot and cold" folk medicine, Baja California and California Latino health.
5. Search the restoration record (Elwha, Klamath, Northwest Forest Plan) for how "balance" is argued.
