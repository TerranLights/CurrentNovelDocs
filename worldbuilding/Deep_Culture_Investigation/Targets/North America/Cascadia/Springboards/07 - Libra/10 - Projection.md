# Cascadia: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (Canada), Mandarin and Cantonese, plus Indigenous languages (terms to be asked, not guessed). **Concept in one line:** seeing in others what one disowns in oneself, and how a culture places its unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other"; the opposite practices of owning one's faults.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast urban and interior or rural settlers:** each is a mirror for the other; check "coastal elites" and "Californians" as labels.
- **Spanish-speaking Baja and the Californias:** *mal de ojo* and *limpia* practice; Judas-burning customs; ask each sphere.
- **Chinese, Japanese, Filipino, Korean and Vietnamese communities:** targets of past exclusion and panic, and also sources of their own images of others.
- **Mexican-heritage communities:** repatriation, Proposition 187, border talk.
- **Indigenous peoples** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): their own accounts of harm and blame; settler images of them.
- **Religious communities** (Catholic, evangelical, Jewish, Buddhist, secular): confession, atonement, accusation.
- **Generations and parties:** each side calls the other's accusation projection.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canadian coast (hate crime, panics, therapy culture); mixed for Baja; thin for Indigenous accounts of blame.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish, French, Chinese; Indigenous languages for any local terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge about harm, sorcery and blame can be restricted; ask through tribal or First Nations channels. Victims' testimony and trial records involving children (McMartin) are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the English and Spanish dictionary entries for the terms above; note regional variants.
2. Pull the FBI, Statistics Canada and CONAPRED tables for the target's lands; note reporting gaps.
3. Search Densho, the Online Archive of California (Tenney Committee papers) and the University of Washington collections for panic and exclusion records (1877 to 1950).
4. Search the Truth and Healing Council and the Truth and Reconciliation Commission for owning-faults material.
5. Ask tribal and First Nations cultural offices what, if anything, may be recorded on blame.
