# Cascadia: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese for parts of British Columbia; Tagalog, Vietnamese and Korean for diaspora communities; Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area): wellness and public-health politics.
- **British Columbia coast:** a single-payer system, Indigenous health governance.
- **Far West interior:** rural access, distance from care.
- **El Norte** (Southern California and Baja California): border health, the pharmacy-and-clinic trade.
- **Sudcalifornia:** a small, older, ranch and tourism society; the data are thin.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): each nation's own ideas of wellness; do not merge.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Mexican Indigenous migrants, among others): medical pluralism; set aside as later layers per the Scope.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canadian parts (surveys, county data); mixed for the Bajas (state-level data, thin on folk practice); thin for Sudcalifornia and for most Indigenous wellness thought outside what a nation publishes itself.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish at minimum; Chinese and Tagalog for diaspora lines; Indigenous languages by unit.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous healing ceremonies and knowledge are often restricted; use only what nations share publicly. Addiction, mental illness and suicide are silences; handle with care. Health data on undocumented people is sparse and sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the concept's own words in English and Spanish into a library catalog and a scholarly search (*salud*, *curandero*, "folk illness", "medical pluralism"), restricted to the Pacific coast and Baja.
2. Open the BRFSS, the Canadian Community Health Survey and ENSANUT pages and note which variables (self-rated health, use of complementary medicine, trust in doctors) exist at state, province or county level.
3. Read the First Nations Health Authority's and a few tribal health programs' public statements on wellness, and list each nation's own terms.
4. Search regional newspapers and archives for the main health disputes (vaccines, fluoride, harm reduction, border care).
