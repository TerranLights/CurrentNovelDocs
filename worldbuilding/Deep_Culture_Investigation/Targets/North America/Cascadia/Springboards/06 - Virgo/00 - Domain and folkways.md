*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast settlers (Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area):** Yankee founding; compare with Far West institutions.
- **British Columbia coast:** British colonial and Chinese-Canadian layers; a different state and welfare setting.
- **Far West (Central Valley, interior):** farm, ranch and corporate labor.
- **El Norte (Southern California, northern Baja):** Mexican-heritage work ways.
- **Sudcalifornia (Baja California Sur):** fishing, ranch and tourism work.
- **Panhandle settlers:** fishing, canneries, Russian and Norwegian layers.
- **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): seasonal rounds, each separate.
- Also: immigrant labor communities, tech-sector workers, and rural against urban.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canada (time use, labor, health); mixed for Baja; thin and scattered for how any one unit holds the concept.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for urban layers; Indigenous languages for seasonal and craft terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a people's restricted or ceremonial knowledge (healing, harvest rites); labor-camp and mission-labor histories need critical reading; ask a nation before using its material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

5. Query the labor archives for work songs, cannery and farmworker materials.
