# Cascadia: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French, plus Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast and Far West settler cultures:** service-economy cities against interior farm and logging towns.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia (Baja):** household help, *cargo*-style obligations and border-city hospitality may differ from the U.S. side.
- **British Columbia coast:** Canadian volunteering and public-service channels.
- **Religious groups:** Latter-day Saint, Catholic, Evangelical, secular volunteering, as a cross-cut.
- **Immigrant service workers and their employers:** a cross-cut, never one voice.
- **Indigenous units (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene):** service to elders, community and nation, and hosting protocols (for example Tribal Canoe Journeys *check*), each described in its own terms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering, tipping and emotional labor in U.S. and Canadian sources; mixed for Baja; thin and uneven for Indigenous understandings, which are rarely in surveys.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish chiefly; French for some communities; Indigenous languages for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** community protocols and ceremonial roles belong to the peoples; use only what they publish, and ask. Household-worker exploitation and harassment are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
