# Cascadia: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinuk Wawa and the Indigenous languages of each unit; Cantonese, Mandarin and Tagalog for the larger immigrant communities. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** (urban Puget Sound, Willamette, Bay Area): tech and service economies, long union history.
- **British Columbia coast:** resource and port economy, Canadian labor law and benefits.
- **Far West** (Central Valley, interior): farm, ranch and mining work; farmworker communities.
- **El Norte** (Southern California, Baja California): border factories, informal work, cross-border commuters.
- **Sudcalifornia:** fishing, mining, tourism and ranching.
- **Panhandle settlers:** fishing and timber.
- **Indigenous units:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin edge, Dene peoples; each separately.
- **Within units:** class, immigrant generation, formal against informal work, union against nonunion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canada (surveys, labor statistics, labor history); mixed for the Bajas; thin or uneven for Indigenous understandings of work, often held in oral and community sources.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish at minimum; Cantonese, Mandarin and Tagalog for immigrant communities; Indigenous languages as each nation allows.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted Indigenous knowledge and ceremonies (first-salmon rites among them); undocumented and informal workers (privacy and legal risk); wage theft and workplace deaths.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the World Values Survey and General Social Survey work items for the U.S. West and, where possible, British Columbia and Mexico; note what states or provinces can be separated.
2. Search the Statistics Canada, BLS and INEGI sites for "time use", "unpaid work" and "informality" by state or province.
3. Search the Grand Ronde Chinuk Wawa dictionary and Spanish slang dictionaries for the work terms in Section 1.
4. Search library catalogs for labor histories of the Pacific coast and Baja California by unit.
5. Ask each Indigenous nation's language and culture program (by query) how to approach the topic of work with community permission.
