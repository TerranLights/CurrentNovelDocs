# Cascadia: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; also French (BC), Chinese varieties, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese; Indigenous languages (Lushootseed, Haida, Tlingit, Nuu-chah-nulth, Kumeyaay/Kumiai, Yurok and others). **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, BC coast, Far West and El Norte settler cultures: different founding ideas of duty and fault.
- Sudcalifornia (Baja): Mexican civic and legal habits of accountability.
- Immigrant communities and their children (Mexican, Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, Central American): family duty and child brokering.
- Indigenous units: Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples; separate each, with their own words for obligation.
- Urban and rural; gender; generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian attitudes (surveys, archives); mixed for Baja; thin for Indigenous peoples' own statements and for the Panhandle's settlers.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish first; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean for community press; Indigenous languages only through the nations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential school testimony; apologies and reparations are disputed; some Indigenous teachings are restricted and must be shared by the nation itself.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the "responsibility" items from the World Values Survey and ISSP for the U.S., Canada and Mexico; then look for state or province breakouts in the General Social Survey and PPIC.
2. Search California, BC and Washington newspaper archives for "who is to blame" after a named disaster; compare the wording in English and Spanish.
3. Read the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's calls on accountability and the California state apology material for how answerability is framed.
4. Search each language's press for the section 1 terms.
5. Ask the Indigenous units' language programs and tribal natural-resource offices what they publish about stewardship and obligation.
