# Cascadia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish; Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese; Indigenous languages (Kumeyaay, Cucapá, Nuu-chah-nulth, Haida, Tlingit, Salish). **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Left Coast against Far West interior (urban against rural views of policing and disorder); coastal British Columbia against its interior; the Bajas' border cities against La Paz and Los Cabos; Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Latino and Black communities, on policing and neighbors' rules; Native nations and First Nations (Nuu-chah-nulth, Haida, Tlingit, Coast Salish, Karuk, Kumeyaay, Cucapá) on order as relationship and on cultural burning; migrant Mixtec, Triqui and Zapotec farmworker communities of San Quintín; people living outside, and their neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime, safety, encampments and policing in the U.S. and Canadian lands; mixed for the Bajas (strong federal surveys, thin local culture); thin for Indigenous understandings, and for the home and the self.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish for most; Chinese, Tagalog and Vietnamese for community media; Indigenous languages for the deepest layer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native nations' restricted knowledge and ceremony; ask before using anything beyond published sources.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Harrington and Gelfand 2014 and Woodard's Left Coast chapter for regional baselines.
2. Pull ENSU and ENVIPE tables for Baja California and Baja California Sur; pull the matching U.S. and Canadian safety data.
3. Search 311 and encampment-ordinance records for Seattle, Portland, San Francisco and Vancouver ("encampment", "sweep", "camping ban").
4. Search library catalogs for Portilla, Atleo and Haida or Nuu-chah-nulth teaching materials.
5. Search the *bandos* of Tijuana and La Paz for how each defines disorderly conduct.
