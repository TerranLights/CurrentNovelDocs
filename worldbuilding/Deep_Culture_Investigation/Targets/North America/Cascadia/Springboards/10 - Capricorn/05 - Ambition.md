# Cascadia: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon, California, the Alaska Panhandle, British Columbia, Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French (British Columbia), Cantonese and Mandarin, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, plus Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast, Far West, El Norte, Sudcalifornia, British Columbia coast, Panhandle settlers:** different founding stories of striving (mission and trade, extraction, boom and bust, migration).
- **Immigrant groups by generation:** Chinese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean and Japanese communities; Mexican and Central American migrants; Oaxacan, Mixtec and Triqui migrants in the Bajas.
- **Class and sector:** tech coasts against farm interior; factory workers against professionals.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene; ambition weighed against the people's good, as each states it.
- **Gender and generation:** women, men who do not rise, the young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. coast and BC (surveys, archives); mixed for the Bajas; thin for Indigenous peoples and for subnational values data below state or province.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean for the community press and archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous knowledge may be restricted; ask each nation's cultural office before using it. Ambition for oneself against the family or people is a contested topic; avoid one voice per group.
- **Held for review:** the Opportunity Atlas (set aside under the source rule); South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the WVS and GSS hard-work, luck and success items for the U.S. West and Canada; note sample limits.
2. Search "aspirations" and "mobility" in the PPIC Statewide Survey, Statistics Canada and INEGI catalogs.
3. Search the Spanish and Chinese terms in each community's press and in corridos for rising from nothing.
4. Search "ambición" and "superación" with Tijuana, Mexicali and La Paz in the regional press and COLEF output.
5. Ask Indigenous language programs and cultural offices how, and whether, they speak of ambition.
