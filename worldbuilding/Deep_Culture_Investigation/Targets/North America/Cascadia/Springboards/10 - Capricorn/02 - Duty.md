# Cascadia: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, French, Chinese, Japanese, Tagalog, and the Indigenous languages of the lands. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | duty, obligation, "do your part", "civic duty", "I owe it to them" | common phrases | also "duty of care" |
| Spanish | *deber*, *obligación*, *cumplir*, *compromiso*, *quedar bien* | duty or must; fulfill; a social commitment; to do right by others | Mexican and Baja usage |
| Spanish | *tequio*, *faena*, *cargo*, *mayordomía*, *manda*, *compadrazgo* | communal work; rotating offices; a vow to a saint; ritual kin | migrant Oaxacan communities |
| Chinese | 責任 (*zérèn*), 義務 (*yìwù*), 孝 (*xiào*) | responsibility; obligation; filial duty | Cantonese and Mandarin readings differ |
| Japanese | 義務 (*gimu*), 義理 (*giri*) | obligation; social obligation | Nikkei communities |
| Tagalog | *tungkulin*, *utang na loob* | duty; debt of gratitude | Filipino communities |
| Indigenous languages | unknown to me | each nation's own words | do not assume equivalents |

English keywords: filial obligation, familismo, caregiving, remittances, jury duty, civic duty, conscription, draft resistance, duty to consult, fiduciary duty, trust responsibility, tequio, cargo system.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Left Coast, the British Columbia coast, the Far West and El Norte (with Baja California Sur as its own case): different founding peoples, so likely different framings of duty.
- Indigenous units (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): duty to land, kin and nation, each in its own terms.
- Migrant and diaspora communities: Oaxacan communities in San Quintín and California; Chinese, Japanese and Filipino communities.
- Religious groups: Catholic, Protestant, Latter-day Saint, Buddhist, unaffiliated.
- Military families; rural against urban residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for caregiving, volunteering and remittances; mixed for small communities; thin for Indigenous and Baja California Sur sources.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; the diaspora languages above; Indigenous languages for the nations' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous nations' teachings on duty to land and kin may be restricted; use only what each nation has published. Oral histories of internment and war carry trauma.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull WVS and ISSP duty items; note that none go below country level.
2. Pull state and provincial volunteering and caregiving figures.
3. Search Densho and the Online Archive of California for "duty", "loyalty" and "obligation".
4. Search FIOB and hometown-association materials for *tequio* and *cargo*.
5. Check each Indigenous nation's own published statements on responsibility.
