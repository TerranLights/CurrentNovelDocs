# Cascadia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinuk Wawa and the Indigenous languages of each unit; French and Chinese-language sources for parts of British Columbia. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | authority, the authorities, chain of command, pull rank | general and workplace words | also "boss", "the Man", "Question Authority" |
| English | respect for authority, obedience, deference | the values vocabulary | survey wording |
| Chinuk Wawa / Northwest English | tyee | chief or boss; also a big salmon | check a Chinuk Wawa dictionary |
| Spanish | autoridad, las autoridades, el que manda, el jefe, la jefa | authority; "the one in charge" | Baja usage may differ from Mexico City |
| Spanish | ¿mande?, a sus órdenes, respeto, cacique | polite answer to a call; at your service; respect; local boss | |

English keywords: legitimacy, leadership, hereditary and elected chiefs, parenting style, police trust, confidence in institutions, civil disobedience. Indigenous-language words for chief, elder and leader: one query per language, through each nation's own language program (some terms may be restricted).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (urban and rural), Far West, British Columbia coast:** different police, county and provincial traditions, and a rural and urban split in how officials are seen.
- **El Norte and Sudcalifornia:** a Mexican state and municipal setting; ejido and municipal authority; federal and military presence.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin and Subarctic Dene peoples:** separate nations, each with its own account of hereditary, elected and elder authority; do not merge them.
- **Within units:** religious communities, generations, immigrant families, urban against rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. and Canadian survey data at state or province level; mixed for Baja California; thin and uneven for Indigenous peoples, where nations' own texts matter more than surveys.
- **Languages needed:** English and Spanish at minimum; Indigenous-language material only through each nation's own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous knowledge on leadership and ceremony is restricted; ask the nation's own offices. Testimony about abuse by clergy, police or schools needs care; survivors' own words first.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the WVS, GSS and AmericasBarometer items on respect for authority, obedience and confidence in police, restricted to the West, British Columbia and Mexico's northwest.
2. Pull INEGI's ENVIPE trust-in-authorities tables for Baja California and Baja California Sur, and Statistics Canada's institutional trust for British Columbia.
3. Read the None Zone volume's chapters on institutions and religion, then search for each nation's own account of chiefs and councils.
4. Search the Chinuk Wawa dictionary for *tyee* and related words, then each Indigenous language program for its leadership terms.
5. Search regional newspaper archives for police-funding and COVID-19-order debates, 2020 to 2022.
