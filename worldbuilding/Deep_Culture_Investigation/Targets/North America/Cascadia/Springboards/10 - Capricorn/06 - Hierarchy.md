## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast** against **Far West** and **El Norte**: different founding populations and economies.
- **BC coast** against **BC interior**: colonial against resource-frontier founding.
- **Panhandle settlers** against Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian.
- **Northwest Coast, Plateau, California and Yuman peoples:** rank may be central in some and slight in others; each describes itself.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese, Filipino, Mexican, Vietnamese, Japanese): rank at home and here.
- **Occupations:** tech, Hollywood, farm labor, ports, domestic work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for class and workplace; mixed for the Bajas; thin and uneven for Native rank, much of it old outsider ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; French, Chinese, Tagalog for communities; Indigenous languages by nation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a nation's rank and house knowledge may be restricted; use what the nations publish. Colorism, undocumented status and domestic work are sensitive and under-reported. Racist terms in the record: describe, do not adopt.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull power-distance and class items from WVS, GSS and ISSP; note what they say below the national level.
2. Search ACS, Statistics Canada and INEGI for inequality by county, census area and *municipio*.
3. Search *jerarquía*, *patrón*, *fifí* in Tijuana, Mexicali and La Paz papers and COLEF.
4. Read the *Handbook* volumes; list what each nation has published about its own rank.
5. Search "flat organization" and "levels" in Pacific tech and Hollywood press.
