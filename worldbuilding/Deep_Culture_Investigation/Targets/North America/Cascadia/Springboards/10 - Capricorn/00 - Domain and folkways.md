# Cascadia: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California whole; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Chinese (Mandarin and Cantonese), French (British Columbia), and the Indigenous languages of each nation. **Concept in one line:** authority, career, status, reputation and responsibility, with the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Coast (S1), with the Panhandle sub-unit:** New England-founded civic institutions against later migrants.
- **British Columbia coast (S2):** a British-colonial founding, set against Woodard's reading of it as a Left Coast extension.
- **Far West (S3):** interior economies tied to federal land and water; the state as owner.
- **El Norte (S4) and Sudcalifornia (S5):** Mexican institutions, the border, a different pre-war state.
- **Indigenous units I1 to I6:** each nation's own offices and duties; hereditary and elected leadership set apart.
- **Immigrant communities** (Chinese-, Japanese-, Filipino-, Korean-, Vietnamese- and Central American-heritage): authority and family-duty ways that may differ by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the U.S. and Canadian lobes; mixed for Baja California; thin for Baja California Sur and the Panhandle; Indigenous governance data is nation by nation and uneven.
- **Languages needed:** English; Spanish; Mandarin or Cantonese; French; Indigenous languages through each nation's language program.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Elders' knowledge, succession rites, and hereditary names and regalia belong to each nation; cite only what a nation publishes. Slurs (*naco*, *fifí*) are described, never used. Index scores are not one Cascadian voice.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull WVS, AmericasBarometer and the time-use and labor surveys; see which cut by state, province or *municipio*.
2. Search regional oral-history indexes for "authority", "duty", "reputation" and "career".
3. Read Woodard on Cascadia's units, then the British Columbia and Baja California historians.
4. For each nation in I1 to I6, find its constitution or governance page and its language program's words for leader, Elder and duty.
5. Collect Spanish- and Chinese-language press on status, reputation and age in Tijuana, Mexicali, La Paz and Vancouver.
