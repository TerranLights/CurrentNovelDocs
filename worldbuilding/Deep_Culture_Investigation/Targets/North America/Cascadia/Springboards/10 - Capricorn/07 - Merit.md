# Cascadia: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Cascadia, region North America (Washington, Oregon and California; the Alaska Panhandle; British Columbia; Baja California and Baja California Sur). **Language(s) for searching:** English, Spanish, Canadian French (federal records), Chinese (Cantonese, Mandarin), Tagalog, Vietnamese, Korean; Indigenous languages by unit. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Settler regional cultures (Left Coast, British Columbia coast, Far West, El Norte, Sudcalifornia): different histories of schooling, patronage and public hiring.
- Class and generation, including tech-sector and farm-labor households.
- Immigrant communities and their children: credentials earned abroad, judged here.
- Native nations (Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, Yuman, Great Basin, Subarctic Dene): earned versus inherited standing, in each nation's own account.
- Mexican-side Baja: migrants from elsewhere in Mexico, border-city residents and long-settled families.
- Canadian side against U.S. side: different admissions, hiring and equity regimes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for U.S. states (surveys, vote returns, admissions records); mixed for British Columbia (national surveys, thin provincial splits); mixed to thin for the Bajas (national Mexican surveys, few Baja splits); thin for Native nations.
- **Languages needed:** English, Spanish; Chinese and Tagalog for immigrant press; Indigenous languages for earned standing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native nations' own knowledge of names, roles and standing may be restricted; ask each nation's cultural office first. Admissions and hiring cases involve named private persons.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the WVS, ISSP and Pew hard-work-against-luck items for California, Washington, Oregon, British Columbia and Mexico; note the smallest unit available.
2. Search county returns for Propositions 209 and 16 and Washington's I-200 and Referendum 88; line them up against Stage 1 units.
3. Search INEGI and LAPOP for mobility and "palanca" items for Baja.
4. Read the University of California test-optional record and the *Vergara* coverage for the arguments each side used.
5. Search Chinese-language and Spanish-language press (query: "scholarship" or "beca" plus "merit") for how the words are used.
