# Alaska: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit and other Indigenous languages; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; Korean; French and Inuktitut for the Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | compassion, mercy, pity, "helping out" | core words and local idiom | also "neighbors helping neighbors" |
| English | "sharing" | giving among households | search with "subsistence" |
| Iñupiaq | *aatchuq* | sharing, listed among published Iñupiaq values | *check*; spelling and meaning vary by dialect |
| Tlingit | *wooch.een* | working together | *check*; ask who publishes it |
| Russian | *miloserdie*, *sostradanie* | mercy; compassion | Orthodox parish sources |
| Tagalog | *malasakit*, *awa*, *bayanihan* | concern; pity; communal help | Filipino community media |
| Spanish | *lástima*, *ayudar* | pity (can condescend); to help | Latino congregations |
| Korean | 연민 (*yeonmin*) | compassion, pity | Korean churches and press |

English keywords: charity, food bank, shelter, soup kitchen, homelessness, volunteering, giving, hospitality, mutual aid, compassion fatigue, community response.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native villages (small, kin-linked, remote) against Anchorage and Fairbanks; separate Native peoples, since sharing customs and their words differ; the Russian Orthodox communities of the south coast; Filipino and Latino congregations; military families, who move often; roadside homesteaders and seasonal fishing and oil workers; unhoused people themselves (their voice, not only the helpers'); Canadian-side Yukon towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Subsistence and charity-sector data are rich; the inner meaning of compassion is thin in print and best found in Native-authored and church sources.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Indigenous languages for first-voice material; Russian for Orthodox archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** use only values and teachings that peoples themselves publish; unhoused individuals' stories need care; do not quote private clients.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Subsistence Division's publications for "sharing" and "distribution of harvest".
2. Collect the published value statements of Native organizations, noting who wrote each.
3. Pull annual reports from Anchorage and Fairbanks shelters and food banks, plus newspaper letters on encampments.
4. Search parish newsletters and diocesan histories for almsgiving and visiting-the-sick customs.
5. Search for volunteering and giving data on the Alaska and Canadian northern pages of national surveys.
