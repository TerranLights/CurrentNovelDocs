# Alaska: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit and others); Russian; Tagalog; Korean; Spanish; French on the Canadian side. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native communities, kept apart by people; Orthodox communities of the south coast and Aleutians; military and veteran families around the large bases; commercial fishing and oil-field families, where work deaths are a known risk; immigrant parents (Filipino, Korean, Latino, Samoan); caregivers of elders in villages against those in cities; Canadian-side northern towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Memorial lists, veteran and work-death records are rich; the meaning people give sacrifice is found mainly in obituaries, sermons and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for church sources; Indigenous languages for elders' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the martyrdom accounts of St. Peter the Aleut are disputed by some; treat as religious tradition, not history. Family deaths and survivor testimony need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Collect the memorial name lists and dedication speeches for fishing, aviation and firefighting deaths.
2. Search the Alaska Veterans Museum and oral-history archives for volunteer and wartime service accounts.
3. Sample a year of Alaska obituaries for the praise words used.
4. Read the Orthodox saints' lives and parish sermon collections for the church's account of sacrifice.
5. Search state aging and caregiving reports for family care in villages and cities.
