# Alaska: Domain and folkways (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war lands from the USA and Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian (church and archive records); the Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian and others, plus Inuktitut and Dene languages on the Canadian side); French for Canadian records; Tagalog, Spanish and Korean for newer communities. **Domain question:** how does the culture meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Iñupiat and Yup'ik/Cup'ik peoples (distinct customs of the unseen); Alutiiq and Unangax̂ (Orthodox-shaped, with a wartime removal history); Athabascan peoples (many separate nations); Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian of the southeast; Russian Old Believer and Creole communities; Anchorage's urban mix; military families; Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino communities; roadside and homestead settlers; Canadian-side Dene, Inuit and Yukon towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Health and vital statistics are good; the unseen and the hidden are thin outside Native-authored and church sources.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Russian for church archives; Indigenous languages for first-voice material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned and ceremonial knowledge, healing practice, and survivor testimony: use only what peoples publish themselves. Suicide data is sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Alaska's suicide, mental-distress and overdose tables, by region and by Native and non-Native residents.
2. Search the Alaska Native Knowledge Network for elders' accounts of healing, spirits and illness, noting each source's rules.
3. Search Orthodox parish and diocesan sources for saints' lives and funeral and vigil customs.
4. Compile the boarding-school and wartime-removal inquiry reports for Alaska and the northern Canadian lands.
5. Find the state's dream, ghost and folklore collections by searching "Alaska folklore archive".
