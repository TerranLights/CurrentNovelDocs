# Alaska: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit and others); Russian; Tagalog; Korean; Spanish; French and Inuktitut on the Canadian side. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native hunters, fishers and elders with place-based knowledge, kept apart by people; bush pilots and mariners; mountain guides; village health aides against urban clinicians; military personnel; Filipino, Korean and Latino families; recent arrivals against long-term residents; Orthodox and charismatic congregations; Canadian-side northern communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Documented local and traditional knowledge is rich; folk intuition (dreams, premonitions) is thin and sensitive; expert judgment appears in safety records.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous languages for elders' first-voice material; Russian for church writings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hunters' and elders' knowledge may carry rules about who may share it, and some of it is intellectual property of communities; accident reports involve named dead.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Barnhardt and Kawagley articles and the Native Knowledge Network guidance on knowledge ownership first.
2. Pull sea-ice and weather knowledge studies and note how each source credits its holders.
3. Search aviation and avalanche safety materials for "judgment" and "decision making".
4. Search health-aide training documents for how clinical hunches are taught.
5. Search elders' recorded accounts for dreams and warnings, subject to each archive's access rules.
