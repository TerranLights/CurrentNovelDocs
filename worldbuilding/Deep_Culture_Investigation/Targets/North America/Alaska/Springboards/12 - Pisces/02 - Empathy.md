# Alaska: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; the Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit and others); Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; Korean; French and Inuktitut on the Canadian side. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Separate Native peoples, since ways of listening and speaking differ; village and city residents; elders and youth; Orthodox, Catholic, Lutheran and evangelical congregations; military families; Filipino, Korean and Latino communities, where reading others is sometimes named in the home language; health and school staff who arrive from outside and those who grew up locally; Canadian-side northern communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin on direct "empathy" research in Alaska; richer in health-care, education and counseling documents that describe it in practice.
- **Languages needed:** English; for first-voice material, the Indigenous languages with a fluent speaker or translator.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** teachings shared in circles and testimony from survivors are the speakers' own; use only what is published with consent.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Nuka System publications for "listening", "relationship" and "customer-owner".
2. Read the Alaska Native Knowledge Network educational standards for how they ask teachers to understand students.
3. Search UAA and UAF theses for "empathy" with "Alaska Native" or "rural Alaska".
4. Ask a language program for the Native word nearest to "understanding another's feeling", for each language in the target.
5. Pull the crisis-line volunteer training outlines and compare them with Native-led peer-support materials.
