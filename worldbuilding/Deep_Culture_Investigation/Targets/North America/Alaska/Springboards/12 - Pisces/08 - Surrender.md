# Alaska: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit and others); Russian; Tagalog; Korean; Spanish; French and Inuktitut on the Canadian side. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher; release and letting go included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native peoples, kept apart (their ways of accepting hardship, death and loss differ); Orthodox, Catholic and evangelical believers; recovery communities, Native-led and mainstream; climbers, mushers and fishing crews, for whom turning back is a daily decision; military families; elders and young adults; Filipino, Korean and Latino households; Canadian-side northern communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin for "surrender" as a cultural concept; better for its practices (recovery, hospice, turning back, funeral rites), which are documented by the organizations involved.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for church texts; Indigenous languages for elders' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths and recovery are intimate; AA anonymity and patient privacy bind published sources; ceremonial and grief practices of Native peoples are theirs to share.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Iditarod and Denali reports for "scratched", "turned back" and the reasons given.
2. Gather the published literature of recovery groups, noting which ones are Native-authored.
3. Read hospice and tribal health program descriptions for end-of-life language.
4. Pull Orthodox funeral texts and sermons on acceptance and God's will.
5. Search the 1867 transfer histories for how Native peoples' non-consent is told by Native and non-Native authors.
