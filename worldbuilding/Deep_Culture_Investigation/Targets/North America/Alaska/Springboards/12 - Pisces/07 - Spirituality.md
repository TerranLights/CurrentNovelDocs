## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native peoples (traditional, Orthodox, Episcopal, Moravian, Catholic, Pentecostal and blended practice), kept apart by people and region; Russian and Creole Orthodox south-coast communities; Old Believers; evangelical and Pentecostal congregations; Filipino Catholic, Korean Protestant, Latino, Samoan and Hmong communities; unaffiliated seekers; military chaplaincy communities; Canadian-side northern parishes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on denominations and surveys; thin on private practice; Native spiritual teachings are largely not for public research.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian or Church Slavonic for Orthodox archives; Indigenous languages for elders' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned sacred things, ceremonial knowledge and healing practice; former mission suppression of Native ceremony; use only what peoples themselves publish and say plainly where access is restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Alaska rows from the Pew religion survey and compare religious and unaffiliated shares.
2. Read the Orthodox diocesan and parish histories for how the faith is told as Native and as Russian-heritage.
3. Search Sealaska Heritage Institute and Native Knowledge Network pages for published statements, noting any access limits.
4. Search mission archives for sermons, letters and conversion accounts, reading them critically.
5. Search local press for the spiritual-but-not-religious and wilderness-as-sacred voices.
