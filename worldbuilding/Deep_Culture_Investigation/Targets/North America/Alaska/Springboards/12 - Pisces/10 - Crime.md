# Alaska: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages of its lands (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit, Haida and others); Russian; Tagalog; Korean; Spanish; French and Inuktitut on the Canadian side. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation, and how it sees those who commit it. Law as written belongs to Libra, and its spirit to Capricorn; this sheet takes only the idea of wrongdoing and of the wrongdoer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native peoples and villages, kept apart (customary ideas of wrong and repair differ), against Anchorage and Fairbanks; victims' and survivors' communities, whose own voice leads; Southeast and Interior fishing and mining towns; Russian Orthodox and evangelical congregations; military families; recent arrivals who came to leave a past behind; Filipino, Korean and Latino neighborhoods; Canadian-side Yukon towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Counts of reported crime and victimization surveys are rich; the culture's moral picture of the wrongdoer is thin and found in journalism, true crime, memoir and sermons.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous languages for first-voice terms; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence, missing persons and abuse involve living families; stigma against Native communities is a known risk; do not name private individuals. Statute and court practice go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Alaska Victimization Survey and the Justice Center's reports for how wrongs are classified and by whom.
2. Collect Native-authored statements on harm and repair, and list who writes each.
3. Search news archives for scandal and outlaw coverage (corruption, serial offenders, poaching) and note the framing words.
4. Pull the Canadian national inquiry testimony for Yukon and northern communities.
5. Search sermons and church sources for crime, sin and redemption talk.
