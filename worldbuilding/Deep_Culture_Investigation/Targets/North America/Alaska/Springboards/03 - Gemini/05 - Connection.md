# Alaska: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, North America, built from pre-war USA and Canada lands; research should follow villages, hubs, ferry towns and the road system. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people, and the work of making, using and keeping them, including the felt sense of being connected or cut off.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Clan and house systems (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian) against family-camp and whaling-crew ties (Iñupiat), namesake ties (Yup'ik and Iñupiat), and Athabaskan band and potlatch ties; village residents against Anchorage's urban Natives; Orthodox against Presbyterian, Catholic and Pentecostal congregations; road-system against off-road towns; military families and seasonal workers; newcomer networks (Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean, Hispanic); Yukon towns if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on kinship and sharing ethnography; mixed on loneliness and online ties; thin on newcomers.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for festival and kin terms; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan names, house names and ceremonial protocols belong to the nations and families; use what they publish. Loneliness and suicide data in Native communities is highly sensitive: avoid individual cases and follow tribal and health-consortium guidance. Politics around Native corporations is heated. Law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

4. Look at Alaska BRFSS and national loneliness surveys for any Alaska row.
5. Sample public community Facebook and radio pages for how towns link across distance.
