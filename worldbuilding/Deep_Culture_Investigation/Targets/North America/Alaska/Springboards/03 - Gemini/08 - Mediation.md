# Alaska: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, in North America; the post-war nation covers lands of the pre-war USA and Canada, with Native villages, ferry towns and a large city. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** a third party who helps sides settle a quarrel or reach agreement without imposing a decision, and the go-between in general: elders, clergy, brokers, interpreters.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian feast-and-clan settlement against Yup'ik and Iñupiaq men's-house and elder councils and Dene band councils; Orthodox villages (priests and readers) against Presbyterian, Catholic and Pentecostal ones; village against Anchorage; fishing and cannery workforces; newcomer households using children as interpreters; military communities with their chaplains; Yukon First Nation communities if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on historical and ethnographic go-betweens and on a few well-known peacemaking programs; thin on everyday informal mediation and on what is rarely written down.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for process words; Russian for colonial and church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peacemaking and feast protocols belong to the nations; read what they publish and ask before using more. Family-violence mediation is sensitive, and the Native women's organizations' own statements come first. Tribal and state courts as written are law: go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Find three Native-run peacemaking programs and read their own descriptions.
2. Look in Fienup-Riordan and Burch for what happens in the qasgiq or qargi when quarrels arise.
3. Search Orthodox parish papers for priest and reader roles.
4. Search for interpreter services at tribal health consortia.
5. Search Alaska newspapers for "mediator" and "peacemaker" to see public usage.
