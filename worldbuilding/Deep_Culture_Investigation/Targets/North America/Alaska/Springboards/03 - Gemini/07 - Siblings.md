# Alaska: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska (North America), covering Alaska and neighboring lands once held by the pre-war USA and Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture reckons who counts as a sibling and what siblings owe, rank and share.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Matrilineal Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian clans against Iñupiaq and Yup'ik household and namesake systems and Dene band kinship; village families against city families; fishing families with shared boats against wage-earning households; Orthodox godparent-sibling ties; military and oil-field families; newcomers where older siblings translate (Hispanic, Filipino, Hmong, Samoan, Korean); families touched by boarding school or foster care; Yukon families if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on kinship ethnography; mixed on modern household life; thin on sibling relations as people feel them.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for kin terms, since English "brother" can hide wider categories; Russian for parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sibling separation through boarding schools and foster care is painful and often family-owned; follow the nations' lead. Clan and house names and enrollment are the nations' to share. Adoption and custody law goes to Libra and Capricorn. Violence among siblings is a sensitive topic with sparse data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
