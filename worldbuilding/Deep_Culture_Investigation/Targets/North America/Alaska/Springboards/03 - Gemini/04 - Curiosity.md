# Alaska: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, in the North American region; the nation holds pre-war USA and Canada lands, so searches should run through both American and Canadian archives. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, find out and explore, and how far a culture encourages, channels, limits or punishes it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Peoples with published research-review rules (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Tlingit, Alutiiq) against those who have published less; village residents who host visiting scientists against Fairbanks and Anchorage science communities; hunters and fishers who observe closely as part of work; elders and youth; seasonal tourism workers; military and oil-field newcomers; newcomer groups; Yukon communities if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on researcher-community conflicts and on science institutions; thin on ordinary curiosity and on what children may ask; national surveys seldom split out Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for verbs; Russian for early naturalists.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains, sacred sites and some stories are not open to every asker; follow each nation's published research and repatriation rules. Cases of researchers taking samples or remains are painful: let the nations tell them first. Law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the nations' published research-review guidelines before searching for any case.
2. Search for the Larsen Bay repatriation in Alutiiq sources first.
3. Look up each language's dictionary entries for "ask" and "wonder".
4. Check the LEO Network for how local observers describe what they notice.
5. Pull tourism visitor-survey data to see what outsiders say they came to learn.
