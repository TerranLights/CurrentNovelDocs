# Alaska: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada; Yukon and adjoining northwestern lands assumed). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena languages, Tlingit and others); Yukon First Nations languages; Russian for Orthodox and Russian-American records. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Alaska Native peoples and regional health organizations, each with its own framing and services.
- Roadless villages (health aides, medevac, telehealth) against the road system and Anchorage.
- Traditional-food households against households leaning on store food; the choices are not the same everywhere.
- Orthodox, Catholic, Presbyterian, Pentecostal and Latter-day Saint communities (faith and healing).
- Military and veteran health systems against civilians; Alaska Native and non-Native health use.
- Yukon First Nations health programs against Whitehorse's territorial system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Alaska Native health status and for the health aide system; mixed for non-Native residents; thin on folk ideas of causation outside Native ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for terms; Russian for older records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** traditional healing, healers' knowledge and some plant knowledge are restricted by their holders; suicide, alcohol, abuse, boarding-school and epidemic history are painful and must not be reduced to statistics; Native health data have community ownership protocols.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Alaska Native Epidemiology Center's health status report for its categories only.
2. Read Southcentral Foundation's description of its model and note its terms.
3. Search the Division of Public Health's BRFSS page for Alaska cells and sampling notes.
4. Look up the Native-language words for sick, healer and strong in the dictionaries.
5. Search oral histories for "tuberculosis", "sanatorium" and "health aide".
