# Alaska: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada; Yukon and adjoining northwestern lands assumed). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages and Yukon First Nations languages; Russian for the Russian-American and Orthodox record. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Alaska Native peoples: those with mission-and-boarding-school histories and those with others; Tlingit and Haida, Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Unangax̂, Dena each differ.
- Native-led revitalization movements against institutional "development" programs.
- Homesteaders and their descendants against recent arrivals who seek a "new start".
- Pipeline-era and oilfield workers; fishers; miners; military families.
- Orthodox, Presbyterian, Catholic and other mission descendants.
- Yukon First Nations and residential-school survivors against Whitehorse residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on the history of imposed improvement (missions, schools) and on revitalization; thin on everyday self-improvement habits for Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for revitalization materials; Russian for early records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school, mission and forced-assimilation history is painful and must be taken in survivors' and communities' own voices; some archives are restricted or under tribal control; avoid treating "improvement" as the Native peoples' own idea where it was imposed.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the boarding-school initiative's Alaska list and survivors' testimonies in the community's own words.
2. Search the Alaska Native Brotherhood records for its stated aims.
3. Search immersion-school sites for their stated purposes.
4. Search the oral-history catalogs for "pipeline", "statehood" and "made something of myself".
5. Look up the Native-language words glossed "grow", "make better" and "mend".
