## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native knowledge-holders (Elders and hunters) by people and region: sea-ice Iñupiat and Yup'ik, Interior Dena, Tlingit and Haida coastal peoples.
- Scientists and agency staff in Fairbanks, Anchorage and Juneau against rural communities they study.
- Oil, mining and fishery analysts (engineers, biologists) against subsistence households.
- Military and aviation workers (weather, navigation).
- Students in rural schools against urban ones; Native-run schools and colleges against state schools.
- Yukon First Nations governments' research offices against territorial agencies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on the meeting of Native and scientific knowledge (a large applied literature); thin on everyday analytic habits.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for observation vocabularies; some Russian for the colonial record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native knowledge is owned by its holders and communities; much of the literature was extracted from them, and communities set terms for any use. Test-score gaps are easily turned into stereotypes; the sheet names variation to check only.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
