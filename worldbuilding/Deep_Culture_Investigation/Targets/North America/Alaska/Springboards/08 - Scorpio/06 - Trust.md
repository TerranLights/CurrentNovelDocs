# Alaska: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish and Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down, in the expectation of not being harmed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Each Native people and each village, separately: kin networks and village councils carry different trust, and each people states its own teaching on relationship (Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Athabascan peoples, Tlingit, Haida).
- Villages against cities: everyone known, against anonymity.
- Homesteaders and off-the-grid settlers, workers on short seasonal contracts, and long-term residents.
- Military families who rotate through, and Alaska-born residents.
- Orthodox, evangelical and other congregations as networks of trust.
- Newer immigrant communities with their own mutual-aid habits (Filipino, Hmong, Samoan, Korean, Hispanic).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Survey data on trust exist nationally but Alaska subsamples and Native respondents are thin; local polling and oral history are the better routes.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the concept's own words; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the history of boarding schools, forced removals and medical experiments as reasons discussed for distrust (read the peoples' own accounts, do not summarize them); teachings on relationship and obligation kept within a community.
- **Law:** no law research here; contracts and courts belong in Libra, legal culture in Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

5. Search for "public-use cabin" etiquette and "search and rescue" accounts to see how strangers rely on each other.
