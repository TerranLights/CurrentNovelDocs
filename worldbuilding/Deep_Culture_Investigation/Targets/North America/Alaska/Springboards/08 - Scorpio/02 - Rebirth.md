# Alaska: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native peoples with active language and dance revival against those with few speakers left: Yup'ik and Cup'ik communities where children still speak the language; Tlingit and Haida, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Dena'ina.
- Orthodox, evangelical, Catholic and other Christian converts; people who left a church.
- Villages choosing relocation against towns rebuilt in place.
- Recovery communities in Anchorage and Fairbanks against village-based healing programs.
- Newcomers who came north to reinvent themselves: homesteaders, military leavers, seasonal workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Revival is richly documented and promoted by the peoples themselves; conversion and recovery are thin except in memoir and program reports.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for revival texts; Russian for older church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonies some peoples do not share; boarding-school testimony; addiction and the privacy of fellowships (anonymity is part of the practice); names of the dead and any teaching about returning souls.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Alaska Native Language Center for "revitalization", "immersion" and "master apprentice" for each language.
2. Read what Sealaska Heritage Institute and Celebration organizers publish about revival, then list which peoples are absent.
3. Search Project Jukebox and Alaska's Digital Archives for "boarding school", "came back" and "relocation".
4. Pull the health consortium's wellness and recovery program pages for how they name renewal.
5. Search news archives for Newtok, Mertarvik and Valdez, noting who describes the move as a new beginning and who as a loss.
