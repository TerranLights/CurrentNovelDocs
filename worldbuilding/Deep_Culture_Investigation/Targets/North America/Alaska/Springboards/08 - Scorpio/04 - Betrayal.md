# Alaska: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** breaking the trust of those who relied on one: turning against, deserting, deceiving or exposing people or a cause to which one owed loyalty.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Small villages, where an informer or a lover is known by name, against Anchorage and Fairbanks.
- Each Native people, separately: do not carry one people's idea of betrayal to another (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Athabascan peoples, Tlingit, Haida).
- Shareholders in the regional and village corporations against the leaders who run them.
- Military and veteran communities (desertion, oaths), and fishing and oil crews (crew loyalty).
- Russian-speaking and Orthodox communities, where the language of denunciation has another history.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Betrayal is not counted; best sources are testimony, news, and fiction.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the concept's words; Russian for church-era and Russian-community material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse survivors' testimony; names of informers in small communities; living people accused in scandals; stories a people keeps for itself.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Berger commission's village testimony for the words used about promises and leaders.
2. Search the Tundra Times archive for "broken promise", "betrayed" and "sold out".
3. Search the language archives for dictionary entries on lie, trick, informer, and "turn against".
4. Search Alaska news archives for "whistleblower" and "embezzlement" in corporation and village cases, noting who is blamed.
5. Compare how a village paper and an Anchorage paper cover the same betrayal story.
