# Alaska: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish and Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coastal communities facing storm and erosion, river communities facing fish closures, and urban Anchorage facing earthquake risk.
- Each Native people, separately, since each has its own remembered crises and its own words for them.
- Subsistence households against wage workers in oil, fishing and tourism, who feel economic crises differently.
- Villages with little road access against road-system towns.
- Military communities, with their own emergency culture.
- Orthodox and other congregations as networks of aid in crisis.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on events and on official response; mixed on how people understand crisis.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for hard-times vocabulary; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** death counts and names from epidemics and disasters; relocation decisions in living communities; suicide and overdose data (use aggregate reports); trauma from storms and floods in recent years.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Alaska Earthquake Center and local news for 1964 and 2018 memories and drills.
2. Pull the state emergency division's list of declared disasters and map them to peoples and regions.
3. Search Project Jukebox for "hard times", "flu", "famine", "flood" and "storm".
4. Read the Denali Commission and Native health consortium material on relocation beside the villages' own statements.
5. Search for salmon-closure coverage on the Yukon and Kuskokwim, noting how river communities frame it.
