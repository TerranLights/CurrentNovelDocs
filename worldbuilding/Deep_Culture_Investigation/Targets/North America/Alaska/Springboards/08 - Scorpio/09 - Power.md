# Alaska: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how a culture judges who holds it and how it is used.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native peoples, separately, with their own leadership forms (house and clan leaders among Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian; elected councils and corporation boards; elders' councils): do not merge.
- Rural Alaska ("the Bush") against Anchorage, Fairbanks and Juneau, and the regions against the capital.
- Native corporation leadership against village tribal councils and traditional elders.
- Oil, fishing, mining, military and tourism interests.
- State officials against federal land managers.
- Russian-descended and Orthodox communities with their own standing, and newer immigrant groups' community leaders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich on formal power (campaign finance, corporate reports); thin on informal or hidden power.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for leadership terms; Russian for older sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan and house rank, names and crests (property of the houses); living leaders in disputes; corruption allegations (stick to charged or published cases); ceremonies that confirm rank.
- **Law:** no law research here; for the letter of the law on corporations and land go to Libra, for obedience and legal culture to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download several Native corporation annual reports and note how each describes its own role and its shareholders.
2. Search the language archives and Sealaska Heritage Institute for terms for leader, house leader, elder and "one who speaks for".
3. Pull the Public Offices Commission's top contributors and lobbyists across a few election cycles.
4. Read Naske and Slotnick and Roderick for the standard account, then compare with Native writers' accounts of the same period.
5. Search news archives for "rural caucus" or "Bush caucus" and "capital move" to see how regional power is framed.
