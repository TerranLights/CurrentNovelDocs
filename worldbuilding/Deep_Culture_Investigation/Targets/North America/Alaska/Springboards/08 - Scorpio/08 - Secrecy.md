# Alaska: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish and Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is researched here as one facet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Each Native people and its families, houses or clans, separately: what is restricted differs, and some knowledge belongs to a family or society (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Athabascan peoples, Alutiiq, Unangax̂).
- Small villages, where little can be hidden, against cities where anonymity is possible.
- Military and defense communities, and oil and mining employers, with formal confidentiality.
- Orthodox and Catholic parishes (confession), evangelical congregations, and lodges.
- LGBTQ residents in villages against those in Anchorage and Juneau.
- Hunters, fishers and gatherers who keep sites quiet, against those who publicize them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin by nature. Secrets are not published; look at what the peoples say about restriction, and at what leaked.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for church material; Native languages for the best terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** this concept is almost entirely sensitive. Sacred and restricted knowledge, clan and house property, ceremonial matters and elders' recordings with limits placed on them are not to be researched or reproduced beyond what each people has published; follow the peoples' own research protocols; abuse-survivor material; closeted individuals.
- **Law:** no law research here; the letter of access and records law belongs in Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Alaska Native research-ethics guidelines first, and note what each says about restricted knowledge.
2. Search Project Jukebox and the Knowledge Network for recordings carrying access limits, and record the limits, not the content.
3. Search the language archives for "secret", "private", "taboo" and "don't tell".
4. Search news archives for "cover-up", "leaked" and "whistleblower" in Alaska, listing which institutions appear.
5. Search the Division of Subsistence reports for "location" and "confidential" to see what households withhold.
