# Alaska: Domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina and other Athabascan languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian; Spanish and Tagalog for newer communities. **Domain in one line:** death and transformation: how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden (death ways, sex ways, inheritance, shared resources, secrets).

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | memorial potlatch; koo.éex' | memorial feast (Tlingit usage) | spelling varies by author; use the community's own |
| English | spirit house; grave house | Athabascan and Russian Orthodox grave structures | search with the people's name |
| Iñupiaq / Yup'ik | tuqu- (verb root, to die) | dying, death | *check* with a dictionary; dialects differ |
| Russian | smert', pokhorony, tayna | death, funeral, secret | for Russian-era church and Old Believer sources |
| Spanish / Tagalog | muerte, velorio; kamatayan, lamay | death, wake | newer communities in Anchorage |

English keywords: death ways, mortuary practice, mourning, memorial feast, burial, cremation, inheritance, subsistence sharing, Permanent Fund Dividend, ANCSA shares, secrecy, taboo, sexual violence, suicide, gold rush sex trade.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Athabascan peoples (several, with distinct memorial customs), Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian: separate peoples and separate rites; do not merge.
- Village Alaska against Anchorage and Fairbanks: death, privacy and sharing differ by small-community scale.
- Russian Orthodox Native communities, Old Believer villages, and newer Orthodox parishes.
- Military families, oil and fishing workers, Filipino, Hmong, Samoan, Korean and Hispanic communities in the cities.
- Pre-war Canadian lands carried: check the tracker's territory entry for which peoples sit there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich oral-history and health data; thin on secrecy and sex, which are mostly known through violence and health surveys.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Russian for church and Russian-era sources; a Native language is needed for the best terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names of the dead and grave locations; clan and house property and songs; memorial rites kept within the people; missing and murdered Indigenous relatives; suicide; boarding-school history. Use only what each people has published or shared.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Alaska Native Language Center and Project Jukebox for "funeral", "memorial", "burial" and the people's own words.
2. Read Sealaska Heritage Institute material for the memorial feast as the Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian describe it.
3. Pull the vital-statistics and BRFSS pages for death causes and end-of-life items.
4. Search Division of Subsistence reports for "sharing networks" and "distribution".
5. Search Alaska's Digital Archives and the Tundra Times for obituaries and memorial notices, then note which topics are never printed.
