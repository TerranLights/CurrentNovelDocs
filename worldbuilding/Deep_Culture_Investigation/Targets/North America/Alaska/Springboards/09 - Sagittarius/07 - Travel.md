# Alaska: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit, Haida; Russian for colonial-era sources; French for Canadian ones; Tagalog and Spanish for labor-migrant sources. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native households** with seasonal camps, regional trade and kin ties, by people (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Athabascan, Alutiiq, Unangax, Tlingit and Haida).
- **Newcomers who stayed** against **seasonal workers who return Outside** and **military families on rotation**.
- **Filipino, Samoan, Korean and other immigrant** communities and their ties abroad.
- **Tourists and the guides, drivers and cruise workers** who serve them; **bush-pilot, ferry and rail** workers.
- **Rural and urban** residents, and those with and without road access.
- **Yukon**: First Nations and settler travel traditions, from Klondike routes to the Alaska Highway.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on tourism and the Gold Rush; mixed on Native travel; thin on how residents themselves value travel.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for travel and place terms; Russian for historical ones.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hunting and fishing places and routes can be private to families; some sites, graves and trails are sacred or restricted. Forced removals and boarding-school travel were not leisure; read survivors' accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
