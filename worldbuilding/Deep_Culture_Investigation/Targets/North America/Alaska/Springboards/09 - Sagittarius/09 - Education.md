## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples** by region, with differences between village-school graduates, boarding-school alumni and urban Natives.
- **Road-system districts** (Anchorage, Mat-Su, Fairbanks) against **hub towns** and **fly-in villages**.
- **Public, charter, correspondence, and homeschool** families.
- **University students** from Alaska against those from Outside; first-generation students.
- **Military families** and **immigrant communities** (Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean, Latino) with their own educational expectations.
- **Yukon**: First Nations education programs and territorial schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in official statistics and Native-education literature; mixed on how people value education; thin on informal and elder-led learning outside formal records.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for immersion and for terms of teaching; Russian for the earliest church schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school histories include abuse and language suppression; read survivors' own accounts and avoid sensationalism. Elders' teachings belong to their families and communities.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull DEED and ACS attainment tables by region and by Native or non-Native group.
2. Read the Alaska Standards for Culturally Responsive Schools and the Barnhardt and Kawagley writings.
3. Search language-center pages for immersion program histories.
4. Search UAF, UAA and Ilisagvik archives for Native student and faculty accounts.
5. Search "boarding school" in Alaska Native and Yukon sources for survivors' testimony.
