# Alaska: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Koyukon, Gwich'in, Tlingit, Haida; Russian for church sources. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native Christians** by denomination (Orthodox, Moravian, Friends, Catholic, Presbyterian, Anglican), **traditional practitioners**, and those who hold both.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal** congregations in Anchorage and the Mat-Su against **secular** residents of Juneau, Fairbanks and Homer.
- **Old Believer** villages, Latter-day Saints, Filipino and Samoan Christians, and other immigrant congregations.
- **Military and transient residents** against **long-term residents**.
- **Yukon**: Anglican and Catholic First Nations, traditional teachings revived in First Nations programs, settler secular communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Surveys are thin; ethnography is rich but often by outsiders and dated; communities' own recent publications give the current voice.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for belief and spirit terms; Russian for Orthodox sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shamanic and ceremonial knowledge, names of spirits and hunting rituals can be restricted; some communities regard older ethnographies as having exposed what should not have been recorded. Treat nothing as publishable without the community's own release.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Retrieve Pew, Gallup and GSS belief items for Alaska or the nearest region.
2. Find Native-language texts on belief and spirit at the Alaska Native Language Center.
3. Read the introductions of the ethnographies above to note each author's stance.
4. Search Alaska news archives for HAARP, ghost and "unexplained" stories, to see who tells them.
