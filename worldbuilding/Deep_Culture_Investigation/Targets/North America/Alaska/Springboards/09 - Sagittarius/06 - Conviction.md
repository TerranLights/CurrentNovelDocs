# Alaska: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Gwich'in, Tlingit, Haida and other Native languages; French for Canadian sources. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content (not the legal sense of a finding of guilt).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native leaders and organizations** by region (Arctic Slope, Bristol Bay, Interior, Southeast), since their campaigns differ.
- **Tribal governments** against **Native corporations** and **nonprofits**.
- **Environmental, labor, resource-industry and fishing-sector** groups.
- **Religious activists** (Friends, Catholic, evangelical, Orthodox) against **secular** activists.
- **Villages that vote for or against alcohol restrictions** and similar local stands, as examples to test.
- **Yukon**: First Nations governments and territorial parties on land and mining questions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in newspapers, memoirs and advocacy documents; thin in measures of the strength of conviction itself. Many sources are partisan; label them.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for original statements of values.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** campaigns and disputes involve living people and live conflicts; cite published statements only and avoid judging motives. Statements of principle by elders belong to the speaker's family and community.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the digitized *Tundra Times* for "conscience", "principle" and "stand".
2. Read Hensley's memoir for how he and others describe acting on conviction.
3. Collect two paired campaign statements (supporting and opposing a resource project) from named groups.
4. Query newspapers for letters using "sold out" to see where the sanction falls.
