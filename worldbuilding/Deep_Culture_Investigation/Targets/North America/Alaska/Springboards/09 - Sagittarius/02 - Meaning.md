# Alaska: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Koyukon, Gwich'in, Tlingit, Haida; Russian for church sources. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alaska Native peoples**, each separately, with differences between village and city residents and between elders and youth.
- **Subsistence and commercial-fishing households** against **oil, mining and pipeline workers** against **government and military families**.
- **Seasonal workers and newcomers** who came north for a purpose, against **multi-generation settler families**.
- **Artists and writers** of Fairbanks, Homer, Sitka and Haines.
- **Yukon**: First Nations communities, Whitehorse and Dawson residents, if the Scope carries them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin on direct surveys of meaning; richer in memoir, nature writing, oral history and educational documents.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Native languages for the terms of value and purpose.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and loss among rural youth are discussed in public health reports; handle with care and do not treat statistics as meaning. Spiritual teachings belong to the people who give them.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Kawagley and the Alaska Native Knowledge Network values statements.
2. Search the Division of Subsistence for "identity", "way of life" and "sharing".
3. Collect memoir and nature-writing titles by Alaska residents; separate Native and settler voices.
4. Search the Alaska Humanities Forum and oral history archives for "purpose" and "why I stay".
