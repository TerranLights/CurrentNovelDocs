# Alaska: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Tlingit, Haida; Russian for archival and church sources; French for Canadian federal material. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how disputes over facts are settled.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native elders and knowledge-holders**, region by region, against Native scientists and university-trained researchers from the same peoples.
- **Agency scientists and managers** against **subsistence hunters and fishers** and **sport and commercial sectors**.
- **Churches** (Orthodox, evangelical, Catholic) as sources of revealed truth, against **secular and academic** communities.
- **Road-system residents with broadband** against **bush residents who rely on radio and word of mouth**.
- **Yukon**: First Nations governments' heritage offices, territorial agencies, Whitehorse media.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in Native oral-tradition publications and co-management documents; thin in measured trust data for Alaska specifically.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for truth, testimony and instruction terms; Russian for church and colonial sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some stories are owned by families or clans and are told only in season or by those with the right; publish nothing the communities have not published. Survivors' testimony is sensitive.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
