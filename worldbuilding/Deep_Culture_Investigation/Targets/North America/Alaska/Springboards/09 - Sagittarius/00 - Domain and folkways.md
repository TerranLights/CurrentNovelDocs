## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alaska Native peoples** separately: Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax, Athabascan peoples (Dena'ina, Koyukon, Gwich'in and others), Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian. Their traditions, mission histories and languages differ.
- **Yukon First Nations**, if carried: Tlingit, Tutchone, Kaska, Hän, Gwich'in groups.
- **Orthodox, Catholic, Moravian, Presbyterian, Episcopal and Friends** mission legacies, each with its own regional footprint.
- **Anchorage and the Railbelt** against **rural hub towns** and **Southeast** towns.
- **Old Believer and other Russian-heritage villages** on the Kenai Peninsula.
- **Immigrant and diaspora groups** (Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean, Latino); military families; evangelical and Pentecostal congregations; humanists and the unaffiliated.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Statewide surveys are thin because of small samples; Native-authored and church-archive material is richer; Yukon data comes by a separate census.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for church sources; Native languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native knowledge, ceremonies and stories belong to clans or families; use what peoples publish themselves and follow their own protocols. Mission and boarding-school history is painful; read it through Native accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
