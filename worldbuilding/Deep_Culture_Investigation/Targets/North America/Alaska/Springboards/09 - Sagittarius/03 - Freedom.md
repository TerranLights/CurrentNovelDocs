# Alaska: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Canadian French for Yukon federal sources; Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit; Russian for historical sources. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or the state; freedom as a belief and a political ideal (not the law of it, which belongs to Libra and Capricorn).

English keywords: Alaska independence; Alaska libertarian; Alaska frontier ethos; tribal sovereignty; subsistence freedom; Alaska homesteading; Yukon "larger than life"; freedom of the road.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alaska Native peoples** in their own definitions, with differences between corporation shareholders, tribal governments and traditional leaders.
- **Libertarian-leaning homesteaders and bush residents** against **road-system suburbanites**, **military families** and **public-sector workers**.
- **Fishing and oil communities** against **tourism towns** (Southeast).
- **Villages with different alcohol-control choices** (local option), a case where communities decide for themselves.
- **Yukon First Nations**, with their self-government agreements, against Whitehorse and Dawson settlers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in rhetoric, political writing and journalism; thin in representative measurement at the state level.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms for freedom may be sparse, so look for what each language says about permission, sharing and obligation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political and tribal-government disputes are live; cite published positions, not guesses about individuals. Law about freedoms is held for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Gather the frontier-freedom phrases from state slogans, license plates and tourism copy, noting who wrote them.
2. Read Alaska Federation of Natives resolutions for how sovereignty and self-determination are framed.
3. Pull opinion-page letters about "rights" and "outsiders" from two newspapers.
4. Search UAF and UAA for homesteader oral histories.
