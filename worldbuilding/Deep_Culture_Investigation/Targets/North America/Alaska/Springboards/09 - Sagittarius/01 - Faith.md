# Alaska: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian (church usage); Central Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Tlingit; French for Canadian sources. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox Native communities** (Unangax, Alutiiq, Yup'ik, Dena'ina) against **Moravian Yup'ik**, **Friends Iñupiat**, **Anglican Gwich'in**, **Presbyterian Southeast Tlingit and Haida**, and **Catholic** river parishes.
- **Traditionalist practitioners** and those who combine church and tradition, in their own words.
- **Anchorage and Fairbanks** congregations against bush, road-system and ferry towns.
- **Military bases** with chaplaincies; Old Believer villages; Filipino, Samoan and Korean congregations; the unaffiliated.
- **Yukon**: Anglican and Catholic legacies among First Nations, and Klondike-era settler churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. State surveys are thin; denominational and Native-authored sources are richer. Secular faith (state, oil, self-reliance) needs opinion polling plus newspapers.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for Orthodox sources; Native languages for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonies, songs and masks can belong to families or villages; rely on what communities publish. Boarding-school accounts are traumatic; read survivors' testimony with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
