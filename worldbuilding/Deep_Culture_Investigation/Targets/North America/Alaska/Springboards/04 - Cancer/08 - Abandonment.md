*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; the Native languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Tlingit, Gwich'in and other Athabaskan languages); Tagalog; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native families affected by boarding schools and removals, against settler and immigrant families.
- Village youth who leave for towns, and the families left behind.
- Mining, pipeline and fishing economies' workers who come and go, and their families.
- Military families; children in foster and adoptive care.
- Elders living alone, in villages or in the Pioneer Homes.
- Old Believer and Orthodox communities with their own norms on leaving a community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; agency counts exist; the lived experience is in survivors' accounts and oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** English; the Native languages for any word of leaving or of being left.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the taking of Native children is described by the peoples as removal, not abandonment, and survivors' testimony needs care; child-welfare records are closed; child removal law goes to Libra. Stories of elders left behind are community property; use published retellings only.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
