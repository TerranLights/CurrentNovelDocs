# Alaska: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Tlingit, Alutiiq, Athabaskan languages; Tagalog; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Grandparent-raised and aunt- or uncle-raised children, common in some regions' accounts, against two-parent homes.
- Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Athabaskan, Tlingit and Alutiiq families: published teaching styles differ by people.
- Village schools against Anchorage and Fairbanks schools.
- Families shaped by boarding schools across generations.
- Old Believer households, Orthodox families and Mormon families.
- Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino parents in the cities.
- Military families; single parents; foster and adoptive families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on programs and curricula; mixed on practice at home; the best sources are elders' publications and oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for teaching words; Russian for church.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare records are closed; teaching stories are held by their tellers. Use only what the nations share. Law on child welfare goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the child and teaching chapters in Fienup-Riordan and Kawagley.
2. Search the ANKN site for "elders" with "raising" and "teaching".
3. Pull the state maternal and child health survey reports for Alaska.
4. Search Project Jukebox for "my grandmother raised me" and "taught me".
5. Find the immersion schools' own program pages and read their stated aims.
