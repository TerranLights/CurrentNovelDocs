# Alaska: Domain and folkways, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada; the exact boundary is not checked here). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; the Alaska Native and Yukon First Nations languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dena'ina, Gwich'in and other Athabaskan languages); Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family: household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coastal Yup'ik and Cup'ik; Iñupiat of the North Slope and Seward Peninsula; Alutiiq; Unangax̂: house and village forms differ by coast.
- Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian: clan and house systems of the Southeast.
- Athabaskan peoples of the interior (Gwich'in, Koyukon, Ahtna, Dena'ina) and Yukon First Nations.
- Russian-heritage communities, including the Old Believer villages of the Kenai Peninsula.
- Descendants of homesteaders and the Matanuska colonists; roadhouse and mining families.
- Anchorage's immigrant communities (Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Latino) and military families.
- Village against city households; urban Native families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on census, housing and published elders' teachings; mixed on the interior and Yukon; thin on newer immigrant households.
- **Languages needed:** English first; the Native languages for kin and house terms; Russian for church and early records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan histories, names and some oral accounts belong to their holders; use only what the nations publish. Boarding school testimony needs care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the household and family tables for Alaska and Yukon (census, ACS) and compare by census area.
2. Search the Alaska Native Language Center catalog for "house", "family" and "relative" in each language.
3. Search Project Jukebox for "growing up", "fish camp" and "my grandmother".
4. Find the Alaska housing assessment and read its building-type items.
5. Search for family and kin chapters in the Calista and Sealaska Heritage publications.
