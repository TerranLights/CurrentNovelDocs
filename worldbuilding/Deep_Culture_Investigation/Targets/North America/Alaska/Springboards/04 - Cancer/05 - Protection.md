## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Village communities, where protection runs through kin, councils and the local officer, against Anchorage and Fairbanks, where police and agencies carry it.
- Native peoples whose published teachings tie protection to land (Gwich'in, Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Tlingit) and settlers' households.
- Women and girls, elders and children: the people protection is said to be for.
- Hunters, fishers, trappers and guides, for whom protection is practical (weather, animals).
- Military families and bases; the Russian Orthodox community.
- Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino households in the cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on agency and survey data; mixed on how people themselves speak of protection; thin on private household practice.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the words; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence and abuse data; missing-person cases; clan and land knowledge. Law on firearms, self-defense and child removal goes to Libra and Capricorn; the sheet holds no legal research.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
