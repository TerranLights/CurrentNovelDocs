# Alaska: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Tlingit, Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Gwich'in and other Native languages; Tagalog; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Alaska Natives and First Nations citizens whose belonging runs through a people and its land, against settlers who built belonging by residence.
- Villagers and city residents, including Natives living in Anchorage and Fairbanks.
- Long-time residents (the "sourdough" line) against newcomers and seasonal workers.
- Military families, who move in and out.
- Russian Orthodox and Old Believer communities, whose belonging runs through church and language.
- Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Latino and other immigrant communities; people of mixed descent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; surveys of attitudes exist but are coarse; the best material is in oral history and nations' own publications.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the peoples' own names and membership words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who is counted a member of a nation is that nation's question; cite nations' own statements. Legal membership rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search ISER and the Alaska Natives Commission report for "identity", "community" and "belonging".
2. Search Project Jukebox for "moved to town", "came up from", "never left".
3. Find the First Nations' own pages on citizens and the Alaska Native organizations' membership pages.
4. Search regional press for "cheechako" and "sourdough" to see where the words are still used.
5. Look up the Alaska Survey Research question wording on residence and identity.
