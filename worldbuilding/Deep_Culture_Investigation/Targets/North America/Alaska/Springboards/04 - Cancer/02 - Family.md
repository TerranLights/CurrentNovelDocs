*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; Tlingit, Haida, Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Athabaskan languages; Tagalog, Ilocano; Samoan; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian (matrilineal clans and houses) against Iñupiat and Yup'ik (kin networks, name-sharing): different shapes of kin, by published accounts.
- Athabaskan peoples of the interior and Yukon First Nations: band and clan histories differ by language group.
- Alutiiq and Unangax̂ communities shaped by Russian Orthodox godparenthood.
- Old Believer families of the Kenai Peninsula.
- Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino families in Anchorage; military families.
- Homesteader and Matanuska colonist descendants; urban Native families; blended and chosen families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on census and academic ethnography; mixed on present-day family; thin on newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Tlingit and Yup'ik for kin terms; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan names, crests and some stories belong to their owners; use only what the nations publish. Adoption and foster-care records are restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the family and household tables for Alaska and Yukon, by census area.

4. Search Project Jukebox for "my grandmother", "my uncle" and "adopted".
