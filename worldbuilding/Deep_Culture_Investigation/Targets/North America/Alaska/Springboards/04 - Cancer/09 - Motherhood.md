# Alaska: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; the Native languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Tlingit, Gwich'in and other Athabaskan languages); Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it, including social mothers and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian, whose clan and house systems should be checked for how descent is traced, against Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Athabaskan peoples with their own kin patterns.
- Mothers in villages and in Anchorage; mothers who work seasonal jobs, such as fishing and canneries.
- Grandmothers and aunts who raise children.
- Single mothers; teen mothers; mothers in the military.
- Russian Orthodox, Old Believer and Mormon mothers; Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino mothers.
- Pioneer and homesteader women against recent arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on health and vital statistics; mixed on ideals and meanings; the best meaning is in elders' publications and oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for mother and kin words; Tagalog for Filipino households.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth, pregnancy loss and maternal death data; women's teachings held by their communities. Use only what the nations publish.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Alaska PRAMS reports and read the question wording, not only the results.
2. Search the ANLC dictionaries for "mother", "grandmother" and "mother's sister".
3. Search Project Jukebox for "my mother", "my grandmother" and "had my baby".
4. Read the women elders' volumes' chapters on children and household.
5. Trace where "mama grizzly" and "hockey mom" were first used in Alaska press.
