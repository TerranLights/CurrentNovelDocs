# Alaska: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Russian; the Native languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Tlingit, Alutiiq, Gwich'in and other Athabaskan languages); Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Village and urban communities: care in a village runs through kin and the aide; in cities through agencies, by published description.
- Native peoples with published teachings on elders and kin (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Tlingit, Athabaskan) against settler families.
- Women and men as carers; children who care for siblings and grandparents.
- Filipino and Samoan households, where migrant care workers and elders appear in other literature.
- Old Believer and Orthodox parishes that care through church.
- Military families; people with disabilities and their carers; hospice and end-of-life settings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on programs and elders' publications; mixed on household practice; thin on unpaid caregiving by community.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for care words; Tagalog for migrant care workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health records; traditional healing and medicine knowledge held by its owners; death and mourning practices. Use only what the nations publish.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Iñupiat Ilitqusiat statement and note its care-related headings.
2. Search the ANLC dictionaries for "take care of", "feed" and "look after".
3. Find the health corporations' own descriptions of care and compare them.
4. Search Project Jukebox for "took care of my grandmother" and "looked after".
5. Pull the state's reports on caregivers and aging and read their definitions.
