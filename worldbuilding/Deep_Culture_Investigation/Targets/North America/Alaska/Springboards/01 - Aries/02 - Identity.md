## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The main Native peoples: Iñupiat, Yup'ik/Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, and the Dene peoples; each with its own self-name and boundaries.
- Native identity as a nation or tribe against pan-Alaska Native identity.
- Multi-generation non-Native families against recent arrivals and transient workers.
- Urban Anchorage against hub towns and villages; Southeast against Interior against the Arctic.
- Creole descendants of Russian-America; Russian Orthodox and Old Believer villages.
- Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean, Latino, African American communities; military families.
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census categories and for Native heritage institutions; mixed for non-Native identity; thin for polls specific to Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms from dictionaries; Russian for some archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who counts as Native is decided by the peoples and their institutions; do not adjudicate. Clan lineage and crest knowledge belong to their holders. Questions of boarding-school history should be read in the peoples' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
