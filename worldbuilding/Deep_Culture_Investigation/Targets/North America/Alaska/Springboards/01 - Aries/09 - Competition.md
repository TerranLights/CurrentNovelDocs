## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native games and community tournaments against mainstream school leagues.
- Dog-mushing communities, commercial and sport, against village sled-dog traditions.
- Road-system towns against fly-in villages in how teams travel and compete.
- Fishing fleets and fishing-derby participants; oil and construction crews; military families.
- Women, men, youth: who competes, in what, and how it is praised.
- Peoples with different views of cooperation and competition (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Tlingit, Haida, Dene).
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for organized sports and events; thin for how communities judge winning, and for cooperative values.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** games tied to ceremony or story belong to their peoples; read organizers' own descriptions. Avoid assuming that every people places the same value on beating others.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
