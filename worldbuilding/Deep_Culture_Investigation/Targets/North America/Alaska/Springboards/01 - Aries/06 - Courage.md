# Alaska: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native peoples' own sayings and exemplars of strength and endurance (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Tlingit, Dene), each separate.
- Hunters and fishers of subsistence communities against commercial fleet crews.
- Rural volunteers (fire, rescue, search) against urban professionals.
- Veterans and military families; Coast Guard families.
- Women, men and youth, and how each is credited.
- Russian Orthodox communities (saints and endurance); immigrants carrying other models.
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rescue, aviation and fishing narratives; mixed for community sayings; thin for moral and social courage.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of deaths and disasters involve living families; hunting and ceremonial knowledge belongs to peoples; war and service histories should come from the veterans' own telling. Avoid equating courage with risk-taking (see Recklessness).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Carnegie Hero Fund database for Alaska cases and note settings and kinds of danger.
2. Get dictionary entries on "brave," "fear," "strength" and "endure" from the language center.
3. Collect oral histories from Alaska Native veterans and bush pilots.
4. Pull occupational fatality context for commercial fishing and aviation.
5. Sample Alaska newspaper rescue stories for what is praised and how.
