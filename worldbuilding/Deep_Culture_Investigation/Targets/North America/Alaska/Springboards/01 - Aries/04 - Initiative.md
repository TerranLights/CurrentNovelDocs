## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native village and regional corporations against individual entrepreneurs and fish-camp families.
- Road-system towns against off-road communities that rely on volunteers for fire, rescue and utilities.
- Longtime residents against newcomers and seasonal workers; fishing, oil, tourism and mining sectors.
- Women, men, youth and elders as starters of projects.
- Immigrant business owners (Filipino, Korean, Hmong, Latino).
- Language-revival and heritage groups of the Native peoples.
- Canadian lands, if held, separately.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Business and nonprofit counts are open; how people judge initiative (admired or presumptuous) is likely thin and must come from interviews and memoir.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some parishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** village corporation financial detail and clan-based decisions on who may start what (such as use of crests) belong to those bodies; stay with published or shared material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** none researched; ballot-initiative rules sit with Libra.
