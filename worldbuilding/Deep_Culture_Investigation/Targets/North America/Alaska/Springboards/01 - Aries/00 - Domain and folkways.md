## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Iñupiat (northern and northwest), Yup'ik and Cup'ik (southwest), Alutiiq/Sugpiaq (Kodiak, Prince William Sound), Unangax̂ (Aleutians): differing naming and kin ways.
- Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian (Southeast): crest and clan systems, hereditary names.
- Interior Dene peoples (Koyukon, Gwich'in, Tanana, Ahtna, Dena'ina): separate languages and kin structures.
- Non-Native settlers: homesteaders, "sourdoughs," oil and fishing labor, military families, Anchorage against bush, Fairbanks, Juneau.
- Russian Old Believers and Orthodox Creoles; Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean and Latino communities; other immigrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for Native languages and heritage institutions; thin for self-concept surveys at state level; settler memoir is plentiful.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned names, songs and crests (at.óow) and some ceremonial knowledge belong to their holders; use only what peoples have published or shared. Names of the dead may be restricted in some communities.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
