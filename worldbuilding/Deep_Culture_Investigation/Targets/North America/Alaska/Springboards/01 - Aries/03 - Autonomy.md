*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Village councils and traditional governance among Native peoples (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Dene, Tlingit and others), each with its own order of deciding.
- Regional corporation shareholders against tribal citizens: different collective bodies.
- Homesteaders, miners, fishers and off-grid residents against salaried urban Anchorage households.
- Rural Alaska residents dependent on air service and supply against road-system communities.
- Families who send young adults to the cities against those who stay.
- Russian Old Believers, military households, immigrant families with family-centered decision styles.
- Canadian lands, if held, as a separate comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Homeschool and household data are open; direct data on how Alaskans experience being controlled or free is likely thin.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** village governance, hunting and fishing practice and clan decision-making belong to the peoples; use published or shared accounts only. Avoid equating independence rhetoric with any one group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** none researched; for self-government and subsistence law, see Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).

## 5. First queries
1. Find ANLC dictionary entries for "decide," "choose," "alone" and "by oneself" in three languages.
2. Pull Alaska homeschool and correspondence-program enrollment by district.
3. List the oral histories and memoirs of homesteaders and bush residents and read how they describe help and independence.
4. Search AFN convention records for "self-determination" and note who uses it and how.
5. Look for a regional measure of freedom of choice in an existing survey.
