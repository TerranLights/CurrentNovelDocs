## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native communities with traditions of sharing and reciprocity (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Tlingit, Dene) against households relying on wages.
- Subsistence households against commercial fishers, oil workers and public employees.
- Off-grid homesteaders and bush residents against road-system suburbs.
- Rural villages tied to air and barge supply; hub towns; Southeast ferry towns.
- Elders aging in place against those moving to cities or care homes.
- Veterans, immigrants, Old Believer farming households, people in recovery.
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for harvest and household surveys and for memoir; mixed for attitudes to dependence.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** harvest locations and hunting practice are held by communities; use aggregate published data. Do not frame sharing as the opposite of independence without the peoples' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** none researched; subsistence and benefit-program rules sit with Libra and Capricorn.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the Division of Subsistence community harvest surveys and note who shares with whom.

3. List Cooperative Extension publications on home food preservation and building.
4. Search Alaska memoirs and journalism on living off the grid and on its failures.
5. Read food-sovereignty program statements from two regional health or Native organizations.
