## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Visitors and newcomers against long-term residents in how risk is judged.
- Native hunters and travelers on ice and sea, with their own caution teachings, against recreational adventurers.
- Commercial fishing and aviation crews; oil and construction workers; military personnel.
- Young men, young women, elders: how each is warned and blamed.
- Rural communities where alcohol policy differs from road-system towns.
- Snowmachine, boat, climbing and flying communities.
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for injury and rescue reports; mixed for local judgment of what counts as foolish; stereotypes about outsiders are common and should be checked.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries; Russian for some sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** injury and death reports concern families and communities; use aggregate data. Alcohol, drug and gambling topics are heavily stereotyped for Native peoples; use the peoples' own health organizations' publications. Cautions about ice, sea and weather are living knowledge, not for reuse without permission.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** none researched; driving, drinking and gambling law sits with Libra.
