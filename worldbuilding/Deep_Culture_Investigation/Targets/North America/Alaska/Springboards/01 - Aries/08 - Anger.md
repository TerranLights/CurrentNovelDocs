## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native peoples with distinct teachings on anger, conflict and forgiveness (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Tlingit, Dene), each separate.
- Village life against urban Anchorage and Fairbanks.
- Winter workers and isolated households (long darkness) against summer seasonal workers.
- Men, women, youth and elders: how each may show anger.
- Military and civilian communities; fishing and oil crews.
- Orthodox and Old Believer congregations.
- Canadian lands, if held.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Institutional and violence-related data exist but frame anger through harm; direct material on how anger is understood is likely thin.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries and peoples' own publications; Russian for some sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic and sexual violence data concern living communities; use published reports and do not generalize to any people. Peoples' teachings on conflict and healing belong to them. Avoid stereotypes tying anger to alcohol or to any group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Law:** none researched; criminal and domestic-violence law sits with Libra.
