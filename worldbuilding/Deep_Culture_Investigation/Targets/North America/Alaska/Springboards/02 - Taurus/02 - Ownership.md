# Alaska: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war USA and Canada lands. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian (Sm'algyax), Dene languages; Russian for colonial records. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Peoples who hold by clan or house (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian); peoples who hold by family camp or use area (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Dene); corporation shareholders against non-shareholders, including those born after the enrollment date; homesteaders and miners; state-land buyers; renters in Anchorage; federal land managers; and Yukon First Nations and settler owners if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on land tenure and corporations; mixed on everyday ideas of owning; thin on newcomers.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous terms for clan or camp claims; Russian for early records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan property and names belong to their owners; ask before citing. The statutes, cases and treaties themselves go to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Alaska DNR for "land status" and read how categories are named.
2. Read two corporation annual reports, one regional and one village, for how ownership is described.
3. Search Division of Subsistence reports for "use area" and "family camp".
4. Read Berger's report summaries for what villagers said about owning land.
5. Search the Alaska Historical Society journal for "homestead" and "claim jumping".
