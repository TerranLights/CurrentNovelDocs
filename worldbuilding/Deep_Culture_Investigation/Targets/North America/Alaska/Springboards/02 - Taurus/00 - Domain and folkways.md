*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war USA and Canada lands (confirm exact Canadian lands at Stage 0). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dena'ina, Koyukon, Gwich'in and other Dene languages; Russian (historic records). **Domain in one line:** possessions and value: what is worth having, and how is it held?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Dene peoples of the Interior (Koyukon, Gwich'in, Ahtna, Dena'ina), and Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian of the Panhandle: each reckons wealth in its own way. Also compare road-system towns against Bush villages; Russian Orthodox communities; fishing, mining and oil-service workers; military families; late-arriving Filipino, Korean, Samoan and Hmong communities; and Yukon First Nations and settler towns if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on subsistence and income; mixed on attitudes; thin for newer immigrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Indigenous languages for terms; Russian for pre-1867 records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan property, regalia, and names belong to their owners; ask before use. Elders' words are the nations' own to publish.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the ADF&G subsistence database for a few communities and read how food, sharing and cost are recorded.

3. Search Alaska Native Language Center dictionaries for "own", "share", "rich", "poor".
4. Read Alaska Dept. of Labor price and wage pages for urban against rural contrasts.
5. Ask Sealaska Heritage Institute what may be cited about clan property.
