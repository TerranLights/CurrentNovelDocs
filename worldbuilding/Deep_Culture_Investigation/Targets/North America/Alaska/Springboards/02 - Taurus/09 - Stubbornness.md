# Alaska: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war USA and Canada lands. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian; Spanish and Tagalog for newcomer communities. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Peoples with different ideas of holding firm against yielding (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax̂, Dene, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian); activists, elders and corporation leaders; homesteaders, miners and fishers; Dividend-defenders and budget-cutters; development and conservation camps; village councils against city governments; newcomer communities; Yukon First Nations if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on public disputes and civil-rights history; thin on private habits of mind.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous languages for the key terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disputes over subsistence, mines and land are live; record each side. Never treat "independent Alaskans" as one voice. Law and court rulings go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search dictionaries for "stubborn" and "persist" in two Native languages and check how each is glossed.
2. Read *Tundra Times* issues from the 1960s for public refusal and campaigns.
3. Read the Pebble and Dividend coverage in two outlets and note who is called firm or obstinate.
4. Read elders' advice chapters on listening and persistence.
5. Check Alaska State Library for homesteader and miner holdout accounts.
