# Alaska: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war USA and Canada lands. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian for Orthodox-era records; Filipino and Spanish for large newcomer communities. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Separate Native peoples by region and language; elders from youth; Native village residents from urban Natives in Anchorage; boarding school survivors and their descendants; Orthodox and Protestant congregations; Panhandle clans; gold-rush and homesteading descendants; military families; Filipino, Korean and Samoan newcomers; and Yukon First Nations if carried.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich survey data on well-being; elders' statements in print; thin on non-Native attitudes.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous languages for worth terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding school harm, suicide and addiction data are painful and often communal; use the nations' own framings and ask before quoting. Statistics must be checked at the source.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

1. Read the Alaska Native Knowledge Network's published values lists for two peoples.

4. Read the self-worth passages in the Fienup-Riordan and Nelson books.
5. Search Alaska Federation of Natives convention archives for "dignity".
