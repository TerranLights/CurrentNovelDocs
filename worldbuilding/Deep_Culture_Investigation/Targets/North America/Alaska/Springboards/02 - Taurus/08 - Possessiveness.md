# Alaska: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war USA and Canada lands. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Central Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Dene languages; Russian; Spanish and Tagalog for newcomer communities. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Separate each Native people's own norms on sharing and refusing; village from city; road-system sport and commercial fishers; subsistence families and sport hunters; long-time residents and newcomers; miners and homesteaders; religious communities (Orthodox, Protestant, Catholic, Latter-day Saint); military families; and newcomer communities with their own expectations of marriage and family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good on measured violence and on resource conflict; thin on everyday possessive feeling.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous languages for faults and sharing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence and abuse data concern real victims and often communities already harmed; use the nations' and survivors' own framings and aggregated sources. Law on trespass, custody and abuse goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
