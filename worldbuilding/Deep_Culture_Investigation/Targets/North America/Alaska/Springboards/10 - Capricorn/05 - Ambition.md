# Alaska: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Dena'ina; Russian for church and settler sources. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is, in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native shareholders and corporate executives against villagers whose work is subsistence and seasonal; peoples with different histories of leadership (Southeast clan societies; Iñupiat; Yup'ik; Dene); youth who leave villages for Anchorage or Outside, and those who return; fishing, oil, mining and tourism households; military and federal families; Alaska's political families and newcomer office-seekers; Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean and Latino immigrant communities; old-timers against newer arrivals drawn by money or by the frontier.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gold and oil booms, Native corporation goals and education statistics; mixed for family ambition; thin for how ambition is judged within villages, so seek the community's own words.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms for aspiration and leadership; Russian for settler communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shareholder-vote and corporate disputes; family and clan matters; elders' interviews with access terms.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read a corporation report and a shareholder-meeting summary for stated aims.
2. Search ISER and the state labor department for migration and education data.
3. Search Project Jukebox for "leaving", "went to school", "got a job".
4. Search the Alaska Native Language Center dictionaries for words for strive and succeed.
5. Pull GSS and WVS hard-work-versus-luck items, filtered to Alaska where possible.
