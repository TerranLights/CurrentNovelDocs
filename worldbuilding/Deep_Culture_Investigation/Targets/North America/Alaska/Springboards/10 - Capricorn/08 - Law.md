# Alaska: Law, the spirit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Tlingit, Haida, Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Dena'ina; Russian for the colonial and church record. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit only; the letter of the law is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native peoples with their own legal orders (Tlingit and Haida clan law, Yup'ik, Iñupiat and Dene customary practice), kept separate; villages with a tribal court or peacemaking circle against those without; road-system towns with state police against off-road villages with village safety officers; Orthodox and Old Believer communities; military and federal households; libertarian-leaning settlers; newcomers and immigrant communities; victims' families and those who have been through the system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Native justice writing and for court and trust surveys; mixed for settler legal consciousness; thin for how ordinary non-Native Alaskans think of obedience.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for law terms; Russian for church and colonial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence, sexual assault and suicide data; child-welfare cases; clan law is owned knowledge; armed antigovernment movements are a political topic to approach with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
