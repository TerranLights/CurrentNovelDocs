# Alaska: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Tlingit, Haida, Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Dena'ina; Russian for church sources. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct, decide for or speak for others, and their acceptance of it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Southeast ranked clan societies (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian), where authority runs through house and clan; Iñupiat and Yup'ik villages, where leadership is local and tied to subsistence; interior Dene peoples, with traditional chiefs and elected councils side by side; Alutiiq and Unangax communities shaped by Russian and Orthodox authority; Anchorage and Fairbanks urban residents; military and federal households; libertarian-leaning settlers on the road system; newcomers against old-timers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Native governance and for state-federal disputes; mixed for everyday deference and household authority; thin for how authority is felt inside non-Native families.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms for local concepts; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan authority involves owned names, crests and speeches that belong to clans; boarding-school and Native child-welfare records; some elders' interviews are restricted; police-encounter material.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Note:** the permission share and correctional-control share are collected at Stage 2; do not research law here.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Alaska Constitution's preamble and bill of rights as a statement of spirit, not as law research.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "elders" and "who made decisions".
3. Search AFN resolutions for "tribal authority" and "sovereignty".
4. Pull Alaska-filtered trust-in-government items from Pew, Gallup and the Cooperative Election Study.
5. Search the Alaska Native Language Center catalogs for words for chief, leader, elder.
