# Alaska: Domain and folkways, authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada; confirm the exact lands in the scope file once written). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Cup'ig, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu (Aleut), Tlingit, Haida, Sm'algyax (Tsimshian), and the Dene (Athabascan) languages such as Gwich'in, Koyukon, Dena'ina and Ahtna; Russian for the Orthodox and Old Believer communities. **Domain in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed (career, status, reputation, authority, responsibility; plus the age, time, rank, order and power folkways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian of the Southeast, with ranked clans and moieties; Iñupiat of the North Slope and Northwest; Yup'ik and Cup'ig of the southwest delta; Alutiiq and Unangax of the Gulf and Aleutians; interior and southcentral Dene peoples; Anchorage urban residents; road-system towns against off-road villages; oil, fishing, military and federal-employee households; Russian-descended Orthodox and Old Believer communities; later-arriving Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean and Latino communities; and old-timers against newcomers. Any exile or newcomer cohort the scope sets is a unit of its own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Alaska Native governance, subsistence and oral history; mixed for political culture and work; thin for newer immigrant groups and for the post-war lands if they include northern Canada.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Native languages for terms; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned stories, crests and songs belong to their owners; ceremonial and personal names; some oral histories carry access conditions; grief and abuse in boarding-school records.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
