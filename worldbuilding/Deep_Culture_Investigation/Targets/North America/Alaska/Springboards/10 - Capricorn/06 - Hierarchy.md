# Alaska: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Tlingit, Haida, Sm'algyax, Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Dena'ina; Russian for the colonial and church record. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Southeast ranked clan societies (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian), where moiety, clan and house rank matter; Iñupiat and Yup'ik communities organized around whaling crews, kin groups and elders; Dene peoples with village chiefs and regional councils; Alutiiq and Unangax communities shaped by the Russian era; Creole descendants; Native corporation shareholders and nonshareholders; urban against rural; military against civilian; oil, fishing and federal workers; old-timers against newcomers; recent immigrant groups and their sponsors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for clan structure in the Southeast and for colonial classification; mixed for modern class and for Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Dene rank; thin for non-Native status systems at the village level.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for rank terms; Russian for colonial documents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan names, crests and house ownership are owned knowledge; ranked-slavery history is painful to descendants; segregation and discrimination accounts; elders' interviews with access terms.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up rank words in the Tlingit and Haida dictionaries and in the Dauenhauer volumes.
2. Search Sealaska Heritage Institute for "clan", "moiety" and "house".
3. Search the Alaska Native Brotherhood and Peratrovich records for "discrimination" and "segregation".
4. Read the Native corporation enrollment rules for how members are classed.
5. Search ISER for rural and urban income and class studies.
