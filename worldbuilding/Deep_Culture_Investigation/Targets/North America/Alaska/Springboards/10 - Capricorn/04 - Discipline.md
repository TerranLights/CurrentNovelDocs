## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Peoples with different traditions of raising and teaching children (Yup'ik, Iñupiat, Tlingit, Haida, Dene, Alutiiq, Unangax); former boarding school students and their families; Orthodox communities with a fasting calendar; Old Believers at Nikolaevsk and other Russian settlements; military families; mushers, fishers and oil-field workers; Anchorage urban residents against off-road village residents; recent immigrant groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school and boarding-school history and for Native education writing; mixed for home child-rearing; thin for self-discipline as a topic in its own right, so expect to read it out of sport, work and church sources.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for words; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school abuse and child-welfare records; corporal punishment testimony; substance recovery narratives; elders' interviews with access terms.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Culturally Responsive Schools standards for what they say about teaching and conduct.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "boarding school", "raised", "taught us".
3. Search the Alaska Department of Education site for discipline and suspension counts by district.
4. Search mushing and Native Youth Olympics sources for "training" and "discipline".
5. Look in the language centers' dictionaries for words for teach, correct and train.
