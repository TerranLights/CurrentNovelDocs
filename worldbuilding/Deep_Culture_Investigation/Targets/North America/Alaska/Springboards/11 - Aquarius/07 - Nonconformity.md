# Alaska: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Dene languages; Russian (church dissent). **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Old Believer communities, as religious dissenters; off-grid homesteaders and recluses; counterculture towns (Homer, Haines, Talkeetna, Ester, Sitka) against Anchorage suburbs; anti-government and independence movement members and their neighbors; Native Two Spirit and gender-diverse people, in their own terms, and settler LGBTQ residents; Native youth music scenes (hip-hop, punk) in hubs like Bethel and Nome; those who left a village or church and those who stayed; veterans and conscientious objectors; Fairbanks artists and writers; and tourists and seasonal workers who stay on the margins.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on settler eccentrics in memoir and journalism; mixed on Native views of difference; thin on youth subcultures and on LGBTQ life outside Anchorage.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms with Native guidance; Russian for church dissent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gender-diverse traditions (outsider records may be unreliable or intrusive; follow Native-authored sources); mental illness behind "eccentric" labels; extremist movements; small communities where individuals are easy to identify.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
