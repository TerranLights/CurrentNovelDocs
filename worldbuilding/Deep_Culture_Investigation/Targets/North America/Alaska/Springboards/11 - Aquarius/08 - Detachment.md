# Alaska: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Dene languages; Russian; Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings or from outcomes; cultures prize some forms (calm, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Athabaskan and Dene communities (whose communication norms the Scollons studied); Iñupiat and Yup'ik villages (check each people's own statements rather than an assumed single style); Tlingit funeral and feast roles; Orthodox and Old Believer contemplative traditions; trappers, winter caretakers and cabin residents; village teachers and nurses who arrive from outside; community health aides who live where they work; oil-field rotation workers in camps; bush pilots and fishermen; military and veterans; journalists; urban residents against village residents; and newcomers and old-timers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography on communication norms (Dene) and in personal essays; mixed on survey measures; thin on Alaska-specific emotional display studies.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms with Native guidance; Russian for monastic sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, depression and isolation data are painful and easily misused; claims that a people is "reserved" or "noninterfering" can harden into stereotype, so test each against the people's own voices and the Scollons' own cautions.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
