# Alaska: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Cup'ig, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Sm'algyax, Dene languages such as Gwich'in and Koyukon; Russian. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, cause), who recognize one another as members and owe one another something.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Village communities by language region (Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangax, Dene, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian); fish-camp and seasonal-round households against year-round residents; Native people living in Anchorage and Fairbanks against home villages; road-system towns against off-road ones; mining, cannery and military towns; homesteader and counterculture settlements; Matanuska Valley farm families; Old Believer and Orthodox villages; Southeast and Kodiak fishing communities with seasonal crews; and newcomers who arrive for a job and leave within a few years.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for subsistence and village profiles; mixed for settler communities; thin for loneliness, friendship and how a newcomer becomes a member.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the words that name the group; Russian for parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** small-village studies can identify individuals; some oral histories are restricted; grief and loss narratives, and boarding-school records, need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
