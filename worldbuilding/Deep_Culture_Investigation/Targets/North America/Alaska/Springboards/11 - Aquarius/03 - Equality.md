## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Northwest Coast peoples whose ethnographies describe ranked houses and clans (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, Alutiiq) beside Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Dene peoples often labeled more egalitarian (*check* the labels); Native residents against non-Native residents; shareholders against descendants of shareholders; Black Alaskans (including descendants of the Alaska Highway construction era), Filipino, Samoan and other Pacific Islander communities; women in male-dominated work (fishing, oil, trades); hub-city residents against off-road village households; seasonal cannery workers against year-round residents; oil-income households against cash-poor subsistence households; and political camps with differing views on equal payments.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the dividend and income statistics; mixed on Native views of equality, which come mostly through elders' statements and ethnography; thin on informal practices of rank among settlers.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language dictionaries for the near-equivalents; Tagalog and Spanish for worker histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accounts of racist treatment and boarding schools; hereditary slavery in the Northwest Coast past, a painful topic for descendants; claims about "egalitarian" peoples can be stereotypes, so check each against the people's own statements.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
