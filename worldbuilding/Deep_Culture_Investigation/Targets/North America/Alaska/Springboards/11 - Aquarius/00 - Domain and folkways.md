# Alaska: Domain and folkways, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada; confirm the exact lands in the scope file once written). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Cup'ig, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Tlingit, Haida, Sm'algyax, and Dene languages such as Gwich'in, Koyukon and Dena'ina; Russian for Orthodox and Old Believer settlements. **Domain in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for (friends, associations, humanitarian ideals, the future, plus the folkways of settlement, hospitality and gathering).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian of the Southeast (clans, moieties, brotherhood halls); Iñupiat whaling crews and villages; Yup'ik and Cup'ig delta villages; Alutiiq and Unangax of the Gulf and Aleutians; interior and southcentral Dene communities; Anchorage as a mixed city (Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean, Latino, Hispanic and Black residents); road-system towns against off-road villages; commercial fishing, cannery, oil, military and federal-service households; Russian-descended Orthodox and Old Believer settlements; seasonal and rotation workers; old-timers against newcomers; and, if the scope includes northern Canada, the Yukon First Nations and Yukon towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Native organizations, subsistence sharing and oral history; mixed on settler associational life; thin on newer immigrant groups and on friendship as such.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Native-language terms for "we", kin and guest; Russian for church and settlement sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned songs, crests and stories; memorial-gathering protocols; some oral histories carry access conditions; boarding-school grief; small villages where individuals are easily identified.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
