# Alaska: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Tlingit, Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages; Russian. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles and visions of how things ought to be) above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Native peoples with published value lists (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Tlingit, Dene and others) and their youth against their elders; Orthodox faithful (monastic and village traditions) against Protestant mission-founded congregations; homesteaders and back-to-the-landers of the 1970s onward; planned-community descendants (Matanuska, Metlakatla, Nikolaevsk); oil, pipeline and development supporters ("North to the Future") against conservationists; military families; seasonal workers and young adventurers who come for a summer; counterculture towns (Homer, Talkeetna, Haines, Sitka) against Mat-Su and Interior libertarian settlements; and exiles or newcomers whom the scope names.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on settler wilderness idealism and on Native value statements; mixed on idealism as a living attitude; thin on direct attitude surveys specific to Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Russian for church sources; Native-language terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in the wilderness (families and communities remember them); missionary-founded settlements carry painful and contested memories; elders' value teachings are theirs to share.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Settle which lands and peoples the nation holds (its Stage 0 scope is not yet written).
2. Find the published Iñupiat values list and the culturally responsive school standards, and note who wrote them and for whom.
3. Search library catalogs for homesteading and wilderness memoirs and diaries from Alaska.
4. Search Project Jukebox for "why did you come" and "ideals".
5. Pull the planned-community histories (Matanuska, Metlakatla, Nikolaevsk) and compare how each remembers its founding.
