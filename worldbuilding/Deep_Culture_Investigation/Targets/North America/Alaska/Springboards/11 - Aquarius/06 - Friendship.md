# Alaska: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Cup'ig, Tlingit, Haida, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Tagalog, Spanish | *kaibigan*, *kumpare*; *amigo*, *compadre* | friend; co-godfather | Filipino and Latino communities; check |
| English | "partner", "pard", "bunkmate", "crewmate", "fishing buddy", "cabin neighbor", "mukluk telegraph" | local friendship words, including the gold-rush partner | Check period and community of each |

English keywords: friendship, namesake, trading partner, godparent, men's friendship, seasonal friends, isolation, loneliness, hospitality, neighbors at a distance, visiting.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Iñupiat and Yup'ik namesake and partnership networks; Tlingit and Haida opposite-moiety relations; Dene hunting and trapping partners; Orthodox godparent webs in Yup'ik, Alutiiq and Dene villages; Filipino and other cannery crews; seasonal workers who return each summer; military friends across bases; roadhouse and bush neighbors at long distances; men's friendships centered on fishing, hunting and flying; women's friendship in villages and towns; elders in pioneer homes and their visitors; newcomers who say friends are hard to find; and LGBTQ residents in Anchorage and Juneau.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on formal ties (namesakes, trading partners, godparents) in ethnography and church records; mixed on everyday friendship; thin on survey data specific to Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms; Russian for church records; Tagalog.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** namesake ties involve names of the dead, and some families do not speak them; loneliness and suicide data are painful; village friendships are easy to identify in small places.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
