# Alaska: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Tlingit, Haida, Sm'algyax, Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages (Ahtna, Gwich'in, Koyukon and others); Russian; Tagalog and Ilocano for the cannery-worker history. **Concept in one line:** a set of people seen as a unit; how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and how members act together.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tlingit | *naa*, *yéil* and *ch'áak'* (moiety names) | clan; the two halves, Raven and Eagle | Check against a Tlingit dictionary; Haida and Tsimshian have their own moiety or phratry terms |
| Iñupiaq | *umialik* | a whaling captain who heads a crew | Search with "crew" and "captain's wife"; check |
| Yup'ik | words for household, village and men's house members | varies | Check Yup'ik dictionaries; Fienup-Riordan's glossaries may help |

| Russian | *artel'*, *starover*, *svoi* and *chuzhoi* | work band; Old Believer; ours and foreign | Old Believer village sources |
| Tagalog | *kababayan*, *barkada* | fellow countryperson; peer group | For Filipino cannery and Anchorage communities; check |
| English | "Alaskan", "Native", "Outsider", "pipeline", "local hire", "Alaskero", "crew", "mushers' association" | self-labels and group names | Check each label's use by different groups |

English keywords: clan, moiety, crew, brotherhood, shareholder, union local, loyalty, outsider, newcomer, solidarity, partisan, Alaska Independence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian clans and moieties; Dene clan peoples of the interior; Iñupiat whaling crews and their kin; Yup'ik and Cup'ig villages; Alutiiq and Unangax communities; Native shareholders against non-shareholders and descendants; Filipino cannery families; longshore and Teamsters workers; fishing fleets; oil-field and pipeline crews; military families; Old Believers; political camps (conservative, libertarian, independence, Native-led, labor); and urban Native residents against village residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Tlingit and Iñupiaq group structure and on labor history; mixed on settler groups; thin on gangs, fandoms and online groups in Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native-language terms for clan and crew; Russian; Tagalog.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan crests, names and songs belong to their holders; enrollment and shareholder disputes are painful; boarding-school history; use of the word "tribe" varies by nation.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
