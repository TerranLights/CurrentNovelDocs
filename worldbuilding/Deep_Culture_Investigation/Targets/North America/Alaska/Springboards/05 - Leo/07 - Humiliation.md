# Alaska: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

English keywords: shaming, bullying, hazing, boarding school punishment, banishment, shunning, public apology, mocking, cyberbullying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by region:** teasing, shunning and banishment may be understood differently in each language group; compare, do not combine.
- **Generations in the same families:** survivors of boarding school punishment, their children and grandchildren, and the language revival that follows.
- **Small village against large town:** everyone-knows-everyone shaming against anonymity and its online replacement.
- **School and workplace worlds:** teams, fishing crews, military units, university dorms.
- **LGBTQ youth** and other targeted groups.
- **Immigrant families:** whose shame rules arrive from elsewhere and meet the local ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Public records on bullying and punishment exist; private shaming rarely appears in them. Oral history is the richest route and the most delicate.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for terms of shame, teasing and repair.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding school and abuse testimony, suicide and trauma data, and clan disputes; some oral histories are closed or carry conditions; people named in them may be living.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the state youth survey's bullying items and note which regions and groups it separates.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "laughed at", "teased", "ashamed", "punished" and "shunned".
3. Read published survivor accounts and tribal reports on boarding school punishment for what was done and what was named.
4. Search the Native language dictionaries for the words of shame and teasing in each unit's language.
5. Search recorded folktales for episodes where someone is mocked or banished, and note who tells them.
