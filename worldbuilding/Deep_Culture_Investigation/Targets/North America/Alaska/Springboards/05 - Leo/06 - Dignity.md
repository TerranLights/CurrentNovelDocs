# Alaska: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by language group,** each with its own teaching on respect for Elders, for animals and for relatives; check for variation, not a single list.
- **Subsistence households against wage workers** and mixed households that do both.
- **Village against city:** access to clinics, housing and jobs changes what "decent" means.
- **The old and the disabled:** at home, in Pioneer Homes and in far-off hospitals.
- **Seasonal and migrant workers:** cannery and fishing crews from other states and countries, with different treatment on the job.
- **Unhoused residents** of Anchorage and other towns, and the volunteers who serve them.
- **Boarding school survivors,** against those who attended local schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on health-care framing and elder events; thin on everyday indignity and on private poverty.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for respect and relation terms; Tagalog and Spanish for worker communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony and burial or repatriation matters are restricted to families and tribes; hospital and care records are private; laws on dignity belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. List the published Native values statements and mark each item on respect, Elders and the care of others.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "respect", "treated", "Elders" and "taken care of".
3. Read the health organizations' public descriptions of how patients are addressed and compare them with patients' accounts.
4. Search the Tundra Times and local newspapers for "dignity", "decent" and "respect" in letters and tributes.
5. Look for any Alaska breakout in "treated with respect" survey items.
