# Alaska: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Peoples with a named values statement** (the North Slope Iñupiat, the Yup'ik, Tlingit clan systems) against peoples whose pride vocabulary has to be found in interviews.
- **Native pride as movement** against pride as ordinary family or village feeling; they may not be the same thing.
- **Long-term residents and newcomers:** sourdough and cheechako status, and children of military families.
- **Workers' pride:** fishers, hunters, pilots, miners, oil workers and mushers, each with a craft pride.
- **Immigrant communities:** Filipino, Hmong, Samoan and Latino residents, who may carry pride of origin alongside pride of Alaska.
- **Town against town and school against school:** rivalries to check in sport and in regional identity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in public ritual and values statements; thin in measured surveys for Alaska alone; sparse on private shame.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the words that distinguish self-respect from boasting.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shame and pride tied to boarding schools, loss of language and loss of land are painful; some oral histories are restricted; clan standing is not a public matter.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the published Native values statements and mark each word that could mean pride, boasting or humility.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "proud", "ashamed", "show off" and "humble".
3. Pull the dates of the state and Native commemorations and list who speaks at each.
4. Search the regional newspapers and radio for "proud" in tributes and in letters, by town.
5. Check whether the national pride polls have any Alaska breakout.
