# Alaska: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by game traditions:** coastal peoples with kick and reach games, Yup'ik girls' storyknifing, Interior hand and stick games, Southeast stick games; each people's repertoire separately.
- **Village and city children:** access to open land, ice and snow against organized sports and screens.
- **Winter and summer seasons,** which may change the whole shape of play.
- **Generations:** elders' childhood games, parents' school games, children's screen play.
- **Settler and military families:** hockey, skiing, snow machines, Little League and base recreation.
- **Immigrant children:** Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino games brought and adapted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for school sport, festivals and a few named Native games; thin for ordinary unorganized play and for adult play outside sport.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for games, string figures and stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some stories told in play are owned by tellers or seasons; some elders' games and songs carry rules on who may share them; trickster tales are told on conditions in several communities.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
