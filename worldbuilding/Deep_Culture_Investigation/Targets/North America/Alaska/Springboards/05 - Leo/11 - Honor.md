# Alaska: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by honoring custom:** Southeast clans, Interior memorial feasts, Arctic whaling communities and Yup'ik dance festivals; compare their forms and their language terms.
- **Veterans and military families,** including Native veterans and the Alaska Territorial Guard's descendants.
- **Frontier and workplace honor:** fishing crews, pilots, trappers, miners and oil workers, where trust in a person's word has high stakes.
- **Church communities:** Orthodox, Catholic and Protestant teaching on honor and conscience.
- **Immigrant communities,** with their own honor vocabulary from home.
- **Gender and generation:** whether honor rests on different conduct for men and women, to be tested, not assumed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for veterans' honoring, public ceremonies and oral history; thin for everyday honor and for honor in disputes, which are private.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for honoring and disgrace terms; Tagalog and Spanish for community veterans.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memorial ceremonies, names of the dead and clan honors are restricted in many families and communities; veterans' moral injury and disgrace are private; legal questions of reputation belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Project Jukebox for "word", "honor", "disgrace", "respect for the dead" and "veteran".
2. Read the Alaska Territorial Guard histories and note what honors were given and by whom.
3. Collect the public descriptions of honoring and memorial events for each unit, and note what is withheld.
4. Search local obituaries and tributes for the conduct the writers praise.
5. Check the language dictionaries for terms of honor, good name and disgrace in each unit's language.
