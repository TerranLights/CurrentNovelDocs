# Alaska: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples of whaling, fishing and hunting communities:** food sharing by kin, clan or village; compare each.
- **Feast-giving peoples** of the Southeast and Interior, and peoples where giving is tied to a catch or a festival.
- **Native corporation shareholders and non-shareholders,** whose giving relationships to the corporations differ.
- **Urban residents,** whose giving may run through charities, churches and workplaces.
- **Settler and newcomer communities:** church groups, service clubs, military families and seasonal workers.
- **Immigrant households sending money to relatives abroad,** especially Filipino-heritage and Latino communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Rich for subsistence sharing, feasts and charitable foundations; thin for informal giving among neighbors and for remittances.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for sharing, hosting and feast terms; Tagalog and Spanish for remittances.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feast protocols, names and gift roles belong to families and clans; harvest data carries privacy rules; some recordings are restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the subsistence division's community reports and note how sharing is counted.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "share", "give away", "feast" and "Elders first".
3. Read the Native values statements for each unit's word and framing of sharing.
4. Pull the Pick.Click.Give and foundation giving numbers, and compare them with the national giving indexes.
5. Search the local papers for obituaries and tributes that praise a person's giving.
