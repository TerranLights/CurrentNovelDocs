# Alaska: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; the Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, crafts, problem solving and everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southeast coast peoples (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian):** crest art and weaving tied to clan and house ownership.
- **Yup'ik, Cup'ik and Iñupiat of the western and northern coasts:** masks, ivory, skin sewing, grass and baleen work with different traditions of who creates.
- **Alutiiq and Unangax peoples:** the island and gulf traditions of weaving, carving and mask making, with revival work to separate from older practice.
- **Athabascan peoples of the Interior:** beadwork, birchbark and moosehide traditions.
- **Urban professional artists in Anchorage, Fairbanks and Juneau,** against village and subsistence makers.
- **Immigrant and church communities:** Orthodox icon and church arts; Filipino and Hmong needlework, among others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for named Native art forms and museum holdings; thin for ordinary settler making, hobbies and invention.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Native languages for what the makers call their own work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crest designs and some objects are owned by clans or houses and are not free to copy or reproduce; some museum photographs are restricted. Ask holders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
