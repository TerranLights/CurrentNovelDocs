# Alaska: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

English keywords: self-expression, dress code, hair rules, Pride, drag, tattoo, bumper sticker, personal style, art therapy, open mic.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by language and region:** speech and dress norms may differ between Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Athabascan, Alutiiq and Tlingit communities; check, never assume one pattern.
- **Revival movements against continuous practice:** traditional tattoo, language and dance revivals, and communities where the practice never stopped.
- **Village and urban residents,** including Native people who move between both.
- **Generations:** elders, parents and the younger people who post online.
- **Religious communities:** Orthodox, Protestant and Catholic congregations, Old Believer villages, and non-religious residents.
- **LGBTQ residents and Two-Spirit people** in Native communities, with the term's own local use to be checked.
- **Rural settlers and urban professionals,** and recent immigrant groups, each with their own dress and speech habits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich in oral history and in public festivals; thin in surveys, since Alaska is seldom reported on its own; very thin on private emotion.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for terms of speech, shyness, song.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** personal songs, names and dances may be owned; some oral histories carry restrictions on use; records of boarding-school dress and speech rules are painful for living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Project Jukebox for "speaking up", "shy", "singing", "dance" and "name".
2. List the public festivals by region and note who dresses how and who may take part.
3. Search student newspapers and local papers for "dress code", "hair", "Pride" by town.
4. Find the language center's dictionary entries for the words of speaking, silence and feeling in each unit's language.
5. Pull World Values and Pew items on free expression and note which carry an Alaska subsample.
