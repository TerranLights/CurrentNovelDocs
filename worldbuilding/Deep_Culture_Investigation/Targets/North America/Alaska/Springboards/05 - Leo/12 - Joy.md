# Alaska: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by festival and season:** whaling and fishing festivals, memorial and feast gatherings, dance festivals, and the church year, each with its own joy vocabulary.
- **Orthodox against Protestant and Catholic communities:** Christmas and Easter customs.
- **Village against city:** community hall and school gatherings against venues, bars and organized events.
- **Winter and summer:** whether the same people describe joy differently across the dark and light seasons.
- **Generations:** elders' gatherings, parents' church and school events, young people's online celebration.
- **Newcomers and long-term residents:** outdoors and recreation as joy among settlers, military families and seasonal workers.
- **Immigrant communities:** Filipino, Samoan, Hmong and Latino celebrations, with their own music and food.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for festivals, church feasts and published stories; thin for private joy; survey series seldom separate Alaska or its peoples.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for terms of gladness; Russian for church feasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some songs and dances are owned and cannot be recorded or shown; memorial gatherings mix grief with joy and are private; recordings of the dead need permission.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the festival calendars for each region and note which are public and which are closed.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "happy", "laugh", "first fish", "dance" and "glad".
3. Check the state health survey for any life satisfaction or happiness item and its regional breaks.
4. Find the Native language words for gladness and festival in the language center's dictionaries.
5. Read the Orthodox parishes' published accounts of Christmas and Easter customs for who takes part.
