# Alaska: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by region:** coastal and island, Interior and Arctic communities may differ in rules on who may be looked at, photographed, named or recorded.
- **Village against city residents,** including those who leave a small place where everyone is known for one where no one is.
- **Elders and the young:** being overlooked in old age against being constantly pictured online.
- **Women, men and two-spirit or LGBTQ residents:** how visibility works as safety or as risk.
- **People living off the road system,** who are thin in statewide data and media.
- **Small immigrant and military communities** that live inside the larger towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on representation and the media from Native newspapers and public radio; thin on private feelings of being seen or not; surveys seldom isolate Alaska.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for the words of witness and watching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** images of people, the dead and sacred places carry restrictions; recordings of living or recently dead people need permission; reports on missing and murdered relatives involve grieving families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Tundra Times archive for "forgotten", "ignored", "overlooked" and "seen".
2. Search Project Jukebox for "watching", "looking", "noticed" and "no one knew".
3. Pull the census enumeration and undercount material for rural Alaska and note which units it separates.
4. Read the public reports on missing and murdered Indigenous people as sources on visibility, and note whom they name.
5. List Native photographers and filmmakers of Alaska, and read their statements on being pictured.
