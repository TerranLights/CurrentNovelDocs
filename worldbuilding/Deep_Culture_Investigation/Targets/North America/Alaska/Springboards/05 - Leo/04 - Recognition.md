# Alaska: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples with giveaway and feast honoring** (Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian; Yup'ik and Iñupiaq dance festivals; Athabascan memorial potlatches) against groups whose honors come mainly from schools, churches and employers.
- **Elders' standing against credentials:** titles from tradition and titles from degrees may point to different people.
- **Settler pioneer lines** and later arrivals, who may rank each other by years in the state.
- **Military families and base communities,** with the ordinary honors of service.
- **Immigrant associations:** Filipino, Samoan, Hmong, Korean and Latino community awards, and whose foreign credentials are accepted.
- **Women and men:** whose work is credited in fishing, hunting, sewing and care work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for formal honors and public ceremonies; thin for private praise and withheld credit.
- **Languages needed:** English; Native languages for honoring and thanking customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memorial feasts and naming are private to families and clans; some recordings and names of the dead are restricted; apologies and boarding school accounts are painful and unfinished for living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the lists of honorees at the Federation convention, the Women's Hall of Fame and the governor's arts and humanities awards; tally by region and field.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "honor", "thanked", "named" and "giveaway".
3. Read recent land acknowledgments in Alaska and list which peoples and languages they name.
4. Search regional obituaries for the phrases that praise the dead, by community.
5. Find how each unit's language marks thanks and honoring in its dictionary.
