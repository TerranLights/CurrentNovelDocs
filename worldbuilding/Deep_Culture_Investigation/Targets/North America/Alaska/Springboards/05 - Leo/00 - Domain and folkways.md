# Alaska: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America; carries pre-war lands of the USA and Canada (exact units are set at Stage 0). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages (Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Tlingit, Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Alutiiq and others, by unit); Russian (church and Old Believer communities); Tagalog and other languages of immigrant communities; French for any Canadian lands. **Domain in one line:** creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alaska Native peoples, separated by language and region:** Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Iñupiat, Siberian Yupik, Alutiiq, Unangax, Athabascan peoples (Dena'ina, Gwich'in, Koyukon, Ahtna and others), and the Southeast peoples (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian). Their festivals, regalia and dance ground differ.
- **Urban against bush:** Anchorage and Fairbanks against road-less villages; many Native people live in both worlds.
- **Settler lines:** gold-rush and homestead descendants, military families, Russian-heritage communities (Orthodox parishes, Old Believer villages), and later immigrant communities such as Filipino, Hmong, Samoan, Korean and Latino.
- **Resource towns and fishing towns:** the cannery, oil and mining workforces, which may differ from the Native and settler communities they sit beside.
- **Any Canadian lands:** the Yukon-area First Nations and settler towns, if carried in.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for Native festivals, arts and sport (institutions, recordings); thin for small-town settler pleasure ways; state-level survey data is often too small for Alaska alone.
- **Languages needed:** English; at least one Alaska Native language per unit for the deep sources; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan-owned songs, dances, crests and stories belong to their owners and may not be shared or recorded; some ceremonial knowledge is closed. Ask holders, not archives alone.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Project Jukebox and the Alaska Native Language Center catalog for the domain's words (song, dance, game, feast) in each unit's language.
2. Pull the festival calendars of the Heritage Center, Sealaska Heritage Institute and the Fairbanks games; list who performs and who wins.
3. Search arts-council grant lists and museum exhibition catalogs for artists by region.
4. Search local newspapers for "talent show", "community hall" and "hockey night" by town to catch settler pleasure ways.
5. Pull the time-use and recreation counts, and note where Alaska's sample is too small.
