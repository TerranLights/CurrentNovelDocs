*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Alaska Native languages by unit; Russian; Tagalog; Spanish; French for any Canadian lands. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native peoples by governance style:** clan and house leaders (Southeast), whaling captains and councils (North Slope), village councils and elected corporations (Yup'ik and Athabascan regions); compare, do not merge.
- **Traditional against elected leaders,** and the Native corporation board against the village council, where both exist.
- **Women and men:** Elders, clan mothers, captains' wives, corporation chairs and council members.
- **Workplaces:** fishing crews, oil field and mining crews, bush pilots, sled dog teams, the military.
- **Urban civic and religious leaders,** including Orthodox and Catholic clergy.
- **Immigrant associations,** with leadership by senior members or by elected boards.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for formal leaders and public figures; thinner for informal leaders and for women's leadership; oral history is the best route to the Elder's role.
- **Languages needed:** English; the Native languages for leader and Elder terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan titles, names and the right to speak for a house are not public; disputes within villages are private; laws and regulations on governance belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. List each unit's governance bodies and the names of the leader roles in its own language.
2. Search Project Jukebox for "captain", "chief", "Elder" and "listen to", and tally who is named and why.
3. Read published biographies of leaders from each region and note what the writers praise.
4. Search regional news for tributes and obituaries of leaders for the qualities mentioned.
5. Look for any survey item on confidence in leaders that has an Alaska breakout.
