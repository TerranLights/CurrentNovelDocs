# Alaska: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages by dictionary (Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian); Russian; Spanish; Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between peoples and governments, and in daily social life. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native nations with their own protocols: the southeast clan houses (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian) with formal guest and host roles, against Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Dene, Alutiiq and Unangax ways of receiving visitors and settling quarrels; separate each nation.
- Native regional and village corporations against tribal governments, as different negotiators with the state.
- Border and coastal communities with Russian, Canadian and Pacific connections.
- Russian-descended parishes and Old Believers, who deal with outsiders on their own terms.
- Military communities; Filipino, Samoan, Korean and Latino communities with their own norms of tact and mediation; interpreters in rural courts and clinics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal agreements, state-tribal relations and circumpolar bodies; mixed for protocol, much of which is held orally; thin for everyday tact.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous dictionaries and the nations' own publications; Russian for older diplomacy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial protocol, names and songs belong to the houses that hold them; use only what a nation publishes. Treaty-era records involve contested accounts: read each side's voice first.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the state-tribal agreements and the Arctic Council and Inuit Circumpolar Council statements.
2. Search AFN convention resolutions for "government-to-government" and "consultation".
3. Ask the State Archives for the 1867 transfer papers and Russian-era records.
4. Search Sealaska Heritage and tribal sites for published accounts of reconciliation ceremonies and guest protocol.
5. Search news archives for the transboundary river talks with British Columbia.
