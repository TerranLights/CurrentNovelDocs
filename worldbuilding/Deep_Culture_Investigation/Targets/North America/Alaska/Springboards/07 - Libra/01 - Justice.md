# Alaska: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages (Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian) by dictionary; Russian; Spanish; Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Villages off the road system, served by Village Public Safety Officers and itinerant magistrates, against Anchorage and Fairbanks courts.
- Native nations that run tribal courts against those with none.
- Southeast peoples (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian) with clan-based compensation traditions, against interior Dene and coastal Yup'ik and Iñupiaq: separate each nation's own account.
- Aleut and Unangax descendants of the wartime evacuation: a distinct redress history.
- Russian-descended and Old Believer communities, with their own church-based settling.
- Military families, newer immigrant communities, and mining and fishing camps.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, court opinions and corrections statistics; mixed for tribal courts, which publish unevenly; thin for immigrant communities' own practice.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous-language dictionaries; Russian for Russian-era records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cases involving living victims and minors; tribal court proceedings may be closed; use what a nation publishes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Alaska Statutes Titles 11 and 12 for offenses, sentencing and the rules on juries and judges.
2. Search the Alaska Court System site for opinions citing "tribal court" and "Native village".
3. Pull the Alaska Criminal Justice Commission's reports and the Department of Corrections' statistics.
4. Search the UAA Justice Center and *Alaska Justice Forum* for "rural" and "Alaska Native".
5. Ask each tribal government's website, then its office, for its justice code.
