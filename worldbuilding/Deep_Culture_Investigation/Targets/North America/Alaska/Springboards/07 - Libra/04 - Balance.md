## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Yup'ik, Iñupiaq, Dene, Alutiiq, Unangax and southeast peoples: each has its own teaching of balance with land and kin; separate them.
- Commercial, subsistence and sport users of fish and game, as the allocation fights frame balance.
- Rotation workers on the North Slope and in mines, seasonal cannery and fishing workers, against salaried city residents.
- Development advocates and conservation advocates over Bristol Bay and the Arctic refuge.
- Road-system residents against off-road villages with different seasonal rhythms.
- Russian Orthodox and Old Believer communities with their own fasting calendars; newer immigrant communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on resource-management rules and ballot texts; mixed on Native teachings, which are published unevenly and by each nation; thin on personal balance for Alaska alone.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous-language dictionaries; Russian for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** traditional healing and ceremonial knowledge belongs to each nation; use only what it publishes. Letter-of-the-law framing only here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
