*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Native nations that decide by consensus in councils against those using corporate boards and elected votes; ask each nation for its own account of pace and respect.
- Legislators from the road system against rural caucuses over fiscal choices.
- Village residents, for whom a decision may mean waiting on weather, supply and season, against city households.
- Resource-development supporters and opponents in long-running project fights.
- Russian Orthodox and Old Believer parishes where clergy or elders decide; newer immigrant communities where family decides jointly.
- Military families, who move on orders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ballot-measure history, legislative records and project timelines; mixed for decision customs inside Native nations; thin for household-level indecision.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous dictionaries; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** council and ceremonial decision-making belongs to each nation; use only what it publishes. Do not treat slowness as a trait of any people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the ballot-measure record for the capital-move votes and the periodic constitutional-convention question.
2. Read ISER and Legislative Finance papers on the fiscal gap and dividend disputes.
3. Trace one long project (gas pipeline or Susitna dam) through agency minutes.
4. Search Alaska Native Knowledge Network for published consensus and council accounts.
5. Pull Alaska Survey Research poll tables on undecided shares.
