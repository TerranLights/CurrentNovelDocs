## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Iñupiat, Yup'ik and Cup'ik, Alutiiq and Unangax peoples: separate languages and separate ways of binding partners.
- Dene (Athabascan) peoples of the interior, with matrilineal-leaning kin lines in some nations: check each.
- Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian of the southeast, with clan and moiety rules on marriage; Metlakatla as a distinct reserve.
- Villages off the road system against Anchorage, Fairbanks and Juneau.
- Russian Orthodox and Old Believer communities on the Kenai Peninsula and Kodiak.
- Military households and rotating workers; Filipino, Samoan, Korean, Latino and Hmong communities.
- Homesteader and gold-rush descendants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state law, court and survey data; mixed for Native custom; thin for small immigrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** English first; Indigenous-language dictionaries for terms; Russian for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan and marriage rules belong to each nation; use only what a people publishes. Violence data involves living victims. Letter of the law only here; spirit of law is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Alaska Statutes Title 25 for marriage, divorce, property and customary adoption.
2. Pull marital-status tables for Alaska boroughs and census areas from the ACS.
3. Search Sealaska Heritage and the Alaska Native Language Center for "marriage", "clan" and kin terms in each language.
4. Find the Alaska Victimization Survey and AFN public-safety resolutions.
5. Search Alaska Court System material for "tribal court" and "peacemaking".
