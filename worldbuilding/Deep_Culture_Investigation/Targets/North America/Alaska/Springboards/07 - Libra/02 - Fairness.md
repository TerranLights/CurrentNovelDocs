# Alaska: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Indigenous languages by dictionary (Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Dene languages, Tlingit, Haida); Russian; Spanish; Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: a due share, taking turns, the same rules for all. Where the law is involved, only its letter.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of road-system cities against residents of off-road villages, where harvest sharing networks and store prices differ.
- Native corporation shareholders against non-Native residents, and shareholders of one regional corporation against another.
- Subsistence users against commercial and sport fishers, as the allocation disputes frame them.
- Southeast clan houses with potlatch distributions by role, against Yup'ik, Iñupiaq and Dene sharing practices; separate each nation.
- Seasonal and out-of-state workers on the North Slope and in the canneries.
- Military households, and newer immigrant communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on dividends, harvest sharing and allocation fights; mixed for attitudes, since national surveys rarely isolate Alaska; thin for fairness inside households.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous-language dictionaries for terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** distributions at potlatches and gatherings are the hosts' own matter; use only what the nations publish. Subsistence-site locations are protected.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Division of Subsistence database for "sharing" and "distribution" by community.
2. Read the Permanent Fund Dividend statutes and the 2020 Ballot Measure 1 pamphlet.
3. Read ANCSA's revenue-sharing section and a regional corporation's annual report.
4. Search Board of Fisheries and Board of Game records for "allocation".
5. Search Alaska Native Knowledge Network and each Native language center for fairness and sharing terms.
