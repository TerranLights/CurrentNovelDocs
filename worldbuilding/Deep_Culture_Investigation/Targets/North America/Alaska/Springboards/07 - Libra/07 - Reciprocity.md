# Alaska: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Alaska, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA, Canada). **Language(s) for searching:** English; Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Alutiiq, Unangam Tunuu, Gwich'in and other Dene languages, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian (by dictionary); Russian; Spanish; Tagalog; Samoan. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nations with whale-sharing, fish-sharing, potlatch or Messenger Feast systems: Iñupiaq, Yup'ik, Dene, Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, Alutiiq and Unangax; each has its own obligations, so separate them.
- Off-road villages, where shipping costs and distance raise the stakes of exchange, against city households.
- Native corporation shareholders and nonshareholders, and the state's dividend recipients.
- Fishing, mining and oilfield crews with their own favor networks.
- Russian Orthodox parishes with godparent ties; Filipino, Samoan, Latino and Korean communities with remittance and communal-help forms.
- Newer arrivals and military families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for subsistence sharing and dividends; mixed for potlatch and giveaway accounts, which each nation controls; thin for urban favor networks.
- **Languages needed:** English; Indigenous dictionaries; Russian for older church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** potlatch protocol, names, regalia and songs belong to the houses that hold them; harvest locations are protected. Use only what a nation publishes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Division of Subsistence database for "sharing", "distribution" and "customary trade".
2. Read the Alaska Eskimo Whaling Commission materials on distribution.
