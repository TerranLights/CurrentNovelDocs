**What the scope must cover** (the template's sections):
1. **Territory:** every pre-war jurisdiction in Cascadia, as the project's territory files define it. The national level counts too (the U.S., Canada and Mexico), and tribal nations with their own courts. There are many tribal jurisdictions (California alone has over a hundred federally recognized tribes; British Columbia has many First Nations, some with self-government treaties). **Don't list every one:** propose a grouping, name representative cases (for example, nations with their own courts or modern treaties), and flag the grouping for the author.
2. **Units:** the regional cultures inside Cascadia, using Woodard's county-level lines where he covers the ground, and the regional frameworks elsewhere (the Bajas, British Columbia, the Alaska Panhandle). For each unit: the zone within Cascadia (core, domain or sphere); the founding population (first effective settlement) and when; the later layers; the framework that defines it. **Indigenous peoples are units in their own right:** propose how to group them (for example, by culture area: Northwest Coast, Plateau, California, the Yuman peoples of the Bajas), with tier-2 peoples named under each.
3. **Weights:** the founders' weight for each unit, then present-day population shares as corroboration, using the latest U.S. census, Statistics Canada and INEGI figures by county, census division or *municipio*. Say where the two disagree.
4. **Seams:** where units meet inside Cascadia, and what divides them (the project already records the Left Coast and Far West seam as Cascadia's main internal weakness).
5. **Scope statement:** which smaller distinctions are left out, and why, stated explicitly (Woodard's "say what you leave out" rule). **No South Asian unit or material:** under the universe's No Subcontinentals law, no South Asian culture is part of this work. Where present-day demography includes a South Asian community, note in one line that it is excluded under that law, research nothing further about it, and flag the line for the author.
6. **Era checkpoints:** propose them from the project files (for example, the year *Streetside Harmonies* is set, the setting of *Starlit Engines*, the Republic-to-People's-Democratic-Republic transition, the exile to Tepenia). The author confirms.
7. **Existing project material:** list every file read, with what it is and whether it's canon or a proposal.
8. **Decisions for the author:** numbered, each answerable in a sentence.
