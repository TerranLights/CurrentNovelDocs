**Founders first:** the peoples present at contact, among the most linguistically diverse regions on earth, with their own institutions: small tribelets and village communities, acorn and salmon economies, controlled burning, and (in northwestern California) wealth and the World Renewal cycle. Then the missions (1769-1823), Mexican secularization, the Gold Rush and the killings, the 1850 indenture act, the 18 unratified treaties of 1851-1852, rancherias, termination and restoration, and gaming.

**What to look for** (starting points, not limits; anything marked "check" is from general knowledge and must be confirmed or dropped):
- **Owned and restricted knowledge.** Many stories, crests, names and songs belong to particular families, houses or clans, and some ceremonial knowledge is restricted. Describe tale types, motifs, genres and what they do socially; don't retell owned or restricted stories or reproduce song texts. Say where a nation restricts something, and stop there.
- **The nations' own voices.** Use each nation's own published materials (constitutions, codes, cultural and language departments; tag official or practitioner) and Indigenous-authored scholarship alongside the classic ethnography, and say where a nation or later scholarship contests the older ethnography.
- **Founders first, for an Indigenous unit,** means the people's own institutions before contact first, then the contact layers (fur trade, missions, disease, reserves and reservations, residential and boarding schools, bans, land loss), then revitalization.
- Handbook of North American Indians vol. 8, *California* (Heizer, 1978); Kroeber, *Handbook of the Indians of California* (1925); Madley, *An American Genocide* (2016); the California Truth and Healing Council (2019 onward).
- **Kinship:** the variety across the area; Ethnographic Atlas and D-PLACE codes for the coded societies (Yurok, Hupa, Pomo, Yokuts and others).
- **Religion:** the Kuksu religion, World Renewal, the Chinigchinich (toloache) complex in the south (check), the 1870 Ghost Dance and Bole-Maru; the missions' Catholicism; revitalization.
- **Language:** many families and isolates (Hokan and Penutian as debated groupings, check); Ishi and the Yahi; speaker counts and revitalization (the Breath of Life workshops, check).
- **Formative events:** the missions; the Gold Rush genocide; the unratified treaties; Ishi (1911); the land-claims settlements (check); termination and restoration (Graton Rancheria 2000); the Alcatraz occupation (1969-1971); the Klamath dam removals (2023-2024).

**Rules:**
- American English.
- **Native, Indigenous or Aboriginal, never "Indian"** (author, 2026-11-10, out of respect for the Indigenous peoples and nations of this land). In your own words say Native, Indigenous or Aboriginal; in Canada also First Nations, Inuit or Métis; in Mexico, Indigenous or *pueblos indígenas*. Keep as published only what isn't yours to rename: published titles and names (the *Handbook of North American Indians*), a nation's own name, quotations, and census category names (write: Native, the census category "American Indian and Alaska Native"). For an official term that uses the word, describe it in your own words and give the term once, in quotes.
- **No South Asian works as sources.** A modern study that would be set aside only because of an author's name goes in `held_for_review_<name>.md` beside your output, not into the text.
- **No Subcontinentals.** Where a South Asian community or an event centered on one appears in the evidence, note in one line that it is excluded under the No Subcontinentals rule, research nothing further about it, and flag the line for the author.
- **Census aggregates** (for example "Asian" or Canada's "visible minority") that include the excluded group: report them as the census publishes them, without a breakdown, and flag them. The author confirmed this on 2026-11-10.
- Short quotations only (a sentence or less), with citations. No copyrighted text, raw datasets or images in the repo.
- **Tag every claim:** measured, official, scholarly, synthesis, classic, practitioner or claim (defined in `<DM>/Shared/Sources_and_Datasets.md`). Sources from think tanks with a declared orientation are tagged practitioner and checked against academic measures.
- **Mark how each source was read:** *(read)* in full or in the relevant part; *(search summary)* if only from a search-engine summary or an abstract; *(not consulted)* if named from general knowledge.
- Founders first, then later layers. **Variation is required** in every profile. **Honest nulls,** not forced matches. Record disagreements between sources; don't resolve them.
- No national stereotypes: a people is never one voice.
- **Research only, up to the present day** (era E0 in the scope). Nothing about the war, the post-war states or anything after 2026: no divergence, no in-world content. That is Stage 6, the author's.
