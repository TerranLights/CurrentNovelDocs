**Founders first:** Alta California's Spanish soldiers, Franciscans and settlers (1769-1782) and the Californios; in Baja California, the Dominican missions (from 1774) and the late, largely foreign-financed founding (Ensenada, Tijuana, Mexicali and the Colorado River Land Company; the 1937 *ejido* seizure). The Anglo-Midwestern wave of the 1880s onward is a **later layer, not a co-founder** (decision 6); so are the Dust Bowl, the war industries, 20th-century Mexican and Central American immigration, and the other communities the scope names.

**What to look for** (starting points, not limits; anything marked "check" is from general knowledge and must be confirmed or dropped):
- **The unit spans the border.** Every profile's Variation answer covers both sides, Southern California and Baja California, and says where they differ.
- Woodard's El Norte chapter; Pitt, *The Decline of the Californios* (1966); McWilliams, *Southern California Country* (1946) and *North from Mexico* (1949); Starr; Garreau, *The Nine Nations of North America* (1981), "MexAmerica"; for Baja California, Grijalva (2014) on Mexicali, the history of the International Company of Mexico at Ensenada, and El Colegio de la Frontera Norte (COLEF) in Tijuana.
- **Religion:** Catholic founders; evangelical and Pentecostal growth; the Anglo-Protestant layer; Pew and INEGI figures.
- **Formative events:** the missions; the Mexican-American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848); the land-title losses of the Californios; the 1880s boom; the Mexican Revolution in Baja California (1911); the Bracero Program; the Zoot Suit riots (1943); Proposition 187 (1994); the maquiladora era; the border wall; the San Quintín farmworkers.

**Rules:**
- American English.
- **Native, Indigenous or Aboriginal, never "Indian"** (author, 2026-11-10, out of respect for the Indigenous peoples and nations of this land). In your own words say Native, Indigenous or Aboriginal; in Canada also First Nations, Inuit or Métis; in Mexico, Indigenous or *pueblos indígenas*. Keep as published only what isn't yours to rename: published titles and names (the *Handbook of North American Indians*), a nation's own name, quotations, and census category names (write: Native, the census category "American Indian and Alaska Native"). For an official term that uses the word, describe it in your own words and give the term once, in quotes.
- **No South Asian works as sources.** A modern study that would be set aside only because of an author's name goes in `held_for_review_<name>.md` beside your output, not into the text.
- **No Subcontinentals.** Where a South Asian community or an event centered on one appears in the evidence, note in one line that it is excluded under the No Subcontinentals rule, research nothing further about it, and flag the line for the author.
- **Census aggregates** (for example "Asian" or Canada's "visible minority") that include the excluded group: report them as the census publishes them, without a breakdown, and flag them. The author confirmed this on 2026-11-10.
- Short quotations only (a sentence or less), with citations. No copyrighted text, raw datasets or images in the repo.
- **Tag every claim:** measured, official, scholarly, synthesis, classic, practitioner or claim (defined in `<DM>/Shared/Sources_and_Datasets.md`). Sources from think tanks with a declared orientation are tagged practitioner and checked against academic measures.
- **Mark how each source was read:** *(read)* in full or in the relevant part; *(search summary)* if only from a search-engine summary or an abstract; *(not consulted)* if named from general knowledge.
