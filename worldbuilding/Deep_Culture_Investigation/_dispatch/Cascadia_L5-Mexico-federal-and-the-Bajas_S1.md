**What to look for** (starting points, not limits; anything marked "check" is from general knowledge and must be confirmed or dropped):
- **Mexico (federal), Part A:** the civil-law family and its sources (Spanish law, the French codes, the 1928 Federal Civil Code); the 1917 constitution; the **amparo** (1847 onward); **jurisprudencia,** binding by reiteration and, since the 2021 reform, by the Supreme Court's precedents (check how it works now); the 2008 reform to adversarial oral trials, fully in force by 2016, and the national codes of criminal procedure (2014) and of civil and family procedure (2023, check its rollout); the **2024 judicial reform** and the election of judges (first elections June 2025; the new Supreme Court from September 2025, check); agrarian law, *ejidos* and *comunidades* (Article 27 and its 1992 reform); Article 2 and Indigenous rights (2001 and the 2024 reform, check); what is federal and what each state sets (each state has its own civil and penal codes).
- **Mexico, Part B:** taxes and social programs; the federal gun law (Article 10, a single army-run gun store, check); federal drug law and organized crime; abortion after the Supreme Court's rulings of 2021 and 2023; same-sex marriage nationwide (2022); the National Guard (2019) and its transfer to the army (2024, check); mandatory pretrial detention (*prisión preventiva oficiosa*) and its expansions; surveillance and emergency powers. Measures: the World Justice Project's *Mexico States Rule of Law Index*; INEGI's ENVIPE (victimization) and ENCIG (trust in institutions); EFNA (practitioner).
- **Baja California:** statehood (1952) and its constitution (1953); the first state governed by an opposition party (PAN, 1989); its civil and penal codes (marital property regimes; testamentary freedom); abortion decriminalized (2021, check); its Indigenous-rights law (2007; job L7 covers the communities); regulated sex work in Tijuana's Zona Norte (check). Variation: Tijuana, Mexicali, Ensenada and San Quintín.
- **Baja California Sur:** territory until statehood in 1974; its constitution and codes; abortion decriminalized (2022, check); its newer Indigenous and Afro-Mexican rights law; variation between La Paz, Los Cabos and the northern *municipios*.
