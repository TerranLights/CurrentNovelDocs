# Springboard clusters: nations that can share agents

**Why:** a springboard agent spends most of its tokens reading the brief, the template and the concept block, not writing. Cascadia cost about 61,000 tokens per sheet, of which the sheet itself is about 1,500. If one agent writes a whole **sign** for a **cluster** of closely related nations, that reading is paid once for the cluster instead of once per sheet.

**The rule that keeps quality:** the sheets stay **nation-level and separate**, one file per nation per concept, exactly as in Cascadia. A shared agent writes each nation's sheet on its own: the search terms, the "where to look first", the "who to compare inside the nation" and the flags must be that nation's, not the cluster's. A sheet that is a copy of its neighbor with the name swapped is a failure; the spot-check looks for it. Keep clusters to 2 to 4 nations (an agent then writes at most about 40 sheets).

**Independent of clusters:** the author's order (Cascadia, Alaska, Sonora, Korea, Sinian Federation, Japan, and so on down the master tracker) holds for the pilots. After the pilots, clusters may be taken in whatever order is cheapest.

## What this saves (estimate, untested)
If the one-agent-per-nation-sign shape costs about a tenth of Cascadia's, a cluster of three in one agent per sign could cost about a third of that per nation. Measure it on the first cluster before relying on it. A small or documentation-thin nation costs less, because its sheets run shorter; population matters little, internal diversity matters much.
