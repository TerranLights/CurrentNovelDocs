**Founders first:** Woodard's Far West is founded by **institutions, not a people**: the railroads, mining companies and the federal government, with the environment trumping ethnicity. In this lobe: the forty-niners and the mining districts; the corporate and federal water works; ranching and dryland wheat in the Columbia interior; the Cariboo rush, the Okanagan orchard colonies and the Kootenay silver rush in British Columbia. Then the Dust Bowl migrants, Mexican and Mexican-American farm labor (California's Far West is now 42.2% Hispanic: the scope's disagreement 2), and the farming communities the scope names.

**What to look for** (starting points, not limits; anything marked "check" is from general knowledge and must be confirmed or dropped):
- Woodard's Far West chapter; Worster, *Rivers of Empire* (1985); Reisner, *Cadillac Desert* (1986); Gregory, *American Exodus* (1989); Limerick, *The Legacy of Conquest* (1987); White, *"It's Your Misfortune and None of My Own"* (1991).
- **Measured links:** Bazzi, Fiszbein and Gebresilasse, "Frontier Culture" (*Econometrica*, 2020), county-level frontier experience and individualism; Nisbett and Cohen on herding and honor (check whether their evidence extends to western ranching counties, and say so either way).
- **Political culture:** Elazar's state classifications and his county-level subculture map; the "libertarian-corporate" politics Woodard describes; the State of Jefferson and Greater Idaho movements as Variation (the scope keeps them inside the unit).
- **Interior British Columbia:** the Cariboo, the Okanagan, the Kootenays, the Doukhobor settlements (a Russian sectarian layer, check), resource towns.
- **Formative events:** the Gold Rush; the water projects (the Los Angeles Aqueduct, the Central Valley Project, Grand Coulee); the Dust Bowl migration; the farmworker movement (the Delano grape strike and the UFW); Hanford and the Manhattan Project; wildfire and drought.
