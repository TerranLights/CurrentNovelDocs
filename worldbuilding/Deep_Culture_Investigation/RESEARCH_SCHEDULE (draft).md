## Phase 0: calibrate the method with Cascadia (before anything else)
**Goal (author):** research and results as deep, extensive and high-quality as possible. Cascadia is the pilot: eleven units, the Stage 0 scope accepted, the springboards done, the Stage 1 run set up (`_dispatch/STAGE1_RUN_PROGRESS.md`). Nothing after Cascadia starts until the method is calibrated on it and the author accepts the calibration.

**Steps (proposed):**
- **C1. Stage 1 (Session A)**, the eleven lineage profiles, run from the springboard under the research rules (follow every relevant branch, research log, `leads_parked.md`, stop at saturation). The author says "go".
- **C1a. Depth tests first** (proposed): before the full Stage 1 run, try the depth mechanisms below on two or three Stage 1 jobs: the dose-response test (mechanism 9) and the blind second agent (mechanism 8). Their results set the floors and the stop test for the full run.
- **C2. Calibration audit of Stage 1** (main session, Stage 7 checklist plus the measures below). Fix the method before going on.
- **C3. Stages 2 and 3** (Session B), then audit again.
- **C4. Aries** (Session C: Stage 4 for the eleven units, then the composite), then audit again. Aries is the first full sign and tests the job shape (one concept x one unit).
- **C5. Gate: the author reviews the audits and accepts or changes the method.** Then the remaining eleven signs of Cascadia run as ordinary research, with the audit repeated after every third sign (proposal), because a method fixed once can still drift.

**What the audit measures** (written so "as deep as possible" can be checked, not just felt):
1. **Springboard use:** for each sheet, how many pointers were checked and resolved (found, absent, dead), and the sheet's "check" items closed. Pointers never touched need a reason.
2. **Beyond the sheet:** leads followed that the sheet did not name; entries in `leads_parked.md`; how many parked leads were later taken up.
3. **Saturation evidence:** the rate of new findings in the last searches of each job. If the last third still finds new material, the job stopped too early.
4. **Verification:** the Stage 7 pass: share of sampled claims confirmed, corrected or unsupported, and the unsupported ones traced to cause.
5. **Source quality and spread:** primary against secondary, the "two kinds of source authority" balance (classics against modern scholarship), recurring-source concentration (the survey saw NHK in 63 of 130 Japan sheets and the *Secret History* in 70 of 130 Mongolia sheets), native-language sources used, minorities and internal regions covered.
6. **Time and use (recorded, not limited):** searches, tokens and hours per job, unit and sign, with the weekly-allotment reading, so the day estimates come from measurement.
7. **The author's blind spot-check:** a few outputs chosen by the author, read against what the author already knows about the place.

**Knobs the audit can turn:** the job briefs and templates; the search budget (the 45 per unit is a placeholder; saturation should decide); wave size and job shape; the number of verification passes; the source-diversity instruction; sheet-length rules; whether a second agent reviews each output; the order of stages.

## Making the research go deep: proposed mechanisms (author, 2026-11-16: "as extensive as possible; find and secure ways to make sure that happens")
**Why agents stop shallow:** they satisfice (the first good answers feel like enough), anchor on the list they were handed, take a search budget as a target, and report "done" without proof. No instruction removes that; what works is making a shallow stop **detectable, costly and not rewarded**. Proposals, to be tested in Phase 0 and kept only if they show a gain:

1. **Depth is set by evidence, not by a budget.** Drop "about 45 searches per unit" as a target. A job ends only when its stop test passes: the lead queue (item 3) is empty or every entry is closed or parked with a reason, **and** the last 15 searches found nothing new of substance. The agent proves it from its own ledger (item 2).
2. **A search ledger for every job** (`research_log.md`): each search's query, language, what it returned, and one of *new finding / confirms / dead end*, plus any lead it spawned. The ledger is the audit trail; a "done" report without it is rejected. It also gives the real saturation curve for the cost study.
3. **A lead queue as a first-class file** (`leads.md`): every finding that points elsewhere (a name, a source, a bibliography, a place, a contradiction, a term in another language) becomes an entry with a status. Each entry records its **hop depth** (a lead found by following a lead is hop 2, and so on); the job's report states the deepest hop reached. Parked leads (`leads_parked.md`) are taken up at the start of the next job on the same unit.
4. **A coverage grid built from the concept, not from the springboard.** For each concept and unit, the cells are: the unit's regions and subgroups; the periods (pre-modern, colonial or contact, modern, today); practice against stated ideal; primary against secondary; each language of the unit. Every cell is filled with findings or marked "searched, nothing found" with the queries used. The springboard fills some cells; the rest must be searched.
5. **Blind-first planning, against anchoring.** Before reading the sheet, the agent writes its own search plan from the concept and the unit alone; then it reads the springboard and adds what the plan missed and the sheet missed. The sheet becomes a cross-check, not the plan.
6. **Citation-chasing as a rule:** for the best two or three sources in each cell, read the source itself, follow its bibliography backward and the works that cite it forward, and search the specialist journals and archives it names. Search in the native script and in each minority language, with synonyms and reformulated queries, not one query per idea.
7. **Several passes, different agents.** Pass 1 maps the cells. Pass 2 sends one agent per thin or rich branch to go deeper. Pass 3 is a **gap reviewer** that did not do the research: it sees the output, the grid and the ledger, plays a skeptical specialist in the unit's culture, and lists what is missing; its list is run as new searches. The author's earlier rule (never one agent grading its own work) applies.
8. **Measure the miss rate directly (the calibration core).** On a few Cascadia concepts, run a second agent blind on the same concept and unit and compare the two source sets (capture and recapture: if the second finds a large share the first missed, the first was shallow, and the size of that share estimates how much both still miss).
9. **A depth dose-response test (also Phase 0).** Run the same two or three concept-unit jobs at increasing depth (for example 15, 30, 60 and 120 searches or until saturation) and compare new findings per search. The point where the curve flattens sets the floor for real jobs; floors are then set above it with a margin, and the stop test (item 1) keeps jobs going past the floor when findings still arrive.
10. **Work is written to files as it goes** (checkpoints), so depth is not capped by the agent's context window; a job that runs out resumes from its ledger and queue rather than starting over. Sessions are limited to about 1,000 searches (confirmed 2026-11-10), so steps start in fresh sessions; the schedule has to allow for that cap.
11. **Smaller jobs go deeper.** Keep the one concept x one unit shape; if a job's grid is large, split it by branch. Test whether a higher effort level for research agents gives a measurable gain on the same jobs; since token cost is not a concern, adopt it wherever it helps.
12. **Guard against gaming the measures.** Quotas on searches or "findings not in the springboard" are diagnostics only (a target gets padded). The checks that cannot be gamed cheaply are the independent gap reviewer, the blind second agent and the author's spot-check.

**Priorities (author, 2026-11-16):** the highest-quality and most-accurate results possible come before everything else. **Token cost is not a concern**; no mechanism here is dropped, shortened or thinned to save tokens, and a mechanism is kept whenever it shows any gain in depth or accuracy. The two limits that remain are outside our control: the weekly allotment (resets Saturday 23:00) and the session search cap (about 1,000). **Rule: when a limit is reached, the work stops and resumes from its files; it is never cut short to fit.** The schedule bends around the limits, never the depth.

**Scope of these specifications (author, 2026-11-16):** the depth and accuracy mechanisms, the "cost is not a concern" ruling and the day estimates apply **only to the actual full research** (Phase 0 onward). They do **not** apply to the springboard survey now under way, which keeps its own rules (the cheap-shape process, the schedule and cost ledger in `_dispatch/SPRINGBOARD_PROGRESS.md`).

**When a limit is reached (author, 2026-11-16):**
1. **Search cap (about 1,000 per session) or weekly allotment:** stop at a clean point and **log the full research progress first**: every job's status, the ledger and lead queue files up to date, the next job and the exact step to resume, and the files touched.
2. **Tell the author** in plain words that a limit was hit, which one, and where the work stands.
3. **The author starts a new session** (fresh search quota) **or waits for the weekly reset**, and asks Claude to continue.
4. **Claude picks up exactly where it left off**, from the logged state (the progress file, each job's STATUS line, ledger and lead queue), not from memory and not by restarting any job. Nothing is cut short to fit a limit, and nothing is started that cannot be logged before the limit.

**Accuracy, as its own set of checks** (on top of the depth mechanisms; proposed):
- **Two independent sources** for every claim that matters, and the claim marked single-source where only one exists.
- **Every finding carries its source** (title, author, date, where it was seen) and a confidence note; nothing recalled from the agent's own memory enters a result unmarked (the springboard sheets are exactly that kind of memory).
- **Every output is verified by a different agent** (Stage 7): a sample of claims is re-found at the source and compared with what was written; failures are traced to cause and the output reworked, not patched.
- **Contradictions are kept, not smoothed:** where sources disagree, both are recorded with their reasons.
- **Quotations and figures are re-read at the source** before they are used.
- **The author's spot-check** of a few outputs against what the author already knows, repeated at the audits.

**Cannot be promised:** completeness. The checks make shallow stops visible and give a measured estimate of what is still missing.

## Per-nation pipeline (what every nation goes through)
Springboard (done or queued) -> Stage 0 scope (author accepts) -> Stage 1 lineage profiles -> Stage 2 measured data -> Stage 3 five axes -> Stage 4 signs (12, one concept x one unit) -> Stage 5 composites -> Stage 6 divergence -> Stage 7 verification (a gate on every output). **The author's expectation (2026-11-16): about one to three days of research per culture**, depending on context, circumstance and historical complexity. I take this as the working target. It is a **planning figure to test in Phase 0, not a cap on depth**: if a culture needs more to reach the quality bar, it gets more. Cascadia (11 units, 12 signs) is the extreme and will very likely exceed three days; Phase 0 measures it. Rough arithmetic: the master tracker has 84 targets, or 92 counting the nine Sinian sub-units; at 1 to 3 days each that is 92 to 276 working days, about 15 to 46 weeks at six days a week, so roughly four to eleven months. Each nation's days are fixed at its Stage 0 scope (more units, more days).
