# Jeju-do — Neutral Ground and the Language-Precedence Rule

2026-09-23. **Author-stated canon.** Recorded here rather than in `TepenianUniverseTimeline` per the standing
rule that nothing enters that repo without an explicit instruction naming it.

**This file carries only what is new.** The Court's founding, its function, the April 27 2318 robot-personhood
ruling and Gyeong-ja Yun's case are already documented in
`TepenianUniverseTimeline/Reference/World_History_Reference.md` — read that, do not restate it here.

---

## The two new facts

**1. Jeju-do is a three-power condominium.** Upon the founding and establishment of the International Court of
Diplomacy at Jeju-do, **Korea delegated Jeju-do as neutral ground, to be legally shared among itself, Japan,
and the Sinian Federation.** The Timeline repo already records that Korea *submitted* Jeju-do as neutral
territory; what is new is that the arrangement is a **shared legal holding among three named powers**, not
merely a venue Korea provided.

**2. Korean is the language of precedence.** Korean, Japanese and Mandarin are **equally valid before the
Law**. But **where a conflict-in-translation arises, the Korean text takes precedence** — explicitly modeled
on the role English plays in present-day international instruments.

---

## Why the second fact is worth more than it looks

**It is a quiet asymmetry inside a symmetrical arrangement**, and asymmetries like that are where institutional
stories come from.

Three powers are equal before the Court, and the Court sits on ground all three share. But the ground was
*Korea's* to give, and the language that settles ambiguity is *Korean*. Korea therefore keeps two advantages
it never has to assert: **it is the host, and it owns the tiebreaker.** Neither shows up in any statement of
the Court's principles, and both are decisive in any hard case.

**Every consequential ruling the Court makes is, at the margin, decided in Korean.** That includes the 2318
robot-personhood ruling — the single most important legal event in the setting's history. A Japanese or
Sinian jurist arguing that case is arguing in a second language against a text that will be read in a first.

⚠️ **This is an observation about what the canon implies, not additional canon.** The author stated the rule;
the reading of what it does is offered for acceptance or rejection.

---

## Consequence for the maps

**Any map set after the Court's founding must show Jeju-do in its own color**, distinct from Korea, Japan and
the Sinian Federation. Before the Court's founding it is ordinary Korean territory.

This creates a **fourth map in the East Asia series**. The specification is in
`../nations/east-asia-map-data/INSTRUCTIONS - Map Revisions and the Early-Era Map.md`, Part 3; the data row is
`2J` in `../nations/east-asia-map-data/regions-main.csv`.

**Jeju is a first-order subdivision** — Jeju Special Self-Governing Province — so drawing it out of Korea does
not break the whole-subdivision rule.

### The window where the unified map is correct without it

From the timing rulings already recorded in the Timeline repo:

| Event | Bound |
|---|---|
| Korea's reunification | ≥3, possibly 4 generations before 2318 |
| The Sinian Federation's founding | ≥3, possibly 4 generations before 2318 — the same bound |
| The Court already established | ≥1 full generation before 2318 |
| The robot-personhood ruling | April 27, 2318 |

**So there is a stretch of roughly two generations in which Korea is unified, the Federation exists, and the
Court does not yet.** The existing late-era nations map sits in that window and is correct as drawn.

## Open

- ⚠️ **The Court's founding date** is bounded but not pinned — the Timeline repo says ≥1 generation before
  2318 and no more.
- ⚠️ **Whether the three-power condominium survives** the Falkland Treaty and the War of Upper Earth. The
  Treaty strips the 2318 ruling; whether it touches Jeju's legal status is not recorded anywhere.
- ⚠️ **What the arrangement cost Korea, and why it agreed.** Submitting sovereign territory as neutral ground
  is a significant concession, and no motive is recorded.
- **Whether the language rule was contested.** A precedence clause favoring the host is the kind of term the
  other two powers would have had to accept deliberately.
