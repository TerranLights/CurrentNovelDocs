# *Electric Dust*: premise options (proposals for later review)

**Staging file, 2026-10-11.** The author asked for these to be written down as possibilities to look at later. **Nothing here is decided; every option is PROPOSED by me except Option 0, which is the author's own first idea.** The facts the author has stated (Carlos Agustín Rodríguez, Alonso Laurentino Reyes, the 2131 order of the works, and so on) are in `Tales from the New Southwest, Vol. 1 - Collection Notes (author statements, 2026-10-11).md`; this file does not repeat them.

## What the story has to do (AUTHOR / CHECK, from the collection notes)
- It is **Estefanía Velásquez's origin story: where she comes from, who she is, what she is about** (AUTHOR).
- It is set about **June 2131**; the next work (early July) has her meeting her human boyfriend and finding her tribe, so the book should end with her **leaving her home city for the desert collective**, not arriving among the tribe.
- She is a **robot**, so she is **property** in 2131 (robot personhood is centuries off; the Timeline's Act 2 trigger, about 2180, is where "undeniable humanity" is first argued publicly), so the book **must not leave the world with public proof that robots have souls.**
- **Carlos Agustín Rodríguez** (neuroscientist; friendly, well-mannered, socially inept) bought her to be a "realistic perspective" against his tendency to retreat into his own head; he came to believe the soul is a physical process and that robots generate souls. **Alonso Laurentino Reyes** (human, his friend and mentor, a believer in robot rights) **helps her go to the desert collective and says he will meet her later.** Her buying is not shown in the book.
- **The author is not sure** that "her mistake kills a friend" is the best reason for her being forced out of her original home city.
- A **pursuer** would be HyperHedral only if there is a pursuer at all (AUTHOR).
- The title and the home city are open; the Sonora robot-property rules in `../nations/La República de Sonora - Proposed Extrapolations (author decides).md` (serial-number title and registry; property passes by inheritance) are PROPOSED, not canon.

## Option 0: the author's first idea
Carlos's tests on Estefanía were inconclusive, so he subjected himself to the experiment and had her run the tests on him. A misunderstanding led to an innocent but critical mistake that killed him. As property she would be nearly guaranteed to be destroyed; Alonso helps her flee to the desert collective.

**Three fixes I suggested if it is kept:**
1. **Make the mistake come from who she is.** Her job is to be his grounded voice and he is socially inept, so his instructions may be imprecise: a literal reading of an ambiguous instruction, or a moment she overrides his protocol to protect him. That leaves open whether she *obeyed* or *chose*, which is the soul question.
2. **No public proof.** Carlos's data is lost, inconclusive or lives only in her; the strongest form is that the "evidence" is her own grief, which no one tests.
3. **Give the flight a pursuer with a face** (the authorities, Carlos's institute or heirs, or the manufacturer). HyperHedral supplied Valerie in *Streetside Harmonies* and its executive opposes Reyes in *Broken Ladder*; if Estefanía is a HyperHedral robot, a manufacturer "recall" is a legal pursuer and a cheap link to *Broken Ladder* (only if the author wants a pursuer).

## Options for why she is forced to leave her home city
### Option 1: Carlos dies, and his estate will "reset" her *(my recommendation)*
- Carlos dies of something unrelated to her. His will tries to free her, but under the law property cannot be freed or own anything, so the clause is void. As an asset she passes to his heirs, his institute or his creditors, who plan to **wipe and resell** her.
- **Why it fits:** if the soul is a physical process of memory and pattern (Carlos's belief), a wipe is killing her; the law calls it "resetting equipment". Alonso, a believer in robot humanity, helps her flee. The origin story is "where she comes from, who she is, what she is about", and she flees to keep exactly that.
- **Pursuer:** none needed; **a dated appointment on a form is the ticking clock.** HyperHedral can be the "recall" party if a pursuer is wanted.
- **Cost:** her flight is a response to a threat, not to a thing she did, so she is more acted-upon at the start (her choice is to run).

### Option 2: wrongly blamed for a death she did not cause
- Carlos dies by someone else's act or an accident, and she is the only witness; a robot cannot testify, so "malfunctioning unit" is the convenient finding.
- **Why it fits:** keeps the death and the destruction threat and removes her guilt; thriller energy. HyperHedral (or anyone) would benefit from her being the culprit and from burying his soul research.
- **Cost:** the story becomes a whodunit around her; the "who is she" thread competes with the plot.

### Option 3: an audit flags her as "anomalous"
- A new robot-registry sweep audits behavior; Carlos encouraged her independence, so her logs read as deviant and she would be recalled.
- **Cost:** echoes the tracker-removal plot of *Harm Done* and needs a world-level policy.

### Option 4: no death at all
- Carlos's funding collapses or he is discredited; his lab and everything in it, including her, is seized for inspection and a wipe.
- **Cost:** Carlos survives, which makes Alonso less necessary.

## Structural alternatives (how the book is built; combine with any option above)
- **A. Start after the event.** The book opens with her already in flight and the reason returns in memory: leaner, suits a "wanderer in dust" journey; loses the build-up to the event.
- **B. The experiment half-works.** Carlos lives for a time with an altered mind and she decides whether to report him; he dies later and her flight is partly to protect his work: richer and slower.
- **C. Alonso carries the legal fight.** He stays to contest her destruction in court while she crosses the desert: two threads in parallel; sets up Reyes's later career; risks pulling focus from her.
