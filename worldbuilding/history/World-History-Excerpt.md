## The War of Upper Earth (~2563–2564)

Ended with the signing of the Falkland Treaty, June 21, 2564. Upper Earth won; the anti-robot side prevailed.
The robot population was too large to exterminate outright — the logistics weren't viable — so the Falkland
Treaty negotiated exile to Antarctica instead. **The Treaty was the terms of their defeat, not a mutual
compromise.** It established the Federation of Tepenia as a formal country in Antarctica, creating the legal
and geographic framework for the exile population. The 2083 nuclear war (480 years earlier) is the deep
foundational rupture whose reconstruction-era politics, power structures, and grievances ultimately produced
the conditions for the War of Upper Earth — the countries/factions involved in 2564 all trace their roots back
that far.

## The Falkland Treaty

Signed June 21, 2564, at Stanley, Falkland Islands.

**Signatories:** the United Assembly of Nations (Upper Earth's own future-era UN-equivalent, near-universal in
membership) against Maggie Aarden, negotiating on behalf of the exiled robot population — confirmed as robot
signatory 2026-07-09. Confirmed member nations of the Assembly so far: Unified Korea, the Sinian Federation,
the Sovereign Republic of Hawaii, Midwestland, the Colorado Republic, the People's Democratic Republic of
Cascadia, the Appalachia Republic, the Prairie Federation, the Republic of Sonora, the Republic of Alaska, and
the People's Democratic Republic of New England — eight of these are named successor-states to the former
United States. Also referenced on the era's own maps: Greenland, Quebec, and a southern polity tentatively
labeled "the CSA" (name flagged as likely to change).

**In-world present-day echoes:** the Treaty Archive Vaults (Libra district, Concordia), holographic Treaty
seals on display. Two factions care about it directly — the Veilkeepers (obsessed with suppressed information
about "why the evacuation happened") and the Treaty Scholars (study its long-term consequences).

**Still open, even in the parent timeline repo:** the exact composition of the Assembly's negotiating
delegation; what "the evacuation" actually was and what was suppressed about it.

## Robot Legal Personhood — the 246-Year Window That Closes at the Treaty

From 2318 (the Jeju-do ruling) to the Falkland Treaty (June 21, 2564) — 246 years — robots had full legal
personhood in Upper Earth. The Falkland Treaty effectively stripped this, exiling robots from Upper Earth
entirely. This is the legal backdrop Maggie's entire adult life plays out against: she's activated into a world
where robot personhood is an established, generation-old fact, and lives to see it revoked at the Treaty she's
forced to sign.

**Human allies** aren't stripped of personhood by the Treaty directly — a human who elects to go into exile
with the robots is instead treated, under Upper Earth's own law, as having *voluntarily and permanently
forfeited* their personhood. Same practical outcome (barred from returning), different legal mechanism
(individual election vs. blanket revocation for robots).

## Ji-Eun Kim (a confirmed parallel/convergence thread — see also `../../Convergence-Threads.md`)

From Unified Korea. Fought her way to the top of her field as a robot nanotech engineer during the period of
extreme anti-robot sentiment immediately before the War of Upper Earth. Exiled to Antarctica under the Falkland
Treaty's blanket robot provision — "she was not named in the treaty... she was exiled because she is a robot."

## Founding of the Federation of Tepenia

Established June 21, 2564, by the Falkland Treaty, to receive the robots and human allies exiled from Upper
Earth. The ~124-year span between the Treaty and Amundsen Tower's completion (~2688) covers, in order: (1)
founding and building out the Federation's cities, (2) subnet-internal highway networks, (3) the Arcanet
extended subnet by subnet, (4) construction of Hwy 22, the Transcontinental Highway. Amundsen Tower
construction proper only began once Hwy 22 reached the South Pole, plausibly ~2671–2676 — the preceding decades
of nation-building, not Tower construction itself, account for most of that span. This is the world Maggie
signs into being and then watches get built, in exile, for the remainder of her post-Treaty life.
