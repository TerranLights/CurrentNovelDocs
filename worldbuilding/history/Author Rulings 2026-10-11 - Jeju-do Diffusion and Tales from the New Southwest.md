# Author rulings, 2026-10-11: how Jeju-do spreads, and *Tales from the New Southwest, Vol. 1*

Staging note (this repo's `worldbuilding/` is not canon until promoted; never write these to the Timeline repo unprompted). Stated by the author in conversation; recorded here once so the country files can point at it.

## 1. The Jeju-do ruling of 2318 does not take effect everywhere at once
- It is **binding law in Korea, Japan and the Sinian Federation** from 2318.
- Its effects reach the other countries with robot populations only **after roughly a generation or two** (the author's estimate: approximately, "around" that long). Working reading: personhood arrives in most other countries somewhere around **2320 to 2380**, not in 2318 itself; the exact year differs by country and is open.
- **Consequence:** "robots are property before 2318" is the safe lower bound only for Korea, Japan and the Sinian Federation. Everywhere else, robots stay property until the ruling's effects arrive (a generation or two after 2318). Any story set in a New North America nation up to roughly the mid-2300s should be checked against this.

## 2. *Tales from the New Southwest, Vol. 1* and *Electric Dust*
- ***Electric Dust*** (Estefanía Velásquez, a robot; Republic of Sonora) is part of the collection ***Tales from the New Southwest, Vol. 1*** ("TftNSW, V.1").
- The collection is **set in 2131**. That is **18 years before *Streetside Harmonies*** (2149), although the collection is written later.
- *Electric Dust* is Estefanía's origin story; other works follow her later progress elsewhere in Arizona; water is not its plot (see the Sonora files).

## 3. A cross-work reference that is now internally consistent
- In **2131** there is a **tribe of robots and humans living communally together in Arizona**.
- A character in *Streetside Harmonies* (2149, Cascadia) can therefore refer, in passing, to **"those hippies in the desert"**: an in-world reference to that Arizona commune, 18 years on.
