# Unit territories

Each unit's counties, census divisions or *municipios*, recorded once so that every target sharing a unit uses the same lines. Whole counties and census divisions only. A target that shares a unit adds its own lobe as a new row; it doesn't redraw an existing one.

**How the lines were drawn (Cascadia).** Woodard's lines were read from the p. 13 map of *American Nations* (2011), rendered at 500 dpi. The map is at book scale, so Hood River and Yolo were uncertain; the author accepted the map reading for both (2026-11-02, decision 14). The top of the page (northern British Columbia and the Panhandle) is covered by a blank band in this copy of the book, and Canada carries no internal divisions on the map, so British Columbia and the Panhandle are assigned from Woodard's text.

## Indigenous units

Indigenous units are culture areas, not county sets, so their territories are given as peoples and homelands, not census geography (Cascadia scope, section 2). Population by culture area isn't computable from census geography; Stage 2 can build it from tribal and First Nations registries.
