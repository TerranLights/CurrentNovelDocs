# Africa — country-borders decision log

**Started 2026-10-03.** The author is going through Africa one subregion at a time to decide where the new country borders get drawn. **The author decides every border; research proposes.** This file records only what the author has settled, so each later session starts from it. Nothing here is projected or invented on my own: an entry exists only when the author said so.

Working inputs for each subregion: the subsection's cultural regions (`subsections/Africa (<zone>)/01 initial generations/`), the continental maps and `full-continent_map/01 initial generations/map-data/zone-seams.csv` (cultures that cross a subsection line), and today's national borders as the reference baseline.

## The author's rule for "current-day terms" (2026-10-03)
"Stays as-is in current-day terms" does **not** mean a snapshot of today's political events. It means: **what has some place `[Place-X]` generally tended to be throughout its own history, and its history in relation to its neighbors.** A border decision is judged by that long-run tendency (state continuity, core and periphery, which neighbors it has been tied to or separate from), with current events as supporting evidence only. (So a 2026 event such as the Azawad Liberation Front taking Kidal is a datum, not a baseline.)

## Order of subregions
All six subsections are decided: Northwest-North, Northwest-South, North-Center, Northeast, Central and South (Madagascar is part of South). The island states (the Comoros, Mauritius, the Seychelles, Sao Tome and Principe) are not yet mapped and are not yet in this process.

## Settled

### South: Madagascar — confirmed 2026-10-03
Madagascar stays exactly as it is in current-day terms: one country, its present borders, nothing added or removed. (Its 13 cultural regions, numbers 37 to 49 in South, are inside that one country and do not become separate countries.)

### South: all eleven countries stay as they are — decided 2026-10-04
After the briefing (`subsections/Africa (South)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders briefing - South.md`: each country's case for splitting, staying and combining, the cross-border pieces and a proposed order) the author decided: **"if there are no valid, practical reasons for changing anything, then all of these countries stay as-is"**. So **Angola, Namibia, Botswana, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Malawi, Mozambique, South Africa, Lesotho and Eswatini all stay as they are**, together with Madagascar (already settled). No new arrangement was made: the Southern African Customs Union, the Common Monetary Area (the rand) and SADC already link several of them. Cabinda, the Lunda east, Barotseland, Matabeleland, the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal were weighed and left as they are. Map: `map-5-country-borders.png` in the South follow-up folder.

### Northwest-North — confirmed 2026-10-03
**Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Mauritania stay as they are in current-day terms.** (Morocco's present boundary is taken here *without* the Western Sahara question: the Moroccan units that hold Moroccan-administered Western Sahara are decided together with Western Sahara, below.) Arguments considered and set aside: the Rif and Morocco's Amazigh languages (Morocco); Kabylie, the MAK proclamation of 14 December 2025, the Aurès and the Mzab (Algeria); the coast-interior divide and Jerba (Tunisia); the Senegal river valley, the caste line and the 1989-91 expulsions (Mauritania).

### Northwest-North: Western Sahara and Mali — confirmed 2026-10-03 (under the long-run rule above)
**Western Sahara stays as it is** in current-day terms, and **Mali stays as it is**. For Western Sahara this means: the part administered by Morocco stays as administered today, and the strip east of the Moroccan Wall stays as it is today; **the territory's sovereignty is left open, not settled** (the author's "as-is" leaves it as the present situation). Reasons from the long-run rule: Western Sahara has never been a state and has always been a Hassaniya tribal borderland tied to Morocco's south and to Mauritania; Mali's core is the Sahelian river basin (Ghana, Mali, Songhai, Ségou, Macina, French Soudan), with Gao and Timbuktu part of that heartland. Considered and set aside: a whole-territory Sahrawi country (two unit splits), a strip-only Sahrawi state, and a Kidal-and-Taoudénit "Azawad" (the strongest long-run case for any change, since the Saharan Tuareg sphere runs through Mali's Kidal, Algeria's Ahaggar-Ajjer, Niger's Aïr and Libya's Ghat).

**Northwest-North is therefore complete at country level: all six countries (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Western Sahara, Mauritania, Mali) stay as they are.** Still to be settled with the neighboring subsections (not here): the Tuareg and Tebu lands on the Niger and Libya side (North-Center), the Libya-Tunisia and Libya-Algeria lines (North-Center), and the Senegal river valley on the Senegal and Guinea side (Northwest-South).

### Northwest-South: the Gambia — confirmed 2026-10-03 (options A and B combined)
**The Gambia stays as it is, and the Gambia and Senegal negotiate a trade union.** No border changes: the Gambia is not absorbed into Senegal. The trade union is a political layer between two countries that keep their borders. Reasons (long-run rule): the Gambia has been a river-valley corridor inside a shared Senegambian world, but a separate British then independent unit for two centuries, and every union attempt since 1964 (the Van Mook study, the 1967 Treaty of Association, the 1982 to 1989 Senegambia Confederation) ended in association or separation, with the Gambia refusing closer integration. Design note from the record: Senegal's 1984 customs-union proposal failed because it would have ended the Gambia's re-export trade, so a negotiated trade union has to deal with that. Research: `subsections/Africa (Northwest-South)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders research - Gambia.md`.

### Northwest-South: seven countries — confirmed 2026-10-03
**Ghana, Cabo Verde, Burkina Faso, Guinea, Liberia, Sierra Leone and Senegal stay as they are** in current-day terms. Reasons (long-run rule; arguments in `subsections/Africa (Northwest-South)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders arguments - Northwest-South.md`): Ghana follows the reach of the Asante Empire (1701 to 1902); Cabo Verde is an island Creole society (settled 1462) whose union plan with Guinea-Bissau failed in 1980; Burkina Faso is the Mossi core, restored at the Mossi's own request in 1947; Guinea holds two real historical cores (Futa Jallon and Wassoulou); Liberia (1847) and Sierra Leone (Freetown 1787) have stable, never-contested territory; Senegal is the durable Atlantic core that left the Mali Federation in 1960 (Casamance remains its one seam). With the Gambia (above), eight of Northwest-South's twelve countries are settled.

### Northwest-South: Benin — confirmed 2026-10-03
**Benin stays as it is.** Reasons (research: `borders research - Benin and Nigeria.md`): the Yoruba are only about 12% of Benin (Fon 38.4%, Adja and Mina 15.1%; 2013 census via Wikipedia), about two-thirds of the Plateau department and a plurality in Collines; the south was Gbe-speaking states (Dahomey, an Oyo tributary for about a century, then a raider of Ketu in 1886 and Abeokuta), not a Yoruba country; the Borgu north looks to northern Nigeria and the Fulbe, not to the Yoruba; the national cleavage is north against south; no movement of Beninese Yoruba to join Nigeria was found. A Yoruba-Nago cultural sub-region on the culture map is a separate matter from the border. With this, nine of Northwest-South's twelve countries are settled; open: Cote d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Togo.

### Northwest-South: Togo, Cote d'Ivoire and Guinea-Bissau — confirmed 2026-10-03
**Togo, Cote d'Ivoire and Guinea-Bissau stay as they are, and Togo and Ghana negotiate and agree a trade agreement** (a political arrangement between two countries that keep their borders). Reasons (research notes in the Northwest-South `01 follow-up - INIT` folder): Togo has no pre-colonial state to restore and the Ewe never formed one; there is no pattern of Togolese wanting unity with Ghana (Olympio refused Nkrumah's 1960 union offer; the unification party built a Togolese state), only Ewe kinship across the border, which stays a cultural sphere. Cote d'Ivoire has no older state inside its borders to restore, its secession attempts (Sanwi 1959, Guebie 1970, the 2002 to 2007 partition) never produced a separate state, and its economy ties north and south together. Guinea-Bissau was one Portuguese colony for four centuries, its Kriol and PAIGC legacy hold it together, and the one merger tried (Cape Verde) failed in 1980; no active demand for union with a neighbor was found.

**Northwest-South is complete at country level: all twelve countries stay as they are** (Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Burkina Faso, Cote d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Cabo Verde). Still to settle with other subsections: the Yoruba-Nago and Borgu seams (Benin with Nigeria and Niger, North-Center) and the Liptako (Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger).

## Political arrangements agreed so far (no border change)
- Senegal and the Gambia negotiate a trade union (the Gambia stays as it is).
- Togo and Ghana negotiate and agree a trade agreement.
- Gabon, Equatorial Guinea and the Republic of the Congo agree a closely-tied trade agreement (no merger; the three already share the Central African CFA franc, the central bank BEAC and the CEMAC customs union, so the agreement builds on an existing arrangement).

### North-Center: all five countries stay as they are (Libya, Niger, Nigeria, Chad, Cameroon) — confirmed 2026-10-03
After the comparison of where each country's divides lie against where its people live (`subsections/Africa (North-Center)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders analysis - divide areas vs where people live.md`), the author first read Nigeria and Cameroon as candidates for splitting, explored a series of options for them (below) and then decided that **Libya, Niger, Nigeria and Cameroon all stay as they are**, and then that **Chad stays as it is** too.
- **Why Nigeria and Cameroon stay (author, 2026-10-03):** every option explored (three Nigerian countries along the Niger and Benue, two along the Niger and a meridian, a Southeast with western or northern Cameroon, four countries, the Yoruba or the Igbo and the oil south on their own, and the hybrids) "sounds way more complicated than it's worth". The nearest evidence on people's own leanings pointed the same way: most Nigerians say they feel equally attached to the nation and their ethnic group, and the Hausa-Fulani and Yoruba are the groups least likely to favor breaking Nigeria up (Afrobarometer-based study; the Igbo and the Eastern Region are the exception); no poll was found on whether Cameroon's north wants to join Nigeria.
- **Chad stays as it is** (author: "let's just leave Chad as-is"). Chad was never partitioned since 1960; its north-south fault and the Tebu, Zaghawa and Sara links across the border are left for the neighbors' subsections. The Tebu in Tibesti and Borkou are therefore cut by today's borders, as the Tuareg lands are.
**North-Center is complete at country level, and no border change has been decided anywhere so far**: Madagascar, all of Northwest-North, all of Northwest-South and all of North-Center stay as they are. Map: `map-5-country-borders.png` in `subsections/Africa (North-Center)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/`. Notes for the options explored: `borders work - Nigeria and Cameroon.md` and `map-15 scenario - how it would take shape.md` in `subsections/Africa (North-Center)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/`.

### Northeast: five countries stay as they are; Somalia and Somaliland are "volatile territory" — decided 2026-10-04
After the briefing (`subsections/Africa (Northeast)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders briefing - Northeast.md`: arguments for and against each country, the cross-border pieces, and a proposed order) the author decided:
- **Egypt, Sudan, Ethiopia, Eritrea and Djibouti all stay as they are.** (Hala'ib and Bir Tawil stay as administered; Abyei and Kafia Kingi go to the Central subsection with South Sudan; the Darfur-Chad seam is closed because both stay as they are.)
- **Somalia and Somaliland are marked "volatile territory"** (the author: "probably something like 'volatile territory'"): no border and no sovereignty decision is made for either; they are drawn as administered (Somalia's 13 southern units, Somaliland's one polygon, which includes Sool and Sanaag) and hatched on the map. "Volatile territory" is a status marker like Western Sahara's "as administered, sovereignty open", not a border change.
Map: `map-5-country-borders.png` in the Northeast follow-up folder (hatching marks the volatile territory).

### Central: all eleven countries stay as they are for now — decided 2026-10-04
After the briefing (`subsections/Africa (Central)/02 reorganization/01 follow-up - INIT/borders briefing - Central.md`) and the arguments for splitting, staying and combining (`borders arguments - Central (splitting, staying, combining).md`), the author decided that **Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, the Republic of the Congo, the Central African Republic, South Sudan, the DR Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania all stay as they are for the time being**, and that **the details of an African Union will be worked out later**.
- **Gabon, Equatorial Guinea and the Republic of the Congo** also get a **closely-tied trade agreement** among them (a political layer, not a border change). Basis: no historical proposal to merge the three exists; Gabon and Middle Congo were once federated in French Equatorial Africa (1910 to 1958) but Gabon stayed out of the 1960 Union of Central African Republics, and Equatorial Guinea was a Spanish possession; they already share the CFA franc, the central bank and the CEMAC customs union (UDEAC 1966, Equatorial Guinea from 1983); Gabon and Equatorial Guinea are still settling islands and borders (ICJ judgment of 19 May 2025).
- Abyei, Kafia Kingi and the Ilemi Triangle stay as administered; Zanzibar, Buganda, Katanga, the Kivus, the Kongo kingdom, the Somali north-east of Kenya and the Coast stay cultural regions inside today's states.
Map: `map-5-country-borders.png` in the Central follow-up folder.
