# Confederacy of Intermarium Nations — Membership Order, Decision Log and Open Items (working)

**Opened 2026-09-30.** The running record of the author's decisions about which Eastern European countries join the Confederacy, in what order, and on what terms, plus what is assumed and what is still open.
The background is in `research/Intermarium - Overview and Synthesis (survey).md` (the four membership options A to D; the research proposes, the author disposes) and the earlier answers recorded there
(§6a): the Confederacy is "more than a Union, but less than a Federation"; NATO and the EU have collapsed; membership starts skeletal and absorbs countries over time; Kaliningrad is a member
("independent" within the confederacy, majority Russian, capital Kaliningrad); the capital begins in Lublin and later moves to Katowice. The map is `confederacy-accession-order.png`
(script `render_confederacy_order.py`). **Status marks:** ✅ settled by the author, 🟡 a working reading or assumption, ⏳ deferred, ⚠️ open.

## 1. Settled by the author (2026-09-30)

- ✅ **Poland is the core, and keeps its present-day borders.** The author chose the research's recommended option: Poland's status as the region's greatest power (about 67% of the northern and eastern tier's GDP
  and 43% of its population, ⚠️ research figures) comes from size and economy, not land. This is the "ULB doctrine" (Giedroyc and Mieroszewski: accept the postwar borders, no claim on Lviv or Vilnius) the research
  found to be the one workable basis for a confederacy.
- ✅ **The order in which the Eastern European countries join, working outward from Poland "ring by ring":**
  1. **Lithuania and Kaliningrad** (Kaliningrad is now a country: the former oblast).
  2. **Czechia and Slovakia.**
  3. **Latvia**, then
  4. **Estonia.**
  5. **Belarus, on a restricted basis**: closer to an *observer* than a voting member, **because of Poland's historical mistrust of Russians.**
  6. **Ukraine** (the remaining Ukraine; see `DECISION LOG - Eastern Ukraine and Crimea.md`).
  7. **Hungary.**
  8. **Romania**, then
  9. **Moldova** (decided 2026-09-30: right after Romania; wave 9), then
  10. **Bulgaria.**
  11. **Later: Croatia and Slovenia, on a voting basis** (author, 2026-09-30: "later in the future, Croatia and Slovenia are allowed on a voting basis"),
  12. **then later, Serbia** (which now includes Kosovo), and
  13. **then Montenegro.**
  14. **Greece** (placed here as an assumption: the author said only that Skoprodija joins *after Greece*, so Greece's slot is after Montenegro and before Skoprodija; its voting status is assumed to be full).
  15. **Skoprodija joins last (author, 2026-09-30: "they would join last (after Greece)")**, beginning as an observer; whether and when it gains a vote is open.
  **Bosnia and Herzegovina is strictly an *observer* (author, 2026-09-30), "due to their being an Islamic country in a confederacy of Christian nations"**; no timing given. This replaces the earlier blanket "Balkans on a partial basis" (that phrase was the author's first wording, 2026-09-30, and is now superseded by the specific order above).
- ✅ **Albania is forbidden from joining, by unanimous decision, at every point. A hard block.**
- ✅ **Hungary's border with Romania changes (author, 2026-09-30):** Hungary takes back **part of Transylvania (Erdély)**, land it lost at Trianon (1920). The author: "perhaps not all of it, but some of it." **Székely Land (Harghita, Covasna) and Mureș stay Romanian (author, 2026-09-30): "there's no logistically-sound mechanism for them to rejoin Hungary"** (they have no land link to Hungary). So Hungary's gain, if any, is a **contiguous strip along the present border (the Partium)**; which counties is open (see §4b). **DECIDED 2026-09-30 (revised, supersedes Satu Mare + Bihor only): Hungary takes five Romanian counties: Satu Mare, Bihor, Sălaj, Maramureș and Arad** (about 30,400 km², 12.9% of Romania; whole counties, so the whole-unit rule holds; contiguous with Hungary). **Cluj (Kolozsvár) is not taken: too heavily populated.** **The author also ruled out any Székely Autonomous Zone**: "Romanians would never, ever, ever grant Székely Autonomous status... knowing Romanian culture, it just wouldn't happen" (so no status change either; Székely Land is simply Romanian). The accession map draws the new shape.
- ✅ **Kosovo is Serbia (author, 2026-09-30):** Kosovo is not a separate country in the setting; it is part of Serbia (the Natural Earth Kosovo units, 10,900 km², about 92% Albanian in the research's census figures, ⚠️ the 2024 census had a Serb boycott). Consequences to work through: the Balkan "partial" tier has one fewer country; the Albania block concerns the state of Albania only, unless the author says otherwise (Albanians inside Serbia are then Serbian citizens); Greece's non-recognition of Kosovo fits; Serbia's border is drawn including Kosovo. **Applied to the accession map 2026-09-30 in the `02 follow-up/` working folder** (`confederacy-accession-order.png`; pre-edit copy in `archive 01/`); the cultural-region unit data (`map-data/`) is **not** updated.
- ✅ **North Macedonia is renamed Skoprodija (author, 2026-09-30).** The author's reasoning: modern Macedonian is a Slavic language (the author, who studied Russian and lived in Poland and Serbia, understood everyday speech in Skopje and nearby cities without knowing any Greek), so the people are Slavic, not Hellenic.
  **Etymology (author's):** *Skop-* (Skopje) + *rod* (the common Slavic root for birth, kin, origin, generation, heritage) + *-ija* (the place suffix, like "-land"/"-ia") = **"the Land of the Skopje-born."** **Spelling confirmed by the author (2026-09-30): *Skoprodija*; the English rendering is *Skoprodia*** (the earlier mix of the two was a misspelling).
  **Checks made:** the language claim is sound (modern Macedonian is a South Slavic language, closely related to Bulgarian; the research lists it under "South Slavic eastern"); *rod* and *-ija* are real Slavic elements; the name is new and unattested (no real place is called this). **Why the name works in the setting:** North Macedonia's name is the most contested in the region (Greece objected; the Prespa Agreement of 17 June 2018, in force 12 February 2019, forced "North"; 65 to 68% of Greeks opposed it at the time) and Bulgaria disputes the language and identity; a name that claims neither Hellenic Macedonia nor ancient Macedon removes both objections, which also helps Greece's possible membership.
  ✅ **Pocket rule (author, 2026-09-30):** "there's a tiny pocket of Skoprodians in the Albanian territory, and a tiny pocket of Albanians in the Skoprodian territory. The Skoprodians in the Albanian territory become part of Albania, and the Albanians in the Skoprodian territory become part of Skoprodia." The geometry has exactly these two pockets: **Vevčani** (37.7 km², a Macedonian-majority municipality, ⚠️ fact from memory, detached inside Albanian territory because Struga surrounds it) **goes to Albania**; **Plasnica** (62 km², in the original 12-municipality belt but detached inside Skoprodija) **stays in Skoprodija**. **Correction (2026-09-30, caught by the author):** an earlier render left **Vrapčište** (162 km²) in Skoprodija by mistake, a pale patch in the middle of the belt: the map data spells it "Vrapcište" (plain c) and my script used "Vrapčište", so the name silently failed to match. Fixed; the script now asserts that every belt name exists. After the fix Albania and Skoprodija are each a single contiguous piece (no enclaves or exclaves). The belt that goes to Albania is therefore **Struga, Centar Župa, Debar, Mavrovo and Rostuša, Gostivar, Vrapčište, Bogovinje, Tetovo, Tearce, Želino, Brvenica and Vevčani** (12 municipalities); the map shows it.
  ✅ **The Albanian districts are removed from the country (author, 2026-09-30):** Skoprodija is the Slavic, Macedonian-speaking part only; the Albanian-majority belt in the west (the research names Tetovo, Gostivar, Debar and Struga, plus Skopje's Albanian neighborhoods; 446,245 people, 24.3%, 2021 census) is no longer part of it. ✅ **The Albanian-majority districts go to Albania (author, 2026-09-30).** Albania (blocked from the Confederacy) therefore grows by that belt. Because Natural Earth's municipalities are finer than first-order units, this would be a municipality-level cut (the project has accepted district-level exceptions elsewhere).
  ✅ **Language and identity (author, 2026-09-30):** the author sides with the Bulgarian position: **in the setting there is no separate Macedonian language or nation, and what is called Macedonian is a Bulgarian dialect**; the people of Skoprodija speak Bulgarian but can have their own society and identity, as with Russian-speaking Belarus, Mandarin-speaking Taiwan and French-speaking Quebec (the author's analogies). ⚠️ **Real-world note:** mainstream linguistics treats Macedonian as a distinct standard language in a South Slavic dialect continuum with Bulgarian, and the research itself says "language versus dialect" disputes here "are really border disputes"; this is a worldbuilding decision, recorded as the author's, not a finding.
  **Drawn on the accession map 2026-09-30:** Skoprodija minus **12 western municipalities** (Struga, Centar Župa, Debar, Mavrovo and Rostuša, Gostivar, Vrapčište, Bogovinje, Tetovo, Tearce, Želino, Brvenica, Plasnica; the same 12 the cultural-region data assigns to the Albanian Lands), which are added to Albania. ⚠️ **Other Albanian-plurality municipalities are NOT in the cut** and stay in Skoprodija (from memory, to verify against the 2021 census by municipality): **Lipkovo, Saraj, Aračinovo, Zajas, Oslomej, Studeničani and Čair** (a Skopje municipality); Skopje city's Albanian neighborhoods are also not cut. Whether to include them is open.
**Still open:** the capital is Skopje (assumed); Cyrillic form (Скопродија, ⚠️ my transliteration); whether Bulgaria accepts it (see §6).

- ✅ **Borders:** no national border on this list changes except (a) eastern Ukraine (the proposed line, other log), (b) Kaliningrad becoming a country, and (c) whatever the author later decides country by country.
  Present-day borders are the default and only *changes* need deciding.

## 2. Working readings 🟡 (my interpretation of the author's words; to confirm)

- 🟡 **"Partial" (the Balkans), superseded:** the author's later answer gave Croatia, Slovenia, Serbia and Montenegro full voting membership in a specific order and Bosnia observer status. **Skoprodija (the former North Macedonia, i.e. what is left after the Albanian communities are repartitioned to Albania) joins last, after Greece, beginning as an observer (author, 2026-09-30); whether it ever gains full voting rights, and when, is OPEN** ("I'm not sure if they gain full voting rights, and if they do, when"). The accession map draws it as an observer with a "may upgrade" legend entry.
- ✅ **Slovenia and Croatia follow the later voting track with the Balkans (wave 11)**, not the Central European track beside Hungary (the author placed them after Bulgaria).
- 🟡 **The order is read as a sequence over time, not a single moment.** No years are implied; the research's note is that unions which lasted began narrow and widened, which this order does.
- 🟡 **Accession by unanimity** follows from "it is unanimously determined" (the Albania block). This is a rule for *admitting* members, which is common (the EU admits by unanimity); it is **not** a decision to use unanimity for everyday decisions,
  which the research warns is the commonest killer of confederations (a weak center plus a veto). That second question is open.

## 4a2. Czechia and Slovakia ✅ decided: present-day borders (author, 2026-09-30)

- ✅ **Czechia and Slovakia keep their present-day borders (wave 2, two separate countries).** **Hungary's border with Slovakia is unchanged** as well.
- **The author's reasoning (2026-09-30, after looking at the districts on Google Maps):** the two Hungarian-majority districts, **Dunajská Streda and Komárno**, are "extremely close to the Slovakian capital"; "based on the geological geography, it actually wouldn't make sense to incorporate these two as Hungarian territory unless Hungary retook half of Slovakia, which is simply not realistically feasible." (Dunajská Streda is very close to Bratislava; Komárno is farther along the Danube, about 100 km from it, ⚠️ my estimate.) **The author's further point on Komárno (2026-09-30):** it is not a matter of distance from Bratislava but of **(1) the shape of the district and (2) the river: the Danube, the border itself, lies between the district and Hungary, so the district is on the far side of it.** A transfer would therefore not be a tidy, contiguous adjustment but would cut across the natural boundary. The author noted they would personally like Hungary to recover all its historical territory, but that "it just very simply wouldn't make sense within the context of the world." This differs from the Partium decision (§4b), which is a contiguous strip of border counties far from Romania's core, because here the Hungarian-majority land is a thin river strip hugging the capital.
- **The evidence behind it** is in `Slovakia - Hungarians by District, 2021 Census (research).md`: only those two districts are Hungarian-majority (68.7% and 61.6%), about 147,750 Hungarians, and the wider Hungarian belt of mixed districts (19% to 32%) is nowhere a majority.
- **The smaller Czech seams stay at present-day borders by default** (Cieszyn Silesia with Poland, Kladsko, Sudeten memory with Germany, the Rusyns in eastern Slovakia); none needed a decision.
- **Consequence for the maps:** nothing changes on the accession map. The Hungarian hatching on the language map stays as the region-level approximation (Nitra and Trnava); a district-level redraw is optional.

## 4b. Hungary's Transylvania ✅ decided: Satu Mare, Bihor, Sălaj, Maramureș and Arad

**2026-09-30 follow-up (author asked "how far into Romania" Hungary can reclaim):** real 2021 county figures are in `Romania - Hungarians by County, 2021 Census (research).md`. Headline: the two decided counties hold about 882,000 people, about 227,000 Hungarian (26%) and about 583,000 Romanian (66%); every county beyond them adds more Romanians than Hungarians and crosses the Apuseni/Meseș mountain edge, or (Arad) adds almost no Hungarians. **The author then extended it (2026-09-30) to five counties: Satu Mare, Bihor, Sălaj, Maramureș, Arad. Cluj/Kolozsvár is excluded as too populous.** Size: about 30,400 km² (12.9% of Romania's area; Hungary's own area rises by about a third). Population: about 1.96 million, using the 2021 figures for the three added counties (Arad about 410,000, Maramureș about 452,000, Sălaj about 212,000) plus the 882,000 above.

**The author's reasoning (from personal field experience in Romania, while approaching the question from Romanian nationalism):** ethnic Romanians in Transylvania could not trace their families in the same place beyond about two generations; their grandparents came from the far south or far east through a Bucharest-run resettlement program; everyone who could trace lineage there three or four generations back was Hungarian. So the transfer is framed as **Hungary "repatriating" ethnic Romanians** (from places like Arad and Baia Mare) to wherever their families came from. ⚠️ This is the author's setting logic and anecdote, recorded as such. It is not in the research, and it runs against the 2021 census shares in the added counties (Arad 77.5% Romanian, Maramureș 84.7%, Sălaj 70.1%; Hungarians 9.6%, 7.1% and 20.8% or so, ⚠️ from memory for the first two). The map does not depend on it, but any prose that cites it should present it as a character's or the Confederacy's claim.

**1910 versus 2021 check (2026-09-30, at the author's request; full table in `Romania - Hungarians by County, 2021 Census (research).md`):** the Romanian majority in Arad, Sălaj and Cluj was already there in 1910 (56% to 59% by mother tongue, and higher in 1880 in Arad and Sălaj), so "only two generations old" is not supported there; only Satu Mare and Bihor were Hungarian-majority in 1910. What did change is that Hungarians and Germans fell sharply and Romanians rose by about 13,000 to 115,000 per county (roughly 15% to 25% of a county's people), which is the real migration effect and fits the cities best. ⚠️ Different measures (1910 mother tongue against 2021 ethnicity) and historic counties that differ from the modern ones.

**The native-family right to stay (author, 2026-09-30):** any ethnic Romanian who can **conclusively prove their family has lived in the county for more than six generations** has a natural-born right to stay. The author counts six generations as the span from the early 2100s, which at 25 to 30 years a generation reaches back to about 1920 to 1950 (⚠️ my arithmetic): roughly the Trianon line and the interwar and communist resettlements. Given the 1910 figures, **most Romanians in Arad, Sălaj and Maramureș could in principle qualify**, so the rule probably makes the "repatriation" a limited one, with the proof of descent (parish registers, land books, the 1910 census) doing the sorting. **Status and schooling (author, 2026-09-30):** those who stay are **Hungarian citizens/residents**. **Romanian-language schooling must be privately funded and receives no Hungarian public money.** **Who adjudicates (author, 2026-09-30): a joint Hungarian-Romanian commission decides the proof of descent.**

**Open consequences of the five-county version (none answered):** (1) where the "repatriated" Romanians go, and whether that is by compulsion or choice; (2) whether the Ukrainian minority of Maramureș (about 6%) matters; (3) what this does to Romania's wave-8 accession, since Romania has now lost 13% of its territory and about 1.96 million people, most of them Romanians; (4) whether Hungary and Romania can sit in one confederacy on unanimity after this.

The research numbers (2021 census, `Intermarium - 3b`): Romania's Hungarians are 1,002,151 (6.0%); **Harghita 85.67% Hungarian (232,157) and Covasna 73.74% (150,468)** are the Székely Land core; **Mureș 38.09% (200,858)**; Hungarian-majority or large-minority pockets also sit in Satu Mare, Bihor and Sălaj (⚠️ those counties' shares are not in the notes, from memory). Reformed 45.3%, Catholic 40.4% of Hungarian Romanians.
**Narrowed 2026-09-30: options 1 and 3 (anything taking the Székely counties) are out; Székely Land remains Romanian.** Options, cheapest to most ambitious (each a whole-county unit; county areas from Natural Earth: Harghita 6,576 km², Covasna 3,686, Mureș 6,544, Satu Mare 4,684, Bihor 7,618, Sălaj 3,858, Arad 7,815, Timiș 8,856, Cluj 6,640, Maramureș 6,440):
1. **The Székely counties only** (Harghita + Covasna): about 10,300 km², about 0.4 million people, a Hungarian-majority block that does not touch Hungary by land (an enclave or exclave, or a corridor is needed).
2. **The Partium** (the western counties on the border: Satu Mare, Bihor, and perhaps Sălaj and Arad): contiguous with Hungary, about 16,000 to 24,000 km², mixed Hungarian-Romanian.
3. **Partium plus Székely Land with a corridor through Mureș/Cluj:** the largest "some of it," joins the two; takes in large Romanian-majority areas.
4. ~~An Autonomous Zone instead of a transfer~~ **ruled out by the author (2026-09-30):** Romania would never grant it.
The research's costs: Romanian opposition to any revision (autonomy statute rejected six times, 2026), Magyar's own 2026 stance (he acknowledged Transylvania as Romanian territory), and that a transfer makes Romania (wave 8) an unwilling member.

## 6. Why Bulgaria may not accept "Skoprodija" (asked by the author, 2026-09-30) ⚠️

The dispute is about **language and identity, not the word "Macedonia."** The research (`Intermarium - 3b` §5.6, `Europe - 6` §2.4) records: Bulgaria holds that there is **no separate Macedonian language or nation** (Macedonian is "a Bulgarian dialect"); North Macedonia rejects that, as do most non-Bulgarian linguists. The history behind it (Bulgarian side from memory, ⚠️):
- **The Exarchate (1870):** the Bulgarian Orthodox Exarchate drew many Slavic Christians in Macedonia into a Bulgarian church and schools; Bulgarians treat the region as part of their national revival (research: "Exarchate 1870").
- **The 1878 settlements (⚠️ memory):** the Treaty of San Stefano briefly put most of Macedonia in a "Greater Bulgaria," reversed at the Congress of Berlin the same year; Bulgaria has never fully given up that memory.
- **The Balkan Wars (1912-13):** Macedonia was partitioned three ways (the research: Greece about 51%, Serbia about 38%, Bulgaria about 10%; Vardar to Serbia, Aegean to Greece, Pirin to Bulgaria). Bulgaria lost the Second Balkan War and most of what it wanted.
- **1944-45:** Yugoslav Macedonia was created and its language codified (the standard written in August 1944 and its alphabet in May 1945, from the west-central Bitola, Prilep and Veles dialects), deliberately different from Bulgarian. Bulgaria was the first state to recognize the republic's independence in 1992 (⚠️ memory) but **not its nation or language**.
- **2017 to 2022:** a friendship treaty (August 2017, ratified January 2018) promised a joint history commission; Bulgaria **vetoed the start of EU accession talks in November 2020**; the 2022 French proposal required North Macedonia to write Bulgarians into its constitution and **failed at 68 votes of the two thirds needed**; and in July 2026 relations were described as "severed."
- **Bulgaria's own minority question:** in **Pirin Macedonia** (Blagoevgrad Oblast), only **1,143 people** declared themselves Macedonian in 2021; the party of the Macedonian minority, OMO Ilinden-Pirin, was banned in 2000 and the European Court of Human Rights ruled against Bulgaria in 2018.

**What this means for "Skoprodija."** The name has two sides for Bulgaria: (a) it **drops "Macedonia"**, which removes Greece's objection and also removes the word Bulgarian nationalists contest (Macedonia as the Bulgarian heartland of the Exarchate), so Bulgaria could see it as a concession; (b) it **names a separate, new nation defined by the Skopje-born**, which still asserts a distinct identity apart from Bulgarian and does not address the language question, which is the actual Bulgarian objection. The author's own argument (the people are Slavic and speak a Slavic language) is close to the Bulgarian argument that they are Slavs close to Bulgarians, but the conclusion differs: the author grants a separate country with a separate name.

**Update after the author's answer (2026-09-30):** because the author's setting accepts the Bulgarian position on language, Bulgaria's central objection is **met**; what remains is that Skoprodija keeps a separate identity and state. Bulgaria's veto over wave-10 admission is therefore much less likely to be used against it.

**The mechanic in the setting:** under the membership order (the author's "unanimously determined" rule for admission), **Bulgaria is a full member in wave 10, before Skoprodija would join in wave 11, so Bulgaria holds a veto over Skoprodija's admission.** That is exactly what happened in real life (the 2020 veto). Skoprodija's partial status is consistent with that. Not decided.
