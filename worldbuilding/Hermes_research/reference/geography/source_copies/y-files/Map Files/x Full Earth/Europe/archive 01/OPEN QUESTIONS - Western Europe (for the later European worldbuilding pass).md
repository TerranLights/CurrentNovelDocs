## Brussels: the earlier notes (superseded in part)
**Superseded 2026-09-30:** the author later reinstated the corridor and a French/Dutch divided city, so the "no corridor, fully French exclave" description below is the earlier state. The treaty items listed still apply to either version.
Brussels is fully part of France, "just a city", surrounded by the Netherlands (Flemish Brabant), with a protected Dutch-speaking minority (Dutch in about 22% of households, 2013 survey). The treaty and logistics items to settle later:
- **Transit rights** across Dutch territory (roads, rail, the high-speed lines to Paris and London from Brussels-South); precedents: Llívia, Büsingen, Campione d'Italia, Kaliningrad's transit regime, Berlin's 1971 arrangement.
- **Brussels Airport** is in Zaventem (Flemish Brabant), outside the region, so it would be Dutch; how does Brussels get an air link.
- **Water, gas and power** supply across the new borders (part of Brussels' water comes from Walloon sources, unverified).
- **Commuters:** a very large share of the workforce lives outside the region (unverified, no figure pulled).
- **EU and NATO seats:** only relevant if those institutions still exist in the setting; with Luxembourg also French, all three EU seats (Brussels, Luxembourg, Strasbourg) would be in France.
- **Dutch-language protections** in detail (schools, services, signage), on the Alsace-Moselle and French Flanders model.
- The rejected alternative (a corridor through Sint-Genesius-Rode, 22.7 km²) is analysed in `Belgium/brussels-corridor.png`.

## Finland and Karelia (carried over from the Russia sessions; being reviewed 2026-09-30)
The Russia notes (`Russia/PROGRESS TRACKER - Read This First.md`; `Russia/02 reorganization/01 follow-up/Russia - Karelia, Murmansk and Finland (pending future decision).md`; `worldbuilding/extractions/Karelia, Murmansk and Finland - Background Research.md`) record the author's plan: **Stage 1** Karelia (Republic) and Murmansk Oblast split from Country C into a "semi-stable proto-country" (built on the Russia maps, gold, capital undecided: Murmansk or Petrozavodsk); **Stage 2** it eventually merges into Finland (**not built**: Finland had no drawn territory); **Stage 3** the ethnic-Russian population is "encouraged" to leave and moves mainly into Country C's northern territory, some into the old Volga Republic and Tatarstan (**not built**). Now that Europe is under way, these need deciding: whether Finland survives as a successor state (assumed), the extent of the merger (Karelia Republic about 174,000 km² plus Murmansk Oblast about 144,000 km², together about 95% of Finland's own area on the map data; the Winter War and Continuation War losses were only about 44,100 km²), whether the Karelian Isthmus and Vyborg (in Leningrad Oblast, not part of the plan) are included, timing, and Finland's stance toward the CIN. Whether the Nordic countries belong on the Western map is also open.

**CONFIRMED (author, 2026-09-30), as a later-stage map in a timeline series (like East Asia): Finland initially lacks Karelia, then gains it. Karelia (the Republic) joins Finland, and Murmansk Oblast joins Idelsk-Uralia (Country C) instead of Finland.** Measured on the map data: Murmansk touches no Russian unit except Karelia (222 km), so Murmansk would be an exclave of Idelsk-Uralia, cut off by land by Finnish Karelia and about 60 km of sea at the narrowest (the White Sea's throat; the author's Google Maps measurement, replacing the generalized map data's 45 km) from Arkhangelsk Oblast; it borders Finland for about 342 km and Norway for about 169 km. Karelia borders Finland for about 774 km and Arkhangelsk Oblast for about 388 km. Finland would gain about 174,000 km² (not about 318,000) and only Karelia's ethnic Russians would need to move. Not yet confirmed. **Logistics idea (author, 2026-09-30):** Murmansk could ship its resources across the White Sea's narrow throat (about 60 km at the narrowest, about a 2-hour crossing each way, per the author's own measurement on Google Maps; a bridge is judged impractical) instead of by land through Finnish Karelia; by sea it would run from Murmansk's ice-free Barents ports, or the White Sea ports (Kandalaksha), to Arkhangelsk Oblast (several hundred km, unverified), with the White Sea freezing for part of the year. The White Sea-Baltic Canal lies in Karelia.

## The Nordic countries (the Scandinavian Trade Union; author, 2026-09-30)
No unification: a mutual, exclusive trade agreement, the Scandinavian Trade Union, **not fully borderless but extremely expedited** (about 5 to 10 minutes per member-to-member crossing: a couple of forms stamped, a quick X-ray or CT scan; non-members face full documentation, hours to days, up to one or two weeks by sea; author, 2026-09-30). **Members confirmed (author, 2026-09-30): Finland, Sweden, Denmark, Norway, the Faroe Islands and Iceland.** **Åland is in; Greenland has "honorary status" (author, 2026-09-30), meaning not yet defined.** **People get the same expedited treatment as goods (author, 2026-09-30; reasoning: the people are under the protection and authority of Denmark; confirmed: it applies to everyone in the Union).** Open: what honorary status means in practice (an associate with trade and movement rights but no vote, represented through Denmark?), whether people as well as goods, what "exclusive" means for outsiders, institutions, currency, defense, the relationship to the CIN and the Baltic states, and Finland's later-stage gain of Karelia (see above). Maps: `Nordic/00` and `Nordic/01`. The Nordic countries are now drawn on the Western working map (2026-09-30).
