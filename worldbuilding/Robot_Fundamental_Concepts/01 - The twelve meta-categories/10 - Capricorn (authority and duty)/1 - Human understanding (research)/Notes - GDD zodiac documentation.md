## 1. Areas of life
- **The tenth house: career and public standing.** Modern astrology gives Capricorn the tenth house. Silva calls it the House of Enterprise and lists its domains as career, society, government, motivation, authority and advantage (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).
- **The twelfth house: confinement and burden.** In the traditional scheme, Saturn finds its joy in the twelfth house, the house of confinement, isolation and solitude. Classical astrologers also called it the house of a bad spirit, tied it to anxiety and mental anguish, and felt it as being weighed down by many cares and responsibilities. The same placement is linked to persevering until every obstacle is beaten (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3 and §10, from Silva). The substrate's summary: the public face is the tenth house, the private weather the twelfth.
- **Mastery.** The sign's keyphrase is "I master"; its mode is *perceptive* and its faculty *discrimination* (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12). ⚠️ The file doesn't name the book; Snodgrass supplies the matching keyword for Sagittarius, so it is probably his.
- **The ruler's domains.** Saturn stands for time, limitation, accountability, structure, and the fear of not being good enough (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10). As a faculty it is limits, structure, time and law: the lord of time; discipline, patience, the deferred reward, the enforceable boundary, the concrete track record; the capacity to say no and make it stick (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Statecraft, and poverty.** Snodgrass's planetary appendix gives Saturn two kinds of people to govern, in the same cell: executives of state, and the poor (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV and Design Note 7).
- **Order in society.** Tierney says the sign's aim is a safe, orderly society for its hardworking, law-abiding members, and its nightmare is a lawless world out of control. Roles, especially professional roles, therefore carry moral weight: everyone must serve a needed function and be productive, or society falls apart (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Making visions real.** Tierney pairs it with Sagittarius: the Archer supplies the ideals, Capricorn the organizing skill that makes worthwhile social dreams come true, putting into practice only the few broad ideas with practical, timely value (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Time and measurement.** Saturn is lord of time, and Martin suggests the temperament is drawn to measurement itself, naming cartographer and clockmaker as fitting trades (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1, from Martin).
- **The turn of the year.** As the calendar's hinge, the sign looks back on one year and ahead to the next, and so stands for gravity and sobriety, the moods that help self-assessment and New Year's resolutions (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **The parent-child axis.** The opposition with Cancer sets the fourth house against the tenth and the Moon against Saturn (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Martin; see §7).
- **Ambition, hierarchy and the long climb**: ambition paired with patience; rigidity about hierarchy as the shadow (Full Attributes, Capricorn; Quick Reference, Capricorn).
- **Age.** The sign is given the age band 63 to 70 (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12); Saturn's return at about 29.5 years marks full adulthood (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).

## 2. Core meaning
- **The archetype.** The Sea-Goat: horned, fish-tailed, climbing a peak (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12). The Full Attributes file calls it ambition given structure, the zodiac's builder, most at home climbing something real and lasting over the long term (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **The organizer.** The substrate's one-line archetype: the organizer who makes other people's visions real in structure (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1). Cardinal earth joins duty to the drive to make things happen, and Tierney calls it the only earth sign with the momentum to reach prominence on a grand scale (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1; `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **The driving motive: standing that can't be taken away.** The substrate argues the motive is not greed. Martin says it takes the hard, lonely road to the top knowing that what it achieves was hard-won, truly deserved, and can never be taken away. Nothing comes free; we reap only what we have sown (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §1, from Martin).
- **The core need: recognition for real effort.** Tierney: to be sincerely recognized for dedicated effort in a field of its choosing. The substrate stresses this is not applause but the acknowledgment that the dues were real and were paid (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **The wound underneath.** Martin's material on Capricorn rising describes a strong feeling of isolation, of being alone in the world, and a conviction that the world is unsafe and one's needs are unlikely to be met. The substrate reads self-sufficiency as armor over the belief that nobody is coming (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §5, from Martin).
- **Worth must be earned.** Silva says Capricorn children tend to believe they must prove themselves to earn respect and love, and tells parents to say aloud that the child deserves love and respect even without coming out on top. The substrate's reading: underneath the need for earned standing is a conviction that unearned standing does not exist and cannot be given, which is why being denied recognition hits this sign so hard (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §5, from Silva).
- **What it needs to be allowed.** Silva's chapter on what the sign needs is a list of permissions rather than achievements: trust in its own judgment; work it actually loves rather than following the crowd; a workplace separate from its spouse's, because it is competitive and controlling; exposure to unfamiliar things; letting others finish their sentences; daily unproductive time; permission to hope; and permission to stop criticizing itself, asking whether it would say those things to someone it loved (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §5, from Silva).
- **The axis question.** Martin names the question the Cancer axis asks: "what is enough and what is not enough" (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §5, from Martin).
- **One trait, two directions.** The Full Attributes file's general rule applies: negatives are positives pushed too far, so discipline becomes rigidity and responsibility becomes workaholism (Full Attributes, *Worth Your Attention*; Full Attributes, Capricorn).

## 3. The area at its best
- **Discipline and follow-through across years**, not just moments (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Ambition with the patience to earn it** (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Responsibility and reliability** (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Practicality** that favors what actually lasts (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Resilience** through real setbacks (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Long-term planning** most other signs can't sustain (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Integrity**: it tends to do what it said it would (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Building to outlast the builders.** Martin's keyword for the sign's waning square is establishing collective or social structures that will stand the test of time (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2; `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §8, from Martin).
- **Winter competence.** Silva: resourceful, always with a plan, the way winter has always demanded of anyone who wants to get through it. Martin: it understands the need to strip things to the bone, cut back and prune so they grow stronger next season (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2, from Silva and Martin).
- **Calculated courage.** It is the one earth sign willing to venture into unknown territory and take calculated risks, but only after carefully weighing the situation, never on faith (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2; `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Honor kept even when unobserved.** Tierney notes the sign knows that brutal authorities have historically fallen in disgrace, wants no lasting stain on its name, and so behaves honorably even when no one is watching. The substrate calls this reputation management that has become real integrity (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Memory as affection.** Silva: it remembers your tastes, your birthday and the name of every relative you ever mentioned, and loves to reminisce about old times (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Humor that carries weight.** Silva says its dry humor exists not only to amuse but to get it and those around it through hard times, and calls it the secret of its perseverance (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Quiet authority.** Silva: it exercises authority even without an official position, and inspires confidence without trying (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Superb in crisis.** Patient, planned, unsentimental, still working when others have given up; Silva says this is where it wins (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Respect for rules, held in good faith.** Silva says Capricorn children have an inborn respect for rules and a good sense of what is right, and that when one breaks a rule, the parent should ask why, because the child usually had a very good reason (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Silva).
- **A strong sense of right.** Silva says Capricorn teenagers turn critical of people at home because of a strong sense of what is right and of social justice, and don't hide their disagreement (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Silva).
- **Endurance under strain.** Receptive signs, Martin says, have a deep inner reservoir and grow very strong in a crisis (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin). Earth is the custodial element that straightens the world up when it gets out of hand (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Exalted Mars: disciplined force.** Silva reads the exaltation as Mars's rashness subdued and its energy stabilized so the person can persevere and endure, producing strategic and tactical minds (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).
- **Faith through work.** Martin's positive reading of Jupiter's fall: hard work and self-discipline are themselves well-known paths of faith, as in monastic traditions. This is a sign where we are asked to "bend the knee" to a higher authority (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Martin).

## 4. The area gone wrong
**Authority: bowing up, pressing down**
- **The sycophant and the snob.** Snodgrass's sharpest line: the shadow becomes a sycophant, a snob, or an accomplice to illegal or ignoble acts, and at worst never hesitates to cozy up to the top brass, earning a reputation for boot-licking (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Strategy, not phoniness.** Tierney says it learned early that adults are suckers for a well-behaved child and became expert at pleasing authorities, without experiencing this as fake, only as strategy. Tierney also calls it fixated on glorifying authority, either by being one or by bowing to one (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Tierney). The substrate's reading: patronage without anyone consciously choosing it.
- **Following because it is safe.** Silva says the sign likes the comfort and security of following others, and would achieve more by taking the less-traveled path; it must learn to value its own decisions to earn colleagues' respect (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **The stern boss.** It demands that others work as hard as it does if they want to keep their positions, and has little patience for incompetent people whose mistakes make it look bad. The substrate notes the injury it feels is to its reputation, not to the work (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3).
- **Domestic supremacy.** Snodgrass describes the risen patriarch who flaunts his supremacy and turns bossy, sexist and judgmental, reducing a capable colleague to a needy supplicant (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Rigidity around process and hierarchy** (Full Attributes, Capricorn). Emerson's first decan can be dictatorial, rigid and inflexible (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Status obsession** (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Status over love in parenting.** Snodgrass: as a parent it may set a child's goals by social standing, prestige, wealth or impressive lineage rather than love and happiness (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Snodgrass).
- **Overcontrol.** Tierney says it feels responsible for teaching people how to behave, and must take care not to overcontrol them while offering safer, more structured ways to live (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Tierney).

**Judging by appearances**
- **How things look over how they feel.** Silva says the sign gets stuck in a negative cycle when it attends to how things look rather than how they feel (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Credentials mistaken for fit.** Silva on choosing partners: it picks people who appear perfect instead of checking whether they truly fit, runs relationships by business strategy and a preset idea of the ideal match, and learns the hard way that someone who looks good and earns well is not necessarily right (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva). The substrate notes the same pattern reads as a description of choosing whom to promote.
- **Visibility rewarded over output.** Silva notes Capricorns often leave as soon as their work is done, even while colleagues are still at their desks, and advises them to look more interested in their work so people will notice their dedication. The substrate points out that the advice asks the worker to perform presence rather than questioning a system that judges dedication by presence (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).

**Rules turned into control**
- **Private standards applied as law.** Silva: each Capricorn's values are set in stone, and its rules and values are applied to everyone it meets (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Moral policing beyond the law.** Tierney says it curbs behavior it finds inappropriate or threatening to conservative society, targeting not only lawlessness but anything it finds immoral (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Fear dressed as stability.** Tierney says it may uphold the status quo for defensive reasons, not only for stability (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Pressure to conform.** It expects everyone to toe the line so the community stays uniform, and distrusts outsiders or anyone who threatens cultural cohesion (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3).
- **Tradition over change.** Silva: strict and so attached to tradition that accepting change is hard; it struggles to open its mind to new things (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Routine as safety.** Silva's child chapter: continuity comforts these children, they keep schedules better than their parents, and disrupted routines throw them off balance. The substrate reads this as why established arrangements go unrevised: changing them feels like losing safety (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3 and §7, from Silva).
- **Earth's enforcement.** Tierney says earth keeps chaos at bay mainly by devising and enforcing rules and regulations; the substrate calls this custodianship one step from policing (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).

**Debts and grudges**
- **The favor ledger.** Tierney says Capricorn and Libra both keep track of every favor asked and later expect repayment; the substrate notes nothing is a gift and everything is an entry (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **The long memory.** Silva: it holds a grudge for a long time, and anything said to it once, even offhand, is remembered for good. The substrate notes that the faculty behind its affectionate memory is the same one that keeps the grudge (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Silence instead of lies.** Silva on two Capricorns together: lying is never the issue; the silence between them is. Debate becomes a silent contest neither wins, and they may refuse to do things together purely out of spite (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).

**Work and self-punishment**
- **Workaholism** that crowds out rest and relationships (Full Attributes, Capricorn). Tierney warns it to watch these tendencies (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Doing others' work.** Emerson calls it outrageously responsible, at risk of ending up doing other people's work (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Emerson).
- **Refusing to rest.** Snodgrass's image: when ill, rather than lose ground at work, it balances a briefcase on a lap board instead of resting and following doctor's orders (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Guilt as a value system.** Silva says it brings guilt into everything, with values built on guilt and a standing sense that nothing is good enough (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Unforgiving standards**, applied to itself as much as to others (Full Attributes, Capricorn). Silva: it holds other people to its own impossibly high standards (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Perfectionism with no floor.** Silva: perfectionism in everything, and any failure or imperfection turns its view of the future negative. The gloom spreads to the people around it (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Pessimism**, assuming the worst before it is earned (Full Attributes, Capricorn). Snodgrass's blunter register, which he partly disowns as overgeneralized: drudges and gloom-mongers forever expecting disaster, accused of meanness and inhibition (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Earth's cynicism.** Martin: a marked lack of enthusiasm, hope, faith and optimism, ending in cynical disappointment, a sense that the world has let them down. Silva: earth signs may settle for a life where they feel unhappy or unappreciated just to keep the security they built (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Martin and Silva).

**Isolation, bitterness and the loss of trust**
- **Emotional coldness or distance**, especially under pressure (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Trouble accepting help**, even when needed (Full Attributes, Capricorn).
- **Isolation dressed as self-sufficiency.** Silva: when insecure or disappointed it shuts the world out and hides pain behind humor and a self-sufficient manner, until life is lonely even among people. Its guardedness can leave it with no one to talk to, leading over the years to depression. Emerson names the endpoint the saturnine blues (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva and Emerson).
- **Cynicism after a wound.** With the Moon in detriment, Silva says, an emotional wound makes it cynical; afterward it finds it hard to trust people again, and it takes a long time to admit it needs emotional support (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3 and §10, from Silva).
- **Collapse when recognition never comes.** If it pays its dues and the standing never arrives, it waits patiently for a long time, and then a sense of being beaten by a cold, uncaring world can bring bitterness and depression (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3).
- **Lost before paradox.** Snodgrass: faced with a true paradox, it is out of its depth, baffled by a level of social or religious meaning that rules cannot explain, and confronts a shallowness in itself that may dismay or depress it (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Disbelief that wounds others' faith.** With Pisces, its plain disbelief can drain the magic and wreck Pisces's faith in its own convictions (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9). Silva says it cannot grasp the magic of life or certain kinds of belief, and trusts only rational thought, hard work and real results (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Silva).

**Generations devoured**
- **Saturn as Cronus.** Silva notes the planet is named for the titan who tried to stay in power by eating his children. The substrate calls it the darkest single line the corpus gives the sign (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3 and §13, from Silva; see §6).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time
- **At work.** Silva: most comfortable overseeing its own work, organizing its own time, setting and details; takes direction but does best with autonomy. It detaches emotionally to decide raises and promotions, and counts the detachment a virtue; Silva warns it can treat people like projects (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Its occupations.** Silva names teacher, professional manager or organizer, accountant or financial planner, business executive, and IT work. The substrate notes the shared shape: each imposes order on someone else's material. Silva adds that people without earth in their charts need a Capricorn to help organize their lives, and concedes that many Capricorns can't relate to young children (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Completion, not presence.** It leaves when the work is done, whatever its colleagues are doing (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva). This runs against the workaholic portrait elsewhere (see §12).
- **When idle.** Silva says Capricorn children grow listless when free or bored too long; the substrate reads idle time as a hazard for the type rather than a rest (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva).
- **When ridiculed or misread.** Silva on the Capricorn boy: he hates being ridiculed or misunderstood, and it can unsettle him for weeks (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Against deadlines.** Silva calls the sign punctual, yet says the Capricorn child sometimes misses deadlines because it likes working at its own pace and in its own way, takes longer as a perfectionist, and hates nagging. The substrate reconciles the two: punctual for other people's appointments, sovereign over its own work (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6 and §15, from Silva).
- **In company.** Low profile by choice; prefers a useful activity (a class, a hike) to a coffee date it finds pointless. Keeps a private A-list; Tierney even credits it with inventing the in-crowd, made up of people who are going places and doing important things (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Silva and Tierney). At a party it shows up when it matters, drinks a few but never more than it can handle, makes polite conversation and leaves when it is acceptable to (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §4, from Silva).
- **Under stress.** It works more. Stressed earth consumes itself rather than burning out; it doesn't feel its exhaustion the way fire does, but it won't stop either. Emerson adds over-preparation (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6, from Tierney and Emerson).
- **After public humiliation.** Its first instinct is to disappear, though it may instead stand firm and defend its honor aggressively (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6).
- **In crisis.** Superb (see §3). The substrate notes the cost: a crisis confirms its worldview, which makes the emergency posture hard to stand down from afterward (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §6).
- **In conflict.** Two Capricorns don't argue; they go silent (§4). As a cardinal sign it is impatient with drawn-out deliberation, deadlock and foot-dragging; frustrated cardinal types stir up a small crisis to force a resolution, and if provoked they attack or defend (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Tierney). Tierney's closing line on Capricorn generalizes to all cardinal signs: they sometimes never know when to stop pushing (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2; `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **When insecure or wounded**: withdrawal, humor as a veil, cynicism, slow return of trust (§4) (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §3, from Silva).
- **When cut off from what it loves.** Snodgrass: it falls into a decline that brings on bodily ailments and despair (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **How others read it.** Martin's bus-stop scene: the Capricorn waits with grim satisfaction because a late bus confirms its view that everyone else is incompetent and irresponsible, and, being a department head, decides to run a survey about it. The substrate reads this as vindicated pessimism plus an immediate procedural response (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §4, from Martin). Silva warns not to mistake its friendliness and courtesy for closeness, since a distance is always kept, and describes a strong, independent core under a soft, approachable cover. Tierney says it slips into whatever manner an occasion calls for, counts this strategy rather than falseness, and is seldom caught off guard in a way that shows its vulnerabilities. Next to a fame-seeking Leo, Silva says, it is after fortune, prefers understated elegance and saves obsessively (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §4).
- **How newcomers are let in.** Slowly, and on demonstrated worth. Silva says getting close takes persistence, since it takes time to judge whether someone is worthy of the inner circle. Loyalty, honesty, hard work and visible skill earn entry. Keeping the friendship is a duty: stay in touch, remember dates, keep showing up; it dislikes people who vanish (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Silva).
- **In childhood.** Tierney calls the sign born old: it arrives with a wisdom normally kept for a tribe's elders, studies power roles from childhood, and knows it has dues to pay before it may become somebody important. It protects like a security guard on patrol (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Tierney). Silva's child chapter (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §7, from Silva):
  - Continuity is the first need; don't change their school or home.
  - They dislike being treated with disrespect and think respect and love must be earned.
  - They respect rules, and a rule-breaking child usually had a reason worth asking about.
  - Play should have a purpose: building or making something, digging in the garden, reading. Competitive games end in tears or sulking, so collaborative ones are advised. At the playground they hang back.
  - Deep single interests (trains, dinosaurs); the museum over the amusement park.
  - They dislike loud noises and unfamiliar adults, and prefer older children and adults.
  - Thrift is taught early, with a piggy bank and spending only on real needs.
  - Parents should praise small achievements like big ones, since the child won't count them otherwise.
  - Too many hours of reading can turn them into loners.
  - As teenagers they become the household's moral critics (see §3).
- **Over a lifetime: born old, growing young.** Silva says Capricorns feel obliged to work hard and be responsible from a very young age, and reach true adulthood only at Saturn's return in their late twenties (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva). Emerson says the sign grows more playful, optimistic and youthful with age, with a troublemaker inside every serious Capricorn (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §14 and §15, from Emerson). The substrate reads Tierney's born-old child and Emerson's reverse aging as one arc, not two claims (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §15; ⚠️ the substrate's synthesis).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations
- **Pricus, the father who could not protect his children** (Silva). A sea-goat with many sea-goat children. When they wandered onto dry land and lost the power to think and speak, he turned back time to save them, again and again, until he had to give them up to the wild. He then made the constellation and gained an immortal home in the sky. Silva says this tale is why the melancholy, sad stereotype attaches to Capricorns (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13, from Silva). What it says about the area: a patriarch remembered for a failure of protection, and for refusing to accept that conditions had changed.
- **Saturn as Cronus, the father who ate his children** (Silva) to keep his throne (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13, from Silva). The substrate notes the sign owns both halves of the generational problem: the father who couldn't save his children and the father who consumed them to stay in power.
- **Pan and Typhon** (Snodgrass). In the war with the giants, Pan kept changing shape to escape; leaping into the Nile, his top half became a goat and his bottom half a fish. What it says: survival by improvised change under attack (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Amaltheia.** The goat who nursed Zeus; her horn was filled with endless fruit and vegetables, and Hercules gave it to the god of the underworld. The substrate reads the cornucopia as provision earned by nursing something greater than oneself (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **Ea, Enki and Makara.** The Babylonian Ea, god of the waters under the earth (the Tropic of Capricorn was Ea's way), is the Akkadian name of the Sumerian Enki, god of wisdom and water, later of intelligence, magic and creation. In Hindu myth the sign's counterpart is Makara, a crocodile rather than a goat (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13; the Enki and Makara links are from Silva). The substrate notes three civilizations placing a maker at the bottom of deep water.
- **Agora and Hestia.** Silva says the Cancer-Capricorn polarity is known in Greek philosophy as Agora and Hestia: the public square and the hearth (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13; `C_Polarity.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Augustus.** Capricorn was his birth sign. His astrologer Theogenes proposed the horned goat as his emblem; it flew on his legions' standards, and in 19 BC he struck silver coins showing Capricorn holding the world's rudder. What it says: a founding ruler chose the disciplined climber as his sign (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **The calendar hinge (Janus).** Self-assessment and resolutions at the year's turn (see §1) (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **Decay and fermentation.** Snodgrass says the sign begins the process of decay or dissolution that Pisces completes, and its planet's divinity is represented by omega, the last Greek letter and a symbol of doom. In Martin's cycle the fruit forms in Sagittarius and decomposes in Capricorn; the matching alchemical stage stands for both putrefaction and fermentation (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13, from Snodgrass and Martin).
- **The goat across cultures**: goodness and peace in Chinese tradition; energy, divinity and power in Hindu, Sumerian and Teutonic traditions; a sacred token of the moon goddess Cybele or Selene, and a good sign for travelers (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **A nineteenth-century zodiac verse** quoted by Snodgrass paints the type as one who climbs and schemes for wealth and position, mourns a brother's fall from grace, yet takes what is owed to it all the same (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Writers and figures.** Weighty prose, sparing with words and focused on substance; Jack London, including *To Build a Fire* (a failed attempt to survive extreme cold) and characters who succeed through extreme self-control. Military exemplars: Stonewall Jackson, Robert E. Lee, Joan of Arc (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **The Herculean labor**: the golden-horned stag, a labor of capture, not killing (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §13).
- **The oldest sign on record.** Chinese astronomers identified Capricornus in 2449 BC, the earliest sign-specific date in Snodgrass's timeline (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).
- **Star names of sacrifice.** The Hebrew name for the alpha star meant the slain kid; the Arabic name for its cluster, Alsa'd Aldhabih, means the lucky slaughterer (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12).
- **Other**: the apostle Simon; the cardinal cross (with Aries, Cancer and Libra), associated with four archangels (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12). Snodgrass's sign-to-place list gives Albania, Bulgaria, the Orkney Islands and Oxford. ⚠️ The GDD's correspondence file marks this geography as stereotyping and not for use (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix III); recorded here only as part of the tradition.

## 7. Structure
**Element: earth.**
- Earth verifies through the five senses, then builds something that lasts. Tierney calls it savvy about what can and can't be done with available resources, running on instinctive know-how rather than book knowledge, and the custodial element that tidies the world when it gets out of hand (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- Martin frames fire and earth as contesting *personal* truth. For earth, truth is objective but still personal, coming from its own senses: if earth doesn't like how something smells, tastes or looks, that is that (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin). The Capricorn file adds that its sense-based realism is less open to debate than other ways of knowing (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10).
- Strengths: perseverance, endurance, realism, delivery, resource accounting, and respect for the body's needs (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3). Blind spot: that the tangible layer is the only real one; it sees no point in the unproven or in endless theory (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).
- Needs: disruption it didn't choose (from air), and water's permission to feel something about what it has built (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Silva).
- Within earth, Capricorn is the cardinal form: the only earth sign that ventures into the unknown and the only one with momentum for prominence on a grand scale (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- ⚠️ Snodgrass once calls Capricorn a water sign associated with fresh and salt water; every other source and his own chart have it as earth. The substrate treats this as a slip, probably prompted by the sea-goat's tail (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9; `10_Capricorn.md` §15).

**Modality: cardinal.**
- Cardinal signs open a season; Capricorn opens winter. The cardinal month is when a new season rushes in and clears away the worn-out remains of the last (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Tierney and Snodgrass).
- Cardinal signs initiate: they take direct action with little hesitation, force decisions that have been avoided, and expect fast results. Emerson calls them the Initiators (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2).
- Failures: starting a new thing instead of finishing the first, pushiness, and looking busy whether or not anything gets built; and, in Tierney's closing line on Capricorn, not knowing when to stop pushing (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Tierney).
- Cardinal means initiating, not loud: an initiator can be quiet, patient and procedurally polite (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2).
- The Capricorn file calls this the sign of upward mobility and the ladder, with a strong instinct to climb (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10).
- In Martin's boulder parable, the cardinal traveler climbs straight over the obstacle (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Martin).

**Polarity: receptive (negative, yin).**
- Martin: inward-moving, reflective, thinking before acting; the Jungian parallel is introversion. Silva: a quiet strength in being self-contained, with inner resolve (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin and Silva). The sources insist receptive does not mean passive or weak (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3).
- In Capricorn, energy turns inward and toward the material; the ambition is carried out rather than announced (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10).
- Failure modes of the receptive mode: withdrawal into isolation, holding on to what should be let go, stagnation, and silence read by others as consent (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3).

**The opposite sign and the axis: Cancer.**
- Martin frames Cancer-Capricorn as the parent-child axis: the fourth house against the tenth, Moon against Saturn, carrying the twin core fears of engulfment and abandonment (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Martin). The ends of the axis: home and hearth against career and public standing; nurture against structure; being fed against providing.
- The substrate's statement of the shared question: who feeds whom, and what counts as enough (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8). Silva's version: the agora and the hearth (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §6).
- The documented failure: when the axis polarizes, the Cancer end grows needier and the Capricorn end more withholding and withdrawn, each confirming the other (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Martin).
- Silva's split between two capable leaders: Cancer is better with many kinds of people, Capricorn better with products (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Silva).
- The Moon, Cancer's ruler, is in detriment in Capricorn. Each sign's exalted planet is the other's fallen one (Jupiter is exalted in Cancer and falls in Capricorn; Mars is exalted in Capricorn and falls in Cancer) (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- General rule for oppositions: the usual failure is projection, where one end is lived and the other met as other people or events (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Ruling planet: Saturn.**
- Saturn's night home is Capricorn, its day home Aquarius (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10). In the old scheme Saturn, the slowest planet, was given the coldest, darkest point of the year (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §1, from Martin).
- Meaning: time, limitation, accountability, structure, law, discipline, patience, the deferred reward, and the fear of not being good enough (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §3). Silva says Capricorn is where Saturn's most authoritative and stern functions are expressed (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).
- Martin's image: Saturn's rings as protective moats around a fortress (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Martin).
- Tierney's distinction: Saturn itself shies from the limelight and is monkish, a hardworking, blue-collar planet, while Capricorn actively creates the conditions for the recognition it craves. The sign is more socially ambitious than its ruler (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Tierney).
- Saturn's return at about 29.5 years: when a person becomes their true self and takes control of their life (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).
- Saturn's joy in the twelfth house (see §1).
- Dignities: Saturn in domicile; Mars exalted; Moon in detriment; Jupiter in fall (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). The substrate reads this as the strongest executive combination in the table, and its characteristic failure as being able to build anything without being able to say what for (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §6; ⚠️ the substrate's reading). Martin on the Moon placement: self-sufficient, more comfortable looking after others than being looked after (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §6, from Martin). Silva on Jupiter's fall: not pessimism but cautious optimism, finding it hard to believe the universe is on one's side (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10, from Silva).

**Squares (conflict): Aries and Libra.** Both are cardinal. Martin says a cardinal square's challenge may even be welcome: each side pushes the other into dynamic, goal-directed action, and the conflict escalates, resolves and recurs (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- *Aries: whether earned rank is legitimate at all.* Tierney's Aries chapter has Aries reading Capricorn as fixated on glorifying authority, and defining itself against a social system that tries to make people obedient sheep instead of fighting rams. Aries pays for its defiance with a worse career, but nobody owns its soul. Emerson calls the pairing extremely hard, practically a losing battle (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Tierney and Emerson).
- *Libra: procedure against deliberation.* They look compatible: both lead, both work well intellectually, both keep favor ledgers. But Silva says Capricorn focuses on facts while Libra plays with concepts, and Capricorn hates Libra's indecision; Emerson says Capricorn gets fed up and leaves. Tierney names the deeper split: Libra knows how unfair society can be when it regulates its members' private lives, which sets it against rule-enforcing Capricorn (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8). Silva's element-level line: Libra prizes its mind, and Capricorn doesn't care about words that produce no results (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva). Martin uses Libra against Capricorn as her example of same-tempo conflict, one side arguing from principle and the other from material result (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin).
- The four cardinal signs form a closed cross of mutual squares and oppositions. Martin describes such a configuration as dynamic, driven and fiercely self-sufficient, not relying on anyone else (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §5, from Martin).

**Trines (support): Taurus and Virgo.**
- *Taurus*: shared appetite for stability, profit and lasting comfort; Emerson calls them wonderfully complementary, and Taurus reins Capricorn in before it burns out (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9, from Emerson).
- *Virgo*: instant mutual recognition; both cautious perfectionists. Silva: you could find no more hardworking and honest colleague. Virgo takes the day-to-day, Capricorn the long horizon; the risk is mutual suffocation, and Virgo burning out under Capricorn's standard (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9, from Silva).
- Earth with earth moves at the same pace and forms a shared reality other temperaments can't enter or bear to listen to (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).

**Sextiles (working partners): Scorpio and Pisces.**
- *Scorpio*: the one relationship where trust runs deep; the one sign Scorpio can completely trust. Comfortable silences, shared dark humor, a shared interest in digging up what really happened. Snodgrass warns the two tend to tyrannize when paired; Emerson that they isolate together (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9).
- *Pisces*: Pisces gets Capricorn to relax and open up; Capricorn helps Pisces put its ideas into words and into form. But Capricorn can drain the magic out of Pisces, and its plain disbelief can wreck Pisces's faith in its own convictions (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9).

**Quincunxes (incomprehension): Gemini and Leo** (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4). The sign file says little about either beyond the Leo contrast of fame against fortune (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §4, from Silva).

**Neighbors (semi-sextiles): Sagittarius and Aquarius.** Mutual respect without comprehension (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9).
- *Sagittarius*: Capricorn turns a few of the Archer's ideas into practice (see §1). Silva says Capricorn can't grasp the magic of life or certain beliefs, trusting only reason, hard work and results (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Silva).
- *Aquarius*: both answer to Saturn in the traditional scheme, yet want opposite things, freedom against boundaries. Silva: Capricorn comes before Aquarius, and pressure has to exist before anyone can feel liberated (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §9, from Silva). Earth against air: Capricorn thorough and slow, Aquarius unreliable and flaky (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).

**Capricorn against Capricorn.** Two compete for first place and go silent instead of arguing; Silva's verdict is that two negatives here are more likely to make another negative than a positive (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §8, from Silva).

## 8. Decans
Emerson is the only decan source (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §5). Decan 1 is the sign doubled; decans 2 and 3 blend it with the other two earth signs (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §1).
1. **Saturn (Capricorn-Capricorn): the legend of determination.** Determination and patience that are legendary; less self-confident than others believe, but unmatched in capability and hardiness; can be dictatorial, rigid and inflexible; a fine sense of humor at its center; realistic and practical, with room for fantasy (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: authority at full strength, with its hardness and its hidden self-doubt.
2. **Venus (Capricorn-Taurus): duty with rest.** Hardworking and patient, but makes time for rest, relaxation and art; slow to take in new experiences; insistent on doing things correctly and seeing projects through (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: craft and the earned comfort of finished work.
3. **Mercury (Capricorn-Virgo): duty through evidence.** A little restless, with a great need for efficiency and function; never impulsive, but less patient than the first two; intelligent, organized, scientific; insists that evidence be empirically backed (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: accountability by measurement.

**Cusps (Silva).** Not decans but the sign's two borders, which Silva treats as full types. People on a cusp carry both signs, with real inner conflict and trouble deciding (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Sagittarius-Capricorn, the Cusp of Prophecy** (about Dec 18-24): a visionary who is also a practical realist able to inspire real change; fire's passion sustained by earth's consistency. Loyal, caring and socially active; sought out for advice; a leader and teacher. Failures: impatience with slower learners, dislike of small talk, isolation that creeps in unnoticed (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Capricorn-Aquarius, the Cusp of Mystery and Imagination** (about Jan 17-23): a hardworking idealist, grounded and determined yet fond of spontaneity. Strengths: self-determination, creativity, wit, empathy, idealism. Weaknesses: detachment, chaos, selfishness, harsh criticism, prejudice, closing itself off, and great procrastination. It sees the world as it is and wants to fix its problems, which makes for fascinating talk but can isolate and intimidate those close to it. It hates the status quo and wants change, yet holds to customs and traditions, using its upbringing and old traditions as the means of change (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §11, from Silva).

**Capricorn's flavor inside other signs** (Emerson's decans of the other earth signs): Taurus's third decan, Saturn-ruled, is a great builder, stern and sometimes dull but fully loyal and the most disciplined in the zodiac, marked by care, patience and prudence. Virgo's second decan, Saturn-ruled, puts security first, is excellent with organization and money, tactful and responsible, a great administrator, and sometimes narrow-minded (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).

## 12. Gaps
- **Collective behavior.** The sign file calls this the largest real gap: every source describes an individual (child, friend, worker, partner), never a population. How a body of people of this temperament governs itself, mourns, strikes or celebrates is not covered; the nearest material is the in-crowd, the distrust of outsiders, and party behavior (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §15).
- **Authority as an idea.** The material treats authority and duty as temperament (how a person relates to rank, rules and work), not as ethics or political theory: nothing on legitimate authority, oaths, obligations to the dead, or the limits of obedience beyond the Aries contrast.
- **Festival, calendar, food and ritual.** Only the New Year association, milk over wine, Saturn's return and the childhood piggy bank (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §15).
- **The tenth house is listed, not developed**; Martin's house volume is not mined for it in depth (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §10).
- **Correspondences are unsettled.** Snodgrass's per-sign color table was cut in extraction. Colors, stones and plants disagree across sources (§9). The sign file says the metal and flower are unattested; the correspondence file gives lead through Saturn (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §15; `G_Correspondences.md` Per-Sign Detail). ⚠️ The two GDD files also disagree on where Emerson's jade, peppermint and root chakra came from: the sign file says they were recovered from Emerson's Cancer block, while the correspondence file says they sit in Capricorn's own block and that the apparent bleed was a slicing artifact; it also doubts jade and peppermint on other grounds (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §12; `G_Correspondences.md` source note 2 and Per-Sign Detail).
- **Decans: one source** (Emerson) (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §5).
- **Disagreements between sources, kept rather than resolved** (Substrate `10_Capricorn.md` §15 unless named):
  - Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* contradicts itself on whether air signs suit Capricorn.
  - Snodgrass calls Capricorn a water sign once (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9).
  - Silva has Capricorns leaving as soon as their work is done, against the workaholic portrait everywhere else.
  - Silva calls the sign at ease with strangers and keen to go out in one chapter, and no fun at a party and early to bed in another.
  - Silva says the sun's light is at its lowest in Capricorn season, then at its highest two paragraphs later (the first is correct).
  - The punctual Capricorn against the child who misses deadlines (reconciled in §5).
  - Tierney's born-old child against Emerson's adult who grows younger (read by the substrate as one arc).
  - Modalities: Tierney and Martin have fixed signs finishing what cardinal signs start, while Emerson and Silva call the mutable signs the finishers (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §8). This sits awkwardly with Capricorn's listed strength of follow-through across years (Full Attributes, Capricorn). ⚠️ Silva also calls the cardinal signs reactive, against every other source (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §8).
