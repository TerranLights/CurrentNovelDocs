## 1. Areas of life
- **The ninth house.** The substrate lists its domains as the higher mind, faith, higher education, law and long-distance travel (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1).
- **Philosophy, and governance alongside it.** Snodgrass's one-line keyword for the sign is "I philosophize," and he assigns it *administration* as its life-domain. The substrate reads this as meaning and governance named in one breath (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Snodgrass).
- **The ruler's domains.** Jupiter stands for growth, expansion, luck, law, religion and higher education (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10). As a faculty it is meaning, scale and generosity: the capacity to say what the work is *for*, to keep the whole forest in view along with the trees, and to extend belonging past one's own clan to community, society, country, ancestors and history (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Judges and money.** Snodgrass's planetary appendix gives Jupiter the judiciary, and investors and financiers, as the kinds of people it governs (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV).
- **What lies far away, and the meeting of difference.** Tierney says the sign is concerned with whatever is happening somewhere else, far off. He calls it the sign of culture shock: the point where difference stops being a novelty and becomes an argument about what is true (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **The universal claim.** Tierney places here the first appearance in the wheel of the idea that a universal mind exists, together with a strong faith in unseen guidance that protects (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **The turn to the collective.** In Tierney's scheme, the last four signs, starting with Sagittarius, put the needs of the many ahead of the individual's (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Freedom, adventure, travel and the search for the bigger picture** (Full Attributes, Sagittarius; Quick Reference, Sagittarius).
- **The journey over the arrival.** Martin says the journey itself matters more to the sign than the goal (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Martin; `B_Modalities.md` §4).
- **Teaching against learning.** The opposition with Gemini sets the third house against the ninth. Martin frames the pair as information against meaning, facts against knowledge, and the student against the teacher (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8, from Martin).

## 2. Core meaning
- **The archetype.** The Archer: a centaur drawing a bow at a target above the horizon. The ninth sign; mutable fire; ruled by Jupiter (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1). The Full Attributes file calls it the zodiac's wanderer and philosopher, always aiming at the next horizon, and more interested in the search for meaning than in any settled answer (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **The driving motive.** The substrate's own summary: meaning, gained by going far enough away to find it (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1).
- **The core need: freedom, as a means.** Silva says the sign needs personal freedom the way everyone needs air, and Emerson's note on Vesta uses the same image (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §5, from Silva and Emerson). The substrate argues the freedom serves something else. What the sign seeks is a warrant to believe, and it wants that warrant outside itself and written down. Tierney says the sign is not mystical (that is Pisces), and that it likes its spiritual beliefs backed by something concrete: a revered holy book, or failing that a visionary leader with answers. The substrate reads this as an orthodox streak in a sign that sees itself as the least orthodox of all (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **Trust in oneself.** Tierney's summary of what the sign's placement in a chart points to: the place where life shows how good things could be, if only we trusted ourselves more (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **Ego tied to the common good.** Unusually for a fire sign, Tierney ties its satisfaction to the part it plays in helping society improve, and says it is not hungry for power (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **The centaur's split: appetite and teacher.** The sign is half animal and half teacher. Silva describes its balanced form as clean, strong instinct joined to deeper human wisdom. Martin notes that the line crossing the arrow in the glyph is said to mark the centaur's half-human, half-horse division (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10, from Silva and Martin).
- **Conflict turned into motion.** Freud, as Snodgrass records him, read the sign as the ego calming a warring id and superego by redirecting both into hard physical activity: the id made to quiet its demands, the superego made to stop its moralizing (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13, from Snodgrass). The substrate builds on this: the split stays stable only while the person keeps moving, and when they are stopped, appetite and sermon are left in one room with nothing to do. It traces the sign's documented pathologies to that stopped state (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10; ⚠️ the substrate's reading, not a book's).
- **One trait, two directions.** The Full Attributes file's general rule applies here: the negatives are the positives pushed past their useful range, so blunt honesty becomes tactlessness and enthusiasm becomes over-promising (Full Attributes, *Worth Your Attention*; Full Attributes, Sagittarius).

## 3. The area at its best
- **Real optimism**: expecting things to work out rather than performing positivity (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Philosophical curiosity**: a real hunger to understand the bigger picture (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Honesty**: blunt rather than politically managed (Full Attributes, Sagittarius; Quick Reference calls it bluntness-as-honesty).
- **Generosity**, especially with knowledge, opportunity and enthusiasm (Full Attributes, Sagittarius). Silva rates it the most generous fire sign and the least interested in credit; it gives more than people see (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Love of freedom, for itself and for others** (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Loyalty that cannot be bought.** Silva says its loyalty is given freely; all it asks in return is not to be exploited, and an ultimatum (choose them or me) always fails with it (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Friendship across groups.** It makes friends in every clique, belongs to none, and brings them all together, leaving the friends unsure how they all came to know each other (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Composure in a real crisis.** Silva says it faces hardship without fear and stays the course, knowing that only change will end the hardship. Snodgrass credits Lincoln and Churchill with the same practical sense and intuitive feel for the right action at the right moment (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Silva and Snodgrass). ⚠️ The substrate doesn't say in what sense Snodgrass counts Lincoln as Sagittarian; his birthday falls under Aquarius, so it may be another chart placement.
- **Field knowledge under book learning.** Snodgrass grants the sign a practical, field-tested knowledge that supports thorough schooling; the substrate notes this is a rare pairing in the corpus (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Snodgrass).
- **Endurance over the long haul.** Of the three fire signs, Aries is the spark, Leo the full blaze and Sagittarius the slow burn, trained for long stretches of energy rather than short bursts (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Faith that recovers.** The elements file says fire signs usually have the faith to pick themselves up after a crash and carry on (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2).
- **Luck as earned expectation.** Tierney reframes the sign's luck as an outlook set to expect gain rather than loss, and calls it a smart kind of luck, not a dumb one (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Incorruptible at work.** Silva says supervisors know they can't buy these people off with a raise, and they will turn down a promotion that costs them freedom (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Protective toward those below.** It will stay in a job it hates because it sees itself as a buffer for its coworkers, and it is first to defend a subordinate against a superior (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva).
- **A conscience that acts.** Martin's example of the Gemini-Sagittarius axis at its best is Mandela, whose chart she reads as setting the collective challenge of speaking out and acting on one's beliefs (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8, from Martin).
- **Frankness without pretense.** Emerson calls the second decan the frankest of all decans in the zodiac, without any superficiality, and a soldier of the spirit and mind (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).

## 4. The area gone wrong
**Belief hardened past correction**
- **Unfalsifiable conviction.** Tierney says it is nearly impossible to convince the sign, once inspiration has taken it over, that its cherished beliefs could be wrong (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Fundamentalism.** Martin says that, cut off from the correction facts provide, the feeling of having privileged access to the one truth can lead to "arrogance and fundamentalism" (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3 and §8, from Martin).
- **Arrogant and patronizing.** Martin's name for the distorted form of fire in this sign; for comparison, distorted fire in Aries turns violent and in Leo demanding and jealous (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Dogmatism.** Emerson warns the first decan it can be dogmatic in its belief system (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Credulity.** The elements file says the sign claims to seek the ultimate reality beneath everything yet has a gullible, credulous nature, and mistakes its own conviction for the general truth (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2).
- **Conviction taken for verification.** The rulerships file's reading of the sign's dignities: it states conclusions with full confidence and cannot show its work (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §6; ⚠️ the substrate's reading).
- **Faking knowledge.** Silva says the sign rising owns every room it enters, and if it doesn't know what is going on it fakes it, cleverly enough to get away with it (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §4, from Silva).
- **Inflated speech.** With Mercury in the sign, ordinary events get embellished into drama, and everything is described in the biggest words available (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §4).
- **Winning the argument after it ended.** Silva says it will dive obsessively into research to prove it was right long after the conversation is over (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva).

**Truth used as a weapon**
- **Tactlessness**: bluntness that forgets how it lands (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Publishing the grievance.** Silva says that when hurt, it takes the matter to the most public forum it can find so everyone knows how it was wronged: blunt truths dropped in meetings, company-wide emails about something between two people (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva). The substrate stresses this is publication, not mere bluntness.
- **Revenge.** When it feels wronged, Silva says, it will stop at nothing to act or get revenge (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Trampling without noticing.** The elements file says fire can run over other people's feelings without registering it, with nothing devious or malicious involved, which is exactly why it is never corrected (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2).
- **Explosive, then forgetful.** Jupiter, read as Zeus, gives a violent temper that is quickly spent but can leave more sensitive people shaken for some time. The sign forgets the fight; the person on the receiving end does not. It repairs only with its inner circle (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3).

**Promises and follow-through**
- **Over-promising and overreach**: enthusiasm outruns follow-through; it promises more than any one person or plan can deliver (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Reneging, with a sense of entitlement.** Tierney says it tends to waver, switch sides, slip out of things, pass the buck or back out at the last minute, especially when a commitment starts to feel like a trap. Mutable signs believe they have every right to change their minds whatever they promised, and the Archer doubly so (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Tierney; `B_Modalities.md` §4).
- **Fear of commitment; restlessness** once novelty fades, literal and emotional (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Impatience with fine detail** once the big idea is grasped (Full Attributes, Sagittarius).
- **Boredom as negligence.** Silva's example is Churchill waving off London's Great Fog as mere fog while people died: a slow hazard downgraded because it was dull. Silva also notes half-finished projects, inconsistency at work, and trouble remembering why something matters when it is also boring (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Scattering.** Mutable signs spread themselves too thin across too many things at once (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- **Disdain for the paperwork of its own plans.** Martin's example is the tax return, which fire treats as an insult: it sees no need to justify its existence, and rules are for ordinary mortals (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin).

**Limits denied**
- **Believing itself immune.** Tierney says it may arrogantly believe itself immune to ordinary human limits, and seldom feels in mortal danger (how could something so much fun be dangerous?) (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Always bruised.** Martin says people with Sagittarius rising are perpetually bruised because they don't know their limits (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §4, from Martin).
- **No stamina.** The elements file calls this the corpus's most counterintuitive fire finding: fire signs can be overwhelmed by accumulated resistance until their fire goes out (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2). Tierney adds that fire drains the people around it to keep its own blaze going (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9, from Tierney).

**Discarding people**
- **Dropping the stuck.** Silva's harshest documented behavior: it ends a long friendship because the friend keeps returning to the same failure and won't move forward. It may forsake family on the same grounds despite real loyalty (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Rejecting the inauthentic.** Anyone wearing a public mask is called out or dismissed (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva). Under stress this hardens into a purge of anyone it judges fake, needy or self-pitying (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6).

**Confinement and self-sabotage**
- **Confinement turns it bitter.** Snodgrass says the sick Sagittarian refuses bed rest and confinement and becomes a bitter, complaining invalid. The substrate notes it is being held still, not hardship, that sours the type (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Sabotage at the point of arrival.** A twelfth-house reading: wrecking a stable relationship with a secret affair, or stalling a career project because failing is easier than facing a step up (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3). ⚠️ The substrate doesn't say which book or which chart placement this comes from.

**Corruption in its own institutions**
- Martin, on Pluto's passage through Sagittarius: what surfaced was corruption in higher education, the church and the law, the three things the sign governs. Her sharpest point is that priests are meant to be above bodily motive, and the exposure was exactly bodily (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Martin). The substrate reads this as the centaur's split failing in institutional form.

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time
- **At work.** Silva: refuses desks and routine; can't stand working for driven, controlling bosses; finds the prospect of blind obedience to authority horrifying; good at many small goals and bad at follow-through (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva). Snodgrass: as soldier, pilot, geologist or animal trainer, it rises into authority (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Snodgrass).
- **In company.** It brings the gathering with it, hosts well and calls out anyone performing. It is drawn into deep conversations it isn't equipped for: it doesn't listen well, though it genuinely wants to learn, and once a talk tips into argument the event can't be saved (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Under stress.** It escalates and broadcasts; its impatience shows. It quits rather than waits: it would sooner leave a job than wait six months for a promotion, because leaving is at least something different (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva).
- **In crisis.** The substrate calls this its best state: it charges, decides for the present and stays the course. Two exceptions bracket it: the crisis it finds boring, which it dismisses, and the crisis that pins it down, which turns it bitter (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6).
- **In conflict.** Martin says mutable signs avoid conflict at all costs; Tierney names Sagittarius as the one exception, because of its fiery nature (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10; `B_Modalities.md` §4). The substrate adds that it starts the fight and then wanders off (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10). Faced with air signs' theorizing, fire just gets impatient and leaves (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin).
- **When wronged**: public denunciation, then revenge (see §4) (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva).
- **When ill or held still**: refuses rest, turns bitter (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Its one insecurity.** The only insecurity the corpus grants the sign appears in intimacy after repeated failure: panic the moment novelty ends (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6).
- **In childhood (Silva's account of Sagittarian children)** (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §7, from Silva):
  - They swing between independence and neediness, wanting to explore with a safety net.
  - They reach milestones early because milestones buy independence, and take real pride in finished chores; given a job, they feel they belong.
  - They should not be confined; play matters more to them than a schedule.
  - They interrogate adults and won't accept vague answers, digging until someone gives them a concrete one they can accept.
  - They are always listening and will repeat anything to anyone.
  - They attach to the adult they judge suitable, not the one assigned to them.
  - They don't lie.
  - Wariness of strangers has to be taught deliberately and mostly doesn't take.
  - The wrongs and good deeds they experience as children become their guide for life, and a common outcome is hating one's own upbringing and building an adult life against it.
- **Friendship over time.** Newcomers are taken in instantly and shallowly; the inner circle is small and slow to form; even close friends may drift away and later turn up again without awkwardness (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §7, from Silva).
- **Over a lifetime.** Martin says each element matures through its three signs, and Sagittarius is fire's last and most mature form (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1-§2, from Martin). Emerson says the first decan's academic pursuits can last a lifetime (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2). The material says little else about how the sign changes with age (see §12).
- **How others read it.** A long, purposeful stride with swinging arms; eyes that can't hide opinions; frantic gesturing to make a point (Snodgrass); a presence that seems to extend beyond the body (Martin) (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §4).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations
- **Three incompatible origin stories, and the corpus doesn't choose** (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13, from Snodgrass):
  - *Greek, Chiron*: the learned herbalist and teacher of Jason, Achilles and Asclepius, famous for wisdom and compassion, unlike the violent and lustful centaurs. What it says about the area: the teacher and healer as the sign's ideal.
  - *Roman, Crotus*: son of Pan, inventor of archery, who lived on Mount Helicon as nurse and watchman to the Muses and was set in the sky at their request. Eratosthenes argued the figure was a satyr, not a centaur. What it says: the guardian of the arts and learning.
  - *Babylonian, Nergal*: horse body, human torso, wings, two heads (human and panther), a scorpion's sting, and a portfolio of war and scorched earth. What it says: the same stars also carry the war god.
- **Chiron, the wounded healer.** He could heal everyone but himself, and escaped an incurable wound by giving his immortality up on Prometheus's behalf (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13). Martin describes the Chironic wound as first felt through isolation, rejection or inadequacy, after which people build strong defenses around the sore place to avoid a repeat. She refuses to assign Chiron to any sign, warning that anchoring it in the zodiac may be an attempt to own or fix it. Chiron in Sagittarius reads as a wound in faith itself, with a compulsion to write from it. The substrate adds that Chiron's orbit passes back outside Saturn's in the late degrees of Sagittarius (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13, from Martin).
- **Arrows that hit the wrong target.** Snodgrass strings the myths together on purpose: Hercules shoots Nessus and is later killed by Nessus's blood; Artemis's arrow kills Coronis; Apollo's arrows kill the Cyclopes and become the constellation Sagitta; Paris's bad shot finds Achilles's heel; Eros's dart drives Dido to a doomed passion. Snodgrass calls this the archer's harmful role, and Ovid lists the archer among the menacing constellations (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13, from Snodgrass). What it says about the area: aiming high and being hit by one's own shot.
- **The stray arrow.** Sagitta, the archer's own arrow, lies some distance away as though it went astray (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §12).
- **Zeus/Jupiter, the rebel who won.** Jupiter overthrew gods who ruled through chaos and fear; the substrate reads this as a founding rebellion that became the establishment (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10).
- **Freud's reading** (via Snodgrass): the sign as a mechanism for turning inner conflict into action (see §2) (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13).
- **History of the figure.** One of the oldest identified star shapes: Babylonian, eleventh century BC, first a mounted bowman; an earlier Babylonian version was a hippo-centaur with no bow (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §12). The Babylonian list of 419 BC already includes Sagittarius (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).
- **Names across cultures**: Akkadian *Nunki* (prince of the earth); Egyptian *Knem* (the conqueror); Arabic *Alkaus* (the arrow); Hebrew *Kesith* (the archer). The nearby asterism *Scutum* is the bowman's shield (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §12).
- **Other associations**: the apostle Matthew; the Herculean labor of the centaurs and Diomedes's horses; Arab cosmology placing the fire triad in the east (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §12). Snodgrass also links nations to the sign, England for its cockeyed optimism and wartime persistence, Spain as buoyant, self-seeking and proud at the negotiating table, plus Arabia and Hungary (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §12, from Snodgrass). ⚠️ The GDD's correspondence file marks Snodgrass's sign-to-nation geography as stereotyping and not for use (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix III); recorded here only as part of the tradition.
- **Mandela** (Martin): his chart's nodes on this axis, and the title of his memoir, *Long Walk to Freedom*, as an image of acting on belief (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8, from Martin).
- **Ophiuchus and the student who surpassed the teacher.** Silva gives Sagittarius the corpus's only Ophiuchus chapter, because the thirteenth constellation covers most of Sagittarius's date range, roughly its middle twenty days (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §16, from Silva). Ophiuchus, the Serpent Bearer, is identified with Imhotep (vizier, architect, sage, later worshipped as a god of medicine) and with Asclepius. Snodgrass supplies the link: Apollo gave the unborn Asclepius to Chiron to raise; Asclepius surpassed his teacher, raised the dead and was killed by Zeus for it, while Chiron could not heal his own wound (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §16, from Silva and Snodgrass). Silva suggests much of the Sagittarian's curiosity could be credited to Ophiuchus, names jealousy as Ophiuchus's signature trait, and contrasts Ophiuchus's focused, concentrated interests with Sagittarius's fleeting ones (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §16, from Silva). What it says about the area: a teacher's pride against a pupil's success; breadth against focus.

## 7. Structure
**Element: fire.**
- Fire acts first, on subjective conviction. Martin frames fire and earth as contesting *personal* truth: for fire, truth is subjective and needs no proof, like a religious experience or a personal conviction, absolutely real to the person and neither provable nor in need of proof. For earth, truth comes through the five senses (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin). Air and water contest *collective* truth instead.
- Martin says fire has an inborn sense of its own specialness, hence great faith in itself and a readiness to take risks (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- Fire's blind spots: objective facts (statistical odds mean nothing to it), the body (felt as confining), and other people's inner lives (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2; the point about facts is Martin's).
- Fire needs earth as fuel and containment; earth puts fire out and fire needs it anyway (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2 and §6, from Martin).
- Within fire, Sagittarius is the mutable, distributed form: Tierney's image is a forest fire started by lightning that spreads far beyond where it began. The boldness here is more intellectual than physical, and the journeys more geographic than psychological (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2 and §10; `A_Elements.md` §2, from Tierney).

**Modality: mutable.**
- Mutable signs end a season. They adapt and mediate, and their job is to break down what fixed signs have hardened and pass it on changed (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1 and §4, from Tierney and Martin).
- They tolerate change easily, care more about the journey than the arrival, and can't stand monotony (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §4).
- Failures: scattering, unclear direction, and the broken promise (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- Sagittarius is the exception in the mode: the one mutable sign that dives into experience as assertively as a cardinal sign and will start a fight (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §4; `09_Sagittarius.md` §10, from Tierney).
- Tierney splits the four mutable signs into two that run on measurable facts and two that run on grand ideas and visionary ideals (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §4). ⚠️ The substrate doesn't name which pair is which; presumably Sagittarius and Pisces are the visionary pair.

**Polarity: active (positive, yang, outward).**
- Martin: active mode moves from a center outward, is forceful and impulsive, and both creates and destroys; its Jungian parallel is extraversion (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin). Silva calls this polarity assertive (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10).
- Failure modes of the active mode: motion mistaken for progress, and strength without inner reserves to fall back on (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin).
- The sign does not process inwardly; Tierney imagines it waving off introspection as something Scorpio has already done enough of (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10, from Tierney).

**The opposite sign and the axis: Gemini.**
- The Gemini-Sagittarius axis runs from the third house to the ninth. Its two ends, in Martin's framing: information and meaning; facts and knowledge; the student and the teacher (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8, from Martin).
- Each end fails alone, in opposite ways. Gemini without the bigger picture becomes trivial and meaningless, even nihilistic. Sagittarius without Gemini's relative truths becomes arrogant and fundamentalist (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8, from Martin).
- The corrective Sagittarius needs is Mercury's: detail, checking, tact, brevity, and the admission that views can be "partial and multiple." Mercury is in detriment in Sagittarius, so the sign is worst at housing exactly what would correct it. Martin says integrating Mercury brings humor and detachment to what can otherwise become an overwhelming, evangelical approach to life (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8 and §10, from Martin; `F_Rulerships.md` §6).
- The sources split on the pair's surface: Silva calls it a true opposites-attract match, each seeking what the other has; Emerson warns both are restless, unsteady and out to seduce and conquer (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8).
- General rule for oppositions: the usual failure is projection, where a person identifies with one end and meets the disowned end as other people or outside events (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin). Opposite signs share modality and polarity and differ only in element (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §1).

**Ruling planet: Jupiter.**
- Growth, expansion, luck, law, religion, higher education (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10). Meaning, scale and generosity; the planet of saying what something is for (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- Two kinds of Jupiter luck: plain good fortune, and a judge-and-jury kind, in which luck can simply mean the lesser of the possible consequences (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10). ⚠️ The substrate doesn't name the book for this.
- Jupiter rules two signs: Sagittarius (its daytime home) and, traditionally, Pisces (where it rests at night). The same urge toward the boundless points outward and geographically in Sagittarius, and inward and dissolving in Pisces (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8; `F_Rulerships.md` §1).
- Dignities: Jupiter in domicile; Mercury in detriment; no exaltation and no fall. The sign has a thinner dignity profile than most, so readings rest on ruler against detriment (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5 and §7).
- Rulership in general means the faculty works without effort and so is invisible to its owner as a choice; detriment means the faculty works against the grain and survives only by being reshaped (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).

**Squares (conflict): Virgo and Pisces.** All four mutable signs form what Snodgrass calls the moveable cross (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8). Squares between mutable signs are the softest kind: both sides accommodate, so nothing gets settled and disputes get re-argued indefinitely (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2; `B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin).
- *Virgo: proof against faith.* Silva says good conversations turn into fights because Virgo asks where the proof is while Sagittarius plays the what-if game. Emerson says Virgo can't stand Sagittarius's irresponsible, ever-changing ways. Tierney says Sagittarius lacks Virgo's power of discrimination, and Virgo is also ruled by Mercury, the sign's detriment planet (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8). Silva also calls Virgo one of the few signs that can value the Archer's unfiltered honesty as honesty rather than abrasiveness, a contradiction the substrate keeps.
- *Pisces: a family quarrel over the boundless.* Both are traditionally Jupiter's. Tierney says Pisces is even more receptive than Sagittarius to theories that unify all existence under one universal law. Silva says Pisces, a true water sign, can't take Sagittarian honesty; Emerson calls the match fantastic (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8).
- ⚠️ No book treats either pair as a formal square; the substrate assembled these from compatibility notes and rulership structure (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15).

**Trines (support): Aries and Leo.** Same element, staged as ignition, blaze and long burn. With Aries: matched energy and little planning, though Aries may resent being outlasted. With Leo: outlaw-pair energy until Leo turns possessive and wants a presence the Archer won't promise (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §9). The general warning on trines: so easy they produce complacency (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Sextiles (working partners): Libra and Aquarius.** Tierney says the Archer can't champion its causes alone and needs to work with people of good faith; Libra teaches it tact, since one of its less attractive traits is bluntness about society's shortcomings. Aquarius supplies the planning, documents and budgeting the sign lacks, while Sagittarius helps Aquarius stop caring what others think (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §9, from Tierney and Silva).

**Quincunxes (incomprehension): Taurus and Cancer.** Silva's Taurus line is the most vivid: the pairing is scorched earth, because the Archer sees possessions as chains and anchors (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §8; `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva). The quincunx shares no element, modality or polarity, and Martin describes it as two parties bound together who don't recognize each other (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §3 and §4).

**Neighbors: Scorpio and Capricorn** (semi-sextiles) (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4). Capricorn is named as the most useful working relationship: Tierney says the Goat knows the Archer's many social theories and will put into practice only the few broad ones with practical, timely value; Silva calls Capricorns good advisors to Sagittarians, who may not want to hear the advice (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §9).

## 8. Decans
Emerson is the only source; Silva has no Sagittarius decan chapter (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2 and §5). The decan scheme refines the sign rather than changing it: decan 1 is the sign doubled, decans 2 and 3 blend it with the other two fire signs (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §1).
1. **Jupiter (Sagittarius-Sagittarius): the respected citizen and scholar.** Social and academic standing; optimistic and frank, sometimes tactless; political, intellectual and artistic ambitions; academic pursuits that can last a lifetime; and a risk of dogmatism in belief (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: belief as institution and learning.
2. **Mars (Sagittarius-Aries): the soldier of the spirit and mind.** Needs discovery and challenge; the frankest decan in the zodiac, with no superficiality; brave and headstrong; quickly bored; strong in sport and outdoor spiritual exercise (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: belief as courage and plain speech.
3. **Sun (Sagittarius-Leo): the showperson.** Always after a spontaneous adventure; great pride and integrity; impulsive; a gambler's streak; disappointments that come from the sheer size of its dreams and ambitions (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson). Sub-theme: belief as grand vision and the gamble.

## 11. Where the author's named concepts appear
- **Justice.** Jupiter rules law, and Snodgrass gives it the judiciary (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §10; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV). The ninth house includes law (§1). Jupiter's second kind of luck is a judge-and-jury luck, luck as the lesser of possible consequences (§10). The sign seeks justice for itself by going public and by revenge (§3, from Silva). Its moral compass is built from the wrongs and good deeds of childhood (§7, from Silva). Corruption in the courts is one of the three institutional failures Martin ties to the sign (§3). Jupiter's myth is a rebellion against rule by chaos and fear (§10). All citations here are to Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` unless named.
- **Crime.** Little directly. The nearest material: revenge when wronged (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3, from Silva); institutional corruption and bodily misconduct by clergy (§3, from Martin); negligence that kills, as in Silva's fog example (§3); the secret affair (§3); and Nergal's war and scorched earth (§13). Fire's disdain for rules that apply to ordinary mortals, shown through the tax return, bears on rule-breaking (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin). Nothing on theft, law-breaking or punishment as such.
- **Trust.** Rich. Faith in unseen protective guidance (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1, from Tierney); trust in oneself as the sign's lesson (§5, from Tierney); loyalty given freely, expecting only not to be exploited (§2, from Silva); trusting strangers too easily, with suspicion hard to teach (§7, from Silva); credulity (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2); unreliable commitments and the felt right to change one's mind (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §3; `B_Modalities.md` §4); children who don't lie (§7); calling out the inauthentic (§3); drifting away and returning in friendship (§7). Taken together, the material has the sign giving trust easily and keeping promises badly.
- **Care.** Generosity without keeping score (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §2, from Silva); protecting subordinates and staying in a hated job for coworkers' sake (§6, from Silva); Chiron as healer and teacher, and the wounded healer (§13); Jupiter's extension of belonging beyond the clan to society and history (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin); ego tied to helping society improve (§5, from Tierney). Against this: dropping friends and family who stay stuck (§3, from Silva); repairing only with the inner circle (§3); a temper that leaves sensitive people shaken (§3); trampling feelings without noticing (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2). Nothing on grief or caring for the dying (§12).
- **Honor.** Pride and integrity (decan 3) and the soldier of the spirit and mind (decan 2) (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson); the respected citizen (decan 1); refusing to be bought (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva); seeking allies of good faith and honorable intent (§9, from Tierney); Chiron giving up his immortality for Prometheus (§13). The dishonor side: reneging (§3) and public denunciation that shames its target (§3). The corpus never names honor as a theme of the sign directly.
- **Duty.** Mostly as resistance to it: the horror of blind obedience to authority, refusing routine and desks (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §6, from Silva); reneging when a commitment feels like a trap (§3, from Tierney); poor follow-through and dismissing boring dangers (§3, from Silva). On the other side: children who take pride in finished chores and feel they belong once given a job (§7, from Silva); duty felt sideways to coworkers (§6); rising to authority as soldier or pilot (§6, from Snodgrass); the turn toward the needs of the many (§1, from Tierney). Capricorn, next door, is where the corpus places the practical carrying-out of the Archer's ideas (§9).

## 12. Gaps
- **The ninth house is thin.** The substrate lists the house's domains in one phrase and does not develop them; Martin's house volume is not mined for the ninth house in depth (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §1). Philosophy as a discipline, comparative religion and the content of any doctrine are absent; the material treats belief as temperament.
- **Doubt, conversion and loss of faith** are barely covered. The only trace is Chiron in Sagittarius as a wound in faith (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §13).
- **Grief is absent.** The corpus covers the sign's response to boredom, confinement and insult in detail, and says almost nothing about loss (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15).
- **Scarcity is absent.** Every money and career passage assumes plenty (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15).
- **Maturation is thin.** Beyond fire maturing through its three signs and lifelong study in the first decan, nothing says how the sign changes with age.
- **Little at collective scale.** The books describe individuals and pairs, not groups or institutions; Martin's institutional-scandal note is the main exception (Substrate `00_Method_and_Sources.md`, *What the corpus genuinely does not contain*).
- **Botany is thin**: one flower, from the weakest source, for a sign whose founding myth is a master herbalist (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15).
- **The squares are reconstructed, not sourced** (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15).
- **Decans: one source** (Emerson) (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §5).
- **Disagreements between sources, kept rather than resolved:**
  - Silva rates Aquarius an excellent friendship and a poor romance (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §9, §15).
  - Silva calls Virgo both one of the few who can take the sign's bluntness and a poor long-term fit (§8, §15).
  - Silva and Emerson disagree flatly on Pisces (§8, §15).
  - Silva and Emerson disagree on Gemini, opposites attracting against two restless conquerors (§8).
  - Martin says mutable signs avoid conflict at all costs; Tierney exempts Sagittarius (§10, §15).
  - The three origin myths (teacher, watchman, war god) are incompatible, and Eratosthenes held the figure was a satyr, not a centaur (§13).
  - Saffron (Emerson) against Snodgrass's Sun assignment; the red candle against purple (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Per-Sign Detail).
- **Source cautions.** Tierney is an OCR'd scan, so the substrate's quotations from it were checked against context but carry noise (Substrate `09_Sagittarius.md` §15). Several points in the sign file are the substrate writer's synthesis rather than a book's; they are marked above. Some attributions are unclear (the twelfth-house self-sabotage reading, the judge-and-jury luck, Snodgrass's Lincoln) and are flagged with ⚠️.
