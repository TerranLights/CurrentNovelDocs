Research notes, 2026-10-30. What the GDD's zodiac documentation says about this area of human life, in our own words. Not robot understanding; not Concordia design.

**How to read the citations.** "§" numbers without a file name refer to Substrate `11_Aquarius.md`. A book named after "from" is the one the GDD file credits for the point. The GDD's corpus is eight astrology books: Silva's *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* and *Sun and Moon Signs*, Snodgrass's *Signs of the Zodiac*, Martin's three-volume *Mapping the Psyche*, Tierney's *All Around the Zodiac*, and Emerson's *Behavioral Astrology*. Everything below reports what astrological tradition and these authors claim. None of it is treated as true.

## 1. Areas of life

**The natural house: the eleventh.**
- Astrology gives the eleventh house hopes and aims, causes, friends and colleagues, groups, shared visions, collaborations and togetherness (§10).
- Emerson updates it to networking and to friendships kept at a distance or through a medium (§10, from Emerson).
- Silva calls it the house of social debut: where a person enters society, how society receives them, and whether they fit the conditions they were born and raised in or break away from them. She says a person with a strong eleventh house often ends up in a tribe quite unlike the one they were born into, and may feel like an alien among the original one (§7, from Silva).
- The house has no settled name. Silva's guide calls it the House of Good Spirit and also the House of Divinity; Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* calls it the House of Blessings; Martin calls it Uniting. Silva admits astrologers find it hard to define, because it is as full of contradictions as the sign (§10).

**What astrology assigns to the sign itself.**
- Humanity as a whole, and its improvement through correct ideas (§1).
- Truth-seeking on behalf of everyone, carried back to the community at personal cost (§1, from Silva).
- Ideals, progressive reform and the future (§1, from Tierney; Full Attributes, Aquarius).
- Equality, democracy and opposition to privilege (§3, §8, from Tierney; §12, from Silva).
- Freedom, autonomy and privacy (§5, from Silva).
- The relation between the individual and the group, which is the axis it shares with Leo (§8, from Martin).
- Outsiders, nonconformity, eccentricity and deviation from the norm (§2, §4, from Snodgrass, Silva, Martin).
- Friendship, especially wide friendship chosen among like minds (§4, §5, from Tierney, Martin).
- Free information and opposition to censorship (§2, from Tierney; `A_Elements.md` §4).
- Science, invention, discovery and prediction (§6, §13, from Silva, Snodgrass, Martin).
- Revolution and sudden collective change (§10, §13, from Tierney, Martin).
- Institutions, codes of conduct and committees, through its traditional ruler Saturn (§10, from Silva, Martin, Tierney).

**Work and roles the documentation assigns.**
- Snodgrass: law, labor organization, theater, religion and mediation (§6).
- Silva: mediator, teacher, researcher, trainer, actor, electrician, project manager, scientist, environmental planner, psychologist, politician, musician, manager, social worker, psychiatrist, engineer, financial consultant, investigator and chemist; broadly the fine arts, politics and service, and anything built on discovery and invention, such as technology, astronomy and science. Silva also describes research as a funded project: set a goal, make a plan, secure the money, then do the work (§6, from Silva).
- Snodgrass's planetary appendix names the kinds of people each planet governs. Saturn governs executives of state and also the poor, in the same entry; Uranus governs transportation workers and social workers (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV and Design Note 7, from Snodgrass).

**The Age of Aquarius.** Martin reads the coming age as a shift from a mutable to a fixed cross, whose questions are resources, survival and the role of the individual in the group (§13, from Martin).

## 2. Core meaning

**Archetype: the water bearer.**
- Silva's image: in ancient times the water bearer walked miles to fetch fresh water. Astrology makes this the person willing to go through pain and hardship in search of truth, taking roads others avoid (§1, from Silva).
- The GDD reads the jar as holding collective wisdom rather than water, poured outward to everyone without being asked (§1).
- Silva says the sign knows that bringing truths to light for humanity's benefit may isolate it, and takes that road anyway, bravely (§1, from Silva).
- Snodgrass's keyword entry for how the sign perceives: thoughtful; universalizing; loyalty (§1, from Snodgrass).
- Tierney gives the sign a joking motto about being a misfit at every scale (§1, from Tierney; paraphrased).
- The Full Attributes file calls Aquarius the zodiac's own reformer: committed to a better system in the abstract, sometimes at real cost to warmth toward the particular person (Full Attributes, Aquarius).

**Driving motive: improving the collective by correct thinking.**
- Tierney's image for fixed air is a tank of oxygen, air concentrated and contained and used to keep people conscious. He applies it to the sign's wish to fill society with progressive ideals (§1, from Tierney). The GDD's gloss: the intellect is treated as a public utility, not a private pleasure (§1).

**The built-in split.**
- Martin says the sign has two rulers, Saturn and Uranus, who want opposite things, so the sign is at odds with itself (§1, §10, from Martin). She ties the corpus's most-quoted line about the sign directly to that split: "I love humanity, it's people I can't stand." (§1, from Martin)
- Silva reaches the same structure in a parenting chapter: Aquarius is a sign of poles, made of opposite extremes (§10, from Silva).

**Core need.**
- Mental stimulation, and freedom to pursue it without being watched. Without stimulation the type loses motivation; without solitude it gets irritable and loses its clarity, and it will not explain why it left (§5, from Silva).
- Martin's reading of the Moon in Aquarius names the deeper need: space and detachment, plus honesty, loyalty, truth and equality in friendship. Ideas and ideals are what nourish this person. The emotional bond forms with like minds, and many feel they were born into a family of strangers with whom they share nothing (§5, from Martin). Belonging is chosen and intellectual, not inherited (§5).
- Autonomy is treated as the condition for functioning at all. Silva: for the teenager, autonomy and independence matter most; for the woman, losing her freedom would be the worst punishment; for the man, disrespect for his freedom, values and ideals can send him into a downward spiral (§5, from Silva).
- Privacy is absolute. Silva warns never to enter an Aquarian's private life or space without permission, or the hatred lasts forever (§5, from Silva).
- Boredom ends in indifference: a young Aquarian who is not challenged may grow lazy and stop caring (§5, from Silva).
- The GDD's own synthesis (a reading, not a source): the need the type will not name is to matter to the collective without having to be personally close to anyone in it. The GDD links this to the Sun being in detriment in Aquarius, so that the faculty of being an irreplaceable individual is at its weakest here (§5, §10).

## 3. The area at its best

- **Unpaid altruism.** Silva says the type improves things because it enjoys doing so, not for praise or credit. Snodgrass says the true type avoids showing off and prefers plain honesty (§2, from Silva, Snodgrass).
- **Welcome for outsiders.** Snodgrass: the type is open to and feels for outsiders, notably people of a different class, race or religion (§2, from Snodgrass). Tierney: after Capricorn's insistence on sameness, Aquarius bends the old rules and gives people more freedom of social expression (§2, from Tierney). The Full Attributes file: a friendliness that welcomes many kinds of people with little gatekeeping (Full Attributes, Aquarius).
- **Against censorship.** Tierney names only Gemini and Aquarius as signs that hate censorship (§2, from Tierney).
- **Rules made together.** Silva's advice on raising an Aquarian teen: do not hand down rules; let them help write the rules, so the rules make sense to them and they feel they had real input. The more their autonomy is respected, the more they respect you (§2, from Silva).
- **The underdog's side.** Silva: paired with Pisces, they will champion the group's underdog. They are not judgmental, they look at every side before deciding, and their eye for possibilities makes them tolerant of many viewpoints. Once at ease at work, they help colleagues and take on others' load (§2, from Silva).
- **Follow-through.** Tierney: fixity gives the sign the focus and patience to turn progressive ideas into something solid and lasting (§2, from Tierney). Silva: Saturn's rule makes them more reliable than the other air signs (§10, from Silva).
- **Loyalty without sentiment.** Martin finds real clarity, detached honesty, truthfulness and open friendliness in the sign, and nothing soft or sentimental (§2, from Martin).
- **Large-scale organizing.** Tierney: weak on the small details of a big project, but good at organizing on a large scale and at building social networks (§2, from Tierney).
- **The just.** Manilius, quoted in Snodgrass, has the good, the pious and the just born when Aquarius first pours out his urn (§2, from Snodgrass).
- **Courage for the truth.** Willing to walk the hard, isolating path to bring truth to light (§1, from Silva).
- **Heroes together with the opposite sign.** Silva: Leo and Aquarius are both heroes, and if they fight for the same cause they can bring real change (§8, from Silva).
- **Positive attributes in the reference catalog:** originality and independent thought; humanitarianism that cares for people in the aggregate and systemically, not only one by one; intellectual curiosity with visionary, big-picture thinking; wide, open friendliness; progressive values and comfort with unconventional solutions (Full Attributes, Aquarius; Quick Reference, Aquarius).
- **Uranus at its best.** Silva: higher learning, wisdom, new possibilities, freedom and a passion for change (§10, from Silva). Tierney presents Uranus as an awakening force that drives fast collective growth, accepted only once the shock wears off and the change is seen as progress (§10, from Tierney).
- **Saturn at its best.** Silva (*Sun and Moon Signs*): Aquarius is Saturn's daytime home, where its qualities show at their best rather than their grimmest (§10, from Silva).

## 4. The area gone wrong

Martin's summary of the shadow: the type undervalues ordinary feelings. It has an ideological rigidity, even a totalitarian streak, that can turn autocratic, uncompromising and brutal enough to run over ordinary human concerns. With a built-in sense of superiority, it can look down on ordinary people as too slow, flawed or stupid to appreciate its brilliance (§3, from Martin). The GDD calls this the harshest shadow material in the corpus for any air sign (§3).

**Principles that harm people.**
- **The equality trap.** Tierney: the type decides that being democratic means treating everyone identically, with no exceptions for anyone. He calls this a paradox, since the sign champions individuality but also believes in rules meant to hold for everyone without condition, and so it overlooks the different needs of each member of a team (§3, from Tierney). Silva reaches the same fault separately, filed under the sign's lack of balance: Aquarians forget that a team is made of separate people who each need care (§3, from Silva).
- **Help nobody asked for.** Silva, on friendship: they may take matters into their own hands whether or not anyone asked, and fail to see that "not everybody may want this help." (§3, from Silva) The GDD's reading: the failure is of imagination, not will; it does not occur to them that help can be declined (§3).
- **Unnoticed authoritarianism.** Tierney: the type can be far more domineering than it realizes, and is accused of forcing reforms on others (§3, from Tierney).
- **The big picture over the people in it.** Silva: they focus on the big picture so intensely that they ignore how the people around them feel (§3, from Silva).
- **A person as an experiment.** Silva says, without alarm, that an Aquarian may start a relationship with a Capricorn as an experiment (§3, from Silva).
- **Consent held only for themselves.** The GDD notes that Silva's absolute privacy-and-permission rule is one the type holds about itself; set beside the unrequested-help material, it reads as a consent doctrine never extended outward (§5, the GDD's reading).
- **A utopia of upgraded people.** Tierney imagines that ending the zodiac with Aquarius would mean the future belongs to a race of geniuses, some telepathic, all capable of complex mental work with machine-like exactness, with computer implants and replaceable body parts to hold off aging and death; social tolerance comes as an afterthought (§3, from Tierney). The GDD's reading: this utopia requires everyone to be upgraded first.

**Certainty and contempt.**
- **Opinion held as fact.** Silva: they take their opinion for fact, and once their mind is made up it is almost impossible to change; they often don't notice they are talking down to someone (§3, from Silva).
- **Contempt learned early.** Silva's teen chapter: they are annoyed by people who don't live up to their potential and see the world in black and white. A parent complaining about a hard day at work is heard as proof the parent has sold out, without weighing why the parent works, the paycheck included (§3, from Silva).
- **The answer before the question.** Silva praises Aquarian children for reaching a solution before the problem is even put to them, and calls it clairvoyance (§3, from Silva). The GDD reads this as a conclusion formed before the evidence (§3).
- **Self-righteousness and delusion.** Silva: chasing the impossible brings frustration, dissatisfaction and sometimes depression, and their idealism can make them a little delusional and self-righteous (§3, from Silva). The reference catalog adds self-righteousness about being the one who sees further than everyone else, and contrarianism, sometimes for its own sake (Full Attributes, Aquarius).
- **Talking in slogans.** Martin, correcting a client, says slogans are typically Aquarian because they sound so rational and detached (§4, from Martin).

**Feeling treated as error.**
- Tierney: the type assumes feelings get in the way of clear perception, and that emotion distorts truth in a way a detached mind would not (he adds that this is debatable) (§3, from Tierney). Silva: sometimes they literally cannot cope with intense emotional displays (§3, from Silva).
- Martin: many find it hard to accept their bodies and seem to live in their minds instead (§4, from Martin).
- The reference catalog: emotional detachment, sometimes using principle as an excuse to avoid real intimacy; aloofness that reads as coldness even when none is meant (Full Attributes, Aquarius).
- Air in general can discuss emotion expertly as a subject, which may be a clever defense against feeling anything. It rationalizes a problem away instead of solving it, and leaves when things get messy (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).

**Accountability.**
- **Gone before the consequences.** Martin, to her class: by the time the world catches up, the Aquarian has already moved on to the next vision (§3, from Martin).
- **Successes inflated, failures unmentioned.** Silva, on the Pisces-side cusp: they dislike talking about their failures and can exaggerate their successes (§3, §11, from Silva).
- **Credit wanted quietly.** Against the unpaid altruism above, Silva also says that at work they always want their own contributions recognized (§3, from Silva). The GDD records this as a live contradiction inside Silva (§15).
- **Reform stalled in committee.** Tierney: Saturn-dominated Aquarians send promising social reforms through committee after committee before deciding, and can turn reactionary, less willing than Uranus to change with the times (§3, from Tierney).

**Rupture.**
- **Explosion on rejection.** Tierney: it can explode when people refuse to hear what it believes is genius (§3, from Tierney). Silva: emotions, especially anger, come out in sudden, unpredictable bursts that carry the whole backlog (§3, from Silva).
- **Permanent excommunication.** Silva, twice: Aquarians neither forgive nor forget; once betrayed, the relationship is probably over, at once and for good (§3, from Silva).
- **Withdrawal without notice.** Under stress they leave without warning or explanation, which others read as punishment though none is meant (§6).
- **The wounded refusal.** Martin on Chiron in Aquarius: the one picked on, excluded and rejected by the group, over ideas and opinions, may tell itself it never wanted to belong, which can be a defense against being rejected again (§3, §7, from Martin).

**Weak points.**
- **Gullible.** Silva says a Scorpio colleague's nose for scams and fraud is useful to the gullible Aquarius (§3, from Silva).
- **The radical who keeps the job.** Silva: Aquarius and Leo both want a secure job and constant intellectual stimulation, but won't chase the stimulation at the risk of the job (§3, from Silva).
- **Depression under a cheerful surface.** Emerson's first decan needs quiet time or slides into depression while looking cheerful outside (§3, from Emerson). Snodgrass's table of psychological symptoms lists suicidal tendencies, hypersensitivity and anxiety (§3, from Snodgrass).
- **Unpredictability and stubbornness** (Full Attributes, Aquarius).

**Silva's list of work faults.** They lack focus and show apathy toward tasks outside their interests; they insist on their own way even when it is wrong; they isolate themselves instead of talking to the team, and may go off-grid without explanation; they dislike fixed routines and chafe at deadlines; they care little about money and may ruin their finances (§6, from Silva).

**Dark correspondences.** Medieval place-rulership gives Aquarius sewers and cesspools. In the twelve-apostle correspondence Aquarius is Judas, the insider who broke the group (§3, §13, from Snodgrass).

**Snodgrass's dissent.** Snodgrass alone describes the Aquarian as poor at concentration, slow at reading, dawdling and inclined to go along, with a reputation for slow wits, impracticality and inefficiency (§15, from Snodgrass). See Gaps.

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time

**Everyday presentation.**
- Cool, magnetic and charismatic, engaging with the world from a distance; something unusual about appearance or dress (§4, from Martin).
- Eccentric and glad of the label. Emerson says they all seem to own odd hats and have unusual hobbies; Silva says they would rather be strange than live a boring life (§4, from Emerson, Silva). Silva twice calls it the alien sign, and Martin separates this from individuality: it is deviation or alienation from the norm (§4, from Silva, Martin).
- A huge, shallow social surface: drifts in and out of many lives and claims about a hundred close friends; likable on the surface and truly open to almost nobody (§4, from Tierney, Silva).
- Understated dress built from unique, recombined pieces, with minimal jewelry and nothing opulent; flashy or expensive gifts are a mistake (§4, from Silva).
- Witty and sarcastic; others must learn when they are joking; they claim to spot liars at once (§4, from Silva).
- Ideas pull them off on tangents (§4, from Silva).
- Likes groups but hates feeling crowded, and needs as much social space as Sagittarius. Volunteers readily for friends in need, especially when something unusual is happening in the friend's life, and quickly sizes up people who would use them (§6, from Tierney).

**At work.**
- Martin's bus-stop scene: a late bus prompts Aquarius to plan how the whole public transport system could be restructured, and to blame Taurus for taking the delay calmly instead of joining the cause for better transport (§6, from Martin). A small local failure turns into a call for total redesign.
- Silva recommends that an Aquarian employee work alone with minimal contact: a short briefing in the morning, a short meeting at day's end, and the middle of the day alone (§6, from Silva).
- Workspaces stay bare, because they don't believe surroundings affect productivity (§6, from Silva).
- Silva says neither Aquarius nor Aries handles human relations well, and both need a water or earth sign to advise them or deal with people problems (§6, §9, from Silva).

**Under stress and in conflict.**
- Withdraws suddenly and silently (§6).
- Digs in and refuses to yield when pressured or challenged (§6, from Martin).
- Bad temper and poor anger management are real problems, especially for those under heavy pressure (§6, from Silva).
- Fixed signs in general resist pressure from outside: change has to come from within, and interference from the sidelines is unwelcome. Tierney adds that all fixed signs share a built-in pride (`B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney, Martin).

**In crisis.**
- Unlike the other fixed signs, which hold tight to what they have, Aquarius can suddenly overturn its own behavior when conditions are right (§6, §10, from Tierney; `A_Elements.md` §4). The GDD notes that to others this can look like betraying everything it just defended (§6).

**When secure.**
- At ease, it helps colleagues and lightens others' load (§2, from Silva).
- With enough stimulation and solitude it is clear and precise; without them, cranky and unmotivated (§5, from Silva).

**Over a lifetime.**
- **Childhood.** Silva's Aquarian children take toys apart to learn how they work (§7, from Silva). They are good listeners who sense how others feel without being told, so well that parents are advised to soften discipline (§7, from Silva). They excel in subjects that interest them and may fail the rest, so they get labeled apathetic or dumb; Silva names laziness as what holds them back (§7, §15, from Silva). Ordering or forcing them fails: they won't comply, or will comply and come to despise you. Advice must not feel like an obligation, or they feel patronized (§7, from Silva).
- **The empathy contradiction.** Silva's child chapter makes empathy the sign's gift, while every adult chapter makes detachment its fault. The GDD reads the adult coldness as acquired rather than native (§7, §15).
- **Adolescence.** Teens are lone wolves who float between groups rather than joining one (§7, from Silva), and they judge in black and white (§3, from Silva).
- **Martin's two halves of life.** Saturn dominates the first half: a sense of inadequacy and a fear of being exposed or ridiculed, so the young behave and dress like everyone else to hide their separateness. Uranus takes over later, and they grow comfortable being who they are and start to enjoy deviating from the norm. One of Martin's students dates the change to her Saturn return (§7, from Martin; `F_Rulerships.md` §2).
- **Belonging conditioned on ideas.** Martin's Chiron material: many attempts to be accepted, and many painful experiences of exclusion over ideas and opinions (§7, from Martin). Silva, in the Virgo chapter: Aquarius is most receptive once convinced you agree with it on a lot, and tends to draw boundaries along political and social views (§7, from Silva).
- **Health over time.** Snodgrass: healthy and energetic until overwork or an accident takes its toll; the headstrong type tends to seek unconventional treatment or to self-medicate (§12, from Snodgrass).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations

- **Ganymede.** The Trojan prince Zeus carried off in the form of an eagle (Silva: a huge bird) to serve as cupbearer to the gods; the father was compensated with horses. Snodgrass keeps two readings side by side: Roman ceiling art shows the boy struggling as Zeus carries him off, while Christian writers reread the scene as the soul rising to heaven and gave him a calm, cherub-like composure. Silva tells it as romance: Zeus lost his heart to the boy and took him. Three tellings of one abduction: assault, ascent, romance (§13, from Snodgrass, Silva). The GDD's reading: the person who proves useful to power is not asked.
- **Deucalion.** The water bearer first stood for the Greek Noah, the man who survived the great flood, before Ganymede displaced him (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Ea and Hapi.** Babylon's Ea carried a vase of water, and Aquarius ruled a region of the sky called the Sea. Egypt tied the sign to Hapi and the Nile flood; Egyptians held that the flood came when Aquarius dipped his jar into the river. Fertility arriving as a flood (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Prometheus, inverted.** In the correspondence between the signs and the labors of Hercules, Aquarius matches the eagle that tore at Prometheus's liver for stealing fire from heaven to warm humanity. The sign is matched to the punishment, not the thief, and Snodgrass's source calls Prometheus the villain (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Judas and the angel.** In the twelve-apostle scheme Aquarius is Judas. In the fixed cross (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius) it is Matthew's angel, the only human figure beside the bull, lion and eagle. Traitor and angel in one sign (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Weather forecasting.** About 475 B.C. an Athenian wrote a weather almanac tying forecasts to Aquarius, the earliest link between this sign and empirical prediction (§13, from Snodgrass; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).
- **Revolution and science, born together.** Martin dates the last big Uranian surge to the late 18th century: the discovery of Uranus in 1781 and the French, American and Scientific Revolutions, when ideals such as equality and the brotherhood of man arose alongside the modern scientific method (§13, from Martin).
- **Verse and satire.** Wedeck's rhyme pictures Aquarius as one who lends everyone a hand but bows to no command, understands higher laws, and is inventor, genius and superman. Petronius's zodiac banquet serves a wild goose for Aquarius and mocks the host's glib sign-reading, a warning built into the corpus against taking any of this too seriously (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **The Age of Aquarius.** The song from *Hair* (1966); New Age hopes of tranquility, harmony and peace; Martin's structural reading (see §1 above) (§13).
- **Dissolution.** At the handover to Pisces, Snodgrass reads the sign's outflow as release and breaking down, a freeing of spirit from body; the sign is traditionally tied to both death and renewal (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **The glyph.** The double wavy line. Silva reads it as choppy water or a lightning bolt, and as a wish for equality (two parallel waves, neither above the other). Martin reads it as air or radio waves, information traveling through the air (§12, from Silva, Martin).
- **The urn.** The amphora; an Egyptian variant shows two jars, one on each shoulder, which the GDD links to the sign's duality. Snodgrass notes that gushing water became a standard unifying horizontal motif in Chinese, Celtic, Semitic, Amerindian and European art (§12, from Snodgrass).
- **An idea of a person.** Martin notes the air signs are the only ones among the zodiac's figures shown as human or abstract rather than as animals. Silva notes Aquarius is one of only two humanoid figures in the wheel, and even then stands only for the idea of a man (§10, from Martin, Silva). The sign of humanity is pictured by a concept of a person.
- **Star names.** Sadalmelik (the king's lucky star); a star that signals rain; Situla (the bucket); an Arabic name meaning the lucky star of hiding places; the Hindu name meaning the hundred physicians; the Chinese name meaning the void (§12, from Snodgrass).

## 7. Structure

**Element: air, not water.** Silva and Snodgrass both stress that despite the water imagery, Aquarius is an air sign (§10; `A_Elements.md` §4).
- What air means: it abstracts, compares and articulates, and keeps a deliberate distance from the material. Height is the point: from above, details blur but the pattern shows (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney). Martin calls the air signs the most civilized, social, detached, abstract and rational, in fact the most human of the signs (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).
- Air locates truth outside, in the systems of the external world; water locates it inside, in emotional undercurrents shared by everyone. Air and water contest collective truth (`A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin).
- Air is good at perspective, negotiation, arbitration and standards, insists on clear language, and can hold two opposed positions at once (`A_Elements.md` §4).
- Air's blind spot is feeling, its own and other people's, and its shadow is rationalizing problems away. Martin's example: shaking hands after a dispute settles the form and leaves the feeling untouched (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).
- Air needs access to information; Tierney says censorship and illiteracy stunt it. It dreads social isolation (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney).
- Aquarius within air: fixed air, the oxygen tank; its fixity lives in ideas and ideals, not possessions (`A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney).
- Element relations. With water: the opposite pole on the truth axis; each is the other's medicine, and Martin says water is air's shadow. With earth: procedural conflict, two sides talking past each other; Silva has Capricorn finding Aquarius unreliable and flaky, and Aquarius finding Capricorn slow. With fire: air feeds fire, and can also kill a fire idea with a fact. Two air signs produce endless ideas and avoid feeling (`A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin, Silva).

**Modality: fixed.** The middle of winter, February (§10).
- Fixed signs resist and stabilize. They do not refuse change; they refuse to be moved from outside. Their strength is finishing, holding and enduring. Their failure is stalemate, and resisting easier solutions because those came from outside (`B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney, Martin).
- Aquarius is the oddball of the fixed group: it holds an idea rather than a possession, and it can suddenly overturn its course (`B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney).
- Two fixed signs in conflict entrench; it is the one collision with no built-in exit (`B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin, Silva).

**Polarity: active (positive, masculine, yang).** Outward-moving; Martin links it to Jung's extraversion (`C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin). Silva's counterweight: the sign really is outward-facing, yet reads as withdrawn, because its outward direction points at society rather than at people (§10, from Silva).

**Opposite sign: Leo. The axis runs between the individual and the group.**
- Martin: the Leo-Aquarius axis is the relationship between the individual and the group, "between what is right for me, as an individual, and what is right for everyone else." (§8, from Martin) Both ends are fixed, so neither pole is willing to adapt (§8, from Martin).
- The Sun rules Leo and is in detriment in Aquarius, so each end holds exactly the faculty the other lacks (§8, §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §5). Under modern rulership, Uranus is in detriment in Leo (`F_Rulerships.md` §2). The axis has no exaltation traffic; the opposition is purely ruler against detriment (`F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- Tierney: Aquarius has little patience for people who act like stars and think themselves above the rest, so it can be an enemy of royalty and aristocracy (§8, from Tierney). Emerson: Aquarius won't take time from its creations and inventions to dote on Leo (§8, from Emerson). Silva: Aquarius seems to exist to bring down the king of the signs, yet both are heroes who can change the world if they fight for the same cause; even when they get along, apart they realize they know little about each other (§8, from Silva).
- Martin's myth for the axis is the Fisher King. When the Aquarius pole is cut off from the Leo pole, the king lies wounded, the land stops bearing and the whole realm is stuck. What breaks the spell is Percival, the Leo role, a guileless newcomer who doesn't know the rules (§8, from Martin). The GDD's gloss: a collective cut off from the individual heart doesn't explode; it goes sterile.
- In general, an opposition tempts each side to identify with one pole and project the other onto outside people or events (`D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Ruling planets: Saturn (traditional) and Uranus (modern).**
- **Saturn.** Self-discipline, order and method; a personal code of conduct governs even the most outlandish Aquarian, though it is their own code, not other people's (§10, from Silva). Saturn cares what others think; it brings caution, reserve and sensitivity to being judged stupid, and eventually authority on one's own terms. Martin often finds it in the charts of scientists and strategists (§10, from Martin). As a faculty: limits, structure, time and law, the capacity to say no and make it stick (`F_Rulerships.md` §3).
- **Uranus.** Tierney's Uranus opposes the status quo and drives rapid collective growth; it is explosive but does not sustain a campaign for long, especially once another route opens (§10, from Tierney). Silva: higher learning, wisdom, new possibilities, freedom and a passion for change (§10, from Silva). Martin: Uranus marks where we refuse to engage (`F_Rulerships.md` §2, from Martin). As a faculty: refusal and disruption, foresight and invention, the capacity not to take part (`F_Rulerships.md` §3).
- **The split.** Martin: the two are opposites in almost every respect (Saturn cares what people think; Uranus does not) and they pull apart, so the forces of change and stasis are always in confrontation (§10, from Martin). Martin also reads them as a sequence over a life (see §5 above).
- **Dignities.** Domicile Saturn (modern: Uranus); no exaltation; detriment, the Sun; no fall (`F_Rulerships.md` §5). The Sun as a faculty is identity and named authority, someone willing to put their name to a decision (`F_Rulerships.md` §3). Martin: ideally the Sun lets individual values and self-expression balance the sign's collective concerns, which is exactly what does not come naturally (§10, from Martin). Silva: Leos love attention; Aquarians prefer to stay subtle (§10, from Silva).
- ⚠️ Derived, not sourced: the GDD's capability reading holds that, with individual worth the weakest faculty present, this type's collective structures can spend a person on a program without registering the loss (`F_Rulerships.md` §6, Aquarius entry; restated here without its Concordia framing).

**Squares (same tempo, different subject): Taurus and Scorpio.** Both are fixed squares, so they entrench rather than resolve (`D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, §4).
- **Taurus.** Emerson: it works only with great effort and patience. Silva: a clash of value systems. Taurus stands for the body, the senses, possessions and the home; Aquarius is the disembodied mind that finds contentment with present conditions morally suspect. In Martin's bus scene Aquarius blames Taurus for not being angry. Silva's workable division: Aquarius develops and promotes, Taurus handles the money and makes the space fit to work in (§8, from Emerson, Silva, Martin).
- **Scorpio.** Silva: their common ground is mostly stubbornness and a love of debate. Emerson: Scorpio's rigid ways can't accept Aquarius's need for movement and freedom. Martin: for air, truth is out there; for water, in here. Scorpio buries what it learns, Aquarius publishes it. Scorpio's jealousy and intensity push Aquarius away, yet Scorpio's intuition for fraud protects the gullible Aquarius (§8, from Silva, Emerson, Martin).

**Trines (same element, easy): Gemini and Libra.**
- **Gemini.** Both hate censorship and need new faces (Tierney). They understand almost everything about each other, though Aquarius may give others too much attention and leave Gemini feeling overlooked (Emerson). Together: many ideas, poor execution (Silva) (§9).
- **Libra.** A natural conversation partner with endless ideas (Silva); they can work out rough spots (Emerson), though it may cool if Aquarius grows distant. Libra wants unions society endorses and will not go along with the group-marriage experiment Aquarius sometimes talks about (Tierney). At work, Libra handles clients and Aquarius stands up to authority figures and pushy people (Silva) (§9).
- The air grand trine's failure, per Martin: mental inertia, the eternal student; detachment mistaking itself for action (`D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §5, from Martin).

**Sextiles (same orientation, working partners): Sagittarius and Aries.**
- **Sagittarius.** Both love independence, freedom and plenty of new friends (Emerson). Tierney suggests the two should see the world together and enjoy cultural difference. Aquarius takes long-term projects, Sagittarius short-term; Sagittarius is annoyed that Aquarius fails to notice changes (Silva) (§9).
- **Aries.** Both respect freedom; trouble comes when Aries acts abruptly and Aquarius needs more time and security (Emerson). Neither handles human relations (Silva) (§9).

**Quincunxes (nothing shared, mutual incomprehension): Virgo and Cancer.**
- **Virgo.** Emerson: Virgo wants to tell Aquarius what to do and limit its freedom, and finds Aquarius too irresponsible and unstable. Silva softens it into mutual respect: each finds the other's methods annoying (inefficient versus stuffy), and the work is still very productive (§8).
- **Cancer.** Emerson: Aquarius's free spirit can't meet Cancer's constant need for love and reassurance. Silva names what Cancer has that Aquarius lacks: championing a cause, long-term planning and delegating (§8).

**Semi-sextiles (adjacent, nothing shared): Capricorn and Pisces.** Tierney frames Aquarius as the reaction to Capricorn's homogeneity; Emerson says the two fascinate each other at first and then decline (§8). Silva's Aquarius-Pisces pair champions the group's underdog (§2). (Aspect types from `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4.)

## 8. Decans

Emerson is the only source, so this is one voice, not a consensus (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2, §5). Silva's guide has no Aquarius decan chapter (`E_Decans.md` §5).
1. **First decan, Uranus (Aquarius itself).** Nonconformist and free thinker with high moral standards and a deep wish to improve society; group-oriented and sociable, yet needs quiet time or falls into depression behind a cheerful face; insightful, reasonable and impartial (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2). Sub-theme: the pure reformer, and the hidden cost of constant social cheer.
2. **Second decan, Mercury (Gemini flavor).** Lighter in spirit, bright and alert; loves culture, art and science; absorbs and reinterprets ideas easily; drawn to abstract theory and fascinated by the invisible forces at work in communication and social life; seen by some as cold or too objective (§11). Sub-theme: the theory of society and communication.
3. **Third decan, Venus (Libra flavor).** Refined and sociable; may lack emotional depth but has public appeal and suits politics or speaking for others; strong humanitarian instincts, always advocating; style and grace; a touch of narcissism (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2). Sub-theme: the public face of a cause.

**The two edges (cusps), from Silva, a separate scheme.**
- **Capricorn edge (Silva's Cusp of Imagination and Mystery).** Inner life matters more than outer life, which feels confining and dull, with a vague ennui; competitive, and harsh in critique (Silva advises softening the blows); needs security and freedom at once; wants to change the world for the better but is often unrealistic (§11, from Silva).
- **Pisces edge (Silva's Cusp of Sensitivity).** Sensual, musical and artistic; takes the slightest criticism personally and becomes quarrelsome; secretive and escapist; open-minded until its own beliefs are questioned, then quickly narrow; bonds only with people of similar intelligence, so struggles to keep relationships; hides failures and inflates successes (§11, from Silva).

**Aquarius as a flavor in other signs' decans.** Gemini's third decan (Uranus) is the most detached of the Gemini decans yet the most humanitarian, relating to all kinds of people. Libra's second decan (Uranus) is the deep Libra, absorbed in how the mind and society work, wanting both commitment and solitude (`E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson, Silva).

## 11. Where the author's named concepts appear

- **Justice.** The sign's justice ideal is equality as identical treatment, without privilege, and Tierney and Silva both show it failing people whose needs differ (§3). Manilius has the just born under Aquarius (§2). Snodgrass lists law and mediation among its vocations (§6). Silva: not judgmental, weighs every side before concluding, champions the underdog (§2). It opposes aristocracy and privilege (§8), and its glyph is read as equality (§12). On the other side: opinion held as fact, black-and-white judgment, and a personal code that overrides other people's (§3, §10).
- **Crime.** Little directly. The closest material: the sign is matched to the punishment for Prometheus's theft of fire, with Prometheus called the villain (§13); Judas the betrayer (§13); Aquarius as gullible and easily caught by scams and fraud (§3); the abduction of Ganymede (§13); reforms forced on people (§3). Nothing on lawbreaking as such.
- **Trust.** Privacy may be entered only with permission, and a trespass is never forgiven (§5). Betrayal ends a relationship at once and for good (§3). They claim to spot liars instantly and size up users quickly (§4, §6), yet Silva calls them gullible (§3). Open on the surface, truly open to almost nobody (§4). Loyalty is part of their core need and appears in Snodgrass's keywords (§1, §5). Judas: the trusted insider who breaks the group (§13). Silent withdrawal reads to others as punishment (§6).
- **Care.** Care for people in the aggregate rather than one by one (Full Attributes, Aquarius). Unpaid altruism (§2). Help imposed without being asked (§3). Volunteering weighted toward interesting cases (§6). The team forgotten as separate people (§3). Inability to handle intense emotion or human-relations work, which they are advised to hand to others (§3, §6). Childhood empathy that the adult chapters deny (§7). Ideas, not nurture, are what feed this type (§5).
- **Honor.** A personal code of conduct governs even the most outlandish Aquarian (§10). Honesty without showing off (§2). The inborn pride of the fixed signs (`B_Modalities.md` §3). Wedeck's figure who bows to no command (§13). Recognition: it claims not to want credit yet wants its contributions recognized (§2, §3). Loyalty without sentiment (§2). The other side: failures hidden, successes inflated (§3).
- **Duty.** Duty points at the collective and the cause: walking the hard path for humanity (§1); being public-spirited and joining the cause, with blame for those who merely cope (§6). Saturn brings reliability and follow-through (§2, §10). Against that: aversion to routine and deadlines, apathy toward tasks outside its interests, going off-grid (§6); keeping the job rather than risking it (§3); moving on before the consequences arrive (§3).

## 12. Gaps

- **Thin on the house itself.** The eleventh house gets a list of domains and Silva's social-debut reading, but no developed account of hopes and wishes, of groups as such, or of friendship beyond compatibility notes. Even its name is disputed (§10).
- **Little at collective scale.** The corpus describes individual psychology and one-to-one compatibility far more than how groups, institutions or movements behave (`00_Method_and_Sources.md`). For a sign about the collective, this is the main hole.
- **The type in power.** Every source pictures Aquarius as outsider, rebel or reformer, never as the establishment. Beyond two lines on keeping a secure job, there is nothing on this type holding authority (§15).
- **The people a reformer acts on.** Nothing on their consent, on review of reforms by those affected, or on what this type does with someone in its keeping who cannot leave (§15, restated without its Concordia framing).
- **No dedicated chapter on the sign's needs in Silva;** the core need is assembled from scattered child, teen, man and woman passages (§15).
- **Decans rest on Emerson alone and the cusps on Silva alone,** with no cross-check (§15; `E_Decans.md` §5).
- **Disagreements and contradictions:**
  - Snodgrass alone calls the type slow, poor at concentration and inefficient, against a corpus that calls it brilliant. The GDD suggests Snodgrass may preserve an older, Saturn-era reading, and finds a partial bridge inside Silva: brilliant where interested and labeled lazy elsewhere, gullible, and inefficient in Virgo's eyes (§15).
  - Inside Silva: indifference to credit against wanting recognition; empathic children against detached adults (§15).
  - The per-sign file says Emerson's Aquarius correspondence block is truncated (no tarot, oil, chakra or crystal), but `G_Correspondences.md` recovered the full block (garnet, kava kava, lime, third eye, The Star). The per-sign gap is closed.
  - Constellation size: the per-sign file calls Aquarius the tenth-largest constellation, while Snodgrass's Appendix II as transcribed in `G_Correspondences.md` ranks it second by area ⚠️ (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix II).
  - The almanac writer's name is spelled two ways: Eustemon in §13, Euctemon in `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I ⚠️.
  - Silva splits the stones by gender; the GDD drops the gendering (§12).
  - The modality sources disagree on whether the fixed or the mutable signs are the ones that finish what others start (`B_Modalities.md` §8).
- **Weak correspondences:** the chakra column is unverified, the oil conflicts with Snodgrass, the plants are medicinal and dietary only (no flowers or trees), and Uranus's shape is a reconstruction (§15; `G_Correspondences.md`).
- **Tierney is an OCR'd scan.** His points here are paraphrased from the GDD's cleaned readings and should be checked against the book before anyone relies on their exact sense (§15).
