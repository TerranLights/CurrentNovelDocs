## Borderline
- `struggle`: the sign's emblems (scarab, raised sword) are read as images of struggle, but the notes give no human situation behind it.
- `disease`: the crab names the tumor, and the notes read it as protective holding that consumes what it holds. That point is filed under `smothering`; disease itself is a name association.
- `facade`: the historical exemplar put imposing buildings and money in place of personal pleasures. Covered by `bluff` and `charity`; it could stand on its own.
- `foreboding`: the gate of death and the ominous sleep belong to the constellation's lore more than to any human habit. Partly covered by `death` and `worry`.
- `keepsake`: an object rather than an idea; the practice is under `custodianship`.
- `attention`: wanting attention when needed and a visible reaction appears in passing (§5, §7). Folded into `reassurance` and `indifference`.
- `selective hearing`: expecting to be told only what it can handle emotionally (§4, no book named). Could be a concept; it is close to `hinting`.
- `absorption`: taking in surroundings without filters. Folded into `sensitivity`; it could be its own entry (for example `porousness`).
- `receptivity`: the receptive polarity (holding, absorbing, not passive). It is close to astrology mechanics, so it is left out.
- `consent`: silence taken as consent appears only as part of the receptive mode's failures.
- `adolescence`: named only as a time when the need for privacy spikes. Folded into `privacy` and `childhood`.
- `career`: careers arrive sideways. Folded into `indirectness`.
- `partnership`: the opposite sign works as a partnership once responsibilities are divided. Too generic to stand alone here.
- `public figure` and `worker`: the Moon's governance column names them, but the notes give no human meaning beyond the label.
- `crime`: the notes say outright that nothing here concerns crime. The nearest points are `duplicity` and `revenge`.
- `grief`: listed in a §10 heading next to sadness and sorrow but never developed. Folded into `sadness`.
