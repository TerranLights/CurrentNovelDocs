## 1. Areas of life
Astrology, as the documentation reports it, gives Leo and its natural house (the fifth) these domains:
- **The fifth house:** recreation, play, creative self-expression, romance, children, gambling and pleasure. The substrate's gloss: not the house of work produced, but of what makes a person glad to be alive (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10).
- **Creativity and self-expression.** Snodgrass's keyword pair for Leo is *I create* and *assurance* (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Snodgrass). In Rudhyar's cycle of phases, via Martin, Leo is the bud, and its verb is *expressing* (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Martin v3).
- **Performance, drama and spectacle.** The zodiac's performer, with a natural gift for drama (Full Attributes, Leo). Snodgrass links Leo to the arts, high fashion, high-church religion, evangelism and theater (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Identity and the self.** The Sun stands for ego, willpower, the need for recognition, integrity and self-dignity (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10). As a faculty, it is identity and named authority: the capacity to be a center others organize around (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Recognition, attention and status.** Being seen; honors, titles, luxury (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §4–5, from Silva).
- **Leadership and authority.** Leadership that inspires rather than commands (Full Attributes, Leo). Snodgrass's planet table gives the Sun *business executives and authority figures* (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix IV).
- **Pride and dignity** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2–3; `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Generosity and patronage** (Full Attributes, Leo; Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2–3, from Silva and Snodgrass).
- **Celebration and occasion-making.** Turning small moments into events (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2; ⚠️ book not named).
- **Love and romance.** Passionate love is on Silva's list of what Leo needs (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5, from Silva).
- **Children and parenting,** both raising Leo children and Leo as a parent (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7, from Silva and Snodgrass).
- **Gambling and risk** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Liberty** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Martin and Silva).

## 2. Core meaning
- **Archetype: the Lion.** Warmth and radiance made into a personality; generous with its light but needing to be seen giving it (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Driving motive, in the substrate's words:** not a wish for attention but a need to be witnessed being itself, so that going unwitnessed feels like being wronged (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1).
- **Birthright.** Tierney describes the Lion as feeling born to shine and to enjoy high status, acting as if first-class treatment were owed to it (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Tierney). His motto for the sign is the show must go on, with Leo as the star (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **The center of one's own myth.** Martin calls Leo the special, unique *divine child* of the zodiac, for whom the most important world is the drama in which it plays the lead (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Not self-sufficient.** Tierney: Leo cannot live only for itself; it must pour its vitality into others, ideally an audience of loyal fans (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1, from Tierney). The substrate adds that the sign depends on an audience it can also look down on (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §1).
- **Core need, by Silva's list:** attention and the spotlight, genuine respect, passionate love, true friendship and luxury (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5, from Silva).
- **Core need, by Martin's deeper answer:** authenticity, integrity and authority "not dependent on the opinions of others" (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5; `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin). The substrate reads the whole shadow as what the sign does while it lacks this (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5).
- **Self-repair.** Snodgrass: in despondent moments the lion is said to quiet doubt by renewing faith in itself (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5, from Snodgrass).

## 3. The area at its best
- **Warmth and generosity,** given freely rather than as a trade (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Confidence that draws people in** (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Creativity, drama and spectacle** (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Loyalty** to those it loves (Full Attributes, Leo). Tierney calls Leo the most dependable fire sign and says its flame is hard to put out once lit; Emerson notes the Sun never goes retrograde, which gives the sign a name for consistency and loyalty (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Tierney and Emerson; `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Bringing out others' talents.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*: Leos find worth in others, and will point out a virtue you neglect or a talent you have not developed and help you make the most of it (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Silva). The Full Attributes file likewise credits Leo with encouraging others to shine alongside it (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Lifting morale.** Tierney calls it a great morale booster; Silva says Leo may not organize the team but will motivate everyone in the room, inspiring a crowd where administrative skill is lacking (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Inspiring leadership** (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Making occasions.** Housewarmings, welcomes, farewells: the sign turns small moments into events (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2; ⚠️ book not named).
- **An honesty code.** Keeps promises, pays debts, and turns a commitment down up front rather than raise false hopes (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2; ⚠️ book not named).
- **Love of liberty.** Martin: Leo would rather be leaned on than lean. Silva, on Leo as parent: never used to being shut in, Leos understand the value of liberty (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Martin and Silva).
- **Dignity under exposure.** Snodgrass's example of Leo at its best is Joseph Merrick, whose life was a spectacle forced on him, praised for keeping dignity and decorum and being courteous and generous to his admirers (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Snodgrass).
- **Fire's general gifts:** starting what nothing else would start, warmth that spreads, morale as a real output, and the faith to get up again after crashing (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Fixed endurance:** staying power, patience, loyalty, finishing what was started (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).

## 4. The area gone wrong
The Full Attributes file's pattern applies: the negatives are the same traits pushed past their useful range (Full Attributes, Worth Your Attention).
- **Applause as a substitute for self-worth.** Martin's central failure mode: the Leo who does not truly believe in itself cannot shine from its own heart, and may grow desperate for approval, demanding recognition and praise in place of real self-worth (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Humiliation as the mortal wound.** Martin: "The worst thing that can happen to a Leo is humiliation." Repeated humiliation in childhood makes Leos close their hearts; they can be crushed and have their sense of specialness destroyed (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Martin). Silva: correct a Leo in public and you are on its bad side for good; correct it in private and it will accept it (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Other people as props.** Tierney doubts the Lion sees others as more than extensions of itself, like props on a stage, loving most those who make it look best (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Shutting out rivals.** Silva: someone who looks, dresses or performs better is a serious threat, and Leo would sooner keep them away than risk losing attention (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva). The substrate notes the rival is not attacked, only left out.
- **Arrogance felt as confidence.** Silva: Leos do not see their pompousness and arrogance, taking it for self-confidence; Tierney adds that Leo will not give itself a bad review (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva and Tierney). Arrogance and vanity head the Full Attributes negatives (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Flattery and favors.** Snodgrass: among flatterers the lion glows with self-importance and showers them with favors, though the substrate notes Leo also has a sharp nose for fake compliments (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Need for constant validation** that exhausts the people supplying it (Full Attributes, Leo).
- **Contempt for service and for thrift.** Silva: Leo would hate serving others, will snap at a rude customer even at the cost of the job, and judges people who cannot afford to give it expensive things (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Refusing the cleanup.** Tierney: every party leaves mess for someone else, and the Lion has no interest in restoring order or in ordinary, low-profile tasks (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Tierney). Martin's party image: fire signs in full flow while the Virgos empty ashtrays and wash up; fire expects to be waited on, served and cleaned up after (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2 and §6, from Martin).
- **Complacency, display and mercilessness in conflict.** Snodgrass: against an adversary the invincible Leo grows complacent while others fight; when it does go to war it strides to the front as a show of might, and at the moment of victory it typically shows no mercy. He also lists pomposity, attention-seeking, victimizing the vulnerable, laziness and insensitivity to the weak or to followers (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Using fear; fighting on because the fight began.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* (Leo Moon): knowing people fear it, Leo uses that, and keeps fighting simply because the fight had already started (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Insecurity that gets louder.** When a Leo does not know something, insecurity spikes and the display grows; silence from others makes it suspect something is wrong (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Draining others.** Tierney: unchecked, Leo's output uses up others' energy until they are exhausted, like a sunburn (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Tierney). Fire in general can drain the people around it while needing outside fuel itself (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9, from Tierney).
- **All or nothing health.** Snodgrass: robust enough to exercise to the limits of endurance, then in illness swinging to collapse (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Spending for status.** Chronic overspending; emptying savings for something that signals status; finding a way to shop even when broke (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3; ⚠️ book not named).
- **Stubbornness, domineering, melodrama.** Stubbornness once an opinion forms; leadership tipping into control; treating ordinary stakes as a stage production; self-centeredness that crowds out others' moments; vulnerability to criticism (Full Attributes, Leo; Quick Reference, Leo).
- **Fire's distortion for Leo.** Martin: when fire's vision goes unfed it distorts rather than dies, and Leo's distortion is to become demanding and jealous (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Fire's lack of stamina.** Martin: fire signs can be oddly lacking in stamina, worn down by the world's resistance until they seem put out (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Fire's blind spots:** objective facts, the body, and other people's inner lives; fire can trample feelings without noticing, with no malice, which is why it is seldom corrected (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Active mode's failures:** motion taken for progress, depletion without inner reserves, dominance drift, and overreach (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Fixed mode's failures:** stalemate, battles of will, and resisting the easier solution because someone else proposed it (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Approval dependence in the pure type.** Emerson's first decan is usually content but can become totally dependent on others' approval (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Never admitting fault.** Emerson's third decan drives people mad by refusing to admit it is wrong (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Snodgrass's pathology table:** psychological symptoms arrogance, obsession, impulsiveness (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Leo, Appendix V).
- **Saturn in detriment.** Restraint, limits and structure sit badly; people with Saturn in Leo must go the extra mile for balance and may overcorrect into self-criticism or inhibition (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time
**At work.**
- Places itself near whoever confers status, makes its work conspicuous, and sulks or throws tantrums when not credited (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6; ⚠️ book not named, probably Silva).
- Prefers the clever route to the hard one: gets subordinates to do a task or finds a simpler fix (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).
- Charms the decision-maker and may win a promotion over a better performer (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).
- Refuses subordinate roles outright; a job with a permanent supervisor is intolerable (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).
- Procrastinates out of boredom yet still delivers on time (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).

**Socially.**
- Steals the show, connects people, keeps a large network with very few close friends; Snodgrass: a pack of acquaintances but few bosom friends (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6, from Snodgrass).
- Hosts compulsively; flirts with servers for free drinks and a little notice; leaves early if under-attended, stays all night if made much of (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).
- Enters a gathering as if a red carpet were being rolled out; walks, Martin says, as if wearing a crown, sometimes with something catlike in the step; Snodgrass notes showy dress and hair, a sturdy frame and regal posture (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §4, from Martin and Snodgrass).
- Silva on Leo design: buildings by Leos are the loudest and most eye-catching on the street and rarely blend into their surroundings unless asked to (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §4, from Silva).
- Insists on titles; expects guests to dress well but not better than the host; leaves any room where attention has dried up (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §4; ⚠️ book not named).
- Friendship forms fast and shallow: once Leos click with someone, that person is a friend or at least a contact. The inner circle is very small and admits only people who can hear the worst as well as the best. The way in is to catch Leo's attention within a group, not to ask for a private meeting (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7; ⚠️ book not named, probably Silva).

**Under stress.**
- Silva: when work and social life get too much, Leos disappear at home for a couple of days until their energy returns, and are starting something new within a day (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6, from Silva).

**In conflict and crisis.**
- Two accounts that conflict. Tierney: on real failure Leo is crushed and heartbroken, and may roar with hurt and anger when it misses the top prize. Snodgrass: it strides to the front as display, then shows no mercy at the moment of victory (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6, from Tierney and Snodgrass).
- Martin: if Leo stops believing in itself, or takes the easy way, everything starts to go wrong, and the collapse is total (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6, from Martin).
- Fixed signs under pressure dig in and outlast tension rather than resolve it; Tierney names pride as common to all fixed signs (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).
- Fire with fire: both want to be the alpha, and one submits only through gritted teeth (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).

**Change.**
- Fixed signs change only from inside; interference from outside is unwelcome (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney). Leo is one-track, unwilling to change course midstream even when it would plainly help (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10).
- Tierney's exception: unlike the other fixed signs, Leo can have trouble waiting to be gratified (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10, from Tierney).

**In childhood (raising Leo children).**
- Praise honesty and conduct, not looks; otherwise the child grows up caring more about appearance than behavior (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7; ⚠️ book not named, probably Silva).
- Always correct in private; public correction wounds pride and is not recovered from (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7).
- Frame tasks as important, never menial; children of this sign need to feel important and worthy of their tasks, and a visible checklist gives them the sense of accomplishment (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7).
- Give energy a channel or it turns into tantrums (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7).
- The pathology to guard against is the belief that one must be perfect to be loved and respected, which leads to burnout at the cost of food, sleep and peace (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7).

**As a parent.**
- Snodgrass: the Leo parent shows off the child's handiwork, report cards and trophies to guests and calls the family to perform; content while the child is small, in conflict once the child becomes a rival (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7, from Snodgrass).

**Over time and in maturing.**
- Martin's developmental note: only over time, and through real concrete achievements, does Leo's authority stop depending on applause (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §6, from Martin). Detriment and fall, in her method, are the developmental task; for Leo that is Saturn, structure and the deferred reward (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4–5, from Martin).
- Fire signs need sunlight, exercise, travel and adventure to recover energy (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §7, from Martin). Snodgrass's health notes: dark, dank rooms and oppressive climates are bad for Leo, and recovery comes in sunlight (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12, from Snodgrass).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations
- **The Nemean lion.** Hercules' first labor: he strangles the lion, skins it in one pull, and wears the hide as a sign of courage and invincibility. Snodgrass notes that in the sky Hercules sits far from Leo, as if the lion avoids its old enemy (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass). What it says about the area: a defeated power whose skin becomes the victor's costume and emblem.
- **The Nile flood.** The Egyptians honored the constellation (as Knem) because its season brought the Nile flood, destructive yet the source of the year's fertility, and welcomed it despite the damage (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Sekhmet and Re.** The lion-headed goddess Sekhmet is the scorching desert wind that dries the body, shooting victims through the heart with fiery arrows; her male counterpart Re, the sun god, gives strength and is the antidote to her (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass). The substrate's reading: one heat with two faces, life-giving and killing.
- **The sphinx.** The Egyptian zodiac at Esneh ran Virgo into Leo, and the union of maiden and lion produced the sphinx, a word Snodgrass glosses as *closely united* (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Thrones and gates.** Tutankhamen's coronation throne carries lion heads and clawed feet, suggesting firm footing and readiness to defend or attack; the Lion Gate at Mycenae sets lions over the entrance. The counter-image: after a Hittite alliance around 1300 B.C., sculptors carved lions lying in submission beneath winged gods (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass). The lion stands for royal power, and also for power subdued.
- **Taming the lion.** A medieval bestiary says a sweet singing voice quiets the lion, and that after bathing it becomes so gentle a child could ride and lead it. Medieval art also pairs lion and maiden as purity civilizing brutality (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Aesop's lions** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass):
  - *The Lion, the Mouse, and the Fox:* the lion is outraged that a mouse ran over his mane and woke him; the moral is "Little liberties are great offenses." Slights to dignity weigh heavily.
  - *The Lion and the Three Bulls:* he beats three attackers by separating them and taking them one at a time.
  - *The Lion and the Boar:* pausing mid-fight, the two notice vultures waiting for the loser and agree to make peace rather than feed them.
  - *The Lioness:* answering rivals who boast of large litters, she has one cub, but a thoroughbred. Quality over quantity.
- **Splendor over quiet faith.** Snodgrass: the lion prefers splendor and an anthem of praise to quiet faith and hidden stewardship, which inclines Leo to the arts, high fashion, high-church religion, evangelism and theater. In Taoism, by contrast, the lion stands for emptiness (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **The four evangelists.** Leo belongs to the fixed cross with Taurus, Scorpio and Aquarius; in the Christian correspondence of the four directions and evangelists, Leo is Mark, the lion of God (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10).
- **Alchemy.** Martin calls gold incorruptible, eternal fire. In alchemy the lion is the raw, untransformed form of gold, the starting material the work refines; Lambsprinck's 1625 treatise shows lion and lioness as two sides of that first substance (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12, from Martin).
- **Heraldry.** A crowned lion holding a sword, orb, fleur-de-lis or scepter, often red on gold; hybrid forms add a leafy mane, barbed claws, a knotted tail or scales (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **Stars** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12, from Snodgrass):
  - *Regulus,* the brightest, marking the lion's heart; it adds light from two companion stars; one of the four Persian Royal Stars; the Hindu *Mahga*, the mighty.
  - *Denebola,* at the tail, ominous in Hebrew tradition as the judge or the lord who comes.
  - *Adhafera* (also *Alserpha*), the funeral pyre, a double star.
  - *Algenubi* at the mouth; *Zosma* at the back; *Leo Minor,* the lion cub, added by Hevelius in the 1600s.
  - The *Leonid* meteor showers peak about every 33 years (spectacular in 1799, 1833, 1866, 1966).
- **The constellation.** A sickle with a triangular tail, also called the Scythe, the Diamond of Virgo and the Spring Triangle, one of the clearest shapes in the sky (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12, from Snodgrass). Third-largest of the twelve by area (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix II). Leo was already in the Babylonian list of 419 B.C. (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix I).
- **Joseph Merrick** as Snodgrass's example of Leo at its best: dignity, decorum and generosity in a life made into a spectacle (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Snodgrass).
- **The anti-privilege generation.** Martin's reading of Uranus in Leo (1955–62): a generation that does not believe in privilege or entitlement, attacks inherited elitism and undeserved status, and aims to establish that every person is special in some way; punk is its visible form. Its trap is that, on principle, its members turn down advancement over others and so erase themselves (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Martin). ⚠️ A generational placement, not the Sun sign.
- **An artist's dark episode.** Martin records under a solar eclipse in Leo that the painter-poet Rossetti buried his poems with his wife and in 1870 had her exhumed to recover and publish them (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Martin). ⚠️ An eclipse case study, not the Sun sign; it bears on creative ambition.

## 7. Structure
**Element: fire.** Fire acts first, on inner conviction, and treats the world as material for a personal drama. Martin: fire signs have an innate sense of their own specialness, which gives them great faith in themselves and a readiness to take risks other elements avoid (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Tierney and Martin). For fire, truth is subjective and needs no proof; earth's truth is objective and sensory, so the two contest personal truth (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin). Fire needs fuel and containment from earth (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin). Within fire, Leo is the contained flame; Tierney compares it to logs burning steadily in a hearth, deeper and steadier than the other two (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10; `A_Elements.md` §2, from Tierney and Martin). Snodgrass calls the fire signs combustive, radiant with generosity, intuition and self-confidence (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10, from Snodgrass).
- Fire's pairings that touch Leo: earth smothers fire yet fuels it, and fire treats paperwork and rules as beneath it (Martin's example is a tax return, which fire sees as an affront since rules are for ordinary mortals) and leaves the cleaning to others; air can kill a fire idea with a single fact, and fire's answer is to leave; fire and water start hot and drain each other, and Silva says Scorpio can be worn out by fire's larger-than-life aura; two fire signs compete to be the alpha (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin, Tierney and Silva).

**Modality: fixed.** Fixed signs hold the middle of a season, when it is at full strength (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Tierney). They do not refuse change; they refuse to be the one who moves first, and change only from inside (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney). Good at finishing, holding and enduring; prone to stalemate and battles of will; pride is common to all fixed signs (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin). Martin gives the fixed motive as to resist and stabilize (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10, from Martin). Leo's odd feature among the fixed signs is impatience for gratification (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10, from Tierney).

**Polarity: active (positive, yang, traditionally masculine).** Outward-moving, expressive, initiating contact (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10). Martin: the active principle moves from a center outward; it creates and destroys; it parallels Jung's extraversion, drawing meaning from activity and achievement in the world (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin).

**Opposite sign: Aquarius. The axis: the individual and the group.**
- Martin calls it the axis of the individual and the group, of what is right for me against what is right for everyone, and a fixed axis on which neither end is willing to adapt (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8, from Martin).
- Recognition is the sticking point: Leo runs on it, while Silva says Aquarius hardly cares about anyone's opinion and does not need validation. Tierney places Aquarius as far from Leo as a sign can be. Emerson: Aquarius will not stop working on its inventions long enough to dote, and Leo cannot take that and flees (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8, from Silva, Tierney and Emerson).
- Martin frames the axis with the Fisher King: cut the Aquarius end off from the Leo end and the king is wounded and the land frozen, until the untaught heart, Percival, thaws it (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8, from Martin).
- The two signs trade rulers and detriments with no exaltation traffic: the Sun, Leo's ruler, is in detriment in Aquarius; Saturn, Aquarius's traditional ruler, is in detriment in Leo (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). Under the modern scheme Uranus (Aquarius's modern ruler) is also in detriment in Leo, a placement forced by the rules rather than stated in the corpus (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §2).
- The same anti-privilege position appears inside Leo's own material, in the Uranus-in-Leo generation (section 6).

**Ruling planet: the Sun (domicile).** Ego, willpower, the need for recognition, integrity and self-dignity (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10). As a faculty: identity and named authority, the capacity to form a center, and Martin's authenticity and authority that do not depend on others' opinions (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin). Tierney pictures the Sun as the solar system's chief with the planets circling like loyal subjects, and distinguishes pure solar types, who see themselves as authorities in command and are not especially playful, from Leo, which plays and clowns to steal the scene (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10, from Tierney). The Greeks gave the Sun to Leo as the hottest point of the year (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Exaltation: none. Fall: none** (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- **Detriment: Saturn.** Restraint, limits, structure, time and the deferred reward sit badly here (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §3 and §6). Martin stresses that a planet in detriment still works, but only against the grain (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).

**Squares: Taurus and Scorpio** (both fixed, so the conflict entrenches rather than resolves; Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2 and §4).
- **Taurus:** Tierney flags this square as a problem for the Lion: same mode, less compatible elements. Both want luxury and notice and neither yields, which Silva says breeds grudges. Taurus's quiet, self-contained pride reads to Leo as a rebuke; Tierney notes that unassuming humility is not what Leo thinks will make its big splash. Emerson: Leo commands and Taurus accommodates, until it stops (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8, from Tierney, Silva and Emerson). The area in conflict: display against quiet worth.
- **Scorpio:** Tierney: Leo sulks royally when its pride is hurt, while Scorpio goes for the throat on the psychological level where it can do lasting harm. Scorpio does not contest the spotlight; it contests power. Silva: balance holds until one grows too hungry for power, and then neither concedes. Emerson calls the pairing unworkable long-term (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8, from Tierney, Silva and Emerson). The area in conflict: visible glory against hidden power.

**Trines: Aries and Sagittarius** (same element; Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4).
- **Aries:** Tierney: Aries feeds Leo's self-interest and its outlook appeals to Leo. The cost: Aries wants power and Leo wants the center, each envies the other's social life, and Aries's blunt honesty is the register Leo cannot bear (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §9, from Tierney).
- **Sagittarius:** the corpus's strongest pairing. Both perform an acceptable self for the world and drop it with each other, bonding over adventure or a quiet cup of tea and comfortable in silence. The cost: Sagittarius sometimes needs to be away, which Leo takes as withdrawn regard (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §9).

**Sextiles: Gemini and Libra.**
- **Libra:** mutual admiration. Leo turns to Libra for taste in books, music, film and art; Libra borrows Leo's glamour, and Emerson says Libra will flatter Leo endlessly. The cost: Libra is mortified when Leo snaps at waiters; Leo is maddened by Libra's indecision (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §9, from Emerson and Silva).
- **Gemini:** Tierney: Gemini encourages Leo to be creatively versatile and playful. Silva: Leo wants to let its inner child out and cannot do it alone, and Gemini can. The cost: Leo is high-maintenance and Gemini's attention is short (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §9, from Tierney and Silva).

**Quincunxes: Capricorn and Pisces** (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4). The documentation says nothing specific about Leo with either.

**Semi-sextiles (neighbors): Cancer and Virgo.** The documentation has real material here (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8):
- **Virgo:** Tierney frames Virgo as the sign that tidies up after Leo's party, its whole program a reaction against Leo's self-promotion, and holds Virgo's charge that Leo sells pieces of its soul for passing fame. Emerson: Leo needs regular ego-stroking, and Virgo is not much of a stroker. Silva disagrees, reading the pair as mature and balanced, with Virgo happy to groom the star it has no wish to be (from Tierney, Emerson and Silva).
- **Cancer:** Cancer finds Leo's need for validation insincere and shallow; Leo cannot stand Cancer's brooding (⚠️ book not named).

## 8. Decans
Emerson is the only decan source for Leo; the scheme blends the sign with itself, then with the other two fire signs (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §1–2; `05_Leo.md` §11).
1. **Leo / Sun: the pure type, and the most fragile.** Self-reliant, warm, sees itself as number one; usually content, but can become wholly dependent on others' approval (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
2. **Sagittarius / Jupiter: principle, learning and luck.** Conservative and traditional in principles, beliefs and values; high regard for spirituality and education; hungry for knowledge and discovery; lucky; a gambler, or at least a risk-taker (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
3. **Aries / Mars: chivalry and ambition.** The most chivalrous and ambitious decan; maddening refusal to admit being wrong; self-righteousness that causes conflict with coworkers and friends; a heart of gold, honest and frank; a strong drive for success carried by optimism (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).

Silva adds two cusp zones (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §11, from Silva):
- **Cancer side:** introverts turning into extroverts, insecure inside and hiding it behind playfulness, sometimes emotionally blocked, swinging from elation to depression overnight.
- **Virgo side:** perfectionists with creative flair who will serve, but with pride, as if performing an obligation; the directors, stage managers and technicians rather than the stars.

## 11. Where the author's named concepts appear
- **Justice:** no direct treatment. Relevant points: Aesop's moral that small liberties are great offenses, a sense of injury out of proportion to the act (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13); Snodgrass's lion that shows no mercy at the moment of victory and victimizes the vulnerable (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3); Denebola, the star known in Hebrew tradition as the judge (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §12); the Uranus-in-Leo position against undeserved status and privilege, a claim about fair recognition (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13, from Martin); and Silva's Leo that judges others by what they can afford to give (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3).
- **Crime:** nothing in this material on crime as such. The nearest points are favors traded for flattery (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Snodgrass), getting subordinates to do the work (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6), and using others' fear as leverage (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Trust:** respect must be genuine, and Leo detects fakeness and answers it by withdrawing (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §5, from Silva); yet it showers flatterers with favors (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Snodgrass). The inner circle admits only people who can hear the worst as well as the best (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7). With Sagittarius, Leo drops its public self and shows its true one (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §9). Keeping promises, paying debts and refusing commitments it cannot meet (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2), and the Sun's never-retrograde reputation for consistency (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Emerson), bear on trustworthiness.
- **Care:** care appears as encouragement and generosity: finding and developing others' talents, lifting morale, giving freely (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2; Full Attributes, Leo). Martin's Leo prefers to be leaned on (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2). Against that, Silva's Leo hates serving others and Snodgrass's is insensitive to the weak (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3). Parental care is shown through pride in the child's achievements (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §7, from Snodgrass).
- **Honor:** central. Pride, dignity and self-dignity (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2 and §10); humiliation as the worst wound and public correction as unforgivable (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Martin and Silva); Aesop's small liberties as great offenses (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13); chivalry in the third decan (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2); Merrick's dignity and decorum (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §2, from Snodgrass); the Nemean hide worn as a sign of courage (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §13); titles and honorifics insisted on (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §4). - **Duty:** weak in this sign. Leo dislikes ordinary low-profile tasks, cleanup and serving (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §3, from Tierney and Silva), and Saturn, the planet of discipline and structure, is in detriment (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §10). ## 12. Gaps
- **The fifth house is listed but barely developed.** Romance, children, gambling and recreation are named as house topics, but the documentation says little about romance or play as such beyond Leo's needs, and nothing about gambling beyond the second decan.
- **Nothing at collective scale.** All material is about individuals; nothing treats this area as a governing body, economy or institution (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15; `00_Method_and_Sources.md`).
- **Thin on equals and on poverty.** Peer material is almost all framed as rivalry; nothing on how the type treats an equal it does not see as a threat. Every claim about generosity and luxury assumes money (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15).
- **Decans rest on Emerson alone,** about three sentences each (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15; `E_Decans.md` §5).
- **Several points carry no book name** in the GDD (occasion-making, the honesty code, the work behavior, the inner circle, child-raising advice, overspending). Marked ⚠️ above; most read like Silva.
- **Disagreements the GDD records and does not resolve** (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15):
  - *Courage:* Snodgrass says Leo's boldness is an illusion, a roar at danger in vain show; Silva and Emerson call it the bravest sign.
  - *Grudges:* Silva says Leos forgive easily, and elsewhere that they forgive but never forget; Emerson says the fixed mode makes them hold resentments and shun anyone disrespectful.
  - *Crisis:* Tierney's heartbroken Leo against Snodgrass's merciless victor (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §6).
  - *Virgo as neighbor:* Tierney and Emerson see friction; Silva sees a balanced pair (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §8).
- **The GDD contradicts itself on correspondences.** The Leo file says no gemstone, flower or tarot card survived its slice; the later correspondences file supplies diamond (with ruby and carbuncle), sunflower and Strength (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15 against `G_Correspondences.md`, Leo). These notes follow the correspondences file.
- **Source defects.** Silva's *Ultimate Guide* Leo chapter opens with a misfiled block of Pisces material (wrong ruler and traits), which the GDD discards; Tierney is an OCR'd scan, and his list of famous Leos is unreadable (Substrate `05_Leo.md` §15).
- **Generational and eclipse material.** Martin's Uranus-in-Leo generation and the Rossetti eclipse case are about planet placements, not the Sun sign; flagged ⚠️ above.
- **Full Attributes names no sources,** so its Leo points cannot be traced further.
