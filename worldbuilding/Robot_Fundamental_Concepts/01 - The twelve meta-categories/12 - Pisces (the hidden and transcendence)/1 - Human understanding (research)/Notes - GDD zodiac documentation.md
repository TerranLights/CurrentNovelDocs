Research notes, 2026-10-30. What the GDD's zodiac documentation says about this area of human life, in our own words. Not robot understanding; not Concordia design.

**How to read the citations.** "§" numbers without a file name refer to Substrate `12_Pisces.md`. A book named after "from" is the one the GDD file credits for the point. The GDD's corpus is eight astrology books: Silva's *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* and *Sun and Moon Signs*, Snodgrass's *Signs of the Zodiac*, Martin's three-volume *Mapping the Psyche*, Tierney's *All Around the Zodiac*, and Emerson's *Behavioral Astrology*. Everything below reports what astrological tradition and these authors claim. None of it is treated as true.

## 1. Areas of life

**The natural house: the twelfth.**
- Silva calls it the house where the self is taken apart. Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* names it the House of Sacrifice (§1, from Silva).
- Emerson lists its domain as emotional and mental health, addiction, crime, fears, sleep and secrets (§1, from Emerson). This is the only place in the corpus that ties the sign to crime (§15).

**What astrology assigns to the sign itself.**
- **Endings and dissolution.** As the last sign it takes apart what the other eleven built, and is said to carry the accumulated experience of all of them, which Silva calls both a heavy burden and a lavish gift (§1, from Silva). Tierney assigns Pisces the job of recycling what Taurus holds once Taurus learns it owns nothing for good (§1, from Tierney).
- **Release from a fixed shape:** formlessness, oblivion, escape from a reality felt as harsh and confining (§1, from Martin).
- **Belonging to the whole,** and being protected by it (§5, from Martin).
- **Spirituality and mysticism:** occult rites and secrets, mystic fusion, epiphany (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Compassion and empathy:** feeling what another person feels, reading people without being told (§2, from Silva; Full Attributes, Pisces).
- **Sacrifice and self-sacrifice** (§1, from Silva; §3, from Martin, Silva).
- **Imagination and the arts:** music, dance, art, fantasy, film, fashion and romance (§2, from Martin).
- **Dreams against reality:** the two fish moving between fact and fantasy (§10, from Emerson).
- **Escape and addiction:** drugs, alcohol and anything that helps a person lose themselves (§3, §10, from Martin).
- **Secrets, hiddenness and camouflage** (§1, from Emerson; §4, from Martin; §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Sharing and charity:** no one should go without while others have more than they need (§2, from Silva).
- **Salvage:** seeing worth in what others discarded (§2, §5, from Silva).

**Work and roles the documentation assigns.**
- Silva: filmmaker, counselor, caregiver, charity work (bringing in donations), and marketing, which she describes as creating a persona for a brand, bringing the unseen to life. She recommends consulting or freelancing over employment (§6, from Silva).
- Emerson's Pisces-flavored decan of Cancer: born nurses (`E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- Snodgrass's planetary appendix names the kinds of people each planet governs. Jupiter, the traditional ruler, governs the judiciary, investors and financiers; Neptune, the modern ruler, governs demagogues and organized labor (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV and Design Note 7, from Snodgrass).

**The Age of Pisces.** The spring equinox sat in Pisces for about two thousand years and is now moving out of it. Martin frames that age as devotion (Pisces) joined to practical service (Virgo), the loaves and the fishes (§13, from Martin).

## 2. Core meaning

**Archetype: the two fish, bound together.**
- The Full Attributes file: two fish swimming in opposite directions, the dissolving of boundaries itself, as capable of deep compassion as of complete escape from reality (Full Attributes, Pisces).
- Emerson: the symbol shows a persona that glides back and forth between reality and fantasy (§10, from Emerson). Martin: two fish or dolphins yoked together (§10, from Martin).
- Silva records the standard reading (opposite directions mean inner tension) and then prefers her own: the fish circle in an endless loop around the mysteries of the universe, lighting them up, a figure eight of pulling and pushing, coming and going, being and not being (§10, from Silva).
- The GDD keeps both readings: opposition (two halves straining apart) and circulation (one closed loop with no fixed direction) (§10).

**Driving motive: release, not gain.**
- Martin places in Pisces "the part in all of us that never wanted to incarnate in the first place and longs to return to formlessness and oblivion." (§1, from Martin) Reality is often too hard, harsh and confining (§1, from Martin).
- The GDD reads the motive as release from having a fixed shape (§1).

**Core need.**
- Martin: Pisces describes our sense of belonging to the whole and being protected by it. Chiron in Pisces is existential loneliness, a sense of being cut off from the source of support, an emotional exile, as if the cord to the universe had been cut. With no alternative, the person becomes heroic (§5, from Martin). Emerson: helping others who have suffered trauma eases one's own suffering (§5, from Emerson). The GDD's reading: the wound is not treated but turned into a service offered to others (§5).
- Silva's chapter on what Pisces needs to thrive names six needs (§5, from Silva):
  - **Reciprocity.** Pisces is the zodiac's advice columnist, yet when it needs a shoulder there is none. Its spiritual aura misleads people into assuming it will sort itself out, as if it were emotionally superhuman.
  - **Cheerleaders.** People who tell it when it has done right, and that it is good, well-meaning, and worth the compassion it gives others.
  - **A creative outlet.** Without one it stagnates, grows resentful and can become depressed; in repetitive work it looks lazy and loses job after job.
  - **The hunt.** The second-hand store and the vintage find; the thrill is finding what it never knew it needed (filed by Silva under romance).
  - **Withdrawal on its own schedule.** Like a run-down battery it must recharge, and the solitude is for processing what it learned about itself and others.
  - **Honesty about identity.** Fail to model honesty and integrity and you are cut off. Silva's principle: someone who is false to themselves cannot be true to others, and that is the real dishonesty.
- A seventh need Silva names but doesn't count: to be believed. Having its intuition dismissed is a major frustration (§5, from Silva).
- Silva's closing formulation: what Pisces needs most is the freedom to keep circling in its figure eight, not freedom from anything but freedom to keep moving without being forced into one shape (§5, from Silva).

## 3. The area at its best

- **Knowing without being told.** Silva: Pisces can talk someone down from a ledge by grasping what is going on with them emotionally, with no briefing; they just know (§2, from Silva).
- **Sharing.** Silva's Pisces child has no use for the words *me* and *mine*, believes everything should be shared, and that no one should go without while others have more than they need (§2, from Silva). Snodgrass's period rhyme pictures Pisces praying, serving and feeding the beggar at the door (§2, from Snodgrass).
- **The open door.** Tierney: the door is always open; the GDD reads the virtue side as being the one who will still deal with you after everyone else has stopped (§2, from Tierney).
- **Refusing to be used.** Silva: Pisces sees hard work as breaking backs to make others rich and won't take part; she adds, "The moral universe of the Pisces bends toward justice." (§2, from Silva)
- **Beauty at full strength.** Venus is exalted here; Martin: acute sensitivity to music, dance, art, fantasy, film, fashion and romance (§2, from Martin).
- **Worth in the discarded.** Silva: the second-hand store delights Pisces; it sees how to make something beautiful from clothes nobody else wants, and the reward is the find itself. The same taste runs to outsider art by makers who get little attention, and to rescued animals (§2, from Silva).
- **Foresight, vindicated late.** Silva: Pisces proposes a solution that sounds impossible, is ignored, and is proven right once someone higher up catches on (§2, from Silva). Silva places this mostly in the third decan (§11).
- **Advocacy.** Silva's Pisces children are first to stand up for someone being bullied and won't tolerate meanness, lying or manipulation (§7, from Silva).
- **Loyalty once earned.** Once someone passes Pisces's vetting, Silva says it is as loyal and nurturing a friend as anyone could want (§7, from Silva).
- **Caution under the soft surface.** Silva: for all its dreamy vibe, Pisces is conservative by nature, thinks before speaking and looks before leaping (§8, from Silva).
- **Jupiter at its best.** Martin: the joyful, ecstatic dolphin nature (§10, from Martin; `F_Rulerships.md` §2). Jupiter as a faculty: meaning, scale and generosity, extending belonging past the immediate clan (`F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Acceptance.** Tierney: Pisces proper helps us accept what we cannot change (§10, from Tierney).
- **Strength in a crisis.** For the receptive signs generally, Martin agrees with a student that they look frail but have a deep reserve and become very strong in a crisis (`C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Positive attributes in the reference catalog:** deep, genuine compassion and empathy; artistic and imaginative gifts, often turned into real creative work; spiritual depth and intuition; adaptability that flows around almost any obstacle; selflessness, sometimes to a fault, but genuinely felt; a gift for dreaming that, channeled well, becomes creative vision (Full Attributes, Pisces; Quick Reference, Pisces).

## 4. The area gone wrong

The GDD finds the recovered shadow material consistent in one respect: this sign's aggression is social and retaliatory, never acquisitive. It does not take things from people; it cuts people off (§3, §15). The reference catalog's list: escapism; victimhood and self-pity; poor boundaries, absorbing others' emotions until they can't be told apart from its own; indecision rooted in emotional overwhelm; susceptibility to illusion, denial, addiction and unhealthy dependency; a martyr streak that quietly resents the sacrifices it insists on making (Full Attributes, Pisces).

**Boundaries.**
- **No filter.** Tierney: Pisces seems to lack the mental filters that keep out what is unhealthy for body, mind and spirit; the door stays open to everything, including what undermines it at the deepest level (§3, from Tierney).
- **A filter that fires but doesn't close the door.** Silva contradicts Tierney: Pisces has a sixth sense for problem people, narcissists and exploiters, can smell bad intentions and drops such people, and can spot a fake from far away (§3, from Silva). The GDD reconciles the two by scope: detection is excellent, exclusion is kept for the inner circle (§3; flagged by the GDD as its own derivation, §15).
- **Absorbing, then losing track of the source.** Tierney: what Pisces feels strongly inside usually came from outside. Martin: planets here soak up the mood of whoever walks in, without knowing why (§3, from Tierney, Martin).
- **Two Pisces together.** Silva: they get lost in each other to the exclusion of all else and feed each other's negativity in a toxic loop; only an earth-sign Moon reliably stops it (§3, from Silva). Water with water in general: total understanding and no differentiation, standoffs of pouting and sulking that never become arguments and so never end (`A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).

**Truth and deception.**
- **Lies that start as courtesy.** Martin on Mercury in Pisces: everything is relative and truth can be whatever you want; if you want black to be white, fine. Asked whether that is lying, Martin answers that the person is responding emotionally, telling you what you want to hear (§3, from Martin). The GDD's reading: deception here doesn't begin as a plan; it begins as accommodation and hardens (§3).
- **No discrimination.** Martin: you can have whichever piece you like, since it doesn't matter either way (§3, from Martin).
- **The sincere con.** Tierney: Pisces can talk itself into believing its own grand spiritual hype, since nobody can prove otherwise, and then use its charisma on others; some see themselves as envoys from higher planes. A longing for sainthood lets the ego come out of hiding and swell (§3, from Tierney). The self-deception comes first; deceiving others follows, sincerely (§3).
- **False transcendence.** Tierney: mistaking a pointless disappearing act of the ego for true spirituality is a major blind spot of the sign (§3, from Tierney).
- **Neptune's version.** Tierney: Neptune soaks up its surroundings and the people in them more than Pisces does, and self-deception tied to misplaced faith may be stronger in Neptunians, who chase miracle cures and quick magical fixes (§10, from Tierney).
- **Beauty that deceives.** Martin: Venus in Pisces also describes disappointment and deception in relationships (§10, from Martin).

**Need and victimhood.**
- **Victimhood as leverage.** Martin: a masochistic attachment to vulnerability, weakness and self-sacrifice, together with refusing healthy boundaries or any responsibility (§3, from Martin). Emerson's second decan is warned against leeching on others (§3, §11, from Emerson).
- **Need without bottom.** Martin on the Moon in Pisces: neediness with no limit, forever hungry and thirsty, never satisfied, leading to addiction and to driving people away because they drown in it. Neptune is where we always thirst for more (§3, from Martin).
- **The martyr's bill.** Silva's teapot story: a friend breaks a stage prop; the young Pisces claims the blame to spare him a second expulsion, is not believed, and is thrown out of drama class for the rest of high school, never returning to acting. Silva calls it noble but not smart. The sacrifice was real and freely made, and the person protected paid nothing (§3, §7, from Silva).
- **Inferiority underneath.** Silva: Pisces carries an inferiority complex and runs on anxious self-criticism, replaying its own words afterward for the wrong one. Its confidence is borrowed from whoever is reassuring it at the moment (§3, from Silva).
- **Unreturned loyalty.** Silva: intense loyalty that is not returned can leave Pisces morose to the point of suicide (§3, from Silva).

**Escape.**
- Martin: Neptune rules drugs, alcohol and any addiction that helps us lose ourselves (§3, from Martin). Silva: Pisces is either a teetotaler who learned the hard way or a wild dancer on the pool table, with no middle setting, and drinks to dull the energies coming at it (§3, from Silva).
- Snodgrass's psychological column for the sign includes secretiveness and addictive behavior (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix V, from Snodgrass).

**Retaliation and exclusion.**
- **The cold door.** Silva: cross Pisces on purpose and you meet total silence; "You are an ex-person." (§3, from Silva) Once cold, it stays cold. An unintended offense thaws in time, but Pisces gives second chances only to those who ask for one, not to those who pay or work it off. Whoever doesn't realize they must ask is simply gone: number lost, blocked everywhere. A grudge can last until the end of the world if not cleared up (§3, from Silva).
- **Idealize, then shut down.** Silva: Pisces bonds with people it has read as safe, which can lead to idealizing them, and then reacts badly to small slights (a canceled meeting, an unreturned call) by shutting down and making the other person move first (§3, from Silva).
- **The blacklist.** Silva: people with bad intentions near a Pisces feel its gaze across a room; without a word it knows they are off-limits for itself and everyone it knows, and the judgment is not revised. If someone steps out of line, she makes sure the others hear about it. Pisces cuts the cord only once evidence confirms its intuition, so there is a waiting period, but the verdict came first. Silva frames this as not personal, just damage control (§3, from Silva). The GDD observes that the evidence is only ever used to confirm, never to clear (§3).
- **Reputation as the weapon.** Silva: cross Pisces romantically and you may find yourself widely known by an insulting label; detect deception and it is shattered, and makes sure you share some of the pain (§3, from Silva).
- **The permanent grievance.** Silva's Cousin Bob is always right even when wrong, fights over imagined slights and constantly demands apologies for things that may not have happened. Silva says the only Piscean thing about him is his pathological sensitivity, like an open wound (§3, from Silva). The GDD reads him as proof the shadow can appear as pure aggrieved aggression with the empathy gone (§3).
- **The single detonation.** Silva: Pisces holds negative feelings in, which corrodes it, and then discharges out of proportion. In her third-grade story a bully throws a chair, the Pisces child answers with a baton to the head, and it is the Pisces child who is sent home (§3, from Silva).

**Exhaustion and carelessness.**
- **Compassion fatigue as coldness.** Silva: deprived of solitude, Pisces turns cold, intolerant and exhausted by all the human energy around it. Draining people are its weakness, and ordinary social load can feel like a threat to its existence (§3, from Silva).
- **Losing what matters.** Silva: phones left in taxis, keys forgotten within minutes, a habit that can cost jobs, friends and lovers, usually laughed off (§3, from Silva).
- **Conflict avoidance.** Martin: the mutable signs avoid conflict at nearly any cost except as go-betweens (`B_Modalities.md` §4, from Martin). The mutable signs also hold that they may change their minds whatever they promised, and can waver, pass the buck or back out when a commitment starts to feel like a trap (`B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- **Withdrawal past self-protection.** The receptive signs' failure modes: withdrawal into real isolation, holding on to what should be let go, grievance stored rather than raised, stagnation, and silence read by others as agreement (`C_Polarity.md` §3).
- **Water stores harm.** Tierney: water signs repressed for long periods suffer psychological damage as the hurt sinks deeper and turns toxic (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney).

**Outside reads.** Snodgrass: indiscreet and slovenly; wins hearts and never lacks friends but often loses supporters (§4, from Snodgrass). Emerson: to outsiders, delusional or at least flaky (§4, from Emerson). Silva notes that its reticence can be read as coldness or snobbery (§6, from Silva).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time

**Everyday presentation.**
- Hard to grip and hard to place. Martin: Pisces rising people are usually well camouflaged and keep changing shape; dealing with a polarized Pisces is like gripping wet soap, and the other person may walk away muddled, unsure what was agreed (§4, from Martin).
- It does not approach. Silva's party picture: Pisces sits in one chair all night and waves once, and the room comes to it; it socializes with those who make the first move; not the life of the party but its soul. It watches people openly and doesn't mind being caught (§4, from Silva).
- Vulnerability shown from strength. Silva opens her chapter with a pop lyric to the effect that being vulnerable takes more toughness than acting tough, and says Pisces wears its feelings openly, from strength, with little interest in hiding them (§4, from Silva). Outsiders misread the openness as weakness (§4).
- Not everyone of the type looks it: Silva's Cousin Bob is gruff and disgruntled, and only the sensitivity underneath gives him away (§4, from Silva).
- Emerson: mysterious, magical and sometimes frightening; master illusionists, escape artists (§4, from Emerson).
- Dress: flowing and sheer; sea-green through teal, blue, lilac, mauve and magenta; embroidery (§4, from Silva, Snodgrass).

**Socially.** Semi-social and watchful. Silva calls people-watching its favorite party game: noting whom to meet, whom to avoid, and who is having a rough time and needs support. It runs on trust, and only the inner circle sees its home. It vets newcomers more thoroughly than a political party vets a candidate for office, needing to be completely sure an emotional investment won't blow up (§6, from Silva).

**At work.**
- It needs to believe in the work. Silva insists the famous laziness is boredom: in a job it doesn't feel connected to, Pisces stares out the window, and postponed tasks slide down the list until they drop off (§6, from Silva).
- It adapts at once, feels at home anywhere, and likes having the goalposts moved (§6, from Silva).
- As a boss: doesn't micromanage, reads what each person needs, spots boredom and bullying fast; glazes over at performance reviews and payroll, and may slip out rather than deliver bad news or fire anyone. Subordinates are warned not to try to steer the agenda. What a Pisces boss needs most is administrative support for the dull side of the job; with it, Silva calls such a boss inspiring and exceptional (§6, from Silva).
- Martin: Piscean speech responds to the feel and atmosphere of words rather than their concrete meaning (§6, from Martin).

**After social exposure.** Every outing is followed by withdrawal, and the withdrawal is where the analysis happens. Even a night out dancing can leave Pisces overstimulated and sleepless. Denied this cycle it gets cranky, then cold (§5, §6, from Silva).

**Under stress.**
- Flees rather than confronts. Silva: Pisces swims as far away as it can when a confrontation looms, and may get up and leave without warning (§6, from Silva).
- Retreats to a bolt-hole: Martin names a public library, a retreat, or a weekend fishing by a river (§6, from Martin). Snodgrass's escalating list: retreating to the shore to fish and think, joining a monastery, building a house on a hill, or going somewhere undisclosed (§6, from Snodgrass).
- Substances stand in for boundaries (§6).
- Bored, it bolts (§6, from Silva).
- Mutable signs in general leave under pressure or take the go-between role, and they go around obstacles rather than through them (`B_Modalities.md` §4, from Martin).

**In crisis.** The GDD sums up three exits, each absolute and none proportionate: the cold door, the single detonation and the blacklist; only the detonation is visible from outside (§6, the GDD's synthesis of §3).

**When secure.** Past the vetting, loyal and nurturing (§7, from Silva). With a creative outlet, cheerleaders and solitude on its own terms, it does well (§5, from Silva).

**Over a lifetime.**
- **Childhood.** Silva: Pisces children cling to a parent's emotions to strengthen the bond, so parents are told to temper their moods around them; they learn by watching and imitating, not by rules; their motto is not to be fenced in; they need unstructured time, small groups or solitude (§7, from Silva). They share freely (§2) and defend the bullied (§7). The baton story shows the absorb-then-explode pattern already in third grade (§3).
- **Adolescence.** The teapot story: taking a friend's blame, being disbelieved by an authority who disliked the friend, losing the activity she loved, and never going back. She never regretted protecting him, only the loss (§7, from Silva).
- **Early adulthood.** Martin on Pisces rising: born into a setting that prized practical skill and an orderly, precise, businesslike approach, the person may spend years defining themselves by how they were mirrored before finding their own shape (§7, from Martin).
- **Through life.** Martin places the whole sign in the lunar Balsamic phase: letting go of the current cycle and preparing for the next, which means surrender and sacrifice but also gestation (§13, from Martin).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations

- **The tether.** Venus and Cupid, ambushed by the fire monster Typhon, escape into the water, the one element Typhon must avoid, and become fish; she ties herself to her son so they can't be separated. Al-Sufi records the yoked pair in the sky. A Greek variant: the fish were given a place in heaven for saving Aphrodite and her child (§13, from Snodgrass). The Greeks also read the star Alpherg as Typhon's head, lying near the northern fish's tail (§10). Bearing on the area: a bond made in an emergency, to protect a child, that outlasts the emergency (the GDD's reading).
- **The fish are not fish.** Silva: in the Greek telling the pair are ichthyocentaurs (sea-centaurs): human upper body, horse forelegs, fish tail. The same beings attend the sea-birth of Astarte in Syrian myth (§13, from Silva). A composite creature passing as one animal.
- **Submerged and reborn.** Silva traces the fish's water symbolism to baptism, in which the person goes under the water (standing for the waters the earth was born from) and comes up as a new creature (§13, from Silva).
- **The fish as a secret sign.** The Christian *ichthys* was an acronym, and Snodgrass says it served as a modest marker for a safe house, welcoming fellow believers (§13, from Snodgrass). A sign in plain view that means nothing to outsiders.
- **Secrets and mysteries.** Snodgrass: like the Orphic mysteries that grew from the dismemberment of Orpheus, Pisces governs occult rites and secrets, and stands for mystic fusion or epiphany (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **The last labor is a rescue.** Pisces governs the completion of Hercules's labors and matches his rescue of Hesione from the Trojan sea monster (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **The substitute apostle.** In the twelve-apostle scheme Pisces is Matthias, chosen to fill the place Judas left (Aquarius is Judas) (§13, from Snodgrass).
- **Sacrifice, release and gestation.** Martin's Balsamic phase (above). Her plant cycle: the fruit decays in Capricorn and the seed is released in Pisces for the next growth; Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces revalue, reorganize and release. Tierney: the sign stands for the hidden underground waters of spirit that feed new life about to be born (§13, from Martin, Tierney). Snodgrass on the handover from Aquarius: the flow becomes release and breaking down, spirit freed from body (`11_Aquarius.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Alchemy.** Lambsprinck (1625): the sea is the body, and the two fish are spirit and soul (§10).
- **Both extremes in one life.** Tierney: humanitarian by day, alcoholic by night; perhaps no sign but Scorpio is capable of such extreme, divergent expression (§10, from Tierney).
- **Mocked from outside.** Snodgrass's period rhyme ends by pitying poor Pisces after praying, serving, feeding the beggar and weeping over old love; Petronius's satirical banquet serves two mullets for Pisces and assigns each sign its vices (Snodgrass mocks the host) (§13, from Snodgrass). The sign as an object of pity and jokes.
- **Escape literature.** Snodgrass names Hurston's *Their Eyes Were Watching God* and Morrison's *Beloved*, the near-tragic story of an enslaved woman who flees being used to breed more slaves (§13, from Snodgrass). Flight from being used.
- **The ending age.** The Age of Pisces as devotion plus practical service (Martin); Tierney notes Tibetans have long been drawn to Pisces's mystical realms (§13, from Martin, Tierney).
- **Star names.** The constellation is also called the Circlet; it looks like a V with a triangle at the end of the short leg and a ring at the end of the other. Alpha star Alrisha means the cord; Alpherg is a double star said to mark the tie between the fish; the omega star's name means the northern wine press; the Hindu name for its zeta star, Revati, means the rich (§12, §10, from Snodgrass).

## 7. Structure

**Element: water.**
- Water absorbs: it reads what was not said and slips past boundaries it wasn't invited through. Tierney: its instinct is to ignore boundaries and seep quietly into hidden places. Martin: water picks up the emotion behind words more than the words, and is very sensitive to atmosphere (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney, Martin).
- Truth for water is inside, in emotional undercurrents shared by everyone (`A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin).
- Good at: reading hidden motives; unconditional support, because unlike air it won't explain the problem away; enduring heavy psychological load; imagination (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney, Martin).
- Blind to its own edges: poor self-protection, not a fighter, no shape or proportion without a container, drowning in its feelings (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney, Martin).
- Shadow: being pulled into someone else's turmoil or becoming the turmoil; and, when repressed, hurt that sinks and festers (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney).
- Needs: a container and a filter, borrowing a little self-interest from fire, territoriality from earth and detachment from air; and real solitude (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney).
- Pisces within water: it takes on the shape and color of its surroundings like a chameleon; it is so inclusive that Martin finds it very hard to define; and it is the only water sign capable of grand, abstract overviews (`A_Elements.md` §5, from Martin). Tierney: fire always starts each cycle and water always completes it (§10; `A_Elements.md` §1, from Tierney). The medieval Arab scheme files the water signs as the cold, wet north (§10).
- Element relations. With fire: mutual depletion; Silva calls Pisces a true water sign that likes to put out a flame. With air: the opposite pole on the truth axis; air explains feelings away, water deflects direct questions. With earth: the natural fit, earth supplying a container and water an inner life, with the risk of opening feelings earth can't handle. Water with water: total understanding, no differentiation, silent standoffs (`A_Elements.md` §6, from Martin, Silva).

**Modality: mutable.**
- Martin: the mutable motive is to adapt, mediate and reconcile; more interested in the journey than in arriving; avoiding conflict at nearly any cost (§10, from Martin). Tierney: mutable signs wear down the resistance of the fixed signs, so that solid structures start to lose their form (§10; `B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- Good at noticing that an adjustment is needed before anyone else, and at going around obstacles (`B_Modalities.md` §4).
- Failure: scattering, and the broken commitment (`B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- The mutable signs can't stand monotony; Tierney counts their lightness on their feet as a kind of youth (`B_Modalities.md` §4, from Tierney).
- Mutable squares are the easiest squares: constant friction, nothing settled, conflict that dissolves and re-forms (`B_Modalities.md` §5; `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Polarity: receptive (negative, feminine, yin).** Snodgrass lists it feminine (§10). Martin: subject-oriented, yielding, enclosing, inward-moving, reflecting before acting; linked to Jung's introversion. Receptive is not passive or weak (`C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin, Silva). The GDD notes that energy here is drawn in and answered rather than projected (§10).

**Opposite sign: Virgo. The axis runs between service and devotion, order and surrender.**
- Martin's Virgo-Pisces axis is the best-documented opposition in the corpus: order and chaos, control and trust, form and content, the practical and the imaginal, realities and dreams, service and devotion, sickness and health, judging and accepting (§8, from Martin).
- Martin frames it through the grail legend: Pisces asks whom the grail serves; Virgo answers that it serves whoever serves it (§8, from Martin).
- Mercury rules Virgo and is in detriment in Pisces; Venus is exalted in Pisces and falls in Virgo; Mercury's exaltation in Virgo and fall in Pisces complete the swap (the last two are derived, not attested) (§8; `F_Rulerships.md` §5, §7). Under modern rulership, Neptune is in detriment in Virgo (`F_Rulerships.md` §2).
- Martin's warnings for each pole: over-identified with Virgo, a person is consumed by duty and drudgery, longs to escape, and attracts more and more disruption; over-identified with Pisces, a person becomes so diffuse there is nothing to engage with, and is eventually forced into practical life, often bitterly. Virgo is drawn both to Piscean people and to the vulnerable or sick, whom it can care for with its practical skills (§8, from Martin).
- Silva describes the pair twice and the accounts don't match. Her Virgo volume calls it one of the few oppositions that works: both service-minded and modest, Pisces soothing, Virgo grounding. Her Pisces volume lists Virgo among signs to avoid: Pisces feels suffocated, judged by standards that seem harsh and unfair, unloved and disrespected, and calls the experience crushing. She also says Virgo may organize and temper some of Pisces's less desirable traits (§8, from Silva). The GDD treats the mismatch as the relationship itself: one side finds it fulfilling, the other slowly deadening (§8).
- The Age of Pisces joins the two ends: devotion with practical service (§13, from Martin).

**Ruling planets: Jupiter (traditional, personal) and Neptune (modern, transpersonal).**
- **Jupiter.** Meaning, scale and generosity; the capacity to say what the work is for, and to extend belonging past the clan (`F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin). Martin: the joyful, ecstatic dolphin nature (§10). Silva: Jupiter's daytime home is Sagittarius and its nighttime home is Pisces (§8, from Silva).
- **Neptune.** Dissolution and devotion: compassion, mercy and imagination, and the erasing of lines between self and other, gift and debt, real and imagined (`F_Rulerships.md` §3). Martin: a place of escape and seclusion; the longing to return to formlessness; rules drugs, alcohol and addictions that help us lose ourselves (`F_Rulerships.md` §2; §10, from Martin). Tierney ranks Neptune as more absorbing and more self-deceiving than Pisces itself (§10, from Tierney).
- **Venus exalted:** beauty at full strength, and also disappointment and deception in relationships (§10, from Martin).
- **Mercury in detriment and fall:** Pisces is the only sign where one planet holds both (`F_Rulerships.md` §5). Mercury is the faculty of analysis, record, transmission and verification (`F_Rulerships.md` §3). Martin's Mercury in Pisces makes truth relative and speech atmospheric (§3, §6, from Martin).
- ⚠️ Derived, not sourced: the GDD's capability reading holds that with verification at its weakest, this type keeps agreements personal rather than written, settles disputes by reputation and story rather than record, and, under Neptune, blurs the line between a gift, a loan and a debt (`F_Rulerships.md` §6, Pisces entry; restated here without its Concordia framing).

**Squares (same tempo, different subject): Sagittarius and Gemini.** Both mutable squares (`D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4).
- **Sagittarius.** Both share Jupiter, Sagittarius by day and Pisces by night (Silva). Tierney: Sagittarius is sure it is on the right path and leaves any contradicting details for Pisces to worry over. Silva: Sagittarius's directness and speed feel crude to Pisces, Pisces takes its lack of commitment as an insult, and Pisces looks like a dead weight to Sagittarius; the cautious Pisces finds Sagittarius impulsive and reckless (§8, from Silva, Tierney). Emerson nevertheless rates the pair a fantastic match (§15).
- **Gemini.** Both are shape-shifters, but Gemini scatters into words and circulation while Pisces dissolves into atmosphere and privacy (§8, the GDD's reading). Silva: Gemini switches priorities instantly, may treat sex and sensitive Pisces as toys, seems fly-by-night to Pisces while Pisces seems a dead weight to Gemini; Pisces privacy against Gemini sociability can come to blows. From Gemini's side, Pisces seems demanding; Gemini backs off, Pisces grows more insecure and needy, and Gemini backs off further. Gemini's jokes can hurt Pisces without meaning to. Emerson: unlikely to last; both end up complaining about each other (§8, from Silva, Emerson).

**Trines (same element, easy): Cancer and Scorpio.**
- **Cancer.** Silva: the nearest thing Pisces has to a twin. Cancer brings the ideas, anchor and planning, Pisces the imagination to transform them; Cancer teaches Pisces to do one thing at a time. Emerson: great mutual emotional support, but neither wants to carry the financial responsibility (§9, from Silva, Emerson).
- **Scorpio.** Scorpio reads others' motives and tempers Pisces's trusting nature with an eye for red flags, and protects those close to it; Pisces draws the guarded sign out and, per Emerson, softens its lack of impulse control. Together they are a mystery to others; their shared projects tend toward personal or public politics, Scorpio plotting the course and Pisces spotting hidden details. Tierney: only Scorpio matches Pisces's extremes and gloomy depths, so the pair amplifies rather than moderates (§9, from Silva, Emerson, Tierney).

**Sextiles (same orientation, working partners): Capricorn and Taurus.**
- **Capricorn** helps Pisces put its ideas into words and into material form; Pisces manages the emotional side while Capricorn does the grinding work. Friction: Capricorn is wary of Pisces's generosity, and Pisces feels hemmed in by Capricorn's care with money (§9, from Silva).
- **Taurus** grounds Pisces and serves as a good advisor, provided it doesn't try to change Pisces; Pisces's forgetfulness frustrates Taurus (§9, from Silva).

**Quincunxes (nothing shared): Leo and Libra.** Silva calls both poor relationships and excellent collaborations. Leo's huge ego grates on humble Pisces, who sees through the claims of superiority and walks away; yet on a shared project Leo happily takes the front and the fame while Pisces works in the background, uninterested in fame or fortune. Libra likewise handles customers and difficult clients while Pisces keeps the back end running (§9, from Silva).

**Semi-sextiles (adjacent, nothing shared): Aquarius and Aries.** Martin's worked example of neighbors with nothing in common is Pisces and Aries, chalk and cheese (`D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin). Silva: Aquarius's cool detachment puzzles Pisces, yet Aquarius is Pisces's best collaborator on humanitarian work, logical Aquarius at the controls while Pisces dreams its visions into being and handles the social side (§9, from Silva). The pair champions the underdog of a group (`11_Aquarius.md` §2, from Silva). Silva's two books disagree on Aries (§15).

## 8. Decans

Two sources here, Emerson and Silva; Pisces is one of only four signs Silva gives a decan chapter (`E_Decans.md` §2, §5). The decan scheme blends the sign first with itself, then with the other two water signs.
1. **First decan, Neptune (Pisces itself).** Emerson: open to every sensation, drifting through life like a leaf in the wind; mystical and highly intuitive; loves comfort more than money; less assertive than the other two decans and warned against being taken advantage of. Silva: the decan of imagination and intuition, near mind-reading, many lovers, driven by philanthropy and service to the community, born caretakers, humble and self-sacrificing (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2). Sub-theme: undiluted openness and service, and the exploitation it invites.
2. **Second decan, Moon (Cancer flavor).** Emerson: needs security both spiritual and financial; the most loyal and sweet of all decans, but a worrier; sacrifices itself for loved ones without a second thought; warned against clinginess and leeching. Silva: Pisces at its most emotionally balanced; wacky, eccentric and funny; content alone; observant inventors whose ideas sound mad and get built decades later; their motto is to live the dream rather than only dream it (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2). Sub-theme: loyalty and security, and the dependence they can turn into.
3. **Third decan, Scorpio flavor (Mars per Silva, Pluto per Emerson).** Silva: works off the steam that builds up in Pisces; forces of nature; take in data like soft computers and turn it into creative action; visionaries with a practical bent, often gifted in science and technology; fate plays a bigger part than planning; fearless because they have been through it all; love matters most to them; passionate listeners and empaths, the warmest of people. Silva puts the late-vindicated foresight mainly here. Emerson: very poetic and spiritual, with a mind full of strange ideas it can sublimate and rationalize, and a vindictive, jealous streak compared with other Pisces (§11; `E_Decans.md` §2). Sub-theme: discharge of pressure, practical vision, and the capacity for revenge.
- **The ruler conflict.** Silva and Emerson both make the third decan Scorpio-flavored and simply name different rulers of Scorpio (traditional Mars, modern Pluto). The GDD treats this as the same traditional/modern split as Jupiter/Neptune, not an error (§11, §15; `E_Decans.md` §5).

**The two edges (cusps), from Silva.**
- **Aquarius edge (Silva's Cusp of Sensitivity).** From the Pisces side: the sign finally gains firm boundaries people take seriously, and intuition sharpens into a weapon (§9, from Silva). From the Aquarius side: sensual, musical and artistic; every criticism taken personally; quarrelsome, secretive and escapist; narrow when its beliefs are questioned (`11_Aquarius.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Aries edge (Silva's Cusp of Rebirth).** Alpha meets omega: the sign stops dissolving into tears and puts up its fists (§9, from Silva).

**Pisces as a flavor in other signs' decans.** Cancer's third decan (Neptune) is perhaps the most sensitive in the zodiac: helps tirelessly, born nurses though moody, with fast-changing feelings that defy logic. Scorpio's second decan (Neptune) seeks authenticity and will give up prestige and comfort for its beliefs; mysterious and cunning, willing to manipulate, seductive and romantic; Silva adds death and resurrection, a felt higher purpose, a craving for mystery and the occult, perhaps a healer, perhaps a god complex (`E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson, Silva).

## 9. Correspondences

- **Colors:** sea-green (Snodgrass), light green (Emerson); blue, teal, purple, lilac, mauve and magenta (Silva), though Silva says a Pisces home isn't confined to the water palette and welcomes sunrise and sunset colors and bright yellow. Candle: purple (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Master Table).
- **Stones:** aquamarine (planetary stone, tied to Neptune); ruby (talismanic, its red recalling blood and passion); bloodstone (to regain personal power after a life crisis); jasper (soothes the restless; said to reveal past lives); jade (rewards the wearer's goodness); amethyst ⚠️ (Emerson, and Jupiter's jewel in Snodgrass, but better attested for Aries); chrysolite (Snodgrass's sign table) (§12, from Silva, Snodgrass; `G_Correspondences.md` Pisces).
- **Metals, through the rulers:** tin (Jupiter); Neptune's metals (lithium, platinum) are a reconstruction of a damaged table ⚠️ (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV).
- **Plants:** lemon balm, well attested (Emerson, and balm under Jupiter in Snodgrass); birthwort and willow (Snodgrass); mosses and ferns that grow near springs and streams; seaweed and algae (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Pisces).
- **Diet:** dried beans, spinach, lettuce, nuts, figs, dates, raisins; Snodgrass's eating habits: bubbly drinks, low spice, casseroles; alcohol forbidden outright, not even a sip of wine (§12).
- **Oil:** grapefruit (Emerson). **Chakra:** sacral ⚠️ (nothing in the corpus verifies chakra data). **Tarot:** The Moon (`G_Correspondences.md` Master Table).
- **Materials and textures:** flowing, sheer, tie-dyed, embroidered; cushions; outsider art; rescued animals; second-hand and vintage clothing as a pleasure rather than a sign of poverty (§12, from Silva).
- **Shapes:** no formal shape in the corpus; the constellation's own geometry (the Circlet, the V with a triangle and a ring, the cord) stands in for one (§12; `G_Correspondences.md` Design Note 8).
- **Places:** tombs (Snodgrass's topography, beside Scorpio's caves and prison cells and Aquarius's sewers) (§12). Snodgrass's geography table appears to give Portugal, though the OCR is scrambled ⚠️, and the GDD marks that appendix "do not use" because it stereotypes nations (§13; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix III).
- **Sky:** fourth-largest constellation by area, 50 stars, only 4 major ones (tied for the fewest), ninth in brightness (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix II).
- **Planetary extras (Appendix IV):** Jupiter's color purple, Neptune's lavender and luminous ⚠️; Neptune's jewels coral and ivory ⚠️ (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV).

## 11. Where the author's named concepts appear

- **Justice.** Silva's line about the Pisces moral universe bending toward justice (quoted in §3 above), tied to refusing work that breaks backs to make others rich (§2). The child's rule that no one should go without while others have more than they need (§2). Defending the bullied and refusing meanness, lying and manipulation (§7). Against that, Pisces's own sanction is anything but due process: a verdict reached on sight, evidence used only to confirm it, no revision, no appeal (§3). The teapot story is about being punished unjustly by an authority after taking another's blame (§3, §7). The Virgo axis sets judging against accepting (§8). Jupiter, the traditional ruler, governs the judiciary (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV).
- **Crime.** Emerson's twelfth-house list (addiction, crime, fears, sleep, secrets) is the only direct link in the corpus (§1, §15). Related material: lies that start as courtesy (§3); the sincere con (§3); illusionists and escape artists (§4); the safe-house sign (§13); addiction (§3); flight from enslavement (§13); the ransom of a hostage (§13). Neptune governs demagogues (`G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV).
- **Trust.** Pisces runs on trust and vets newcomers hard; past the vetting it is fiercely loyal (§6, §7). It detects bad actors on sight (Silva) yet is said to have no filter at all (Tierney) (§3). The entry price is honesty about who you are (§5, §7). Betrayal brings the cold door, and readmission comes only by asking (§3). It idealizes people it has read as safe and then withdraws over small slights (§3). Its trusting nature needs Scorpio's eye for red flags (§9). Venus's bond to Cupid is trust tied in an emergency (§13).
- **Care.** The richest of the six here: compassion and empathy (Full Attributes); knowing what someone feels without being told (§2); sharing and feeding the beggar (§2); caregiving, counseling and nursing as vocations (§6; `E_Decans.md` §2); the advice-giver who gets no care back (§5); healing one's own wound through others (§5); self-sacrifice (§3); and the failures of care: leeching, bottomless need, attachment to vulnerability, codependent loops and compassion fatigue (§3). Virgo, the opposite sign, is drawn to the vulnerable and sick so it can care for them (§8).
- **Honor.** Silva calls the taking of a friend's blame noble but not smart (§3). False identity is the one unforgivable offense (§7). Humility and indifference to fame (§9, §11). The yearning for sainthood that inflates the ego (§3). Reputation used as a weapon to disgrace others (§3). Matthias, chosen to replace the traitor (§13). The sign as an object of pity and mockery (§13). Foresight that goes uncredited (§2).
- **Duty.** The Virgo-Pisces axis sets duty and drudgery against devotion and escape (§8). Pisces works only for what it believes in, procrastinates the rest, and avoids reviews, bad news and firing (§6). Martin names a refusal to take responsibility (§3). The mutable signs claim a right to change their minds whatever they promised (`B_Modalities.md` §4). Against that: the second decan's sacrifice for loved ones without a second thought (§11); service to the community in the first decan (`E_Decans.md` §2); and the Age of Pisces as devotion joined to practical service (§13).

## 12. Gaps

- **The twelfth house is a bare list.** Emerson's six domains and two house names are all there is; nothing develops sleep, fears or mental health. ⚠️ Outside this material, astrology is often said to link the twelfth house with confinement and institutions; the GDD documentation does not mention this, and a later source should check it.
- **Crime rests on one sentence** from one source (Emerson) (§15).
- **Little at collective scale.** As the method file warns, the corpus describes individuals and pairs, not groups (`00_Method_and_Sources.md`). The GDD finds nothing on disputes between two insiders, on succession, or on territory; every source shows Pisces judging an outsider (§15).
- **Transcendence material leans heavily Christian and mystical** (the fish acronym, Matthias, baptism, loaves and fishes, the Orphic mysteries, alchemy), with only passing notes on other traditions (Tibet, a Hindu star name, Syrian Astarte) (§13, §15).
- **Money and value are thin.** Unconcerned with money, bad with finances and indifferent to fame, with the hunt for the find as the only commercial instinct; nothing ties the sign to wanting gain (§15).
- **Disagreements and contradictions:**
  - The filter: Tierney says Pisces has none; Silva gives it an unerring sense for exploiters. The GDD's reconciliation (detection everywhere, exclusion only at the inner circle) is its own derivation (§15).
  - Virgo: Silva's Virgo volume calls the opposition a good match, her Pisces volume a sign to avoid (§8, §15).
  - Leo, Libra and Aquarius: poor personal matches but good collaborators, per Silva; Emerson's rating of Leo as a wonderful match describes the working side (§9, §15).
  - Aries: Silva's two books disagree. Sagittarius: Emerson calls it a fantastic match although it is a square in his own aspect table (§15).
  - Third decan ruler: Mars (Silva) against Pluto (Emerson), both rulers of Scorpio (§11, §15).
  - Decan dates: Pisces's first decan is given as February 19-29, a range that exists only in leap years (`E_Decans.md` §5).
  - Mercury's fall in Pisces is derived from the rules, not attested anywhere in the corpus (`F_Rulerships.md` §7).
  - Stones: amethyst, chrysolite, aquamarine and ruby compete, and the amethyst is better attested for Aries (`G_Correspondences.md` Pisces).
  - Snodgrass alone describes the water signs as persuaders and fun-lovers who enjoy close relationships, against every other source's private, withdrawn water (`A_Elements.md` §9).
- **Weak correspondences:** no formal shape or architecture; Neptune's metals and jewels are reconstructions; the chakra is unverified; the geography (Portugal) is uncertain (§15; `G_Correspondences.md`).
- **A truncated thought.** Tierney's sentence on the sincere con breaks off mid-clause in the source, and the rest is not recoverable (§15).
- **Tierney is an OCR'd scan;** his points here are paraphrased from the GDD's cleaned readings and should be checked against the book before anyone relies on their exact sense (`00_Method_and_Sources.md`).
