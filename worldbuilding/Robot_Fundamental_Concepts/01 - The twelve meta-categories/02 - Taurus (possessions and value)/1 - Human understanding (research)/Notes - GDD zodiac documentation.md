Research notes, 2026-10-30. What the GDD's zodiac documentation says about this area of human life, in our own words. Not robot understanding; not Concordia design.

**Reading notes.** Everything below reports astrology as human tradition and belief, never as fact. Where a GDD file credits a book, the citation names it ("from Tierney"). The GDD corpus is eight astrology books: Silva's *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* and *Sun and Moon Signs*, Snodgrass's *Signs of the Zodiac*, Clare Martin's *Mapping the Psyche* (three volumes), Tierney's *All Around the Zodiac* and Emerson's *Behavioral Astrology* (Substrate `00_Method_and_Sources.md`, The corpus). The GDD files were written to design one city district; that application is left out here.

## 1. Areas of life

What astrology gives Taurus, as far as this documentation goes:
- **Possessions and ownership.** Three sources give the sign the motto "I have." Snodgrass's key words: sensuous, *I have*, *integration* (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §1, from Snodgrass and others).
- **The second house: possessions, personal wealth, value and security** (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10). Against the eighth house, it forms an axis of my resources against our resources (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8; `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- **Values and self-worth.** Tierney's reading is that the sign's real task is to cultivate a sense of its own value that does not come from outside sources such as wealth or titles (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §5, from Tierney). Martin: "the question with Taurus is usually about values" (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §5, from Martin).
- **Money.** Silva's guide says Taureans love money and claims many of the world's wealthiest people are Taurus (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §15, from Silva).
- **Stability and security** (Full Attributes, Taurus; Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **The senses, pleasure, comfort, beauty and appetite.** The sign's ruler is the planet of beauty and pleasure, and its stability shows through appetite, comfort and loyalty rather than discipline for its own sake (Full Attributes, Taurus). Every source agrees it meets the world through the five senses first (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2).
- **The body's needs.** It takes delight in meeting the body's need for food and sleep (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10). Earth signs in general do not neglect nourishment, rest and exercise (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney). Snodgrass describes a big eater with a simple palate who loves potatoes, and generally sturdy health kept up by attention to health and frequent outdoor walks (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Taurus; `02_Taurus.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **Material craft and manual work.** Valens is quoted as saying the sign produces artisans, laborers, farmers and builders. Martin adds sculptors, singers and dancers: anyone whose instrument is a material or their own body (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2, from Valens via the corpus, and Martin).
- **Land, growing and the garden.** Tierney says the type likes to grow plants patiently from seed and stay near its garden (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §12, from Tierney). Its topography is the plains (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **Territory and boundaries.** Tierney credits Taurus with inventing the fence: it is the first sign to mark off its ground against intruders (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Fertility and the established crop.** The sign's season is tied to Beltane at the start of May, when crops are taking hold (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Martin).
- **A stage of growth.** Rudhyar's plant cycle places Taurus at germination, after the seed and before the shoot. Snodgrass's life-stage mapping places it at the seventh to fourteenth days of the cycle ⚠ (units unclear; the Aries file gives Aries as conception to six) (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10, from Rudhyar via Martin and Snodgrass).

## 2. Core meaning

- **The archetype: the consolidator.** Where Aries starts things, Taurus holds them. Tierney credits it with a talent for giving raw energy a usable, lasting shape (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §1, from Tierney). The GDD sums up its whole labor as turning impulse into inventory.
- **The driving motive: lasting possession of something real.** Not comfort for its own sake, but holding a body, a plot of land, a routine, a person or a trade (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §1). Tierney's comic motto has the sign claiming a thing as its own the moment it goes on sale (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **The core need: to know what is truly its own and truly worth having, by its own measure.** Comfort is the symptom, not the need. Tierney: self-appreciation is something no one else can hand us, and it cannot come from wealth or a prestigious title. Martin names the wound as seeking security by other people's or society's values instead of one's own (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §5, from Tierney and Martin). The GDD's reading: when Taurus cannot find its own measure it accumulates instead, and the hoard never satisfies because it was measured by someone else's ruler (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §5).
- **The most static position in the system.** Fixed earth: every source independently calls it the least moving combination of element and modality (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **Truth through the senses.** Earth's truth is objective but personal, based on what the five senses report. Martin: if earth does not like the smell, taste or color of something, that settles it (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Receptive, not passive.** The sign draws toward itself instead of pushing out. Martin describes Venus as a pulling power (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10, from Martin).

## 3. The area at its best

- **Reliability that never wavers.** Taurus is the sign people build long-term plans around, because it actually shows up (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Patience** with long, slow processes that other signs would abandon (Full Attributes, Taurus). Fixed signs can wait; they do not need what they want immediately (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Practicality** grounded in what works, not in what sounds impressive (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Deep, unglamorous loyalty and devotion** (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Sensory appreciation** of beauty, comfort and pleasure, treated as legitimate rather than frivolous (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Determination** that does not quit once committed (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **It delivers and finishes.** It delivers on time, takes commitments seriously, and will not do a careless job or leave one half done (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2, from Tierney). The fixed signs are the builders who give substance to what the cardinal signs start (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Skilled hands**, in craft, farming, building and the arts of the body (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2, from Valens and Martin).
- **Good in an acute emergency.** Several sources agree it takes charge in an emergency and handles it responsibly (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2).
- **Hospitality without limits.** It will not let an occasion pass modestly, and it remembers every date (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §2).
- **Earth's general gifts.** Perseverance, endurance, realism and delivery; turning intention into an object; accounting for resources; and caring for the body (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney). Earth is the custodial element that keeps ordinary systems working (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Quiet strength in a crisis.** Martin: the receptive types often seem frail but have a deep inner reserve and become very strong in a crisis (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).
- **A steady anchor for others.** More sensitive signs, Cancer above all, rely on the stable Taurus to keep things steady, and Taurus takes the call in the middle of the night without complaint (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9).
- **Humor and discretion.** Silva, writing from the Scorpio side, says Taurus keeps secrets safe and has a sense of humor that finds something to laugh at even in sad or annoying things (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Silva).
- **An unpretentious beauty.** Taurus's Venus is the earthy, unpolished one. It is less shaped by social programming and looks for a beauty rooted in the natural world, where Libra's Venus can seem contrived (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **Constancy.** With the Moon exalted, the sign stands for nourishment, roots and something to rely on (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **A gentle voice.** Martin gives the type a beautiful, gentle voice and slow speech that commits only once a decision is made (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §12, from Martin).

## 4. The area gone wrong

**The pattern the GDD names:** a sign's vices are mostly its virtues pushed past their useful range (Full Attributes, "Worth Your Attention"; Quick Reference, closing pattern). For Taurus: steadiness becomes stagnation, loyalty becomes possessiveness, and appreciation becomes excess.

- **Stubbornness** severe enough to hold a position long after it stopped making sense (Full Attributes, Taurus). Tierney makes Taurus the most entrenched sign of all in its attitudes, and says that its deep inner resistance, especially when it feels manipulated from outside, makes it do things the slow, hard way (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **The first-impression lock.** Tierney says that once Taurus has formed an image of a thing or person, newer evidence will not easily move it; it sticks to first impressions (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Tierney). Its verdicts are gut reads that are never stated and never re-checked (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3).
- **Possessiveness** over people, objects and territory alike (Full Attributes, Taurus). Tierney's warning is about what happens when the possession is a person rather than an object (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Tierney). Martin insists possessiveness belongs to Taurus and not to its opposite, Scorpio: jealousy, suspicion, and the feeling that others belong to it body and soul (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Materialism** that measures worth by what can be held or owned (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Resistance to change**, even change that is clearly needed (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Complacency and stagnation.** Comfort can harden into inertia (Full Attributes, Taurus). Tierney: the sign's calm can harden into stagnation, its senses dulled by monotony or glutted by self-indulgence, so that what looks like rest is really vegetating (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Tierney). Martin makes it structural: the simple biological drive toward stability becomes stagnation, which in turn provokes the kind of intense crisis linked with Scorpio (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Jealousy, and self-indulgence** that tips generosity toward oneself into excess (Full Attributes, Taurus).
- **Grudges.** Snodgrass lists grudge-bearing as a core trait, not a lapse. Emerson warns that grudges make the type lose focus (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Snodgrass and Emerson).
- **Other named vices:**
  - greed and unrestrained hedonism;
  - refusing to admit a mistake and blaming subordinates instead;
  - refusing risk: it cannot face uncertainty, so good opportunities go unpursued;
  - self-absorption;
  - procrastination passed off as good timing.
  (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3.)
- **Laziness, or conservation?** Silva's guide calls Taurus lazy, easily distracted, quick to take credit and given to procrastination. Tierney reads the same behavior as deliberate energy-saving and separates waiting for the best moment from simple immobility. Emerson: a very hard worker who never gives up, but who can struggle with laziness (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §15).
- **Aggression under pressure.** It can turn aggressive and push away the people it loves because it does not know how else to react. In childhood, a broken routine brings on destructive tantrums until the parents give in (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3).
- **Betrayal and loss.** Silva's darkest reading of the sign's myths: someone who betrays their closest friends and most sacred relationship, loses everything, then wanders in search of a replacement while unable to make the one change that would bring it back (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Silva).
- **False gentleness.** Silva reads the sign's myths, such as Europa carried off by a meek-looking white bull, as stories of false façades ending in loss (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Silva).
- **Self-sufficiency turned into wariness of obligation.** It feels everyone should be as self-sufficient as it is. It is reward-oriented to a fault: it will not keep giving without getting something solid back (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6, from Tierney).
- **Staying too long.** It will not concede that a slow crisis is happening. Taurus takes longer than others to end a failing relationship, and a long time to admit a job does not suit it (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6).
- **Earth's shadow: calcification, then cynicism.** Martin describes a lack of enthusiasm, hope and faith that becomes a cynical sense that life has let one down. Silva describes settling for a life where one does not feel happy or appreciated, emotionally, intellectually or financially, just to keep the security one has built (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Martin and Silva). Earth keeps chaos at bay mainly by making and enforcing rules (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3).
- **Earth's blindness.** It assumes the tangible layer is the only real one, so it cannot see the point of anything unproven, and will not sit through endless talk of theories (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).
- **The fixed signs' failures.** Stalemate and battles of will; pig-headed resistance aimed at others' easier solutions; clinging to daily rituals when variety would make more sense; an inborn pride (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney). Silva adds that Taureans do not think they are stubborn; they believe their way is simply right (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Silva).
- **The receptive mode's failures.** Withdrawal past self-protection into isolation; holding what should have been released; storing a grievance instead of raising it; stagnation; silence that others take as consent (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Weak self-defense.** With Mars in detriment, assertion comes slowly and shows up as endurance, not attack. Martin's view is that Mars would give it the courage to defend itself and its own values, instead of being drawn into power struggles that seem to come from others' emotional demands (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §6, from Martin).
- **Treating a person as a holding.** Tierney says Libra, which shares Taurus's ruler, sees relationships very differently and partly exists to correct this habit (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Tierney).
- **Ego.** Buddhists saw the bull as the incarnation of ego (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Spoiling.** In Silva's child-rearing advice, an indulged Taurus child grows into an entitled, unappreciative adult (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §7, from Silva).
- **Snodgrass's list of temperamental failings:** brooding, anger, intractability and a love of luxury; also taciturnity, slowness and indolence (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Taurus; `02_Taurus.md` §12).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time

**Under stress.** It digs in and stops moving. Martin's image is the activist who simply used his own body to block the tree cutters and would not move until he was carried away: no petition, no speech, just his weight in the way. Cornering it brings defense, not reconsideration (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6, from Martin). Under enough pressure it can turn aggressive and push away the people it loves (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3).

**In conflict.** It cannot be argued out of a position without a genuinely better argument. Its fights are frequent, loud and short, and its temper cools quickly (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6). ⚠ That sits uneasily with grudge-bearing as a core trait (§4); the documentation does not reconcile them. The fixed signs do not resolve tension; they outlast it. They will only change from inside, and interference from outside is unwelcome (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin). In Martin's parable of the boulder blocking the road, the fixed traveler spends five years drilling a hole through its center (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Martin). Two fixed parties in conflict have no built-in way out; each is sure it is right, and the grudge can outlive the people who started it (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin and Silva).

**In crisis.** Excellent in an acute crisis and very poor in a slow one. It puts out the fire but will not admit the building is failing (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6). Receptive types grow strong in a crisis (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).

**Over time.** Martin's cycle: stability slides into stagnation, and stagnation provokes the crisis that breaks it open. Successful holding generates its own upheaval (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §3, from Martin; `08_Scorpio.md` §8).

**At work.** One long, hands-on project, a tidy workspace, method over improvisation, and a strong attachment to its habitual way of doing things (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6, from Tierney). It expects others to be as self-sufficient as it is, and expects a solid return for what it gives (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §6, from Tierney).

**Socially.** It vets people silently, often without realizing it is doing so. It over-hosts. Nothing hostile happens to a newcomer; the vetting simply runs, and warmth arrives later, all at once, or never (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §4 and §6). It looks placid and is not. An old rhyme quoted by Snodgrass sketches the type who smiles through life except when crossed and thinks he knows best. Silva calls its reputation for being easygoing a myth, overridden by its determination. Tierney says the slowness is overstated and is really caution toward anything that rushes at it. It does not perform and likes to get things done in peace (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §4, from Snodgrass, Silva and Tierney). The sources disagree on its sociability (see §12).

**Dress and presentation.** Martin gives two styles: natural fabrics in soft, earthy colors, or conspicuous jewelry worn to show substance (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §4, from Martin).

**How the type is said to learn** (Silva's chapter on raising Taurus children):
- Routine is the tool: fixed sleep, planned meals, a set plan with almost no surprises. Silva presents this as stabilizing for both child and parent.
- It learns from facts and reasons, not orders. Silva's advice is to negotiate logically and share your own thinking with the child.
- Its learning is sensory and methodical: strong in math, physics, sport, art and music, weaker in languages and history.
- It needs time with any change. Rushing a transition backfires; the adult should accept the child's pace and show that they accept it.
- It is visibly closed until it feels comfortable, and then becomes very friendly.
- Spoiling is the known parenting failure. Silva's corrective is to make the child earn what it wants through chores.
(Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §7, from Silva.)

**As it matures.**
- Martin's growth edge is taking in Mars: the courage to stand up for itself and its own values (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10, from Martin).
- Tierney says Gemini follows Taurus in the wheel for a reason: Gemini keeps us from settling into thoughtless habit (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Tierney).
- Capricorn, the one earth sign willing to take calculated risks in unknown territory, is the ally that gets Taurus moving at all (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9).
- Silva says earth needs air, the element it gets on with worst, to grow (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Silva). Earth also needs water's permission to feel anything about what it has built (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3).

**When secure.** Content and sensory: it enjoys comfort, good food and rest. Once comfortable with someone, it becomes warm and loyal (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §4 and §7). The documentation says nothing about the type without security; see §12.

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations

- **An old constellation.** Silva dates observation of the bull to between 5000 and 1700 B.C., links it to cave paintings and notes that Babylonian astronomy fixed the image (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Silva).
- **A substitution.** Snodgrass's timeline has the Babylonian list of 419 B.C. carrying the Pleiades in this position; the bull was put in that slot later (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix I). ⚠ This seems to sit uneasily with Silva's early date for the bull image; the documentation does not reconcile them.
- **The bull as light and renewal.** Babylonians, Syrians, Sumerians and Arabs read the bull as a symbol of light and of the earth's renewal. The Egyptians identified it with Osiris. The Chinese saw a white tiger or a great bridge (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **The whispered lie.** Babylonian priests who sacrificed a black bull at the New Year festival whispered in its ear that it was the celestial Great Bull. The GDD reads it as a comforting untruth told to the thing being given up (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Io and the denial of rest.** Hera turns Zeus's lover Io into a cow and has a gadfly sting her forever, so that she can never settle or be comfortable (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13). The GDD reads it as the opposite of everything Taurus wants: not death or exile, but permanent unrest.
- **The horn as refuge.** In the *Chanson de Roland* the bull is reduced to its horn, and the hero's blast on the horn means refuge and safety (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Europa.** Zeus, as a meek white bull, carries her off. The myth is of a false gentleness, which Silva reads as part of a pattern of false façades ending in loss (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Silva).
- **The Cretan bull** in the labyrinth, killed with the help of Ariadne's thread (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13).
- **The bull as ego** in Buddhist thought (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13).
- **Manilius' plodding dray animal.** Manilius pictures the bull as dull, honest and strong, an unadorned beast laboring in the fields, manuring the furrows and producing a good crop (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Snodgrass). Labor, honesty and productive work.
- **Virgil.** The *Georgics* open with the white bull whose golden horns open the year (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13).
- **Christian.** Luke, in the fixed cross of the evangelists; the apostle Andrew (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13).
- **Beltane and the green man.** Martin ties the sign to Beltane in early May, an ancient fertility festival held as the crops establish themselves, and to the green man, the male fertility principle. The festival falls in the middle of the sign's season, not at a boundary (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §13, from Martin). Across the axis, Beltane (Persephone's return) faces Samhain (her descent) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- **Midas.** Martin presents Midas as entirely identified with the Taurus end of its axis. The flowers in his garden turn to gold, he starves among his food, and his daughter turns to metal in his arms: preservation that kills what it preserves (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Martin). In the Scorpio file's telling, the reversal from the Scorpio side restores the garden (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- **The stars.**
  - Aldebaran, the alpha star, a rose-orange giant at the heart of the constellation. The Persians named it Watcher of the East, and it is one of the four Royal Stars.
  - Alnath, the beta star, on the north horn, which Hebrew astrologers called the shepherd. Tradition says it was rising during the 1664-65 plague and the 1666 London fire.
  - Alhecka, on the south horn, linked with harsh and fearful moods.
  - The Pleiades: the Babylonians called one of them Temennu, the foundation stone, and Hindu tradition called it Amba, the mother. Their epithets include the stubborn-faced and the sufferer.
  - The constellation's other names are the V, the Winter Octagon and the Winter Oval.
  (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §12 and §13, from Snodgrass.)
- **The constellation in numbers.** It is second in brightness and sixth in area, and has the most stars of any zodiac constellation (98, 15 of them major) (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix II).

## 7. Structure

**Element: earth** (with Virgo and Capricorn).
- Earth verifies through the five senses and then builds something that lasts. It understands the material world and what can be done with the resources at hand, by instinct rather than book learning (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- Tierney describes earth as holding both its position and its form, so it already leans toward fixity before modality is applied (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10, from Tierney).
- Within earth, Taurus is the most static form. Virgo is earth made workable, and Capricorn is the only earth sign willing to leave the known (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §3, from Tierney).
- Earth with the other elements (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6):
  - Earth smothers fire and is also its fuel. Fire finds earth a party pooper; earth demands rest to conserve vitality. Silva's verdict on Taurus with Sagittarius is scorched earth, because the archer sees possessions as a chain or an anchor (from Martin, Tierney and Silva).
  - Earth and air talk past each other without raising their voices. Earth does not care about words that bring no results; air finds earth slow and literal (from Silva and Martin).
  - Earth and water are the natural fit. Earth gives water a container, and water gives earth an inner life, but earth may open feelings it cannot handle (from Silva).
  - Two earth signs move at the same pace and share a world that others find tedious (from Silva).

**Modality: fixed** (with Leo, Scorpio and Aquarius).
- Fixed signs hold the middle of the season, when things are at their peak and the days seem much alike (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Tierney).
- Their motivation is to resist and stabilize. They do not refuse change; they refuse to be the one who moves first, and they change only from inside (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).
- Martin calls the fixed mode the resisting one and links it to the part of Plato's soul that desires food and procreation (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Martin).
- Emerson calls the fixed signs the workhorses, the project managers of the zodiac (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Emerson).
- Tierney's breathing image: the fixed phase is the held breath, possession of what was taken in, with static rather than gathering power (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Tierney).
- Martin's law of three: Aries, the primal force, meets equal resistance in Taurus, and Gemini resolves the deadlock (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1; `C_Polarity.md` §4, from Martin).

**Polarity: receptive** (negative, yin, feminine; the corpus's terms). Inward-moving, enclosing, reflective, thinks before it acts; Martin links it to Jung's introversion. The sources stress that receptive is not passive or weak (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin and Silva).

**The opposite sign: Scorpio, and the axis of my resources against our resources.**
- In house terms: the second house (possessions, personal wealth, value, security) against the eighth (shared resources) (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8 and §10; `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- Taurus retains and Scorpio purges. Martin frames it as nature's balance, in which "retention leads to release, incorporation to elimination" (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Martin).
- Silva frames the same axis as personal possessions against shared possessions and grand legacies (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Silva).
- Both are fixed, so Martin says the confrontation can be especially entrenched and unyielding, with no mediating gear. Fights on this axis concern ownership, inheritance, shared property and what may be destroyed (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Martin).
- Martin's dynamic: a Taurus-end attachment to holding produces the crises that break it open (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8; `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin).
- Possessiveness: Martin places it at the Taurus end, saying Scorpio is where we are forced to let go. Silva and Emerson treat possessiveness and jealousy as core Scorpio traits. Martin's reading is that a possessive Scorpio has collapsed onto the Taurus end (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- The axis also works well. Silva: Taurus keeps Scorpio's secrets and makes it laugh; Scorpio's intuition protects Taurus from grave mistakes, especially in love; and Taurus understands Scorpio's jealousy and is even flattered by it (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Silva).
- Seasonally: Beltane against Samhain (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- Myth: Midas (see §6).

**Ruling planet: Venus, and the dignities.**
- **Venus rules** (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). As a faculty it means valuation and relatedness: what things are worth, and to whom. Martin speaks of an individual's values, talents and joys; Venus also means beauty, comfort and gentleness (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Venus rules two signs, Taurus and Libra.** Taurus is the earthy face: the instinctive, natural sense of beauty and worth, against Libra's more social and idealized one (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **Venus's metal is copper**, which Martin describes as the emblem on Venus's throne (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §12, from Martin).
- **Exaltation: the Moon.** The Moon's faculty is care, belonging and the emotional container: the capacity to house and feed people and to give them somewhere to belong. Here it means nourishment, roots and constancy (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3; `02_Taurus.md` §10).
- **Detriment: Mars** (assertion, initiative, force). In Taurus, Mars works slowly and comes out as endurance (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §4).
- **Fall: none.** This is a feature of the classical system, not an extraction gap (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5 and §7). The GDD reads the absence as there being nothing the sign actively despises (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §6).
- **Across the axis**, the Moon goes one way only: exalted in Taurus, fallen in Scorpio (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- **Modern rulers:** under the modern scheme, Pluto's detriment would fall in Taurus. This is not stated in the corpus; the GDD derives it from the opposition rule (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §2).

**Squares (conflict): Leo and Aquarius.** Both are fixed, so the friction entrenches rather than escalates (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Leo: whose way is right.** Fixed against fixed, with no shared element. Neither yields, and the argument is never about facts, only about whose way is correct. Silva calls the chemistry explosive, an endless power play. In Emerson's version, Leo is social and commanding and Taurus accommodates, until it suddenly stops accommodating and will not move (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Silva and Emerson).
- **Aquarius: the material against the ideological.** Silva: Aquarius cares little about accumulating things, while Taurus enjoys it. Martin's image is a delayed bus. Taurus sits calmly in the sun, and Aquarius blames it for accepting the situation instead of joining the campaign for better public transport. Private contentment against collective reform, and each sees the other as a moral failure (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Silva and Martin).

**Trines (support): Virgo and Capricorn.**
- **Virgo** shares the practicality and the dislike of drama, and keeps Taurus's indulgence in check. Taurus finds Virgo's nervous tension a waste of energy (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9).
- **Capricorn** is willing to take calculated risks in unknown territory, and so gets Taurus moving. Capricorn's work without rest jars with Taurus's rest-taking (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9, from Tierney).

**Sextiles (working partners): Cancer and Pisces.**
- **Cancer** leans on the stable Taurus to keep things steady. The documentation separates two kinds of care: Cancer brings a warmer, deeper caring than Taurus's comforting presence. Taurus gives a warm room and a full plate, not emotional processing (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9).
- **Pisces** is kept grounded by Taurus, and gives Taurus the abstraction and inner life it cannot generate (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §9).

**Awkward aspects.**
- **Libra** (quincunx): the same ruler, with incompatible conclusions drawn from it. Tierney says the two see relationships very differently (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Tierney).
- **Sagittarius** (quincunx): the scorched-earth pairing (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Aries and Gemini** (semi-sextiles): nothing in common. Gemini is Taurus's corrective against routine (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §8, from Tierney).
- **The full Taurus row of the aspect matrix:** opposition Scorpio; squares Leo and Aquarius; trines Virgo and Capricorn; sextiles Cancer and Pisces; quincunxes Libra and Sagittarius; semi-sextiles Aries and Gemini (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4).

## 8. Decans

Emerson's sub-rulers. The decan material rests on two books and a few sentences per sign (Substrate `E_Decans.md`, header and §5).
1. **First decan: Taurus with itself, ruled by Venus.** Stable, determined and very sensual; the lover and the peacekeeper. It is set in its own values and ideas with little flexibility and has trouble adjusting. Indulgence and sexuality matter greatly to it. It reaches great heights in areas it has truly made its own (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
2. **Second decan: the Virgo blend, ruled by Mercury.** Very communicative but careful about what it says; musical and charming; often read as shy. Much more flexible than most Taureans, but its very pragmatic, realistic outlook can bore others (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
3. **Third decan: the Capricorn blend, ruled by Saturn.** A great builder, stern and sometimes dull, but completely loyal and as stable as anyone. Funny once people get past its stony manner. Highly sensual, it loves the finer things, and it is the most disciplined decan in the zodiac, with care, patience and prudence (Substrate `02_Taurus.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
