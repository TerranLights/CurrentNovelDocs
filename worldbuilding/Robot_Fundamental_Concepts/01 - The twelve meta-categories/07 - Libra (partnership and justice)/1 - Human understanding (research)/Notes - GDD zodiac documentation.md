## 1. Areas of life
- **The seventh house.** The documentation gives Libra's natural house as relating, contracts, marriage, and declared enemies. It stresses that the seventh house covers open adversaries as well as partners, so the partner and the opponent fall in the same house (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10).
- **Partnership as the point.** Libra is said to be most alive in real partnership. Cooperation, not solo achievement, is what it treats as the purpose (Full Attributes, Libra).
- **Justice, diplomacy and equality.** As Libra's ruler, Venus stands for these, alongside valuation (what things are worth, and to whom), beauty, comfort and plain gentleness (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Law and contracts.** Martin says the Venus that rules Libra governs laws, contracts, galleries and boulevards. Saturn, which rules law, is exalted in Libra (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Martin).
- **Justice as an impersonal principle.** Libra's scales are the only inanimate symbol in the zodiac. Tierney reads this as the point where impersonal justice enters the cycle: no special treatment, penalties for every rule-breaker, nobody above the law (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Consequence and delayed accounting.** Tierney frames Libra as the sign that teaches what Aries never learns: unchecked action has social consequences, and the reckoning can come later (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Civility.** Martin argues that without Libra there would be no orderly queues, no manners or etiquette, and no graceful buildings or boulevards. In her reading civility is structural, not decorative (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Beauty and harmony** in people and in spaces (Full Attributes, Libra).
- **Diplomacy, negotiation, arbitration and standards.** These are given as the strengths of the air family, of which Libra is the cardinal member (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney and Martin).
- **Measuring and weighing in trade, and even distribution.** The sign is named after a market scale. Its old names and emblems are commercial or agricultural, and a Roman coin pairs its scales with the even sharing-out of grain (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Social interaction.** Air is the people-watching element. Libra is drawn to how people behave across different cultural settings and to making dealings between people smooth (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Occupations.** Snodgrass's planetary table gives Venus goodwill ambassadors and social leaders (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV, from Snodgrass).
- **Living for others.** Tierney says Libra marks where in a person's life they do not wish to live only for themselves (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §5, from Tierney).

## 2. Core meaning
- **Archetype: the Balancer.** This is Tierney's term. He rejects "the Scales" as too impersonal (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1, from Tierney). The Full Attributes sheet calls Libra balance itself and the zodiac's diplomat (Full Attributes, Libra).
- **The turn toward the other.** Libra is the point in the zodiac where the self first admits that other people's claims are as valid as its own. It can no longer simply do as it pleases at others' expense (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Driving motive (the substrate's reading).** The motive is not kindness. It is the belief that a fair procedure is what keeps a community from tearing itself apart: get the process right and the underlying wounds will not break anything (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1).
- **A market tool made a symbol.** Snodgrass notes that Libra was modeled on an ordinary market measuring device yet serves as a mystic symbol of equity (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §1, from Snodgrass).
- **Core need (the substrate's reading).** Libra's need is standing, not peace: being the indispensable third party in someone else's arrangement. Silva puts it bluntly: Libra needs to be needed. Tierney adds that it cannot reach its own verdict; it has to hear how others respond before it knows whether it is on the right track (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §5, from Silva and Tierney).
- **Inner balance shows in outer relations.** Tierney's diagnostic: if the Balancer is secretly out of balance with itself, its partnerships show that discord plainly (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **The ideal union.** Martin's ideal for Libra is a union of two intact, self-determining people in which neither plays parent and neither plays child (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Martin).

## 3. The area at its best
- **Holding opposites.** Libra can keep two incompatible positions open without collapsing either. Tierney says Libra explores whether any two unlike things can still join forces (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Justice tempered with mercy.** Because Libra understands motive well enough to see conflicting drives inside bad behavior, it resists rigid black-and-white judgment (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2).
- **Impartiality.** Snodgrass says the type's appeal is a built-in trustworthiness and an absence of prejudice or bias. It has no favorites, and this is real, not performed (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Snodgrass).
- **Defending the absent.** Silva says Libra will not join a pile-on against someone who is not there to defend themselves (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Courage over clear injustice.** Libra hates confrontation, but Silva says it will lead a revolt over a clear injustice and give up its own freedoms for others (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Integrity under tyranny.** Tierney describes a hard hand inside the soft glove: under tyrannical pressure Libra would rather die for its principles than compromise and lose its integrity (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **The listed positives.**
  - a real, felt commitment to fairness and justice;
  - diplomatic skill at finding the solution most people can live with;
  - charm and social grace;
  - a sincere love of harmony and beauty;
  - a cooperative instinct.
  (Full Attributes, Libra; Quick Reference, Libra)
- **Calming heated rooms.** Libra reliably knows the right thing to say when tempers flare (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6).
- **Air's gifts.** These include:
  - perspective, negotiation, arbitration and standards;
  - insisting on clear, precise language;
  - holding two opposed positions without falling into either.
  (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney and Martin)
- **Persuasion and tact.**
  - Libra has the persuasive skill that decisive Aquarius lacks (Tierney).
  - It teaches blunt Sagittarius tact.
  - With Sagittarius it is one of the two signs that see the bigger picture and draw broad conclusions (Snodgrass).
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9)
- **Seeing persons, not possessions.** Tierney says Libra exists partly to correct Taurus's habit of treating a person as a possession (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9, from Tierney).

## 4. The area gone wrong
The substrate's summary of the material: Libra's harmony is often bought with someone else's suffering, and Libra experiences the purchase as fairness (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3). The sources converge on this.

**Fixing the surface, not the wound**
- **Band-aid solutions.** Silva says Libra adopts palliative fixes, papering over cracks when something more substantial is needed, and observers read this as turning a blind eye to the real issues (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Form mistaken for resolution.** Martin's example: shaking hands after a dispute deals with the form but leaves the emotional undercurrent untouched. Her reason is that Libra is not a water sign. Emotions are messy, uncivilized and often unfair, which is not Libra's territory (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Rationalizing a problem out of existence.** This is air's characteristic failure. When the buried feeling does surface, it arrives like a possession, an outburst the person cannot account for afterward, and air tends to run from anything messy (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).
- **Superficial harmony.** Libra can smooth the surface without resolving what is underneath (Full Attributes, Libra).

**Suppression, projection and the delayed explosion**
- **Pretending all is well.** Tierney describes Libran types who keep up too much sweetness and light. They suppress darker material, which then gets projected onto the antagonistic people in their lives. Libra can keep up the pretense indefinitely as long as nobody makes waves or touches a raw nerve (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **The unexploded bomb.** Silva says Libra is so set on never giving offense that it turns passive-aggressive and may then explode in rage at the smallest thing (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva). Tierney adds that the stored grievance eventually breaks out as outrage at being badly treated. Once that point is reached, Libra's sweet side is almost impossible to reach (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Projecting its own force.** Libra sits at the Venus end of an axis with Mars at the other. Martin says that identifying with the Libra end means a person's own potency, agency and focus get projected onto partners, which produces an uncomfortable double bind (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Martin). See also section 7.

**Keeping score**
- **Nothing given unconditionally.** Martin says Libra gives nothing without conditions, because that would upset the balance. Everything is quid pro quo (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3 and `A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).
- **The long memory.** Tierney says Libra never forgets abuse, always keeps score, and waits for the right moment to deliver an ultimatum. He pairs Libra with Capricorn as the two signs that track every favor asked and expect repayment (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Tierney).

**Bending fairness to its own advantage**
- **Rigging the scale.** Silva says Libra is not above weighting the scales in its own favor to convince people that something is unfair (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva). The substrate reads this as not experienced as cheating, but as correcting a distortion.
- **Manipulation with a public-spirited excuse.** Tierney calls Libra something of a manipulator and opportunist. It justifies this as coordinating a project that will, it hopes, reward everyone (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Tierney). Silva describes the Libra man as a socially adept manipulator who flirts his way into elite circles (same section, from Silva).
- **Exempting itself from the rules.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*, on the Libra Moon, says it ends up breaking rules it finds unconventional, because "you are the law" and so decides for itself what is right and wrong (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Justice as a weapon of exhaustion.** Silva says Libra's demands for justice can border on the extreme. It will debate opponents into the ground and stop at nothing to make sure people know when justice has not been served. It sulks visibly when it is not listened to (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva).

**Decision failure**
- **Delay felt as care.** Libra deliberates until the moment for action has passed and counts the delay as diligence. Silva warns not to rely on Librans in an emergency: they dither even in real danger, weighing options when action is needed. Martin tells of a client so good at balancing ideas that she was completely paralyzed (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva and Martin).
- **The listed negatives.**
  - indecision that leaves real decisions unmade;
  - conflict avoidance that lets problems fester unnamed;
  - people-pleasing that quietly wears away its own stated opinions;
  - procrastination, especially over unpopular calls.
  (Full Attributes, Libra; Quick Reference, Libra)
- **The same trait, pushed too far.** The Full Attributes sheet's closing pattern note: fairness pushed past its useful range becomes an inability ever to decide (Full Attributes, "Worth Your Attention"; Quick Reference, closing note).
- **Avoiding sole responsibility.** Libra avoids roles where a decision rests on it alone, and knows this about itself (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6).
- **The cardinal hustle.** Cardinal signs can look busy whether or not they produce anything. They start new things before finishing old ones, and sometimes they don't know when to stop pushing (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Tierney).

**Self and worth**
- **Narcissism with good intentions.** This is Silva's phrase. A confident front may hide a tortured soul that craves recognition. Libra hates being criticized but is comfortable criticizing others, and its charm turns petulant and aggressive when admiration is withheld (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Self-worth collapse.** This is named as one of Libra's greatest pitfalls: it cares for everyone else and does not count itself among those owed care. Emerson adds that Libra tends to treat a mistake as the end of the world (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Silva *Sun and Moon* and Emerson).
- **Dependency.** Libra can depend on others for its sense of identity or validation (Full Attributes, Libra).
- **Image over substance.** Tierney says Libra polishes itself into a class act because a good image draws social approval. Looking marvelous can matter more to it than actually feeling well (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §4, from Tierney).
- **Exploitable eagerness to please.** Silva says the Libra child's wish to please makes it unsafe around strangers. The Libra teenager falls for flattery and ends up doing the whole group project alone without complaint (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §7, from Silva).

**Grayness**
- **Bureaucracy and colorlessness.** Snodgrass, alone among the sources, blames Saturn. The type becomes stodgy, dull and bureaucratic, depends on consensus, and puts off quick judgment. He calls colorlessness a predictable Libra quality, born of sheltering on the safe side, out of harm's way; Libra is never a risk-taker (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Nobody's ally.** Silva says Libra is bipartisan at work and does not favor one person over another, which makes it a poor bet for anyone looking for an ally (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §4, from Silva).

**Smaller notes**
- **The first decan.** For all its focus on others, the first decan can be argumentative and unbalanced (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §2 and `07_Libra.md` §11, from Emerson and Silva).
- **Psychological symptoms.** Snodgrass lists jealousy for Libra as a pathology rather than a trait, alongside hypersensitivity and melancholia (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §12 and `G_Correspondences.md` Libra, from Snodgrass).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time
**At work**
- Libra suits front-of-house roles, complaints desks, protocol, VIP arrangements and event organizing (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6).
- It won't push a petty dispute upward. It turns a blind eye and keeps the distress inside.
- On its own it is easily distracted, and it misses deadlines by talking.
- It sometimes makes a sudden, unconsidered countermove. This unsettles people precisely because they expect it to deliberate (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6).

**Socially**
- It steers group talk away from contentious ground to keep the room comfortable.
- It treats every associate equally, refuses exclusive ties, and resents being claimed.
- It is last to arrive and last to leave, and drawn to whatever conversation is going on in the next room (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6).
- **How the type is said to read from outside.**
  - understated elegance, Silva's single pearl earring rather than a ruby necklace;
  - clean symmetrical lines;
  - chronic lateness that comes from deliberating, not indifference;
  - long messages and conversations that won't close;
  - beautified workspaces and ornate place settings.
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §4, from Silva and Snodgrass)
- Martin and Silva both note a gender balance in the type: something feminine about Libra men and masculine about Libra women (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §4, from Martin and Silva).

**Under stress**
- **Stalling.** Libra stalls to buy time (Tierney). Then come moody silence, slights taken inward, and an apathy that looks like laziness. When its equilibrium is off, so is Libra, and it cannot bear long stress without breaks to release the tension (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6, from Tierney).
- **Criticism.** Silva contrasts Libra with Cancer: Cancer bursts into tears at criticism, while Libra feels it deeply and broods on it in silence (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Silva).

**In crisis**
- **Paralysis, then one hard measure.** The substrate calls this paralysis-then-emergency shape Libra's native failure, not a contradiction of it (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6, from Tierney).
  - Libra dithers past the point of decision.
  - Then, being a cardinal sign that cannot stand long deliberation and wants tension released quickly, it swings hard into a single decisive measure that cannot be reviewed.
- **Cardinal reflexes.** Cardinal signs, frustrated by deadlock, stir up a small crisis so that something has to give. When provoked beyond limits, they feel the urge to attack or defend (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2 and `07_Libra.md` §6, from Tierney).
- **Retribution.** Pushed far enough, Libra wants the ax to fall so that justice is done, and becomes an agent of karmic retribution (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6, from Tierney).

**When secure.** The documentation says little directly. The nearest material:
- Martin's ideal of two intact, equal partners (section 2);
- the trine aspects' hidden reserve of strength, which the documentation gives only as a general property of trines (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Childhood and growing up** (Silva, Substrate `07_Libra.md` §7)
- **Exact fairness.** The Libra child insists on exactly equal shares, down to counted raisins. Otherwise it opens negotiations over fairness and equality.
- **Company and choices.** It is very social, talks early, and cannot bear being left alone. It agonizes over small choices, suspects it chose wrong, and then wants what everyone else has.
- **Analysis.** It is analytical enough to question the story of Santa Claus and break the news to its siblings.
- **Filing unfairness away.** When something feels unfair, the child withdraws and keeps the distress inside rather than raising it. The injustice is filed, not forgotten. It needs classrooms that are orderly but warm.
- **The teenage years.** As a teenager it gravitates to committees, debate and drama. It is sought out to arbitrate but walks away from disputes it wasn't invited into.
- **Origins of the fear of discord.** Martin says a Libra Ascendant is often shaped by a childhood home that was itself at war. That leaves a real fear of discord, anger or violence, felt as a threat to survival (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Martin).

**Over time**
- **The flower at the peak.** Rudhyar's cycle, via Martin, has the seed planted at Aries and rooted in Cancer, and the flower culminating in Libra: maximum clarity and objectivity. The waning phase begins right after. Libra is the peak, and the peak is where decline starts (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Martin).
- **The developmental task.** Martin's general method treats a sign's planets in detriment and fall as its developmental task, not a verdict (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin). For Libra those are Mars and the Sun (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). Read that way, maturing means owning its own force and its own center. ⚠️ This is the general rule applied to Libra's row. The documentation never spells it out for Libra.
- **Information.** Air develops the more freely a person can know things. Censorship and illiteracy stunt it, and all air signs dread social isolation (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations
- **Carved out of Scorpio.** The Greeks had no separate sign here, only *Chelae*, the Scorpion's claws. Roman astrologers parted the claws off as a separate constellation, and Ptolemy's *Almagest* made the name Libra standard. Martin calls Libra a later addition that borrowed Scorpio's claws to form the scales, and the Arabs still called those two stars the claws (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass and Martin). The substrate's reading: the instrument of justice is a severed limb, renamed.
- **Astraea.** Snodgrass links Libra to Astraea, goddess of justice. After the golden and silver ages ended, she took justice away from the earth and left humanity to its violence. The founding story here is not a court being set up but a judge leaving (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Themis, Justitia, Nemesis, Fortuna.** These figures show justice joined to retribution, and to impartiality toward the powerful (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13).
  - Silva traces the scales to Themis (Greek divine law) and Justitia (Roman).
  - Snodgrass ties the figure to Nemesis, glossed as comeuppance: a dispenser of retribution and righteous anger, shown with scales, yardstick and sword.
  - He also ties it to Astraea and Fortuna, the decisive judge who hands out penalties and rewards without regard to prestige, wealth or power.
- **Sword and scales.** Martin points to the Old Bailey statue holding a sword in one hand and scales in the other, and calls it an exact picture of the Aries–Libra axis: force and weighing held together (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Martin).
- **A measuring device, not a virtue.** *Libra* is the Roman balance-beam scale for weighing dry goods. The abbreviation lb. comes from it, and "pound" comes from *pondus*, the weights piled into the empty pan. Other cultures' names are commercial or agricultural:
  - Hebrew *Mozanaim* (the weighing scales);
  - Arabic *Alzubena* (the purchase), pictured as a figure holding scales in one hand and a lamb in the other;
  - Chinese *Ti* (the bottom);
  - Hindu *Visakha* (branched);
  - Coptic *Lambadia* (the gracious branch).
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass)
- **The Annona coin.** A coin from Titus's reign (about 80 A.D.) shows Annona, a harvest goddess, in front of an Egyptian grain ship. She holds a cornucopia, and a figure of justice holding scales as the emblem of even distribution of produce. Justice and the sharing-out of food appear on one object (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Judges and Rome.** Manilius held that Roman judges were born under Libra, and that Rome was founded with the Moon in Libra, so in righteousness and balance. His proof was that day and night hours were equal (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Herakles.** Libra corresponds to the labor of cleaning the dung-choked Augean stables, glossed as a restoration of balance. The substrate's reading: the labor that restores balance is the filthy one (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13).
- **Satire.** In Petronius's *Trimalchio's Feast*, a zodiac platter serves Libra as a balance with a tart in one pan and a cheesecake in the other. The host then mocks each sign in turn; the excerpt breaks off before Libra's line (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13 and §15, from Snodgrass).
- **Christian and ritual links.**
  - Libra is linked to the apostle Philip.
  - It belongs to the cardinal cross (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn), associated with the four archangels.
  - In Mylius's alchemical *Philosophia reformata* (1622), the third stage of the work begins in Libra.
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Snodgrass)
- **Pluto in Libra, 1971–1983.** Martin reads this transit collectively. Corruption and decay around equality, fairness, justice and trust came to the surface. She names Watergate, and dates from it the general loss of the assumption that people in power would behave ethically, legally and honorably (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13, from Martin).
- **The caduceus.** Snodgrass mentions the twin-snaked medical icon as the basis of Libra's heraldry of justice. He reads it as an equilibrium between self-governance and strength that carries over into health and well-being. The GDD notes that the corpus does not tell the caduceus apart from the single-snake staff of Asclepius (Substrate `13_Ophiuchus_Hub.md` §11, from Snodgrass).
- **Symbols.**
  - The glyph is two horizontal lines, and the scale is two pans on a central pivot, loaded until both hang level.
  - Other recorded readings of the symbol are a dairymaid's yoke balancing two milk pails, and the sun setting on the western horizon.
  - The constellation looks like an irregular triangle trailing two uneven lines.
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §12)
- **An abstract emblem.** The air signs are the only ones with human or abstract emblems instead of animals (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).
- **Old naming.** A Babylonian list from 419 B.C. already names Libra among the signs (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I, from Snodgrass). ⚠️ See Gaps on how this sits with the account of the Romans carving Libra out of Scorpio.

## 7. Structure
**Element: air.** (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, §4, from Martin and Tierney)
- **Mode.** Air abstracts, compares and puts things into words, keeping a deliberate gap between itself and the material.
- **Truth.** Martin pairs air against water on collective truth. For air, truth lies outside, in the systems people build; for water it lies inside, in shared emotional undercurrents.
- **Blind spot.** Air can lecture expertly on emotion as a subject while using that as a defense against feeling anything.
- **Its limits.** Martin warns that an air sign often doesn't grasp that reason alone cannot solve some problems (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Martin).
- **Libra's place in air.** Libra is the cardinal member. It forces issues into the open and trades (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Tierney and Martin).
- **Often mis-sorted.** Martin warns that because Libra rules relationships, people often assume it is a water sign, and it is not (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §4, from Martin).

**Modality: cardinal.**
- Initiating, goal-driven, fast, impatient with deadlock, and marking a seasonal hinge (the autumn equinox) (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10; `B_Modalities.md` §1–§2).
- The substrate's reading: this corrects any picture of Libra as passive. It starts things, and its stalling is a tactic inside a cardinal temperament, not a temperament of its own (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10).
- Cardinal means initiating, not loud. A soft-spoken, patient, procedurally polite sign can still be an initiator (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Martin).
- In the breathing image, cardinal is the in-breath (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Tierney).

**Polarity: active (also called positive, yang or masculine).**
- Martin calls Libra the masculine, active ("doing") cardinal air sign (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Snodgrass and Martin).
- Active mode moves outward from a center: it creates and destroys, and it parallels Jung's extraversion (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin).
- Active types are strong in the world but may lack inner reserves to fall back on (same section).
- The substrate's reading: Libra's balance is enacted on the world, not held privately (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10).

**Opposite sign: Aries. The axis is self versus other.**
- The two ends are autonomy against compromise and self-assertion against cooperation: Mars against Venus, the first house against the seventh (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Martin).
- **The default failure is projection.** Each side identifies with one pole and meets the other as an outside enemy, and the party that brings the buried material up gets blamed for it (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- Martin, applying this to Libra, says that when a person identifies with the Libra end, Mars gets projected: their own potency and agency show up in their partners (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Martin).
- Tierney's inversion: Libra is an Aries who has learned to charm others into cooperating. Aries fights for what it wants; Libra persuades others to become allies so the goal is reached anyway (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Tierney).
- **What the opposition does.** The opposition is the deepest conflict, not the loudest. The two signs share modality and polarity and differ only in element, which is why oppositions read as complementary and, when they resolve, resolve into balance (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Exaltation and fall trade across the axis.** Aries exalts the Sun, which falls in Libra; Libra exalts Saturn, which falls in Aries (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).

**Ruling planet: Venus, in domicile.**
- Martin's key distinction: Libra's Venus is not Aphrodite but closer to Athena, the warrior goddess and patron of architecture and the civilized arts (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, from Martin).
- Here Venus rules laws, contracts, galleries and boulevards (same section).
- As a faculty, Venus is valuation and relatedness (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- A ruler is the faculty a sign exercises without effort and cannot see as a choice (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §1, §4, from Martin).

**The other dignities.**
- **Saturn exalted.** Saturn also rules law, so law-making is where Saturn is comfortable. Martin and Silva read this as positive: discipline, duty, law. Snodgrass reads the same placement as the source of Libra's grayness. See Gaps (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, §15).
  - The documentation's general meaning of exaltation: a faculty that is a guest of honor, amplified and somewhat performed (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4).
- **Mars in detriment.** Libra cannot comfortably hold its own capacity for force, which is why it projects it (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10).
  - Detriment generally means the faculty works against the grain and survives only by being disguised as something else (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).
- **Sun in fall.** The substrate's reading: a self with no center of its own, defined only by its relations (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10).
  - Fall generally means the least-trusted faculty, one the sign looks down on (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin). As a faculty, the Sun is identity, and authority that does not depend on others' opinions (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).

**Squares (conflict).** Both squares are cardinal, so the conflicts move: they escalate, force decisions and recur, and both parties may welcome the challenge (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2; `B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin).
- **Cancer: air against water, reason against feeling.**
  - Tierney says Cancer gets a square of blockage or resistance from argumentative Libra.
  - Martin explains why: Libra is uncomfortable when emotional issues are stirred up, because emotion is messy and unfair.
  - The substrate's reading: grief is not something Libra's scales can weigh, and the sources treat this as a structural incapacity, not malice.
  - Silva notes the two share the wound of criticism but express it in ways each cannot read in the other.
  (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8)
- **Capricorn: principle against practical result.**
  - Martin frames this as rational principles against the need to achieve something concrete.
  - Tierney pairs the two as overly proper and formal, and as signs that track favors owed.
  - Tierney also says Libra knows how unfair society can be when it tries to regulate its members' private lives, and here it is at odds with rule-enforcing Capricorn.
  - Emerson: Capricorn eventually gets fed up with Libra's wavering and leaves (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8).
  - Silva on the earth–air pairing: Libra values its mind, Capricorn doesn't care about words without results. They can work together but stay a mystery to each other, and the conflict they produce is procedural, not personal (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva and Martin).

**Trines and sextiles (support).**
- **Gemini (trine).** Both are people-watchers. Gemini watches to collect; Libra watches to understand and to be liked (Tierney). Gemini supplies the information Libra's deliberation needs, and Libra supplies balance (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9).
- **Aquarius (trine).** The strongest ally in the corpus, because Aquarius is decisive and Libra takes its lead from it (Silva). Libra in turn has the persuasive skill Aquarius lacks (Tierney). Silva's most compatible signs for Libra are Gemini and Aquarius (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9).
- **Air's own failure.** Trines generally risk complacency. Martin's specific failure for a grand trine in air is mental inertia, the perpetual student, where detachment mistakes itself for action (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2 and §5, from Martin).
- **Leo (sextile).** Easy and mutually flattering, warm on the surface and shallow underneath (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9, from Emerson).
- **Sagittarius (sextile).** Productive. Libra teaches tact, and Sagittarius pushes Libra to act (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9, from Tierney and Snodgrass).

**Quincunxes and semi-sextiles (awkward).**
- **Taurus and Pisces (quincunx).** Awkward, not hostile.
  - Taurus and Libra are the two Venus signs with very different views of relationships, and Libra corrects Taurus's habit of treating a person as a possession (Tierney).
  - Emerson: Taurus needs certainty, and Libra offers doubt (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §9).
  - The documentation gives nothing specific on Libra and Pisces.
- **Virgo and Scorpio (semi-sextile, the adjacent signs).** Neighbors that share nothing structurally (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, §4). Scorpio's tie to Libra is historical: Libra was cut out of Scorpio's claws (section 6).

## 8. Decans
Silva and Emerson agree on all three. The GDD notes that Silva's Libra text shares wording with Emerson's and is not independent confirmation (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, §5).
- **First decan, Libra/Venus: peace and dependence.** Called the most peace-loving of the zodiac: natural aesthetes, ceremonious, wanting the finer things, and acutely aware of every kind of relationship. Shadow: despite their focus on others, they can be quarrelsome and unbalanced. Their central dilemma is how much to depend on others and how much to go it alone.
- **Second decan, Aquarius/Uranus (Silva adds Saturn): the mind and society.** Called the deep, or deepest, Libras: absorbed in how the mind works and in the workings of society, drawn to abstraction and higher learning. A paradox in intimacy: they want total sharing and commitment, yet need far more solitude and space than they realize. The substrate glosses them as the people who study why anyone obeys.
- **Third decan, Gemini/Mercury: restlessness and communication.** Called the most restless Libras: ambivalence on the surface, romantically unsettled and always looking for the perfect romance; intuitive and logical at once; extremely versatile; effortlessly charming. They want communication more than any other sign.

## 12. Gaps
- **The house material is thin.** The documentation names the seventh house's domains (relating, contracts, marriage, open enemies) but develops only one-to-one relating. Contracts, marriage as an institution, and open enemies as a category get one line each (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §10, §15).
- **Almost nothing at group scale.** The GDD itself says nearly every Libra passage is about one-to-one relating. It has no source treatment of Libra as a committee, institution or majority (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §15). Collective justice, courts, legislatures and diplomacy between groups are therefore barely covered.
- **No material on scarcity.** Silva assumes affluence throughout. The sources say nothing about how this temperament behaves when there is not enough to share fairly (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §15). For an area that includes fair distribution, this is the most consequential hole.
- **No Egyptian lineage.** There is no Ma'at, no weighing of the heart and no Anubis anywhere in the corpus. The scales-of-judgment lineage is entirely Greco-Roman (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §15).
- **"When secure" is barely covered.** The documentation says almost nothing about Libra at ease or at its healthiest beyond the light-side trait lists.
- **Maturity is barely covered.** The only developmental material is Rudhyar's cycle image and Martin's general rule about detriment and fall. Nothing describes Libra in later life.
- **Source disagreements recorded in the documentation.**
  - **Saturn.** Snodgrass calls Saturn the self-righteous planet Libra obeys and the source of its gray, bureaucratic side. Everyone else has Venus as ruler and Saturn only exalted, and Martin and Silva read that exaltation as positive (discipline, duty, law) (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §15). The GDD treats the tension itself as meaningful.
  - **Opal.** Silva gives opal to Libra as birthstone; Emerson and Snodgrass's Moon row give it to Cancer (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Cancer).
  - **Lapis lazuli.** Libra's stone in Emerson and Silva, but Snodgrass's planet-keyed table puts lapis under Uranus. The GDD reads this as two systems, not a contradiction (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Libra).
  - **Modality.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* calls Libra a fixed sign in its own chapter header, against the same book's list of cardinal signs. The GDD treats this as an error (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §15).
  - **Passive or initiating.** The Full Attributes sheet stresses conflict avoidance and indecision. The substrate, following the cardinal reading and Silva's and Tierney's material on revolt and retribution, stresses that Libra initiates and can strike hard. These are compatible (stall, then strike), but the two GDD files weight them differently (Full Attributes, Libra; Substrate `07_Libra.md` §6, §10).
  - ⚠️ **Libra's origin.** Snodgrass's myth section says the Romans parted Libra from Scorpio's claws, and Ptolemy formalized the name. Yet the GDD's timeline from Snodgrass's Appendix I has a 419 B.C. Babylonian list that already names Libra. The documentation does not reconcile the two (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §13; `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).
