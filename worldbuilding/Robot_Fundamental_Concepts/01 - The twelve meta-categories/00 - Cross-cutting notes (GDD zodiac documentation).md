## 1. Elements: four families of areas

**What the four group.** The elements do not sort the signs by what they want. They sort by how experience is taken in before it becomes a want: which channel of information a temperament trusts, and which it discounts (A §1).
- **Martin's truth axis** (A §1, from Martin):
  - fire and earth contend over **personal truth**. Fire's truth is an inner conviction that needs no proof. Earth's truth is what its own five senses report about the world, and if earth dislikes how something smells or tastes, that settles it.
  - air and water contend over **collective truth**. For air it lives outside, in the systems people build. For water it lives inside, in the feeling undercurrents everyone shares.
- **Each element holds one sign of each modality:** one initiator, one holder, one distributor. An element is a shared channel, never a single personality (A §1, §8).
- **Order.** Martin says each element grows more mature through the zodiac's order. Tierney adds that fire opens each run of four signs, water closes it, and earth always follows fire as its brake (A §1).
- **Pairing.** Fire and air are the outward pair; earth and water the receptive pair (Silva, Emerson). Emerson's rough formula is that fire needs air to burn and earth cannot live without water (A §1).
- **Absence.** Martin: a person with no planets in an element cannot manufacture that function. The GDD reads this as: a missing element is replaced by a substitute that never fully works (A §1).

**Inside each family** (A §2-5). Modality makes the three signs of one element different:
- **Fire.** Aries is primal and fastest, impatient for results. Leo is contained fire, the most dependable and loyal, living at the center of its own myth. Sagittarius is fire spreading far and wide: the most restless, and the one that mistakes its own conviction for the general truth (credulous, per Tierney).
- **Earth.** Taurus is the most entrenched. Virgo is earth made workable, the regulator that tones things down so nothing wears out from overstimulation (Tierney). Capricorn is the one earth sign willing to venture into the unknown for calculated risks and large-scale prominence (Tierney).
- **Air.** Gemini gathers knowledge for itself, skims, and takes feelings apart rather than feel them (Tierney). Libra trades: nothing is given unconditionally, because that would upset the balance (A §4; `07_Libra.md` §3 attributes this to Martin). Aquarius holds fixed ideals rather than possessions, so it can suddenly revolutionize itself (Tierney).
- **Water.** Cancer is the most demonstrative and cannot hide what it is going through; it is tidal, and when the tide is out only the shell is left and nobody answers (Martin); it expects to hear only what it can handle (Tierney). Scorpio is a deep, still well, composed in a crisis, compelled to bring polluted feeling to the surface so it can be cleared, and prone to treat vulnerability as weakness (Tierney). Pisces is the chameleon that takes the color of its surroundings, hard to define, and the only water sign capable of grand overviews (Martin, Tierney).

**How the families wear on each other** (A §6). The sources describe mechanisms, not just compatibility:
- **Fire and earth (fuel and smother).** Fire needs what smothers it. The friction is over pace and permission: fire treats paperwork for its own plan as an insult and does not clean up afterward (Martin's party image: Virgos washing up while the fire signs hold forth). Earth meters fire and shuts the party down to save energy (Tierney). Earth tends to give up on fire before fire gives up on itself (Silva).
- **Fire and air (combustion).** Air feeds fire and supplies the planning fire lacks, but air can kill a fire idea with one fact (Martin). Fire does not argue back; it leaves (Martin). The result is much energy and many abandoned conversations.
- **Fire and water (mutual loss).** Water douses fire's enthusiasm; fire dries water out (Martin, Silva). Fire's offense is volume and unfiltered honesty; fire in turn scorns water's worry about the future (Tierney). It is the pairing that starts best and depletes both sides (Silva).
- **Earth and air (talking past each other).** No hostility and no comprehension: Libra values the mind, Capricorn values only results (Silva). It produces procedural conflict (committees, disputes over standards) rather than grievance (GDD derives).
- **Earth and water (the natural fit).** Earth gives water a container; water gives earth an inner life (Silva). The trapdoor: water can open feelings in earth that earth cannot handle, and two receptive elements agree too easily to correct each other (Silva; GDD derives the second point).
- **Air and water (the opposition).** Martin's dialogue with a mother whose son is a water sign: she will never get a straight answer, only deflection, because evasion is water's defense. Water finds it intolerable that air settles a feeling by explaining it. Air simply cannot handle intense emotional demands (Silva). They are also each other's medicine: water is air's shadow (Martin). The pair works well until it gets serious, then fails on the axis that made it work.
- **Same-element pairs.** Fire with fire: instant recognition, two parties who both need to be the center, rivalry with no stopping mechanism (Silva). Earth with earth: genuinely compatible and closed to everyone else (Silva). Air with air: endless ideas, no decision that costs a feeling (Silva). Water with water: total understanding without differentiation; disputes become silent, sulking standoffs that never resolve (Silva).

**Disagreements and errors the GDD records** (A §4, §9):
- Aquarius is an air sign despite the water-bearer (Snodgrass). Libra is air, though often assumed to be water because it governs relationships (Martin).
- Snodgrass calls Capricorn a water sign once, apparently reasoning from the sea-goat's tail. Every other source has earth.
- Snodgrass alone describes the water signs as persuaders and fun-lovers. Every other source has them private and solitude-seeking.
- Air-against-air conflict is thinly covered, and the GDD partly infers it.

## 2. Modalities: three modes of approach

**What a modality is.** It is a sign's position within a season (B §1, from Snodgrass and Tierney).
- **Cardinal** opens a season: the turning point when the new season rushes in and clears out the old.
- **Fixed** is the season at its height, when the days can seem almost the same.
- **Mutable** is the season breaking up, getting unpredictable and preparing the next turn.

**One process, not three temperaments** (B §1).
- Tierney: each stage exists because the one before it exhausted itself. Mutability breaks down what the fixed phase has hardened past usefulness.
- **Tierney's breathing image:** cardinal breathes in, fixed holds the breath, mutable breathes out, and what goes out has been changed.
- **Martin's terms:** initiating, resisting and mediating. She ties the number three to relating two opposed principles, and to Plato's three-part soul: a spirited part, an appetitive part and a reasoning part.
- **Martin's law of three:** Aries (force) meets equal resistance in Taurus, and the deadlock is resolved by Gemini, which relates and mediates without taking sides (B §1; C §4). Every run of three neighboring signs has this shape.
- **Martin's boulder in the road.** Three travelers find a boulder blocking the road. Cardinal climbs over it. Mutable goes around, or takes another road. Fixed spends years drilling through it. Each is answering a different question: how do I get past this, where else can I go, and how do I make this stop being an obstacle for good.

| Mode | Signs | Approach to change | Good at | Fails as | Under pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cardinal** (initiating) | Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn | Starts the change and expects others to follow. Acts directly, with little preplanning (Tierney); has a vision and the drive to start (Martin). Emerson calls these signs the Initiators | Opening; taking an opportunity in the window it exists; forcing a decision others avoid; open negotiation (Tierney) | Starting the next thing before finishing the last; looking busy whether or not anything gets built; not knowing when to stop pushing (Tierney) | Stirs up a small crisis to break a stalemate; cannot stand drawn-out deliberation or foot-dragging; attacks or defends when provoked (Tierney) (B §2) |
| **Fixed** (resisting) | Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius | Does not refuse change; refuses to be moved from outside. Its motivation to change is always internal, and interference is unwelcome (Tierney); it digs in when pressured (Martin). Emerson calls these signs the workhorses | Finishing, holding, enduring; patience; loyalty; perseverance (Tierney, Martin) | Stalemate and battles of will; resisting the easier solution because it came from outside; clinging to routine when variety would help (Tierney) | Holds, and outlasts the tension rather than resolving it (Martin). Tierney finds an inborn pride in all four, and Silva says they believe their way is correct, not stubborn (B §3) |
| **Mutable** (mediating) | Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces | Takes in change and passes it on modified: wears down fixed resistance and redistributes the energy for the next start (Tierney) | Noticing where adjustment is needed before anyone else; going around obstacles; mediating between the unstoppable force and the immovable object (Martin); caring more for the journey than the arrival | Scattering its energy; finding long-range direction hard; wavering, switching sides and reneging when a commitment starts to feel like a trap, sure it has a right to change its mind whatever it promised (Tierney) | Leaves, or becomes the go-between. Martin: mutable signs avoid conflict at all costs, except as mediators. Silva (on Virgo): takes the path of least resistance (B §4) |

**Notes on each mode** (B §2-4):
- Cardinal means initiating, not loud. Cancer gets what it wants quietly, by waiting for the tide (Martin).
- Aquarius is the odd fixed sign: it holds an idea, not a possession (Tierney).
- The mutable signs share an intolerance of monotony, and their resilience reads as youthfulness (Tierney). Sagittarius is the mutable exception that plunges in like a cardinal.

**How the modes collide** (B §5). Signs three apart (squares) and six apart (oppositions) always share a modality and always differ in element (Martin; Silva confirms the opposition half). So in this system the hard relationships are all same-tempo, different-aim; the GDD's summary is that sworn enemies are found inside one's own modality.
- **Cardinal against cardinal:** a welcome fight, energizing and goal-directed (Martin). It ends because both sides need it to end.
- **Fixed against fixed:** the real war (Martin: rigid, unyielding, resisting until something forces change). Neither side escalates or goes around, and each is sure it is right (Silva). The GDD derives that such grudges are inherited.
- **Mutable against mutable:** the easiest square, full of accommodation (Martin), so nothing gets decided (Silva). From outside it looks like agreement.
- **Cardinal and fixed:** never a square, so the friction is chronic rather than explosive. Fixed signs dislike pushy people (Tierney). The pipeline version: fixed finishes what cardinal starts. Martin warns that cardinal plus fixed with no mutable can start and finish but cannot find a way around anything.
- **Fixed and mutable:** erosion. The mutable side offers no argument; it routes around the position until it no longer matters (GDD derives from Tierney).
- **Cardinal and mutable:** a coin flip. It works if the mutable side is asked to follow, and fails if it is asked to commit in advance (Martin).

**The crosses.** The four signs of a modality form what Snodgrass calls a ritual cross: the cardinal, fixed and mutable crosses (B §5). The sign files carry associations for them:
- the cardinal cross with the four archangels (`07_Libra.md` §13; `10_Capricorn.md` §12);
- the fixed cross with the four living creatures (bull, lion, eagle and the human figure; Aquarius is Matthew's angel, the only human among them) (`11_Aquarius.md` §13);
- the mutable cross under the name *moveable cross* (`09_Sagittarius.md` §12).

**Disagreements and gaps** (B §8):
- **Who finishes.** Tierney and Martin have fixed finishing what cardinal starts. Emerson and Silva call the mutable signs the Finishers. The GDD follows Tierney and Martin, as the only version derived from the seasons.
- Silva alone calls the cardinal signs reactive, without explanation.
- No source gives the etymology of "cardinal" (Latin *cardo*, a hinge).
- No source covers a group missing a whole modality; Martin covers only individuals, through compensation.

## 3. Polarity: the active-receptive split and the six axes

### 3a. Active and receptive

**The split** (C §1).
- The signs alternate around the wheel, six and six. Fire and air are active; earth and water are receptive (unanimous in Martin, Emerson and both Silva books).
- Martin gives the old physical reason: earth and water have weight and move downward; air and fire move upward. The split is about direction, not value.

**Names.** The sources also say masculine/feminine, positive/negative, active/passive and yang/yin. They disclaim the gendered pair themselves (Martin: about psyches, not biology; Emerson likewise). The GDD uses Active and Receptive, which Emerson attests, and rejects "passive" (C header).

**The GDD's caution.** Polarity is a coarser cut of the element split and adds no per-sign information. Its one addition is which elements are allies: fire with air, and earth with water, are complementary, not identical (C §1).

**Active** (C §2).
- Martin: motion from a center outward; driving, forceful, impulsive and dominant; it creates and destroys. It corresponds to Jung's extraversion: meaning drawn from activity and achievement in the world.
- Failures: motion mistaken for progress; strength in the world without inner reserves to fall back on (Martin, from her class); dominance; overreach into others' business.

**Receptive** (C §3).
- Martin: inward-moving, yielding, enclosing, reflective, thinking before acting. It corresponds to Jung's introversion. Silva: a self-contained quiet strength.
- **Not passive or weak.** Martin agrees with a student that yin people seem frail but are stronger, with a deep inner reservoir, and become very strong in a crisis.
- Failures: withdrawal past self-protection into isolation (Jung: hesitation and distrust of things; Whitmont: the introvert fears the object); holding what should be released; stagnation; and silence that others read as consent.

**Alternation** (C §4). Neighbors on the wheel always have opposite polarity. The awkward aspects all cross polarity; the workable ones, including the opposition, share it. Opposites fight in a shared mode; mismatched neighbors fail to make contact at all (GDD derives).

**Projection** (C §6, from Martin after Jung). Identify with one pole and the other becomes unconscious and is projected onto other people or the world. It works as compensation: what we cannot accept in ourselves comes back to us as fate.

**Set aside.** The GDD notes and drops Silva's gender essentialism, which Silva himself questions without answering (C §6).

### 3b. The six axes of opposite signs

**What an axis is.**
- Opposite signs share modality and polarity and differ only in element (D §1). They are near-twins aimed at incompatible objects, so they read as complementary, and when they resolve, they resolve into balance rather than victory.
- The usual failure is to identify with one end and meet the other as an outside enemy (Martin) (D §2).
- Each axis also pairs two natural houses six apart.
- On three axes the two signs trade exaltation and fall: what one end amplifies, the other despises (F §5).

## 4. Aspect geometry: which areas clash and which support each other

**Why distance matters** (D §1). The twelve signs are built from three overlapping cycles: element repeats every four signs, modality every three, and polarity alternates every sign. So the distance between two signs fixes exactly what they share.

| Distance | Aspect | What the two signs share | How the documentation reads it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Conjunction | everything | Merger with no outside view; any criticism of one half is felt as an attack on the identity of both (D §2, from Martin) |
| 1 | Semi-sextile, 30° | nothing, but they are neighbors | They see each other only in peripheral vision and have nothing in common (Martin); the friction of living next to someone who does everything differently (Emerson) |
| 2 | Sextile, 60° | polarity only | Complementary and productive when put to work: Martin's image is bees and honeycomb; Emerson's words are communication and friendship. Failure: a comfortable arrangement nobody ever renegotiates |
| 3 | **Square, 90°** | modality only | Same urgency, different object. Martin: a real issue that strikes to the core, and also the energy that makes things happen. Its temperature depends on the modality: cardinal squares move, fixed squares entrench, mutable squares dissolve and re-form (D §2). The GDD derives that a blocked square turns its pressure onto whoever is nearest and least able to answer |
| 4 | Trine, 120° | element and polarity | Easy and accepting, an inner reservoir found when needed (Martin). Failure: complacency. A grand trine is so complete it cancels itself out (Martin), and medieval astrologers thought the trine evil because its luck made people lazy (Emerson) |
| 5 | Quincunx, 150° | nothing, and not neighbors | Martin: awkward and vaguely annoying; bound together, yet they do not recognize each other. Constant small adjustments, the same misunderstanding repeating (Martin, Emerson). Emerson: either a soulmate or Beelzebub. Two quincunxes plus a sextile form a Yod, whose apex becomes a pivot (Martin) (D §3) |
| 6 | **Opposition, 180°** | modality and polarity | Maximum tension: two ends that seem mutually exclusive yet balance each other; feeling caught between poles (Martin). The default is projection, meeting one's disowned half in other people, and whoever brings a group's buried material to the surface is blamed for doing so (Martin). Productive as a governor: neither side can go to an extreme (Martin). Emerson: cooperate or break it off |

**Reading notes** (D §1, §2, §7).
- **Martin's number symbolism:** One is unity, Two division, Three reconciliation, Four manifestation. The conflict series gets easier as it subdivides, while the harmony series gets more stressful (trine, then sextile, then semi-sextile and quincunx). Neither kind of aspect is morally good or bad.
- **Silva:** signs in square make better relationships than signs with no aspect at all. Engagement beats indifference.
- **Martin:** squares are not necessarily hard and trines not necessarily easy; each aspect must be read in its own context. Her *dissociate* aspects keep the arithmetic but not the structure.

**The full matrix** (D §4). The GDD derives it from zodiac order; four statements in the books corroborate it. Each sign has one opposite, two squares, two trines, two sextiles, two quincunxes and two semi-sextiles.

**Closed groups** (D §5).
- Every square and opposition falls inside one of three closed groups of four, the grand crosses, one per modality. Martin describes a closed grand cross as driven and fiercely self-sufficient, relying on no one.
- Every trine falls inside one of four closed triangles, the grand trines, one per element. Martin's failure for a grand trine in air is a mental inertia, the perpetual student: detachment mistaking itself for action.

**Corroborations and limits** (D §4, §8).
- The corroborations:
  - Tierney has Cancer squared by both Aries and Libra.
  - Silva has Virgo square Sagittarius, Capricorn square Libra, and Capricorn sextile Scorpio.
  - Martin uses Aries-Virgo as her worked quincunx and Pisces-Aries as her worked pair of neighbors.
- No book gives systematic sign-pair readings; Martin teaches by planet pairs.
- Martin vol. 3 distinguishes waxing from waning squares: the waning square carries a Capricorn theme (collective structures meant to last), the waxing square a Cancer theme (putting down roots for personal growth).
- Half-square aspects (45° and 135°) do not fall on sign boundaries and have no sign-level reading.

## 5. Decans as a system

**What a decan is** (E §1).
- Each sign covers 30° and is split into three 10° parts, called decans or decanates.
- Silva traces the word to the Greek *dekanoi*, from an Egyptian calendar of twelve 30-day months, each split into three ten-day parts.
- Both sources agree that a decan **refines a sign without changing it**. The decan's planet is a sub-ruler; Silva compares it to a vice president.

**The scheme** (E §1). The corpus gives one scheme and never mentions another.
- The first decan is the sign blended with itself, at double strength, under its own ruler.
- The second decan is the sign blended with the next sign of its element.
- The third decan is the sign blended with the remaining sign of its element.
- The sub-ruler is simply the ruler of the blending sign. So every sub-theme inside a sign borrows from one of its two element siblings, in zodiac order.

| Sign | 1st decan | 2nd decan | 3rd decan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aries | Aries (Mars) | Leo (Sun) | Sagittarius (Jupiter) |
| Taurus | Taurus (Venus) | Virgo (Mercury) | Capricorn (Saturn) |
| Gemini | Gemini (Mercury) | Libra (Venus) | Aquarius (Uranus) |
| Cancer | Cancer (Moon) | Scorpio (Pluto) | Pisces (Neptune) |
| Leo | Leo (Sun) | Sagittarius (Jupiter) | Aries (Mars) |
| Virgo | Virgo (Mercury) | Capricorn (Saturn) | Taurus (Venus) |
| Libra | Libra (Venus) | Aquarius (Uranus) | Gemini (Mercury) |
| Scorpio | Scorpio (Pluto; Silva: Mars and Pluto) | Pisces (Neptune; Silva: Jupiter and Neptune) | Cancer (Moon) |
| Sagittarius | Sagittarius (Jupiter) | Aries (Mars) | Leo (Sun) |
| Capricorn | Capricorn (Saturn) | Taurus (Venus) | Virgo (Mercury) |

(E §2. Sub-rulers are Emerson's unless marked. Each sign's decan descriptions belong to its own notes.)

**The pattern the GDD reads across the twelve** (E §3, GDD derives from thin data):
- **The first decan** is the sign undiluted. Silva calls one first decan the purest form, "enhanced in good and bad ways," and the one still learning to use its energy. So the sign's shadow is most exposed here.
- **The second decan** pursues the same motive by a sibling sign's method: redirected, not softened.
- **The third decan** is, across the corpus's entries, the most versatile, articulate and public-facing, and also the most compromised. Examples: the most humanitarian Gemini decan, the Aquarius decan that appeals to the public, the Aries decan that will never let anyone control it to the point of ruined relationships, the Leo decan that refuses to admit being wrong.
- The GDD notes that its spatial reading of this pattern is its own design decision, not anything the books say.

**Extra layers for two signs only** (E §2):
- **Virgo** has keywords and constellations: *Analyze* (Crater, the cup of Apollo: emotional generosity), *Efficient* (Hercules, who defeats evil) and *Discriminate* (Boötes, the herdsman: using wisdom).
- **Scorpio** has decan names and tarot cards: *Intensity* (Five of Cups: grief, loss, anger), *Transmutation* (Six of Cups: peace, forgiveness, harmony) and *Manifestation* (Seven of Cups: finding where one fits, soul-searching).

**Weakness** (E header, §5):
- **Coverage.** Only two of the eight books mention decans (Emerson; Silva's *Ultimate Guide*). Silva covers only four signs (Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Pisces), and his Libra text shares Emerson's wording, so it is not independent. Eight signs rest on Emerson's three to six sentences each.
- **Dates.** They conflict between the books and within each.
- **Errors.** The Pisces third-decan ruler is disputed, and there are typos (a "fifth decan"; a Libra range printed as 10°-30°).
- **What is missing.** The entries are mostly lists of positive traits with little shadow. Nothing covers the edge between two decans, no other decan system is acknowledged, and Ophiuchus has none.

## 6. Rulerships: the planets as drives that cut across areas

**What rulership claims** (F §1).
- A sign's ruling planet is the faculty the sign is organized around: used without effort and not seen as a choice. Martin: the planet is at home and comfortable in the sign.
- **The scheme is symmetrical** (Martin). The Greeks assigned the rulers outward from the two lights, in order of the planets' observed speed:
  - the Sun to Leo, at the hottest point of the year, and the Moon to Cancer;
  - then Mercury (Gemini, Virgo), Venus (Taurus, Libra), Mars (Aries, Scorpio) and Jupiter (Sagittarius, Pisces);
  - and slowest, Saturn (Capricorn, Aquarius), at the coldest and darkest point of the year.
- Every planet except the two lights rules one active and one receptive sign. **So each faculty appears in two postures.** Martin's example is Mars: in Aries it is instinctive force, while Scorpio's Mars is the trained professional soldier rather than the brawler, the knife used with skill by a surgeon.

**Traditional and modern rulers** (F §2).
- Three signs have two rulers: Scorpio (Mars, later Pluto), Aquarius (Saturn, later Uranus) and Pisces (Jupiter, later Neptune).
- Martin prefers the traditional rulers, because they stay within Saturn's boundary and keep the reading grounded.
- She also reads one pair as a life sequence: in Aquarius, Saturn's conformity rules the first half of life, and Uranus emerges later as the person grows comfortable deviating from the norm.
- Under the modern rulers, the detriments shift too: Pluto's detriment falls in Taurus, Uranus's in Leo and Neptune's in Virgo. The GDD forces these from the opposition rule; they are not in the corpus.

**The planets as drives** (F §3, mostly Martin, restated in human terms):

| Planet | The drive | Notes from the documentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun** | Identity and named authority | The capacity to be a center others organize around; authenticity and authority that do not depend on others' opinions (Martin) |
| **Moon** | Care, belonging, the emotional container | Martin on the fourth house: the quality of the nurture we received, and how safe we feel about belonging |
| **Mercury** | Analysis, record, transmission, verification | Objectivity; truth as partial and multiple; the humor that comes from holding more than one frame |
| **Venus** | Valuation and relating | A person's values, talents and joys (Martin); beauty and comfort; as Libra's ruler, justice, diplomacy and equality (Martin); gentleness |
| **Mars** | Assertion, initiative, force | Standing up for oneself and making a claim against someone; either instinctive and hot, or trained and disciplined (Martin) |
| **Jupiter** | Meaning, scale, generosity | Seeing the wood as well as the trees; extending belonging past the clan to community, society, country and ancestors (Martin) |
| **Saturn** | Limits, structure, time, law | Discipline, patience, the deferred reward, the boundary that holds; the faculty that says no and makes it stick. Martin calls Saturn the lord of time |
| **Uranus** | Refusal and disruption | Foresight, invention, challenge to the status quo; Martin: where in the chart we refuse to engage |
| **Neptune** | Dissolution and devotion | Compassion, mercy, imagination; erasing the line between self and other; escape; Martin: the part of us that longs to return to formlessness |
| **Pluto** | Purging and regeneration | Bringing up what has turned poisonous or taboo so it can be cleared; power and its abuse, which poisons self and others (Martin) |

**The four dignities** (F §4):
- **Domicile:** the faculty is native and free. Its owner does not experience it as a skill and is worst at explaining it.
- **Exaltation:** the faculty is an honored guest. It is amplified and celebrated, a little overdone, and a source of pride in a way the ruler never is.
- **Detriment:** the sign opposite the planet's home. The faculty works against the grain but is not absent. Martin is emphatic: told that Venus in Aries works badly, she answers that it is a powerful Venus, because it knows what it wants. It survives by being reshaped.
- **Fall:** the sign opposite the exaltation. This is the weakest state (Martin: debilitated), and the GDD reads it as the least trusted faculty, the one looked down on.
- **Martin's method:** detriment and fall are not a verdict but the developmental task. A person grows by consciously integrating the planets in detriment and fall.

**Structural facts in the table** (F §5).
- **Virgo is the only sign whose ruler is also its exalted planet** (Mercury twice). **Pisces is the only sign where one planet holds both detriment and fall** (Mercury again). So the Virgo-Pisces axis is maximum against minimum on a single faculty: analysis and verification.
- **Exaltation and fall trade across three axes:** Aries and Libra swap the Sun and Saturn; Cancer and Capricorn swap Jupiter and Mars; Virgo and Pisces swap Mercury and Venus. Taurus and Scorpio trade one way only (the Moon). Gemini-Sagittarius and Leo-Aquarius have no such trade; their opposition is purely ruler against detriment.
- **Missing dignities.** Taurus has no fall, Scorpio no exaltation, and Gemini, Leo, Sagittarius and Aquarius have neither.

**Martin's developmental tasks, per sign** (from F §6; only Martin's points, without the GDD's institutional readings):
- **Aries** matures by integrating Venus (valuing others) and Saturn (patience and self-discipline): the two capacities it is worst at.
- **Taurus** gains from Mars the determination to stand up for its own values.
- **Leo's** authority stops depending on applause only over time and through real achievement.
- **Virgo's** missing planets balance an all-work-and-no-play approach.
- **Libra** is comfortable with Saturn, since Saturn also rules law.
- **Scorpio** softens its otherwise brutal effectiveness by integrating Venus and the Moon (gentleness, peace, care).
- **Sagittarius** has to accept that truths can be partial and multiple.
- **Capricorn** is self-sufficient, more comfortable looking after others than being looked after.
- **Aquarius** needs individual values and self-expression to balance the collective.

## 8. Jung's *Aion*: the astrological ages and other zodiac material

Jung reads the astrological ages as a frame for religious history. These notes report his argument; neither he nor these notes claim the stars cause anything. Jung's own word is synchronicity: things that coincide in time without a provable causal link.

**The idea of the aeon** (Pt 1, Foreword, pp. 15-17).
- The book's theme is the aeon. Jung treats the roughly two thousand years of Christianity as running alongside the astrological age of the Fishes, Pisces, which he calls a *Platonic month*.
- Christian tradition, from the start, carries an expectation that at the end of time the dominant principle will reverse into its opposite: Christ, then Antichrist.

**How the ages move** (Pt 1, ch. VI, pars. 136, 149).
- The spring equinox point drifts slowly backward through the signs (the precession of the equinoxes). Ancient astrologers knew this.
- Origen used Hipparchus's discovery of it against astrology: the real constellations are not the same as the imagined signs.
- Jung's figures: a Platonic month of about 2,143 years, and a full cycle of about 25,726 years. He says himself that where the constellations begin and end is arbitrary.

**Aries, the age that was ending** (Pt 1, ch. VI, par. 147; ch. VIII, pars. 161-162).
- The shepherd, the ram and the lamb belong to the expiring age of Aries. In the first century the two ages overlap: Attis and Christ are both shepherds, rams and fishes.
- For those who knew astrology, Christ was born "as the first fish of the Pisces era, and was doomed to die as the last ram," the lamb (par. 147).
- A ram sacrificed at the turn of an age is the old rite of the seasonal god.

**Pisces, the age of the two fishes** (Pt 1, ch. VI-IX; Pt 2, ch. IX-X).
- **A double sign.** Jung reads the two fishes as hostile brothers: Christ and Antichrist. The first fish stands for Christ, and the second, which follows, for the Antichrist at the end of time (par. 149).
- **The turning point.** The reversal begins midway between the two fishes, around the year 1000. That was the time of heresies and new movements, from the Cathars to Joachim of Flora's prophecy of a coming age of the Holy Spirit (pars. 137-141, 230-235).
- **The second fish.** The Renaissance and the Reformation come near the second fish, and with them the spirit that ends in the modern age.
- **From vertical to horizontal.** After the 16th century, the vertical striving of the Gothic age was cut across by a horizontal movement outward: voyages of discovery and the conquest of nature. Jung reads it as increasingly anti-Christian (ch. VII, par. 150).
- **The hidden devil.** In that age the spirit decays into rationalism, intellectualism and doctrinaire thinking. Jung says the devil now hides under high-sounding ideals (public welfare, lifelong security, peace among nations), the most dangerous being doctrinairism (par. 141).
- **Pisces as end and beginning.** As the twelfth sign, Pisces ends the astrological year and opens a new one (par. 177).
  - Jung finds six parts of the Christian myth already in the astrological fish: the cross; the moral conflict split into Christ and Antichrist; the son of a virgin; the tragedy of mother and son; danger at birth; and the savior who brings healing.
  - He also insists that no old text derives the Christian fish from the zodiac, so the zodiac sign cannot be the source of the fish emblem.
- **Mother and son.** The two fishes were also read as mother and son: the fish goddess Derceto-Atargatis and her son Ichthys. The great mother is a danger to her son (pars. 173-174).
- **The fish as appetite.** Because Venus is exalted in Pisces, the fish also stands for devouring desire (ambitious, lustful, greedy, avaricious, per Picinellus). Yet those born under Pisces were said to become fishermen and sailors (par. 174).
- **Opposite directions.** The picture of two fishes swimming in opposite directions cannot be shown before Christian times. Jung suspects the later emphasis on their counter-movement was tendentious (par. 147).
- **The Cathar text.** A Cathar text has two fishes yoked like oxen, ploughing the earth at the creation. Jung reads them as the zodiacal Fishes rising at the birth of the world, and so as the driving forces of the coming world of consciousness (Pt 2, pars. 230-231).
- **Alchemy.** Lambspringk's two reversed fishes stand for the double nature of the alchemists' arcane substance (par. 234).

**Aquarius, the coming age** (Pt 1, ch. VI, pars. 136, 142, 149).
- The spring point enters Aquarius sometime in the third millennium: between 1997 and 2154, depending on which star is taken as the start.
- If the age of the fishes was ruled by the motif of hostile brothers, Jung expects Aquarius to raise the problem of **uniting the opposites**.
- Evil could then no longer be written off as a mere absence of good; its real existence would have to be acknowledged.
- He says the problem cannot be solved by philosophy, economics or politics, only by the individual, through experience of the living spirit.
- The Talmudic reckoning he discusses would put a turning point around A.D. 3239, when the spring point would be 18° into Aquarius.

**Other signs Jung touches** (Pt 1, ch. VI, VIII):
- **Gemini.** If the 7 B.C. date is right, the sun at Christ's birth was in Gemini, which Jung calls a double sign, recalling hostile brothers such as Horus and Set. A Jupiter-Saturn conjunction in Gemini in 531 is another pair of brothers (pars. 130, 134).
- **Virgo and Libra.** Medieval astrologers cast ideal horoscopes of Christ in Virgo (Albumasar, Albertus Magnus) and in Libra (d'Ailly, Cardan). Jung notes a great conjunction in Libra in 1246 and calls Libra a double air sign (pars. 130, 137).
  - Virgo carries a wheat-sheaf or a child, and some link her to the woman clothed with the sun in Revelation (par. 166).
  - At an Alexandrian festival, the cry was that the virgin had given birth to the Aeon (par. 165).
- **Christmas midnight.** By the old reckoning the sun enters Capricorn at Christmas. At midnight Virgo stands on the eastern horizon, followed by the Serpent held by Ophiuchus (par. 173). This is the only place the *Aion* extraction mentions Ophiuchus.
- **The winter signs.** The fish belongs with Aquarius and Capricorn to the winter rainy season (par. 148).
- **Sagittarius.** Jung's footnote says the conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn awaited by a medieval Jewish commentary actually fell in Sagittarius, not in Pisces (par. 128) ⚠️.
- **Incidental signs.** Leo, Taurus, Cancer and Scorpio appear only as shared animal symbols (lion, bull, scorpion), not as signs.

**Planets in Jung's sources** (Pt 1, ch. VI-VII):
- **Jupiter and Saturn.** Medieval astrology held Jupiter beneficent (life) and Saturn death. Their conjunction therefore meant a union of extreme opposites (par. 130).
- **Saturn.** Saturn was held to be the star of Israel and later the seat of the devil. Snakes, scorpions and other sly creatures were assigned to Saturn (pars. 128-129).
- **Jupiter and justice.** Jupiter stands for the king of justice. A Jewish commentary calls Pisces, Jupiter's house, *the house of justice* (par. 128).
- **Mars.** In 7 B.C. Mars stood in opposition to the conjunction. Jung reads Mars as the planet tied to the instincts, standing hostile, which he finds characteristic of Christianity (par. 130).
- **Religions from conjunctions.** Medieval theory (Albumasar, d'Ailly) derived religions from planetary conjunctions and dated religious turning points by Saturn's cycles. Nostradamus's prophecy of an anti-Christian upheaval in 1792 rests on that theory (ch. VII).

**The horoscope and the number twelve** (Pt 2, par. 212; Pt 3, pars. 351-352, 415).
- **The wheel of birth.** A horoscope (twelve houses, the first being the ascendant) is a *wheel of birth*. Jung: it maps a person's basic character and is the equivalent of the individual psyche.
- *Setting fire to the wheel* means a catastrophic revolt of all the psyche's components, like a panic or a fatal outburst of emotion.
- **Twelve as wholeness.** Twelve (3 x 4) appears among the symbols of wholeness, as in the zodiac and the year: it resolves the dilemma of three plus a different fourth.
- **Geomancy** arranges its sixteen figures in a scheme of astrological houses.

**What the Jung material touches, by sign:**
- Pisces: sacrifice, redemption, the split of good and evil, mother and son, devouring desire, end and beginning.
- Aries: shepherd, ram and sacrificed lamb.
- Aquarius: the union of opposites, recognizing evil, individual experience.
- Gemini: hostile or twin brothers.
- Virgo: virgin mother, birth of a new age.
- Libra and Sagittarius: only as places of conjunctions and ideal horoscopes.
- Capricorn: the winter solstice.
- Ophiuchus: once, in the Christmas sky.
- Jung does not treat the twelve as areas of life.

**Jung's own cautions** (Pt 1, pars. 148-149, 177; Gaps and cautions):
- No causal connection can be proved between Christ and the start of the age.
- The gospel stories show no disguised star myths.
- The age of the fishes left little trace in Eastern cultures, which he admits he does not know.
- The extraction adds that Jung's dates and astronomy are given as printed, and that planet and zodiac symbols in the source are partly unreadable.

**The GDD's binding constraints** (00, header; A §0).
- **The zodiac is not in-fiction.** Nobody in the GDD's world planned anything by the zodiac. The material is design input, never lore.
- **The four classical elements must never be confused with Tepenia's eight-element robot elemental system.** That system is in-fiction canon and is tied to subnets: `Storyline/DLC-Questlines/Subnet_Symbolic_Associations.md` and `.../City_Symbolic_Substrate/Robot_Elementals.md`. The GDD forbids putting the two systems in one sentence outside its disambiguation table. ⚠️ This matters for any robot work drawn from these notes.

**Quality caveats** (00, Quality caveats; Method):
- **Tierney is an OCR'd scan** with corrupted words. Never quote it.
- **Coverage is even across the twelve signs** (1,109 to 1,833 mentions each). So where a sign is thin, the gap is real, not a sign the books ignored it.
- **Ophiuchus is very thin:** about 2,000 words, in two books.
- **Decans are weak:** two books of eight.
- **Silva has real holes:** no dark-side or decan chapter for Virgo, and no chapter on what Aquarius needs.

**How the extraction worked, and its defects** (00, Method).
- **Method.** Each book was converted to text and sliced by sign and by theme. One agent per slice filled a fixed 15-section template, told to put shadow and conflict first, record disagreements and report gaps rather than pad.
- **Defects found and fixed:**
  - a slicing bug that had discarded over half of Silva's material for Gemini, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces (fixed in a supplement round);
  - keyword slices that missed passages not using the keyword;
  - a data warning that itself over-claimed;
  - correspondences that looked missing only because Snodgrass keys them by planet.
- **The GDD's lessons:** a gap logged against one source should be re-checked before it is trusted, and gaps recorded before the supplement round should be re-verified.

**What the corpus does not contain** (00; 99 §7).
- **Little at group scale.** The books describe individual psychology and one-to-one compatibility, not what a population of one temperament does or what institutions it builds. Everything at institutional scale in the GDD files is the GDD's own derivation.
- **Qualified.** The supplement round showed this was partly a slicing artifact. Scorpio and Aquarius gained some institutional material.

**The shadow is over-weighted on purpose** (99 §7).
- The per-sign files give the dark side heavy weight because the extraction was told to capture failure modes first.
- The GDD warns this is an artifact of extraction, not a claim about how much of a sign is shadow. The same holds for the sign notes built from those files.

**Disagreements are kept, not resolved** (99 §7). Examples are whether Scorpio is possessive, whether Capricorn is a workaholic or leaves work promptly, and Snodgrass's contrarian Aquarius. The GDD treats them as menus, not errors.

**The two summary sheets** (Full Attributes; Quick Reference).
- `Zodiac_Signs_Full_Attributes.md` lists element, modality, ruler, dates and positive and negative attributes per sign. It names no source book; it is a general compilation built as a creative-input tool. `Zodiac_Signs_Quick_Reference.md` condenses it into one table. ⚠️ Treat their per-sign lists as general summaries, not as traced to the eight-book corpus.
- **The pattern both sheets draw across all twelve:** nearly every sign's negative attributes are the same trait as a positive one, pushed past its useful range. Their examples:
  - Aries' courage becomes recklessness;
  - Virgo's precision becomes paralysis;
  - Cancer's protectiveness becomes a wall;
  - Libra's fairness becomes an inability to decide.

**Snodgrass's own caveat** (G, Appendix V): his table of sign symptoms is a lesser area of sign lore that stereotypes human tendencies.

## 10. Topics that cut across several meta-categories

Each topic is one that the cross-cutting material spreads over more than one sign. The signs in parentheses are where the documentation places it.

**Ways of taking in the world**
- **Which truth a person trusts:** inner conviction, the senses, shared systems, or shared feeling (fire / earth / air / water) (A §1, from Martin).
- **Facts against conviction; verification:** Mercury's signs against Jupiter's; earth against fire (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces, Aries, Leo) (A §6; F §3, §5; §3b above).
- **What one does with feeling:** air explains it, water absorbs and stores it, earth sets it aside for the tangible, fire tramples it without noticing (A §2-5).
- **Freedom to know and censorship:** air's growth depends on access to information (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) (A §4, from Tierney).
- **The body and its needs:** earth looks after it, fire finds it confining (A §2-3).

**Change, conflict and commitment**
- **Starting, holding and adapting:** the three relationships to change (all four signs of each mode) (B).
- **Keeping promises:** fixed finishes; mutable reserves the right to change its mind; cardinal starts and may not finish (B §2-4).
- **How a deadlock breaks:** cardinal forces a crisis, fixed outlasts it, mutable goes around it (B §2-5).
- **Pride and the conviction of being right:** all four fixed signs (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius) (B §3, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Mediation and going between; not taking sides:** the mutable signs, with Gemini as Martin's mediator. Every run of three neighboring signs repeats the pattern (B §1, §4; C §4).
- **Kinds of relationship failure:** disagreement (square), mirror (opposition), incomprehension (quincunx, semi-sextile), complacency (trine), a comfortable arrangement nobody renegotiates (sextile) (D §2-3).
- **Projection and scapegoating:** meeting one's disowned half in an opponent; blaming whoever surfaces buried material (all six axes) (C §6; D §2, from Martin).
- **Stalemate and inherited grudges:** fixed against fixed (B §5).
- **Rivalry for the center:** fire with fire (A §6).
- **Engagement against indifference:** Silva's point that a hard relation beats no relation at all (D §2).

**Holding on and letting go**
- **Grievance.**
  - Water stores grievances until they fester (A §5, from Tierney).
  - The GDD derives that fixed grudges are inherited (B §5).
  - Libra trades and keeps score (A §4).
  - One Scorpio decan never forgets and takes revenge when the chance comes (E §2, from Silva).
  - Gemini forgives at once but never trusts again (`03_Gemini.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Retention against release:** the Taurus-Scorpio axis, and the receptive failure of holding on to what should go (§3b; C §3).

**Self and others: the six axes**
- **Self against other; autonomy against compromise** (Aries, Libra) (§3b).
- **Mine against ours; possession and shared resources** (Taurus, Scorpio) (§3b).
- **Information against meaning; student against teacher** (Gemini, Sagittarius) (§3b).
- **Being held against standing alone; hearth against marketplace** (Cancer, Capricorn) (§3b).
- **The individual against the group** (Leo, Aquarius) (§3b).
- **Order against chaos; control against trust; service against devotion; judging against accepting** (Virgo, Pisces) (§3b).
- **Boundaries:**
  - water has too few (A §5);
  - Tierney names Taurus the sign that first thought of putting up fences (`02_Taurus.md` §1);
  - water is told to borrow earth's territoriality (A §5).
- **Solitude and isolation:** water needs solitude; air dreads isolation; the receptive half can withdraw into isolation (A §4-5; C §3).

**Drives that cut across areas: the planets** (F §3, §5)
- **Identity and named authority (Sun):** at home in Leo, exalted in Aries, weak in Aquarius and Libra.
- **Care and belonging (Moon):** at home in Cancer, exalted in Taurus, weak in Capricorn and Scorpio.
- **Analysis and verification (Mercury):** at home in Gemini and Virgo, weak in Sagittarius and Pisces.
- **Valuing and relating (Venus):** at home in Taurus and Libra, exalted in Pisces, weak in Aries, Scorpio and Virgo.
- **Force and assertion (Mars):** at home in Aries and Scorpio, exalted in Capricorn, weak in Taurus, Libra and Cancer.
- **Meaning, scale and generosity (Jupiter):** at home in Sagittarius and Pisces, exalted in Cancer, weak in Gemini, Virgo and Capricorn.
- **Limits, time and law (Saturn):** at home in Capricorn and Aquarius, exalted in Libra, weak in Cancer, Leo and Aries.
- **Refusal (Uranus), dissolution (Neptune), purging (Pluto):** the modern rulers of Aquarius, Pisces and Scorpio.
- **One faculty in two postures:** each planet's two signs show the loud and the quiet form of the same drive (F §1).
- **Growing through the weak faculty:** Martin's developmental task (F §4, §6).
- **Occupations by planet:** Snodgrass's governance column (G, Appendix IV).

**Strength, health and stamina**
- **Stamina and depletion:** fire lacks stamina and drains others; the active half lacks inner reserves; the receptive half grows strong in a crisis (A §2, §9; C §2-3).
- **Sickness, health and healing:**
  - the Virgo-Pisces axis (§3b);
  - Jung's savior who brings healing, one of the six parts of the fish symbol (Pt 1, par. 177);
  - Asclepius (see the Ophiuchus notes).
- **Character read as illness:** each sign's psychological symptoms (G, Appendix V).

**Time, cycles and history**
- **Cycles:** the seasons behind the modalities; the Rudhyar cycle from seed (Aries) to flower (Libra); Pisces as end and beginning; twelve as the year (B §1; §7 above; Jung, Pt 1, par. 177; Pt 3, par. 351).
- **Ages of history:** the movement from Aries through Pisces to Aquarius (§8).
- **Replacement and substitution:**
  - three signs took over older constellations (G, Appendix I);
  - Matthias replaces Judas in the apostle mapping (99 §5c);
  - Ophiuchus was left out of the count altogether (Ophiuchus notes).

**Good, evil and opposites**
- **The split of good and evil, and their union:** Jung's hostile brothers in Pisces, the union of opposites expected in Aquarius, and the double symbols (the twins, the two fishes, the scales) (§8; G).
- **Virtue and vice as one trait at different strengths** (Full Attributes; Quick Reference).
- **The shadow as the unacknowledged half,** and the GDD's caution against over-weighting it (C §6; 99 §7).

**Voice, work and credit**
- **Speaking and holding:** the active half acts on the world; the receptive half absorbs, and its silence is read as consent (C §2-3).
- **Who gets credit:** the GDD derives that mutable adaptation leaves no trace, while cardinal action looks busy and fixed refusal is visible (B §2, §6).
- **Starting work against finishing it:** see "Keeping promises," and the disagreement over who finishes (B §8).

## 11. Where the author's named concepts appear across the system

**Justice**
- Martin gives Venus, as Libra's ruler, justice, diplomacy and equality (F §3), and says Saturn is at ease in Libra because Saturn also rules law (F §6).
- Libra's tarot card is Justice (G).
- **The Aries-Libra axis** carries Martin's image of Justice with sword and scales: assertion and balance, both needed (`01_Aries.md` §8).
- **Planets and justice.** Snodgrass gives Jupiter the judiciary (G, Appendix IV). In Jung's sources Jupiter is the king of justice and Pisces, Jupiter's house, the house of justice (Pt 1, par. 128).
- Sagittarius's ninth house includes law (`09_Sagittarius.md` §1).
- Saturn is the faculty of law and the enforced boundary (F §3).
- Air's skills include arbitration and standards (A §4).
- **Heraldry.** Institutions that want to look fair, just and impartial show the maiden (Virgo) holding scales (Libra) (`06_Virgo.md` §13; ⚠️ the GDD does not name the book, probably Snodgrass).
- **Myth.** Snodgrass's Virgo carries Astraea, goddess of justice, who withdrew justice from the earth after the golden and silver ages ended (`06_Virgo.md` §13).
- **In the dignities**, force (Mars) is in detriment in Libra and named individual authority (the Sun) in fall there (F §5).
- Jung cites a Gnostic image of justice looking down from heaven while truth sprouts from the earth (Pt 1, par. 131).

**Crime** (thin; no part of the system is about crime as such)
- Emerson lists crime, with addiction and secrets, in Pisces's domain, the twelfth house (`12_Pisces.md` §1).
- Snodgrass's landscapes give Scorpio caves and prison cells and Pisces tombs (G).
- The seventh house governs open enemies alongside partners (`07_Libra.md` §10).
- **Saturn.** It is the malefic planet and, in Jung's sources, the seat of the devil (Pt 1, pars. 128-129). Snodgrass puts the poor in Saturn's governance cell beside executives of state (G, Appendix IV).
- Snodgrass gives Mars weapon makers and Neptune demagogues (G, Appendix IV).
- Appendix V lists vengefulness (Cancer), destructiveness (Scorpio) and addictive behavior (Pisces) as symptoms.
- **Verification.** Mercury is weakest in Pisces, in both detriment and fall. For a group, the GDD derives that verification then has no home, and disputes are settled by reputation rather than record (F §5-6).
- Jung: in the age of the second fish, evil hides under high ideals (Pt 1, par. 141).

**Trust**
- Martin puts control against trust on the Virgo-Pisces axis (§3b).
- **The mutable signs** reserve the right to change their minds (Tierney). Martin: they can be trusted to follow but not to commit in advance (B §4-5).
- Fixed signs offer loyalty (B §3).
- Silva: earth sees air as unreliable (A §6).
- Water offers support with no conditions (A §5).
- The GDD reads a planet in fall as the least trusted faculty (F §4).
- **Apostles.** Judas is Aquarius and Matthias, who replaced him, is Pisces (99 §5c).
- Gemini forgives without ever trusting again (`03_Gemini.md` §3).

**Care**
- **The Moon** is the planet of care and belonging: at home in Cancer, exalted in Taurus, weak in Capricorn and Scorpio (F §3, §5).
- Martin: Scorpio softens by integrating Venus and the Moon, and Capricorn would rather care for others than be cared for (F §6).
- Water gives unconditional support at the cost of its own boundaries (A §5), and earth looks after the body (A §3).
- Neptune is compassion and mercy (F §3).
- **The axes** set service against devotion and sickness against health (Virgo-Pisces), and being held against standing alone (Cancer-Capricorn) (§3b).
- Jung: the savior who brings healing, and the healing fish of Tobit (Pt 1, pars. 174, 177).

**Honor** (thin; the word does not appear in the cross-cutting files)
- Tierney finds an inborn pride in all four fixed signs (B §3).
- **The Sun** is authority and integrity that do not depend on others' opinions (F §3). Martin: Leo's authority stops depending on applause only through real achievement (F §6).
- **Exaltation** is the faculty a sign is proud of (F §4).
- **Decans.** Emerson calls one Leo decan the most chivalrous (and unable to admit being wrong), one Sagittarius decan full of pride and integrity, and one Scorpio decan a decan of great integrity (E §2).
- The tenth house (Capricorn) covers public status (`10_Capricorn.md` §10).

**Duty**
- **Saturn** is limits, discipline and the deferred reward: the faculty that says no and makes it stick. It is at home in Capricorn and Aquarius, exalted in Libra, and weak in Cancer, Leo and Aries (F §3, §5).
- **Earth** is the element that keeps ordinary systems working and tidies the world, and it drifts toward enforcing rules (A §3, from Tierney).
- **Modes.** Fixed signs finish what others start (B §3); mutable signs reserve the right to change their minds (B §4).
- **Axes and planets.** Martin sets service against devotion on the Virgo-Pisces axis (§3b). Snodgrass's Saturn governs both executives of state and the poor (G, Appendix IV).
- **Obligation as balance.** Libra's give-and-take holds that nothing is given unconditionally (A §4).
- Jung places the work of reconciling opposites on the individual, not on institutions (Pt 1, par. 142).

## 12. Gaps

**In the documentation**
- **No file treats the twelve houses as a system.** The houses appear sign by sign, and the sources disagree on several house names:
  - eleventh: House of Good Spirit, House of Divinity, House of Blessings, or simply *Uniting* (`11_Aquarius.md` §10);
  - twelfth: the house where the self is deconstructed, or the House of Sacrifice (`12_Pisces.md` §1);
  - eighth: the House of Transformation (`08_Scorpio.md` §10);
  - tenth: the House of Enterprise (`10_Capricorn.md` §10).
  Martin vol. 2 is the corpus's house source and was never mined as a whole.
- **Group and institutional behavior is not in the books.** Every such reading in the GDD is derived (00; 99 §7; B §8; C §6).
- **Polarity is thin.** About 1,500 usable words, with no polarity-specific failures and nothing on how polarity combines with modality (C header, §6).
- **Decans** rest on two books (E §5).
- **Aspects** have no systematic sign-pair readings; the matrix is arithmetic (D §8).
- **The dignity scheme is incomplete.** Two of its cells are derived, not attested (F §7).
- **Correspondences.**
  - The chakra and essential-oil columns cannot be checked.
  - Neptune's and Pluto's stones and metals are reconstructed.
  - Snodgrass's per-sign sympathy tables were never sliced.
  - Most of Appendix IV's color, form and sense columns are unrecoverable (G, Data Quality Log).
- **No book covers temperament under confinement or scarcity** (A §9).
- **Air against air** is under-covered (A §9).
- **Jung** treats Pisces, Aries and Aquarius at length and the other signs only in passing, and never as areas of life.
- **The two summary sheets cite no books.**
- **Of the named concepts,** honor and crime are thin.

**Disagreements between sources** (kept, not resolved)
- **Who finishes:** fixed (Tierney, Martin) or mutable (Emerson, Silva) (B §8).
- **Classification:** Snodgrass makes Capricorn a water sign once, and describes water as fun-loving and persuasive (A §9).
- **Decan rulers:** Pisces's third and Scorpio's decan rulers (E §5).
- **Cardinal as reactive:** Silva alone (B §8).
- **Dignities:** Silva confuses exaltation with domicile (F §7).
- **Stones and colors:** several contested, among them Taurus's and Virgo's colors and Aries's and Pisces's amethyst (G).
- **Virgo's size:** the largest zodiac constellation in Snodgrass's Appendix II (G), but the second-largest in Snodgrass's prose as quoted in `06_Virgo.md` §13.

**Gaps a later source should fill**
- A treatment of the twelve houses as a system of life areas: Martin vol. 2 directly, or another house text.
- A source on astrology at group scale. ⚠️ Mundane astrology is the usual name for it; this is a lead from outside the corpus, not yet checked.
- Snodgrass's main per-sign chapters, for the contested correspondences.
- For honor, crime and duty, a source that ties virtues and vices to the signs more directly than these books do.
