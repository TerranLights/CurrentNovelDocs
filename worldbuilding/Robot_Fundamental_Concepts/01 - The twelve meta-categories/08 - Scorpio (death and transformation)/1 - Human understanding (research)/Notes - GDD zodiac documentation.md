## 1. Areas of life
- **Death, endings and regeneration.** The documentation calls Scorpio the sign of death, permanent closure and forced regeneration (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1).
- **The eighth house.** Silva calls it the House of Transformation. His gloss covers rejuvenation, cycles of birth and death, resources, finances, karma and self-transformation (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Silva).
  - The substrate notes that the financial half is easy to miss. Read with Snodgrass's investor who manages money better for others than for himself, it points to other people's resources: inheritance, estates, debts, and what is owed after an ending (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, §10, from Snodgrass and Silva).
  - `00_Method_and_Sources.md` lists this resources-and-estates assignment among the few collective-scale findings.
- **Elimination and purging.** Martin says Scorpio senses when something hidden has turned poisonous, polluted or distorted, and feels driven to bring it to the surface so it can be eliminated and the whole system cleansed and healed (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Martin; `A_Elements.md` §5, from Martin).
- **What is hidden beneath the surface.** The Full Attributes sheet calls Scorpio the zodiac's investigator, drawn to what is hidden, uncomfortable, or true beneath a polished surface (Full Attributes, Scorpio). Tierney places Scorpio's job just past Libra's: Libra understands how people think, and Scorpio gets under their skin (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Desire and control.** Silva gives the key phrase as *I desire*; Snodgrass's perception table gives *I control*. Silva ties them together: for this type, being out of control means being in psychic danger (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Silva and Snodgrass).
- **Sexuality and desire.** Desire is the sign's key phrase (above), the first decan is called the most sensual, the second decan is seductive, and sex therapy is among the listed occupations. Silva's compatibility material includes chapters on sex (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, §11, §15).
- **Transformation and surrender.** Martin says Scorpio is where a person is forced to surrender and let go (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin).
- **Power, and the taboo.** Pluto, Scorpio's modern ruler, stands for purge and regeneration, for bringing up what has become polluted, poisonous or taboo, and for power and its abuse (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Disciplined force.** Mars, the traditional ruler, appears here in its trained, precise form (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §1, §3, from Martin).
- **Healing and medicine, and the keeping of the dead.**
  - The Egyptian figure linked to Scorpio, Selket, was patron of herbal healing and keeper of burial vaults and canopic jars (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
  - Martin says both Virgo and Scorpio are associated with medicine and healing (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Martin).
- **Crisis.** Scorpio is said to come into its own in crisis and emotional turmoil (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Tierney and Martin).
- **Occupations.** Silva's list:
  - detection and law: detective, spy, police officer, tax inspector, auditor;
  - research and records: data analyst, journalist, researcher, chemist, biologist, anthropologist, biographer;
  - medicine and the dead: coroner, oncologist, pharmacist, surgeon, EMT, urologist, OB-GYN;
  - the mind: psychiatrist, therapist, guidance counselor, hypnotist, medium, sex therapist;
  - organizations: human resources and cultural architect.
  Other occupations given:
  - Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*: research, investigation, forensics, intelligence, surgery, funeral work and the occult.
  - Snodgrass: the investor; the soldier who volunteers for dirty or dangerous missions.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6)
- **The planets' occupations.** Snodgrass's planetary table gives Mars military leaders and weapon makers, and Pluto idealists (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix IV, from Snodgrass).
- **Setting.** Scorpio sits in mid-autumn, when the sun weakens and darkness gains on the light. It is marked by Samhain, the festival of death (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Silva *Sun and Moon* and Martin).

## 2. Core meaning
- **Archetype.** The documentation names the sign of death, closure and forced regeneration, and the Full Attributes sheet adds the investigator (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1; Full Attributes, Scorpio).
  - The Full Attributes sheet calls Scorpio intensity itself (Full Attributes, Scorpio).
  - The decans give a sequence of names, *Intensity*, *Transmutation* and *Manifestation*, and a phoenix reading of the glyph (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11–§12, from Silva).
- **Driving motive: elimination, not curiosity.** Martin's purging sense is the core (section 1). The substrate stresses that the motive is to get rid of what has gone toxic, not to know for knowing's sake (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Control as safety.** Silva's equation: being out of control reads as danger (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Silva). The Full Attributes sheet lists controlling behavior among the negatives, especially once trust has been broken (Full Attributes, Scorpio).
- **Fixed water as ice.** Tierney's image is ice, the hardest, least fluid water, with most of its mass hidden below the waterline, where what you cannot see may be more dangerous than what you can (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Core need: to be met in full depth and not abandoned for it.** Tierney says the Scorpion suspects that showing its secret feelings and desires would make it a target for rejection. So it hides them, and the hiding produces the isolation it feared (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Tierney).
  - The substrate's reading: the substitute need is control as safety, and the deeper need is a witness who does not flinch.
  - Tierney's bottom line is a stark choice between redeeming the darkness and suffering in isolation.
- **Being understood.** Silva says nothing pleases this type more than being understood. He also says it often feels too complex to be understood and lacks the words to say what it feels. The thing it wants most is the thing it has no vocabulary to ask for (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).
- **The organizing fear is betrayal.** Silva says the type is deathly afraid of being betrayed. It keeps secrets to protect itself from people who would exploit its feelings, believing full exposure would end in disaster (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).
- **The pair as the primary need.** Silva says a well-matched romantic partner is Scorpio's primary need. It can also love a small group of friends just as fiercely, but does not need one (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).
- **Permanence against intensity.** Silva says Scorpio wants security and so values permanent, committed relationships. Yet in his double-Scorpio material he says intensity of experience matters more than permanence. The substrate keeps both (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).
- **Meaning as the final need.** Silva advises finding one's deepest calling and following it with reckless abandon. The substrate reads Scorpio's endurance as a capacity meant to be spent on something, which turns inward when it is not (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).
- **The cure it resists.** Silva: peace comes only through letting oneself be emotionally open. When the type uses its powers for selfish gain, it ends up alone and becomes its own worst enemy (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §5, from Silva).

## 3. The area at its best
- **Crisis competence.** Composed in the face of danger and excellent in a crisis (Tierney). Martin says no crisis or emotional turmoil is too much for Scorpio; those are the conditions where it comes into its own. The fixed signs generally hate pressure; Scorpio is the exception (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Tierney and Martin; `A_Elements.md` §5).
- **Endurance of the depths.** Few signs (perhaps only Pisces) are said to sink as deep into pain and remorse, and the depths are treated as survivable and instructive (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Earned loyalty.** Once its loyalty is earned, Martin says, Scorpio will go through anything for you. It never makes a promise it does not mean to keep, and it repays good twice over (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Diagnostic honesty.** Martin says Scorpio goes to the heart of the emotional truth, sometimes with a brutal honesty many people cannot take: "If you don't want to know the truth, then don't ask a Scorpio" (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Empathy for the excluded.** Tierney says Scorpio feels for people who do not fit society's idea of normal, and wants to know why people become outcasts, loners and undesirables (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Skilled, disciplined force.** Martin's Mars in Scorpio is the professional soldier rather than the hooligan, the surgeon's knife rather than the robber's. Its courage serves the survival of the whole, not personal survival (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2; `F_Rulerships.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Fixed goal, flexible route.** Silva says no one is quicker to spot obstacles and find many ways around them. Defeat produces a new attempt, not surrender. The substrate notes this corrects a purely fixed reading: the goal never moves, the method always does (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Gentleness inside the circle.** Silva objects that astrologers rarely mention how kind, loving, loyal, generous and gentle Scorpio is. His children instinctively turn gentler when a family member is unwell, and his adults set their own needs aside for the family's. The substrate notes that every example he gives is inside the family or the trusted group (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Recovery.** The first decan is said to rebound well from failure and to start anew, with bulldog tenacity (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Integrity.** The third decan has emotional integrity, trusts its own feelings, refuses to compromise them, and has great willpower (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Silva and Emerson).
- **Eloquence with compassion.** The third decan can be a compelling writer or speaker who moves people by force of eloquence and compassion (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Sacrifice for the real.** The second decan will give up prestige and comfort for what it believes and for what is true (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Silva and Emerson).
- **Patience for discovery.** It will spend years on a single discovery (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva *Sun and Moon*).
- **The listed positives.**
  - genuine intensity and passion;
  - resourcefulness under real pressure;
  - fierce loyalty once earned;
  - a gift for investigation, for finding what is true rather than comfortable;
  - resilience through hardship most signs would not survive intact;
  - willingness to name hard truths;
  - a transformative power that can turn crisis into real change.
  (Full Attributes, Scorpio; Quick Reference, Scorpio)
- **Dark humor.** Among Scorpios, a shared dark sense of humor is what carries them through hard times (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Silva).

## 4. The area gone wrong
The richest section of the source file. Grouped by theme; the groups are ours.

**Forcing the end**
- **Manufactured crisis.**
  - Where other signs wait, Tierney says Scorpio picks at a problem until a crisis forces a resolution.
  - Snodgrass says it savors finishing a task for the sheer satisfaction of closure.
  - Silva describes the adolescent drumming up crises where there are none, out of a need to oversee everyone and exert influence.
  - The substrate's reading: crisis-forcing is both a way to get closure and a way to establish control.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Tierney, Snodgrass and Silva)
- **All-or-nothing thinking** that struggles to hold nuance (Full Attributes, Scorpio).
- **Obsession** that fixes on something past the point of usefulness (Full Attributes, Scorpio). Tierney says fixed water's single-minded feeling can tip into obsession (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).

**Revenge and the grudge**
- **Cold revenge, deferred indefinitely.**
  - Silva says Scorpio will wait as long as it takes for the perfect opportunity.
  - Martin says Scorpios invented the saying that revenge is best served cold, and takes *The Count of Monte Cristo* as the type case: decades of patience, then the wrongdoer dismantled piece by piece.
  - Silva adds that Scorpio saves every useful piece of information for the right moment.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva and Martin)
- **Vindictiveness.** A real capacity to hold and act on a grudge (Full Attributes, Scorpio).
- **Aiming for permanent damage.** Tierney contrasts Leo, which sulks when its pride is hurt, with Scorpio, which goes for the throat on the psychological level where it can do lasting harm, and doubts it can ever play nice again after being violated (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **The delayed detonation on the wrong target.** Martin's bus-queue image: under the calm exterior is an unexploded bomb. It will go off sometime, perhaps later that day at an unsuspecting coworker (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Martin).
  - Silva's related sequence: effort spent on people and things that do not deserve it, then the realization that it was all for nothing, then wallowing in regret and self-loathing that turns destructive to self and others (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
  - The substrate notes that the destruction lands on whoever received the misspent effort, not on whoever caused the misjudgment.
- **Cutting people off.** Silva says that if you cross a Scorpio, it is fine with cutting you off, family or not; you may as well be dead to it. Snodgrass says forgiveness and understanding come only in small pinches, because compassion in Scorpio's heart is limited (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva and Snodgrass).
  - Silva adds two details. There is only a brief moment of feeling terrible before the cut. And there is a way back: the pursuit ends with revenge, or when the offender shows deep, real sorrow and a commitment to change.
  - The substrate notes that elapsed time alone never restores someone (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3).
- **The unforgivable interference.** Silva warns that if you hinder a Scorpio's personal growth, it will ruin you. The substrate reads obstruction of growth as an even worse trigger than betrayal (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Humiliation.** Tierney: deliberately humiliate a Scorpio and watch for barely contained rage (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Tierney).
- **Presence as a sign of a plan.** Silva says a Scorpio cannot stand to be in the same room as an enemy unless it is part of a plan for revenge. Other signs can play nice; Scorpio feels the hostility too strongly to stay (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).

**Control, manipulation and cruelty**
- **Contempt for weakness.**
  - Snodgrass: impatient with weakness or timidity, the archetype tramples on underlings.
  - Silva: with strangers it can be impolite or even cruel, especially at any hint of weakness.
  - It equates vulnerability with weakness, first in itself and then in others.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Snodgrass, Silva and Tierney; `A_Elements.md` §5)
- **The unwanted makeover.**
  - Tierney says Scorpio is notorious for trying to remake people completely, inside and out: it spots another person's hidden treasure and pushes to extract it.
  - Tierney adds that Scorpio unfairly demands total candor and commitment from a partner while forbidding access to itself.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Tierney)
- **The scheming.** Silva says no one is a more cunning schemer, and concedes that this is useful only in war. In ordinary life the costs are being kept at a distance by wary people, and a vindictiveness that holds back a career (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Reserve as a restraint, not a temperament.** The substrate calls this the most important line in the recovered material (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
  - Silva says a socially loose Scorpio, especially a young one, gives in to manipulation, backstabbing, cattiness and gossip more than any other sign.
  - Its famous discretion exists to stop itself using its traits for ill. The mask is self-restraint, not privacy.
  - The unevolved Scorpio manipulates people and situations for gain or greed, and its inner tug of war between light and dark is real and turbulent.
- **Manipulation.** The Full Attributes sheet lists it, used when directness feels too exposing (Full Attributes, Scorpio). The second decan's description puts sincere self-sacrifice and manipulation in one person: a healer who may have something of a god complex and uses cunning to control others (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Silva and Emerson).
- **Secretiveness.** It can shade into dishonesty by omission (Full Attributes, Scorpio).
- **Exclusive loyalty as policy.** As an employer, Silva says, Scorpio demands that employees deal with it alone. Contact with anyone it counts as the enemy reads as defection, even when the caller is family (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
  - Silva's corrective: resist cutting the other connections you disapprove of, or you will alienate the very people you want to keep.
  - `00_Method_and_Sources.md` lists this rule among the few institutional-scale findings.
- **Jealousy and possessiveness.** The Full Attributes sheet lists them (Full Attributes, Scorpio). Martin disputes that possessiveness is a Scorpio trait; see section 7 and Gaps.
- **Tyranny.** Snodgrass says Scorpio and Capricorn together tend to tyrannize (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Snodgrass).
- **The prima donna surgeon.** Snodgrass's stereotype: an incisive diagnosis, curt orders to the team, credit taken for success, and failure blamed on the patient's weakness or an assistant's ineptitude (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Power demons.** Martin's warning about Pluto: without integration, power poisons both the self and others (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).

**Turning it inward**
- **Stinging itself.** Tierney says Scorpio turns its destructive energies inward, stinging itself repeatedly, whenever it feels powerless to control the outer world. Its fired-up feelings can come back as bodily inflammations or infections (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Blocked feeling becomes illness.** Silva states the mechanism: emotion that cannot be expressed blocks up and shows in the body as illness. Illness comes at unexpected times in an otherwise energetic person (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, §12, from Silva).
- **The cost of shutting down.** Silva says Scorpio can shut out all stimuli and retreat into its own world, but it takes enormous concentration. The substrate reads dormancy as a reserve being run down, not rest (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Dark environments.** Silva says the type is vulnerable to stagnant, heavy, toxic places of low spiritual energy (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Contamination by the work.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* says the dark side of human nature rubs off on Scorpios, a real problem since many feel at home working with troubled people (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Water's festering.** Repressed water stores its injuries, which sink deeper and turn toxic. Water does not discharge a grievance; it stores it, and the store degrades (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **Repression versus self-discipline.** Tierney says Scorpio's emotions are well contained. How contained they are decides whether that is unhealthy repression or admirable self-discipline (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).

**Suspicion and isolation**
- **Paranoia.** Martin says Scorpio is often accused of stirring up trouble, of being suspicious, and of smelling rats where there are none. Sometimes it is right; from the inside it cannot tell which (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Isolation it causes itself.** While acting guarded, it wonders why it so often feels alone and cut off from the social rituals others enjoy (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §4, from Tierney). The concealment that guards against rejection produces the isolation it feared (section 2).
- **Not a team player.** Silva says ambition and secrecy are harmful in a team, and Scorpio may treat coworkers as competition and wall itself off (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Limited reach of gentleness (the substrate's reading).** Gentleness is plentiful inside the family and trusted circle but does not reach outsiders without being taught. With strangers the same person ranges from indifferent to cruel. Sympathy offered from outside is refused: Silva warns against cloying sympathy and patronizing support (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Silva).

**Psychological symptoms listed by Snodgrass**
- Nettlesomeness, dominance, destructiveness (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §12; `G_Correspondences.md` Scorpio, from Snodgrass).

**The pattern**
- **The listed negatives:**
  - jealousy, possessiveness, secretiveness, vindictiveness;
  - controlling behavior, obsessiveness, all-or-nothing thinking;
  - manipulation.
  (Full Attributes, Scorpio; Quick Reference, Scorpio)
- **The same trait, pushed too far.** As the closing note puts it, negatives are usually the same trait pushed past its useful range (Full Attributes, "Worth Your Attention"; Quick Reference, closing note).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time
**At work**
- Scorpio prefers the parts of a job that need no one else, and reports after the fact: it does things first and announces them afterward (Silva) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6).
- Fear of failure fuels rather than paralyzes it (same section).
- **Authority without visibility.** Silva says it dislikes taking orders, gives them very well, would do well in the military, yet avoids the limelight. It prefers to pull strings behind the scenes, where it believes real power lies (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva).
- **As a boss.** Its perception serves control rather than empathy. A delegated task is a done deal. Subordinates get an example to follow rather than warmth, and are expected to be honest, hardworking and loyal (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Money and risk.** Snodgrass's Scorpio manages money better for others than for itself, and in the military volunteers for dirty or dangerous missions if they raise its standing (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Snodgrass).

**At home**
- It needs a retreat in which to recharge alone, ideally far from city noise (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva).
- Its tastes are understated luxury: metal, marble, leather, rare woods, dark colors (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §12, from Silva).

**Socially**
- **Selective, not antisocial.** The trusted group may be under ten people, perhaps as few as three. Inside it Scorpio behaves entirely differently; outside it, indifference shades into contempt. It treats small talk as a waste and will say the impolitic thing (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Silva).
- **How it reads from outside.**
  - **Magnetism.** People are strongly drawn or strongly repelled, and the room changes when it enters (Martin).
  - **Two states.** It is either a dormant volcano, still and impenetrable with eyes that give nothing away, or an active one that leaves no doubt (Martin).
  - **Watchfulness.** It observes without seeming to, and reads as a walking lie detector.
  - **Leadership.** Leaders of the type keep followers slightly off balance (Snodgrass), and bursts of wit may hide lurking passions.
  - **Camouflage.** It runs two ways: utterly ordinary, or dark with tinted windows (Martin).
  - **Magnetism by choice or not.** Some Scorpios switch their magnetism on and off. Others draw a crowd while wishing to go unnoticed (Silva).
  - **The usual misreading.** The uninformed may take it for a sociopath (Silva).
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §4)

**Admitting people to the trusted circle** (Silva) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §7)
- **Stage one is conduct.** The applicant must prove trustworthy, never break a promise, show loyalty, and be willing to communicate emotionally. Silva says this alone is not enough.
- **Stage two is intangible.** Examples:
  - chemistry, and the feeling of having known each other far longer than one has;
  - conversation that flows, and comfortable silence;
  - reading moods without words;
  - sharing emotion beyond what society considers normal;
  - being able to change the Scorpio's life for the better;
  - keeping secrets to the grave;
  - a solemn vow to nurture the friendship as one would a romance.
- **The verdict is fast.** A Scorpio knows within moments whether someone belongs. Past intimacy does not qualify anyone.
- **The circle's existence is hidden.** Outsiders may never know a Scorpio has a circle at all, and may wrongly conclude it has no friends. Even its family may not know.
- **Exclusion is not meant as an insult.** Silva insists it is "a matter of survival, not snobbery," grounded in an energy budget: the type often gives others energy at its own expense.
- **Attention is a gift.** A Scorpio's full attention validates and salutes the person. It is rare, deliberate, and not to be dismissed.

**Under stress**
- **Pressure builds slowly and knowingly.** Tierney notes that Scorpio is usually aware it is becoming enraged, and holds anyway. Deliberately holding back strengthens the feeling, and when it finally comes out it can be too strong to control. On withdrawing its heat, Scorpio turns into an iceberg (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Tierney).
- **Fixed signs dig in.** Under pressure they hold, and the holding becomes the point. Fixed signs have an inborn pride (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).

**In conflict**
- It escalates to force closure, aims for lasting damage, cuts people off, or waits for revenge (section 4).
- **Among Scorpios** (Silva) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8):
  - concealment stops working, since two Scorpios see through each other;
  - despair spreads between them, but so does dark humor, which steadies them;
  - blow-ups are certain and are followed by reconciliation;
  - the fault line is that neither will surrender to the other, so any dispute that needs someone to yield has no native way to resolve.

**In crisis**
- **Its best condition.** It is composed and resourceful, and marshals every resource it has (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Receptive types grow strong in a crisis.** Martin says the receptive, yin types have a deep inner reservoir and get very strong in a crisis (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Health is neglected until collapse.** It ignores its health until exhaustion or illness, self-medicates, and will accept an extreme regimen sooner than rest and sleep (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6, from Snodgrass).
  - Snodgrass's patient profile also includes distrust of sugar-coated remedies, a preference for harsh alternative treatments, obsessive self-diagnosis, and the conviction that doctors are bunglers because they reject the patient's own diagnosis (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §14.6, from Snodgrass).
  - Silva twice prescribes mindfulness meditation as the ordinary way to decompress. The substrate notes the tension: the recommended remedy is exactly what the type is said to refuse (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §6).

**When secure**
- Inside the trusted circle and the family, Scorpio is gentle, generous and self-sacrificing (section 3).
- **Leisure with Cancer.** The only depiction of this type simply passing time is Silva's Cancer friendship (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Silva):
  - shared handwork such as baking, sculpting and gardening;
  - pleasure in being together however mundane the task;
  - a quiet, unadmitted delight when the friend cooks its favorite meal.
- **With a well-matched partner** it is said to be fine (section 2).

**Childhood** (Silva; Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §7)
- **Privacy.** The Scorpio child's private room is a sacred space, not to be entered uninvited.
- **Truth.** Adults must tell it the truth, including admitting when they don't know; a made-up answer leaves it spinning and withdrawn.
- **Outbursts.** An outburst comes from something held a long time and should not be dismissed as a tantrum.
- **Art.** Emotion is channeled into art, and talent is assumed.
- **Comfort with an endpoint.** The child is held only until comfortable again, then released.
- **The unconventional.** Its unconventional interests must not be shamed (*Sun and Moon*).
- **Two lessons that must be taught.** Gentleness toward other people's feelings, and losing without retaliating.
- **Regular individual attention.** Without it, resentment builds, and childhood slights survive into adulthood.
- **Sons and daughters.** The source splits advice by sex; recorded as the source's shape only.
  - Sons: respect for authority; a healthy attitude to losing; affection given as structure, or the boy retreats into his shell; a private room from adolescence; and the lesson that partners are people, not trophies.
  - Daughters: room to explore rather than forced focus; being trusted with secrets, which she treats as an honor; bedtime structure against a night-owl habit; and help letting go of old hurts she hides.
- **Intuition.** It is trained deliberately, with a sender-and-receiver guessing drill.
- **Schooling.** It goes by the child's own interests, because compulsion does not work.
- **Withdrawal.** Total withdrawal from stimulus is common enough that adults sometimes misread it as autism.
- **Ritual.** Snodgrass says the type clings to tenets adopted in youth, prefers plain philosophy without sweetening, and finds comfort in predictable, all-encompassing ritual: radical subject matter, rigid liturgy (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §7, from Snodgrass).
- **Disputed parenting.** Snodgrass says the Scorpio parent takes pride in family but fails to discipline children or heal family rifts. Silva credits the Scorpio parent with exceptional attunement (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).

**Birth, death and the life course**
- **Birth as struggle.** Martin says a Scorpio's birth is often a real struggle for survival, and that the way we enter life is the way we live it (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §14.4, from Martin).
  - The folk belief she reports: when a Scorpio is born, someone in the family has just died or is about to; when a Scorpio dies, a birth follows.
- **Several lifetimes in one.** The phoenix cycle is a life lived as several lifetimes in one. Each "death" is a crisis that eliminates what has been outgrown, followed by a completely new chapter (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §14.2). ⚠️ The source author is not named in that line; the surrounding citations are Martin's.
- **The decans as a sequence** (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11, from Silva and Emerson):
  - the first is the one just learning to use its energies, who will make mistakes;
  - the second seeks a higher purpose through death and resurrection;
  - the third is settled and aimed.
- **The Moon decides the third decan's direction.** Badly placed, Scorpio buries its feelings and feels lost and abandoned. Well placed, it can observe its pain and forgive itself and others (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11, from Silva).
- **Transmutation.** Tierney: Scorpio's upward-pointing stinger resembles Sagittarius's arrow. The concentrated energy must be transmuted and aimed in the right direction before it can become a source of inspiration (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Tierney).
- **Integration.** Martin's developmental task is consciously integrating the planets in detriment and fall. For Scorpio these are Venus and the Moon, whose gentleness and care would soften the sign's otherwise brutal effectiveness (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).
- **Carrying the collective's poison.** Martin says a Scorpio is often born with the task of carrying, bearing, suffering and finally purging deep ancestral or collective poisons. It will often be blamed for this and will certainly not be thanked (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §14.5, from Martin).
  - Martin also says the party that brings a group's buried material to the surface gets blamed for doing so (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations
- **Named as a threat almost everywhere.** The names amount to danger, oppression, deviance and death (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass):
  - Arab astronomers: *Elakrab* (the scorpion, or war);
  - Copts: *Isidis* (the oppressor);
  - Sumerians: the outlaw;
  - Hebrews: *Lesath*, from *Lesha* (the perverse);
  - the Maya: the death god's sign.
- **Selket, the one benign reading.** Egyptian star-gazers linked Scorpio to Selket (Selek), patron of herbal healing and keeper of burial vaults and canopic jars, often named in *The Book of the Dead*. Healer and keeper of the dead in one figure (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass). Tierney notes the Egyptians' fascination with Scorpio's world of the dead and the afterlife (same section).
- **Orion.** Artemis sent the giant scorpion to sting Orion's foot and stop his joy in killing, then set it on the opposite side of the sky. That is why Orion sets as Scorpio rises (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
  - Pherecydes (about 450 B.C.) already used Orion's setting as Scorpio rises (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).
- **Libra cut from Scorpio's claws.** Roman astrologers parted the claws to form Libra. Scorpio's neighbor was carved from its own body (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Phaëton.** The scorpion stung a horse of the sun chariot and the boy fell to his death. Snodgrass reads it as the piercing sting of truth: hubris met by a correction it could not survive. Ovid describes the Scorpion sweeping its savage arms (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Punishment.** Rehoboam threatens to punish with whips and scorpions, and Ezekiel is commissioned among briers, thorns and scorpions. The naval equivalent was the cat-o'-nine-tails (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Satire.** At Trimalchio's banquet in the *Satyricon*, the zodiac tray puts a small sea fish on Scorpio, and the host calls Scorpios poisoners (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Literature.**
  - Snodgrass names Robert Louis Stevenson, Victor Hugo and Martin Luther as writers known for vigor. They wrote of turbulent times in which principled heroes must state their beliefs and hold to them.
  - Hugo's Valjean, though he earned sainthood, is buried in a potters' field under an unmarked stone, far from the grand cemetery.
  - Martin's revenge exemplar is Dumas's Edmond Dantès.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass and Martin)
- **The seasons and the Taurus axis.** Martin sets Beltane (Taurus, Persephone's return) against Samhain (Scorpio, the festival of death, Persephone's descent and Demeter's mourning) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin).
  - King Midas, wholly given over to the Taurus end, sees his garden die of pure retention until a Scorpio-side reversal restores it (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin).
- **The fixed cross.** Leo, Taurus, Scorpio and Aquarius symbolize the four directions and the four evangelists. Scorpio takes John, the eagle. Its apostle is Bartholomew (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **War.** The Roman *scorpio* was a deadly dart fired by siege engines against walled cities (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **The phoenix.** Silva says the glyph stood for the phoenix in ancient times. The phoenix is also among Scorpio's animals, with the eagle and the serpent (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §12, from Silva).
- **Antares.** The heart of the Scorpion, one of the four Royal Stars. Around 3000 B.C. the Persians named it the watcher of the west (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §12, from Snodgrass).
- **Old naming.** The 419 B.C. Babylonian list already names Scorpio among the signs (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md` Appendix I).

## 7. Structure
**Element: water.**
- **Mode.** Water absorbs and reads what goes unsaid (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, §5, from Tierney and Martin):
  - it seeps past boundaries into hidden places;
  - it reads hidden motives;
  - it offers support without asking why;
  - it endures heavy psychological load;
  - it treats emotion as the primary channel of information.
- **Truth.** Martin pairs water against air on collective truth. For water, truth lies within, in the emotional undercurrents everyone shares (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1).
- **Water's weaknesses.**
  - poor boundaries;
  - weak self-preservation;
  - a tendency to be pulled into, or to become, other people's turmoil;
  - stored injuries that fester.
  Tierney prescribes a filter borrowed from the other elements: fire's self-interest, earth's territoriality and air's detachment (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §5; `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Scorpio within water.** It is the fixed member. Tierney's image is a very deep, dark, still well (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §5, from Tierney).
  - Fixity steadies water's moods, so Scorpio stays composed in danger.
  - Its function is purgative.
  - Its typical error is equating vulnerability with weakness.
- **Water does not fight, except Scorpio.** Water signs are not fighters by nature, Scorpio being the exception, though even it can feel conflicted in emotional battles (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Snodgrass dissents.** He calls the water triad persuaders and fun-lovers; every other source has water private and withdrawn (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9).

**Modality: fixed.**
- **Middle of the season.** The season is at its peak and the days are much alike (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Snodgrass and Tierney). In the breathing image, fixed is the held breath, a static power (same section).
- **It won't move first.** A fixed sign does not refuse change; it refuses to be the one who moves first, because its motivation to change is always internal (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §3, from Tierney and Martin).
- **Strengths and failures.** Fixed signs finish, hold and endure. They fail through stalemate, through stubborn resistance to easier solutions offered by others, and through rigid habit. They have an inborn pride (same section).
- **The boulder test.** Faced with a boulder in the road, Martin's fixed traveler spends five years drilling a hole through it (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Hardest for Scorpio and Taurus.** Tierney singles out these two as having the most difficulty with fixity (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).

**Polarity: receptive (also called negative, yin or feminine).**
- **Inward-moving.** Martin describes the receptive mode as subject-oriented, enclosing and withdrawing, parallel to Jung's introversion (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Not passive.** Martin agrees that yin people get very strong in a crisis (same section).
- **Failure modes.** Withdrawal into isolation; holding what should be released; grievance stored rather than raised (same section).
- **The cold front.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* says Scorpio's edgy, shady or cold front is a natural façade of its inward orientation and deep contemplation (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Silva).

**Opposite sign: Taurus. The axis is retention versus elimination.**
- **What each end holds.** Taurus holds on and takes in; Scorpio lets go and eliminates. Martin: retention leads to release, and taking in leads to elimination, which keeps nature in balance (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin).
  - Taurus's drive for stability can harden into stagnation, which provokes the intense crises associated with Scorpio. Those crises break down fixed structures for the sake of growth (same section).
  - Silva frames the axis as personal possessions against shared possessions and grand legacies (same section, from Silva).
  - The documentation calls this the resources and ownership axis.
- **Fixed against fixed.** Martin says the Taurus–Scorpio confrontation can be especially entrenched and unyielding (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Martin). It is the only collision in the system with no built-in exit, so grudges are inherited (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §5, from Martin and Silva).
- **Possessiveness disputed.** Martin says possessiveness belongs to Taurus and is not a Scorpio trait at all; Scorpio is where we are forced to surrender and let go. On her reading, a possessive Scorpio has collapsed onto the Taurus end. Silva and Emerson treat possessiveness and jealousy as core Scorpio (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8).
- **A working mode too.** Silva is warm about Taurus as a friend (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Silva):
  - Scorpio's secrets are safe with Taurus;
  - Taurus's humor helps Scorpio laugh even at sad or annoying things;
  - Scorpio's accurate intuition keeps Taurus from grave mistakes;
  - Taurus understands Scorpio's jealousy and is even flattered by it.
- **The Moon's one-way trade.** Taurus exalts the Moon; Scorpio is where the Moon falls (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- **Oppositions in general.** The default failure is projection: each side meets its disowned half as an outside enemy (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**Ruling planets: Mars (traditional) and Pluto (modern).**
- **Mars.** Scorpio is Mars's nocturnal home, as Aries is its daytime home. Here aggression turns covert and precise, second nature: the dog that does not bark but can bite. It is the professional soldier and the surgeon's knife (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §1, from Martin).
  - As a faculty, Mars is assertion and force, in a trained, disciplined mode (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Pluto.** Pluto adds the collective-scale purge: forcing buried material to the surface so it can be healed (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §2, from Martin).
  - As a faculty, Pluto is purge and regeneration, and power with its abuse (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
  - Tierney says Pluto never takes the easy way out and is as good at complicating life as its sign (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **The corpus can't choose between them.** Silva's summary table names Mars, his body text names Pluto, and his lucky day is Tuesday, Mars's day (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Silva). Martin prefers the traditional rulers (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §2).
- **No exaltation.** No faculty is amplified beyond its native strength (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). Silva says in passing that Scorpio exalts Uranus. Nothing else supports this, and the GDD does not adopt it (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §7).
- **Venus in detriment, Moon in fall.** The two planets of gentleness and care are at their weakest here (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, from Martin and Silva).
  - Martin says integrating them would soften the sign's brutal effectiveness.
  - Silva says Venus in detriment brings too much love and affection or too little, sometimes against social norms.
  - The substrate's reading: the gentleness exists but stops at the edge of the trusted circle.
  - The documentation's general meanings: detriment is a faculty working against the grain; fall is the least-trusted faculty (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).
  - Venus as a faculty is valuation, relatedness and gentleness. The Moon is care, belonging and the emotional container (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).

**Squares (conflict).** Both are fixed squares: rigid, unyielding, entrenched (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Leo: a visibility war between two prides.** Leo displays its pride openly; Scorpio's pride is hidden and harder to trace. Each reads the other's method as an insult (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Tierney, Emerson and Silva).
  - Emerson: Leo would have to surrender all authority, which it won't, and Scorpio won't have it any other way.
  - Silva adds Leo's jealousy to Scorpio's possessiveness.
  - Both experience yielding as humiliation.
  - In Silva's friendship material, Scorpio finds Leo's attempts at control funny. It thinks the lion too innocent to beat a lifelong strategist, and should occasionally flatter its ego.
- **Aquarius: the attachment problem.** Fixed signs hold on tightly. Aquarius can drop its attachments overnight, while Scorpio is the sign most obsessed with its attachments. Scorpio reads Aquarius's ease as fraud or inhumanity (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, from Tierney and Emerson).
  - Emerson: Scorpio's rigidity clashes with Aquarius's need for freedom, and if Scorpio cannot loosen the reins, Aquarius leaves.
  - Emerson also prescribes Aquarius as Scorpio's corrective: borrowing its wide-angle view against Scorpio's tunnel vision.
  - Silva's *Ultimate Guide* puts Aquarius among the worst matches: too distant and analytical, and blunt enough to say something Scorpio would never forgive.

**Trines and sextiles (support).**
- **Cancer (trine).** Both read emotion at the same speed. Cancer accepts Scorpio's control, and Scorpio is less aggressive with Cancer (Emerson). Same-element pairs risk sulking standoffs, and Cancer struggles with Scorpio's intensity and silence (Silva). The leisure material is in section 5 (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9).
- **Pisces (trine).** Emerson calls it a match made in heaven: Pisces's sweetness softens Scorpio's lack of impulse control (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9).
  - Tierney's contrast: Scorpio needs to know why someone became an outcast; Pisces accepts and loves them regardless. Investigation against unconditional acceptance (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9).
- **Water with water.** Total comprehension and no differentiation. Disputes stay non-verbal and never end (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6, from Silva).
- **Virgo (sextile).** Rated Scorpio's best working partnership (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9).
  - Virgo can handle Scorpio's jealousy and explosions and praises its successes (Emerson).
  - They share perfectionism in love and value security and privacy (Silva).
  - Martin notes their glyphs come from the same ancient medical symbol, the feminine and masculine versions of it, and both signs are linked to medicine and healing.
  - The friction runs both ways: Virgo's nitpicking against Scorpio's grudges.
- **Capricorn (sextile).** One of the few signs that gets along well with Scorpio (Silva). Capricorn's organizing complements Scorpio's investigating (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9).
  - Emerson warns them not to isolate themselves to extremes.
  - Snodgrass says together they tend to tyrannize.

**Semi-sextiles and quincunxes (awkward).**
- **Libra (semi-sextile).** Libra was cut from Scorpio's claws (section 6). As friends, Scorpio misreads Libra's casual remarks as criticism and does not voice its displeasure, which can wreck the friendship (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Silva).
  - Silva's advice reverses Scorpio's default: speak up right away rather than stew, trusting the scales to weigh complaints fairly.
  - On compatibility the sources differ. Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* says the two have little in common; Emerson says it can be stable and healthy; the *Ultimate Guide* says it works if both compromise (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
- **Sagittarius (semi-sextile).** Tierney says Sagittarius may believe in the hells Scorpio has been trapped in but has no wish to go there. Scorpio's upward stinger, transmuted, becomes Sagittarius's arrow (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Tierney).
  - Silva calls Sagittarius one of the worst intimate matches: privacy against disclosure, depth against lightness, prudence against impulsive spending, and Scorpio's dislike of being challenged.
  - As a friend, though, Sagittarius opens Scorpio's mind, and Scorpio teaches discernment in return.
- **Aries (quincunx).** As friends they spar, and Scorpio relishes it. Aries rarely holds grudges and forgets an argument within half an hour, which models the lesson Scorpio needs. Their shared Mars rulership is named as the source of their tussle for control (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Silva).
- **Gemini (quincunx).** Gemini lifts Scorpio's moods with youthful optimism, and both love a mystery and unanswered questions (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, from Silva).
- **The pattern (the substrate's reading).** Silva lists Aries, Gemini and Sagittarius among the worst intimate matches and the better friendships. Relationships at this distance work at arm's length and fail up close (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §9, §15). ⚠️ The substrate says it assembled this pattern across three of Silva's chapters, and that Silva contradicts himself between them.

## 8. Decans
Silva and Emerson agree on the second and third decans' sub-rulers and differ on the first. Silva's dates are internally coherent and his names read as a sequence (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2).
- **First decan, *Intensity* (Scorpio/Scorpio; Emerson: Pluto; Silva: Mars and Pluto): raw intensity and grief.**
  - The purest Scorpios, enhanced for good and ill: always ready for the worst, and prone to extreme fears when unsupported.
  - Very sensual, obsessive and driven, and open to grief, loss, sorrow and suffering as well as pleasure.
  - Strong at rebounding and starting again.
  - A loner, intimidating and seemingly cold.
  - Emerson calls it the highest intensity in the zodiac: dramatic feelings, instant verdicts on anything new, rarely changing its mind.
  - Silva calls it the one still learning to use its energies.
  - Tarot: Five of Cups (grief, loss, anger).
- **Second decan, *Transmutation* (Scorpio/Pisces; Emerson: Neptune; Silva: Jupiter and Neptune): death, resurrection and the hidden.**
  - Death and resurrection are its central themes, along with a sense of higher purpose.
  - It loves hidden meanings and the occult, and will sacrifice anything to find what is real.
  - Healer and manipulator in one: self-sacrificing, yet cunning, seductive and controlling (Silva and Emerson).
  - Tarot: Six of Cups (peace, forgiveness, harmony). The substrate notes the card's keywords do not match the described behavior.
- **Third decan, *Manifestation* (Scorpio/Cancer; the Moon): integrity, care and the long memory.**
  - Emotional integrity and refusal to compromise trusted feelings; caretaking of those closest.
  - Easily hurt, never shows it, never forgets, and takes revenge generously when the chance comes.
  - The Moon's placement decides between buried loneliness and the ability to observe its pain and forgive.
  - A compelling writer or speaker.
  - Tarot: Seven of Cups (finding where one fits in, soul-searching, self-examination).
- **The cusps (border bands, from Silva).**
  - **Libra/Scorpio, the drama cusp:** blunt honesty that wins admiration, with jealousy as its failure.
  - **Scorpio/Sagittarius, the revolution cusp:** a life against convention, self-taught, at loggerheads with authority, unsuited to a nine-to-five job.
  (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11)

## 10. Topics in this area

## 12. Gaps
- **Death itself is thin as a human topic.** The material gives myths (Selket, the Maya death god), a festival (Samhain), occupations (coroner, funeral work) and a folk belief about births and deaths. It says nothing about dying, about mourning as a process beyond the first decan's keywords, or about how cultures handle the dead apart from the Egyptian keeper of tombs.
- **The eighth house is barely developed.** Silva gives a one-line gloss (rejuvenation, birth and death cycles, resources, finances, karma, self-transformation), and Snodgrass adds the investor. The documentation does not develop inheritance, shared money, debt, sex or the taboo as eighth-house topics. The estates reading is the substrate's (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10).
- **Sexuality is thin.** Beyond desire, the decans' sensuality and seduction, and sex therapy as an occupation, sexuality is barely discussed. The substrate notes that Silva's large compatibility and rising-sign sections are mostly unusable (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
- **Group scale is thin but present.** The few collective findings are:
  - the eighth house's shared resources;
  - the employer's exclusive-loyalty rule;
  - the trusted circle's admission test and concealment;
  - what happens when many of the type are together.
  Everything else is individual psychology (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15; `00_Method_and_Sources.md`).
- **Silva has no chapter on what Scorpio needs.** Section 2's core need is assembled from statements spread across his chapters (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
- **Source disagreements recorded in the documentation.**
  - **Possessiveness.** Martin says it belongs to Taurus, not Scorpio; Silva and Emerson make it core (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, §15).
  - **Libra compatibility.** Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*: little in common. Emerson: stable and healthy. The *Ultimate Guide*: workable with compromise (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
  - **Aquarius.** Emerson has a hard square; Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs* calls it a possible exception; the *Ultimate Guide* puts it among the worst matches (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §8, §15).
  - **Parenting.** Silva's attuned parent against Snodgrass's parent who fails to discipline children or heal rifts (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
  - **Gentleness.** Silva's native gentleness against Martin's weak Venus and Moon. The substrate reconciles them by radius (inside the circle against outside), and asks that this not be reopened as a conflict (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §10, §15).
  - **Silva against himself.** Ruler (Mars in his table, Pluto in his text); stone (coral against topaz); lucky numbers (nines against 2 and 4); sign dates (ending Nov 21 against Nov 22) (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).
  - **First decan's sub-ruler.** Emerson says Pluto; Silva says Mars and Pluto (Substrate `E_Decans.md` §5).
  - **Exaltation.** Silva alone has Scorpio exalting Uranus, which is not adopted (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §7).
  - **Snodgrass on water.** He calls the water signs persuaders and fun-lovers, against every other source (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9).
  - **Six of Cups.** The second decan's card keywords (peace, forgiveness, harmony) do not match its described behavior (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §11).
- ⚠️ **The GDD files disagree with each other.**
  - **Stones.** `08_Scorpio.md` §15 says the commonly cited Scorpio stones, obsidian among them, appear nowhere in the corpus. `G_Correspondences.md` gives obsidian as Emerson's Scorpio stone.
  - **Metal.** `08_Scorpio.md` says iron is never stated for Scorpio and remains extrapolation. `G_Correspondences.md` derives iron through Mars from Snodgrass's planetary table, a derivation it validates against five signs, and confirms plutonium through Pluto.

  - Silva's line that Scorpio is very calm because it is a water sign.

  - A reference to a nonexistent fifth decan (Substrate `08_Scorpio.md` §15).

  - revenge and forgiveness outside astrology;
  - secrecy and privacy;

  - transformation and rebirth in religion and psychology. The documentation's only rebirth figure is the phoenix.
