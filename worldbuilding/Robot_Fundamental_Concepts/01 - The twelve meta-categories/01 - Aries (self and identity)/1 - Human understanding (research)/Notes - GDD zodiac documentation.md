Research notes, 2026-10-30. What the GDD's zodiac documentation says about this area of human life, in our own words. Not robot understanding; not Concordia design.

**Reading notes.** Everything below reports astrology as human tradition and belief, never as fact. Where a GDD file credits a book, the citation names it ("from Tierney"). The GDD corpus is eight astrology books: Silva's *Zodiac Signs: The Ultimate Guide* and *Sun and Moon Signs*, Snodgrass's *Signs of the Zodiac*, Clare Martin's *Mapping the Psyche* (three volumes), Tierney's *All Around the Zodiac* and Emerson's *Behavioral Astrology* (Substrate `00_Method_and_Sources.md`, The corpus). The GDD files were written to design one city district; that application is left out here.

## 1. Areas of life

What astrology gives Aries, as far as this documentation goes:
- **The self as a separate identity, asserted.** Snodgrass's key words for the sign: motto "I am.", key word *aspiration*, perception *intuitive* (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §5, from Snodgrass).
- **Beginnings and initiative.** Aries is the first sign and the opening act. Tierney reads it as the urge to set something going right now (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Tierney). Martin, following Rudhyar, treats 0° Aries as the seed point of every cycle, holding the cycle's whole potential (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10, from Martin). The Full Attributes file calls it the first spark of a new cycle (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Personal survival.** Martin ties the sign's spontaneity, courage and goal-focus to personal survival (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Action, will and force.** These come through the ruler, Mars: assertion, starting things, standing up for oneself, making a claim against someone (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **The physical body in action.** Tierney's Aries is physically assertive but does not protect its own body, and the tradition reported by Snodgrass has it taking perfect health for granted (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney and Snodgrass; see §4).
- **Appearance and first impression.** The documentation gives a physical stereotype and a set of recognition markers (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §4, from Snodgrass, Silva and Martin; see §5 below).
- **Earliest childhood.** Snodgrass assigns the sign the life-age from conception to six (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §5, from Snodgrass). Silva calls it the infant of the zodiac's sequence (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Silva).
- **Courage, competition, leadership and protection** as the sign's listed strengths (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **The natural house.** The Aries file has no house section. The only house statement in this material is in the Libra file, which sets Aries against Libra as the first house against the seventh (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8). The documentation never says what the first house covers. ⚠ See §12.

## 2. Core meaning

- **The archetype: the opener.** Astrology reads Aries as the compulsion to be the first to move, sure that if it hesitates someone else will get there first. Tierney separates this from Capricorn's ambition to build a legacy (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Tierney).
- **Nothing behind it.** Emerson's point: being first in the sequence, Aries has no inherited wisdom to consult, so it goes forward on blind optimism (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Emerson).
- **Worker and warrior fused.** The oldest layer of the figure is a laborer, not a ram (see §6); the warrior image came later (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1 and §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Infant and warrior at once.** Silva: "Aries wishes to save and be saved simultaneously." The type wants to be the rescuer and also the one everyone worries about (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1, from Silva).
- **The driving motive:** to act now, and to be first (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1).
- **The core need: a selfhood nobody owns, proven by action.** Tierney's reading is that Aries builds its identity by putting its own stamp on everything it does. It wants to find its strengths alone, on its own terms. It refuses to belong to anyone, whether a lover, a spouse, an employer or a religion, who could then direct its moves. The question underneath is whether it can live life its own way (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **Reflection is avoided, not merely missing.** Tierney puts part of the sign's innocence down to an unwillingness to look inward, because reflection slows any sign down (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §5, from Tierney).
- **Truth is subjective and needs no proof.** This is the fire family's way of knowing. Martin says fire signs have an inborn sense of their own specialness, which is why they trust themselves and take risks other temperaments avoid (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1 and §2, from Martin).

## 3. The area at its best

- **Courage and initiative.** A real willingness to go first, try the untested thing and take the blow nobody else wants (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Directness and honesty.** Blunt, but rarely dishonest (Full Attributes, Aries). Transparent: no mind games and no façade, and quick to spot a lie (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Silva).
- **Leadership from the front, and decisiveness.** Once it faces a choice, it seldom agonizes (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Healthy competition** that pushes performance up instead of tearing others down (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Protectiveness** toward the people and causes it has committed to (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Competence in a crisis.** It works best in short, intense bursts, and people turn to their Aries first in an emergency, at any hour (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Tierney and Silva).
- **A native public-service instinct.** Silva's career list is built around emergencies: firefighting, law enforcement, the military, surgery, rescue. Her reason is that the type wants the simple, clear purpose such work provides, and likes to fight under it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*).
- **Intolerance of injustice, even on behalf of strangers.** It will stand up for the weak. Tierney extends this to politics: Aries takes authoritarian control personally and resists systems that try to turn people into obedient followers (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Silva and Tierney).
- **No festering.** Nothing is left to build pressure inside. It is first to anger, first to cool and first to make amends (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2).
- **Generosity**, with money even when there is none (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Silva).
- **It wants everyone else strong.** Tierney says Aries wants others to be as self-reliant as it is. At best this is an equal's refusal to talk down to anyone (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **Fire's general gifts.** Starting what nothing else would start; warmth and morale that spread; the faith to get up again after crashing; and risk-taking that comes from real unconcern with the odds, not from bravado (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Nobility in the venture.** Martin finds something noble in the Aries hero's quest however foolhardy it is, along with codes of honor and chivalry (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Martin).

## 4. The area gone wrong

**The pattern the GDD names:** a sign's vices are mostly its virtues pushed past their useful range. For Aries, courage becomes recklessness (Full Attributes, "Worth Your Attention"; Quick Reference, closing pattern).

- **Impulsiveness and recklessness.** Impulses skip past consequences, and speed is mistaken for courage (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **It cannot finish.** Patience with sustained, unglamorous follow-through is short (Full Attributes, Aries). Tierney says Aries moves on before finishing what it started with such energy, and drops labor-heavy tasks without warning, each one abandoned when it stops being exciting (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney). Emerson credits Aries entrepreneurs with a reputation for starting big projects and a chronic lack of follow-through (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §6, from Emerson). For cardinal signs generally, Tierney says they look busy whether or not they produce anything, and never know when to stop pushing (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Tierney).
- **No stamina, and no awareness of that.** Martin calls this a trait shared by all the fire signs (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Martin). Fire can be extinguished by the resistance the world piles up against it (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin). It also drains the people around it to refuel itself (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §9, from Tierney).
- **Contempt for weakness.** Tierney says Aries has little respect for anyone who uses weakness or insecurity to win sympathy, and despises weakness in itself as well as in others. Caution and deliberation read to it as cowardice (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Temper.** It flares fast and burns out fast, and often leaves real damage behind (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **It cannot compromise.** The need to win can outweigh what is actually at stake (Full Attributes, Aries).
- **Insensitivity.** Its directness lands hard on people who needed gentleness (Full Attributes, Aries). Martin says fire tramples feelings without noticing and without malice, and that is exactly why nobody corrects it (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Idleness is the danger.** Tierney pictures the idle Aries as a bomb waiting to go off, triggered by a small provocation (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Neglect of the body.** Tradition links Aries with perfect health. So, Snodgrass reports, the type is said to reject medical advice, refuse prescribed drugs and deny aging, and to die either in accidents caused by headstrong behavior or by sudden collapse in old age (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Snodgrass). Tierney: physically assertive, but not protective of its own body (from Tierney). Martin adds that many fire signs dislike their bodies as confining (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Blocked energy turns savage.** What enrages Aries most is being denied the right to act at once when it is ready. When others block it, its instinct is to lash out like a cornered animal (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Black-and-white thinking.** It makes no fine distinctions. Details bore it, and ignoring them gets it into trouble (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney).
- **Bravado as a locked door.** It keeps itself unowned by refusing real intimacy. Nobody sees its vulnerable side, only a hard front and a show of needing nobody (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney). Underneath, Silva finds a fear of being unworthy of love and real abandonment issues (from Silva).
- **Entitlement.** Silva's version is that what is Aries' is its own, and what is yours is also its own. She asks who will refill the coffers once they are empty (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Silva).
- **Violence when its self-belief breaks.** Martin separates the three fire signs here: when their faith in themselves is broken, Aries becomes violent and destructive, Leo demanding and jealous, Sagittarius arrogant and patronizing (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3; `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin). Silva: at the extreme, bossy, even a bully (from Silva).
- **Domination at collective scale.** Snodgrass reads nations he links with Aries through empire-building and the domination of weaker countries. It is the only collective-scale statement in the Aries material (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3 and §15, from Snodgrass). ⚠ This is a national stereotype. The GDD itself marks Snodgrass's sign-to-country geography as not for use (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix III). It is recorded here only as what the tradition says.
- **Self-ruin.** Snodgrass reads Shakespeare's Hotspur as the sign's case study: the same courage that makes him the natural rebel, with rashness and an uncontrolled temper, destroys him (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Snodgrass).
- **Self-righteousness and egocentricity**, with some pomposity, in the second decan. These faults are not always obvious at first (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
- **Independence that wrecks relationships**, in the third decan: it will never let anyone control it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §11, from Emerson).
- **The active mode's failures.** Motion mistaken for progress; depletion, because outward strength is not backed by inner resources; drift toward dominance, since the same energy creates and destroys; and overreach into others' business, because outward motion has no natural stopping point (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin).
- **Petty friction.** Ancient texts treated Mars as the bringer of small misfortunes: not catastrophe, but constant low-grade tempers and quarrels (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10).
- **Blindness to facts, and exemption-seeking.** Objective truths such as the odds against winning a lottery mean nothing to fire. Fire will not do the paperwork its own plans need, and treats a tax return as an insult, as if rules were for ordinary people. It does not clean up after itself (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2 and §6, from Martin).
- **No humility, and no habit of asking for help.** Silva says humility has to be taught to the type deliberately, because it does not grasp the word (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §7, from Silva).

## 5. Behavior under pressure and over time

**Under stress.** Loud, fast and brief. It makes a noisy scene, then quits once it has had enough action, often just as its opponent is warming up. It discharges stress through physical effort; denied that, it turns on whoever is nearest (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §6, from Tierney).

**In conflict.** First to anger, first to cool, first to make amends (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §2). Blocked, it lashes out (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney). Cardinal signs cannot stand prolonged deliberation, deadlock or foot-dragging. Frustrated, they stir up a small crisis so that something has to give, and when cornered they feel the urge to attack or defend (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Tierney). Two cardinal parties at odds tend to welcome the fight, escalate and force a decision rather than stall (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §5; `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).

**In crisis.** This is its best version. It becomes single-minded, shuts everything else out and ignores danger to itself. Tierney's image is a toddler running into traffic, caught up in the thrill of rushing ahead. Aries also pushes people to stake everything on one throw and take present-moment risks (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §6, from Tierney).

**When idle or bored.** It becomes more dangerous, not less (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Tierney).

**When its self-belief is broken.** It turns violent and destructive (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Martin).

**Over a long haul.** It can manage a sprint but not a siege, because fire lacks stamina (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Martin). Fire can be worn out by accumulated obstruction, yet it usually has the faith to get up and carry on after crashing (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).

**At work.** It volunteers first and takes the lead unasked. It hates routine and monotony, and stays engaged only while the work is live. It is poor at closing tasks. Silva places it either in the break-room crowd, gossiping freely but never maliciously, or alone with headphones on, indifferent to whether anyone follows (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §6, from Silva and Emerson).

**Socially.** Outgoing by default. It mixes across every social class, hosts at no notice without planning details, and is competitive even at party games. It guards its downtime fiercely and vanishes without explanation, expecting that to be respected (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §6). It has probably never waited for a bus (Martin), insists on driving rather than riding, energizes a room from the moment it arrives (Silva), and is brutally frank without malice. Martin says it walks leaning forward for speed and focus. Martin's Moon-in-Aries note: it eats fast, as if every meal were a race; Snodgrass gives its eating as hurried and finicky (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §4 and §12, from Martin, Silva and Snodgrass).

**How the type is said to learn** (Silva's chapter on raising Aries children):
- It learns from consequences, not from advice. Silva recommends firm boundaries with consequences stated in advance, so the child links action to result.
- Impulses are redirected, not forbidden: a raised hand instead of an interruption, a pillow instead of a person. Physical work and sport are the outlets.
- It responds to praise and listens more when it expects praise. Punishment damages the relationship and teaches nothing. Criticism lands as an attack on its character, and shame is the one thing that reliably breaks it.
- Its independence is fed: nothing should be done for it that it can do itself.
- The adult must never lose their calm or show disrespect; the child reads both as proof it is not good enough.
- Humility, patience and asking for help all have to be taught on purpose, against its pride.
(Substrate `01_Aries.md` §7, from Silva.)

**Context decides the verdict.** Martin: the same combative child becomes the problem child in a gentle, peace-loving family, and is admired and encouraged in a competitive one (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §7, from Martin).

**As it matures.** Martin's developmental reading: Aries grows by consciously taking in Venus (valuing and relating to others) and Saturn (self-discipline and patience), the two capacities it is structurally worst at (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10; `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin). Tierney says Sagittarius, which wishes good things for everyone and not only for itself, can widen Aries' aim, and Leo shows how fire can be contained instead of spent (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §9, from Tierney).

**Over a lifetime.** It denies aging, and the tradition has it die by accident or by sudden collapse in old age (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §3, from Snodgrass).

**When secure.** The documentation says almost nothing (see §12).

## 6. Myth, history and cultural associations

- **The laborer before the ram.** Snodgrass records that the Babylonian twelve had no ram in this position; a laborer stood there, and the ram replaced it later. The oldest form of the figure is a worker (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §1 and §13, from Snodgrass). ⚠ The GDD's correspondence file reports Snodgrass's timeline listing Aries among the Babylonian constellations of 419 B.C. (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix I). The two statements may refer to different periods; the documentation does not reconcile them.
- **Names that speak of righteousness and rule.** Akkadian *Baraziggar*, glossed as the altar of righteousness; Egyptian *Tamelouris Amon*, the Reign of Amon; Hebrew *Taleh* (the lamb); Arabic *Alhamal* (the sheep); Syrian *Amroo* (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **The stars.** The alpha star Hamal (glossed as *the ram*) sits on the ram's forehead. Alsharatan is on the northern horn (Hindu *Asvini*, the horsemen); *Bharani* means the bearer. The Chinese made two asterisms from its stars: Leu, glossed as *the train of the garment*, and Oei, *the belly*. The constellation is small and dim (39th in size overall, 66 stars in Snodgrass's text) and is also called the Northern Fly (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass). Appendix II ranks it 11th of twelve in area with 28 stars, 4 of them major ⚠ (the star counts differ between the two passages) (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix II). Hooke found its gamma star, Mesarthim, in 1664 (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix I).
- **Dionysus and the spring: rescue, not war.** Crossing the Libyan desert, Dionysus' followers were dying of hunger and thirst. He called up a ram, which appeared beside a spring that saved them, and he set the ram in the sky to mark it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass). The origin story is about saving life.
- **Phrixus and Helle: sacrifice and one broken rule.** A stepmother plots to sacrifice two children to save a failing harvest. Their mother Nephele has Hermes send Zeus's golden-fleeced ram to carry them off. Helle breaks the one condition, not to look down, and falls. The ram is later sacrificed and set in the heavens. In Silva's version, Poseidon takes ram form and sires the winged ram, and Phrixus returns it to him. The GDD keeps both versions: rescue by gift against rescue by descent (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass and Silva).
- **Courage with an expiry date.** Ibn Bakhtishu, court physician in Baghdad, wrote in 1295 that a dose of goat's bile in wild-lettuce juice, taken while the Sun is in Aries, removes fear for exactly one year, until the Sun returns to Aries (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Ram cults and spring.** Egypt kept rams as sacred temple animals, lined the entrance at Karnak with an avenue of rams and showed Amon-Ra with ram's horns. Egyptians and Persians held a spring festival at Aries' rising that combined worship, the end of winter and the planting of fields, and meant to win the sign's favor for the harvest (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass). The sign stands for beginnings, renewal and the start of the agricultural year.
- **Judaic.** The shofar, the ram's horn, opens Rosh Hashanah, the new year. David strings a ram's ten sinews into a lyre (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Thunder and might.** In Yoruba and Germanic lore the ram is thunder, a blast on the horn, and Thor holds it as a symbol of physical and political might (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Christian.** The sign corresponds to the apostle Peter (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **The Jungian note.** Jungian reading grants the ram its butting strength but notes its awkward, limited movement: powerful in one direction, clumsy in every other (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass).
- **Heroic templates** (Martin): Achilles insisting on being first ashore at Troy, chasing personal immortality and indifferent to who wins the war; Jason and the fleece; Theseus and the Minotaur; Perseus and Andromeda; Robin Hood; Joan of Arc, on fire from the moment she got her quest, burned, and still a symbol centuries later. However foolhardy, the venture has something noble about it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Martin).
- **Literature.** Chaucer sets the pilgrimage of the *Canterbury Tales* in spring, timed by the Sun's course through Aries. Shakespeare's Hotspur is the cautionary portrait (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass). Addison's satirical verse, quoted by Snodgrass, sketches the type who works from dawn to dusk, hates being outdone and walks almost at a run (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §4, from Snodgrass).
- **The battering ram.** The Roman siege engine *aries* was a single tree trunk with a carved ram's head on its rounded end, swung on ropes against a gate. The GDD notes its use at Masada. It is force applied in one direction (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §12, from Silva or Snodgrass ⚠ the GDD file does not say which).
- **Mars in the Roman reading.** Silva stresses that to the Romans war was as much about discipline and strength as about destroying the enemy (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10, from Silva).
- **Alchemy.** In Mylius (1622) the first stage of the alchemical Work begins in Aries. Lambsprinck (1625) shows the alchemical Mars slaying the primordial dragon to release the tincture that leads to gold: violence done to matter to free what is valuable in it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10, from Martin).
- **The glyph.** One line divided in two: ram's horns, or a new shoot breaking up through the soil past roots and rock (Silva, Martin). Silva also reports a fertility reading. Silva notes that Aries has a single symbol where Scorpio has four, and takes the simplicity as the point (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §12, from Silva and Martin).
- **Nations.** Snodgrass links England, Germany and Rome with Aries through empire-making (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §13, from Snodgrass). ⚠ This is a national stereotype the GDD marks as not for use (Substrate `G_Correspondences.md`, Appendix III).

## 7. Structure

**Element: fire** (with Leo and Sagittarius).
- Fire acts first, on personal conviction, and treats the world as material for its own story. Its truth is subjective and needs no proof (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1 and §2, from Martin and Tierney).
- Fire opens every cycle, and earth always follows it as the brake (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §1, from Tierney).
- Aries is fire's primal and fastest form. It wants results immediately and has a short attention span, but needs little time to react. Tierney's image is a pilot light that never goes out (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Tierney).
- Fire's needs: fuel and containment, which come to the same thing. Earth supplies both, and that explains the mixture of attraction and repulsion between them (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §2, from Martin).
- Fire with the other elements (Substrate `A_Elements.md` §6):
  - Earth smothers fire, yet fire needs it as fuel. Fire hates being metered; earth hates fire's demand to be exempt from rules and chores (from Martin, Tierney and Silva).
  - Air feeds fire, but can also kill a fire idea with a single fact. Fire's answer is to leave, not to argue (from Martin and Silva).
  - Water douses fire, and fire dries water out. Fire has little respect for water's worry about the future (from Martin, Tierney and Silva).
  - Fire with fire: instant recognition, and a rivalry over who leads, with nothing to stop it (from Silva).

**Modality: cardinal** (with Cancer, Libra and Capricorn).
- Cardinal signs open each season (Snodgrass) and take direct action with little hesitation or planning (Tierney). Martin calls them the initiators, Emerson the Initiators who get the energy flowing (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1 and §2).
- Cardinal means initiating, not necessarily loud (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §2, from Martin).
- In Martin's parable of the boulder blocking a road, the cardinal traveler climbs straight over it (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1, from Martin).
- Martin's law of three: Aries, the primal force, meets equal resistance in Taurus, and the deadlock is resolved by Gemini, which mediates without taking sides (Substrate `B_Modalities.md` §1; `C_Polarity.md` §4, from Martin).

**Polarity: active** (positive, yang; the corpus's terms). Outward-directed. Martin describes it as motion from a center outward, forceful and impulsive, that creates and destroys, and links it to Jung's extraversion (Substrate `C_Polarity.md` §2, from Martin; `01_Aries.md` §10).

**The opposite sign: Libra, and the axis of self against other.**
- Martin names the axis as the struggle to balance autonomy against compromise, self-assertion against cooperation, and "respect for the self and respect for the other" (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Martin).
- Her image for the axis is the statue of Justice on the Old Bailey, with a sword in one hand and scales in the other; both are needed (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Martin).
- In house terms it is the first house against the seventh; in planetary terms Mars against Venus (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8).
- Both signs are cardinal: same urgency, opposite method. Tierney: Libra is an Aries that has learned to charm others into cooperating. Aries fights alone for what it wants; Libra recruits allies to get the same thing (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Tierney).
- The axis is also about accountability. Tierney: with Aries, life lets us make a mistake and start again without fuss; Libra is the sign that teaches the consequences of actions (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Tierney).
- Emerson's picture of the friction: Libra keeps pointing out better ways for Aries to act, and Aries does what it was going to do anyway (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Emerson).
- Projection: Martin says people who identify with the Libra end tend to project the Mars end, their own sense of potency and agency, onto their partners (Substrate `07_Libra.md` §8, from Martin). In general, an opposition tends to be lived by identifying with one end and meeting the other in outside people and events (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- Structurally, opposite signs share modality and polarity and differ only in element. Oppositions therefore read as complementary, and resolve, when they do, into balance rather than victory (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §1).

**Ruling planet: Mars, and the dignities.**
- **Mars rules** (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5). As a faculty it means assertion, initiative and force: standing up for oneself, starting things, being willing to make a claim against someone. It has two modes, instinctive and hot, or trained and disciplined (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3, from Martin).
- **Mars rules two signs, Aries and Scorpio.** In Aries it is loud and obvious; in Scorpio it is subtle and hidden (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Silva's *Sun and Moon Signs*). Martin calls the Scorpio Mars the professional soldier as opposed to the football hooligan, which implies the instinctive form for Aries (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §1, from Martin).
- **Mars is more capable than Aries.** Tierney: Mars fights to the end and means to succeed at any cost, while Aries tends to move on before finishing. The sign falls short of its own planet (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §10, from Tierney).
- **Exaltation: the Sun.** The Sun's faculty is identity and authority that does not depend on others' opinions (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §3 and §5, from Martin). Exaltation means amplified and celebrated, slightly overplayed (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4).
- **Detriment: Venus** (valuing things and relating to others). Martin insists the faculty is not absent but works against the grain: Venus in Aries is powerful because it knows what it wants (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4, from Martin).
- **Fall: Saturn** (limits, patience, time, discipline, deferred reward). Martin calls a planet in fall debilitated (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §4 and §5, from Martin).
- **Across the axis**, Aries and Libra trade the Sun and Saturn: each one's exalted faculty is the other's fallen one (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §5).
- **The rejected co-ruler.** Silva gives Pluto as a modern co-ruler of Aries. This is a minority position, unsupported elsewhere in the corpus, and the GDD does not adopt it (Substrate `F_Rulerships.md` §7).

**Squares (conflict): Cancer and Capricorn.** Both are cardinal, so the friction escalates instead of stalling (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Martin).
- **Cancer: need against self-reliance.** Water is too sensitive for Aries' rough handling. Aries finds Cancer spineless (Tierney). The core of the conflict is that Cancer's central skill, showing need in order to be cared for, is what Aries reads as manipulation. From the other side, Silva warns that Cancer's nurture can spoil Aries into a demanding dependent and then resent it, and the pair sours into jealousy, brooding and silent treatment (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Capricorn: authority and legitimacy.** Tierney says Aries cannot relate to Capricorn's devotion to authority, whether holding it or bowing to it. Aries will not conform to get promoted, often to its own cost, but in return its inner self stays its own. The sources disagree: Silva sees the one relationship where Aries defers, like a knight to a king; Emerson calls it a losing battle (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Tierney, Silva and Emerson).

**Trines (support): Leo and Sagittarius.**
- **Leo.** Leo learns to make containment work, while Aries fights it; Leo can hold a flame that Aries can only spend. The alliance breaks if either tries to outshine or embarrass the other (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §9, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Sagittarius.** Shared independence and blunt humor, and no clinginess. Both have short attention spans, but Sagittarius' comes from appetite for the world and Aries' from disinterest. Sagittarius wishes good fortune on everyone, not just itself (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §9, from Tierney and Emerson).

**Sextiles (working partners): Gemini and Aquarius.**
- **Gemini** watches and learns secondhand while Aries learns the hard way (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §9, from Tierney and Silva).
- **Aquarius.** Neither demands emotional upkeep, and both respect freedom. They split on method: Aries keeps at a problem until it is solved, while Aquarius uses logic or leaves it until a solution appears (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §9).

**Awkward aspects.**
- **Virgo** (quincunx): Aries wants to act, Virgo wants to get it right; Virgo's correction feels to Aries like an attack on its character (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Martin and Silva).
- **Scorpio** (quincunx): the same ruler deployed in opposite ways. Both fear abandonment enough to shut people out first, which the GDD calls the self-protection paradox (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Silva and Tierney).
- **Taurus** (semi-sextile): the fastest sign followed by the slowest; Taurus finishes what it starts and Aries does not (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §8, from Tierney and Martin).
- **Pisces** (semi-sextile): Martin's worked example of adjacent signs that have nothing in common (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §2, from Martin).
- **The full Aries row of the aspect matrix:** opposition Libra; squares Cancer and Capricorn; trines Leo and Sagittarius; sextiles Gemini and Aquarius; quincunxes Virgo and Scorpio; semi-sextiles Pisces and Taurus (Substrate `D_Aspect_Geometry.md` §4).

## 8. Decans

Emerson's sub-rulers, following the fire sequence. The decan material is the weakest in the corpus: two books, a few sentences each (Substrate `E_Decans.md`, header and §5).
1. **First decan: Aries with itself, ruled by Mars.** The most energetic and pioneering of all decans. Endearing innocence; lives fully through adventure or spiritual exploration. The purest form, and the least self-aware (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
2. **Second decan: the Leo blend, ruled by the Sun.** Principled, noble and ambitious under adversity; focused and single-minded, yet still sees the big picture. Its shadow is self-righteousness with some pomposity, and egocentricity, which damage relationships in ways that are not obvious at first. This is heroism that knows it is being watched (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).
3. **Third decan: the Sagittarius blend, ruled by Jupiter.** Enthusiastic, intellectual and individualistic; the last person to become a follower. Its independence goes so far that it harms relationships, since it will never let anyone control it (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §11; `E_Decans.md` §2, from Emerson).

The documentation gives no constellation images or keywords for the Aries decans (Substrate `01_Aries.md` §15).
