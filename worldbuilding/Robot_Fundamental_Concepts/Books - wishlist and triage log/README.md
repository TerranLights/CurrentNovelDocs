# Books: wishlist and triage log

**Two kinds of authority, neither above the other** (author, 2026-10-30):
- **Modern scholarly works** (20th and 21st century) carry weight from experiment, citation, empiricism and observation. They are encouraged.
- **Classic works**, fiction and non-fiction, carry weight because they have conclusively withstood the test of time. Ideas that have proven themselves consistently reliable are worth listening to on their own.

The emphasis on classics means giving them that hearing. It does not rank them above contemporary scholarship. The sections below are grouped by kind, not ranked.

**Where the books go:** the material dump on the Home-Theater drive, `/media/kuroskalacs/Home-Theater/Zodiac-dump/` (author, 2026-10-30). Any filename, any order; no sorting needed.
- **Formats:** EPUB, or PDF with a text layer, is best. Image-only scans work but are slow; Brown's *Human Universals* is one.
- **This folder** holds only this wishlist and the triage log. The books stay off the repo, which is public.

**What happens to them:**
1. **Triage:** which meta-categories and concepts a book feeds, how good the source is, and whether the text is readable. Logged in a `TRIAGE LOG.md` in this folder.
2. **Extraction:** paraphrased, cited notes go into the right `1 - Human understanding (research)/` folders. New concepts go into the concept tables.

**"Free"** below means a public-domain translation exists, for example on Project Gutenberg or the Internet Archive.

## Maps of human concepts as a whole
These help most with the concept index now, and with the general categories later.
- **Mortimer Adler (ed.), *The Great Ideas: A Syntopicon*** (1952). It has 102 essays on ideas such as justice, duty, honor, courage, love and fear, each with references across the Western canon.
- **Peter Mark Roget, *Thesaurus of English Words and Phrases*** (1852 edition), **free**. Roget arranged every concept into one classified scheme.
- **Christopher Peterson & Martin Seligman, *Character Strengths and Virtues*** (2004). A classification of 24 strengths under 6 virtues.
- **Tiffany Watt Smith, *The Book of Human Emotions*** (2015). An A-to-Z of 154 emotions.
- **André Comte-Sponville, *A Small Treatise on the Great Virtues*** (1995). One chapter per virtue.
- **Anna Wierzbicka, *Semantics: Primes and Universals*** (1996). The concepts every human language seems to have.
- **George P. Murdock et al., *Outline of Cultural Materials*.** The anthropologists' catalog of every area of culture. It's **free** online from HRAF (Human Relations Area Files).
- **Steven Pinker, *The Blank Slate*** (2002). Its appendix is Brown's well-known list of universals, which the 1991 book itself doesn't have.
