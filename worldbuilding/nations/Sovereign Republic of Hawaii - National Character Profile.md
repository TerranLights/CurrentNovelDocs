# Sovereign Republic of Hawaii — National Character Profile

2026-09-26, first pass. Confirmed Assembly of Nations member (`history/World-History-Excerpt.md`,
`history/Falkland-Treaty/Falkland_Treaty_Draft_v1.md`) — the only part of Oceania already settled as canon,
per `nations/World-Nations-Roster.md`. Built for two specific narrative reasons the author gave directly: it's
the birthplace of robots (U.R.U.K., where En.Ki.Du was created in 2083) and the home of Doris Morikawa's Hall
of Archives, a pre-war repository of the entirety of human knowledge. **This file does not touch the wider
Pacific/Australia/NZ research in `extractions/Oceania - Coastal Cohesion and the Import Lifeline (survey).md`**
— Hawaii's own canon status predates and sits apart from that broader-Oceania Stage 1 work.

**Two layers, per the template**: the cultural layer from established GDD lore (not Woodard — Hawaii isn't
covered by *American Nations*, so this leans entirely on the existing Doris Morikawa/U.R.U.K. canon instead);
the material layer from real-world research below, extrapolated the same way every other nation's material
position is built in this project.

---

## One-Line Character Summary

> A neutral research haven and the keeper of civilization's memory, built on some of the most import-fragile
> ground in the Pacific — its entire reason for existing depends on supply lines a post-2083 world may not
> have been able to keep open.

## Established Canon (not this file's own research — cross-referenced from existing GDD/Timeline lore)

- **U.R.U.K. (the Underwater Research Union: Kappa)**, where the first sentient robot, En.Ki.Du, was created
  in 2083, sits in what *later became* Hawaiian territory. U.R.U.K. is one instance of a broader pre-war
  naming system — see `history/Research Unions - Pre-War Scientific Institution Naming.md` (added 2026-09-26,
  same session): a domain (Underwater/Terrestrial/Aerial/etc.) plus "Research Union" plus one or more Greek
  letters identifying one specific facility. A second confirmed instance already exists in Hana Jinn's own
  character files: **A.R.U.Ta.G. (Aerial Research Union: Tau Gamma)**, where Hana — originally of U.R.U.K.
  herself — was later relocated after being requested by name for her exceptional ability, to research
  metamaterials for the mass drivers behind Tepenia's early orbital infrastructure.
  **Author-corrected 2026-09-18** (per the Roster): the facility itself predates the war and was under United
  States dominion at the time — the Sovereign Republic of Hawaii didn't exist yet when U.R.U.K. was built or
  when En.Ki.Du was created there. The connection is geographic (where the site now sits), not a claim that
  Hawaii's own post-war government had
  anything to do with the creation of robots. Source: `nations/World-Nations-Roster.md`, Oceania section.
- **Doris Morikawa's Hall of Archives**, in the Sovereign Republic of Hawaii, is described in existing canon
  as "a politically and economically neutral zone" for scientific research. Doris Morikawa (a gynoid, Social
  2w3, Head Librarian) was responsible for cataloging and preserving the entirety of pre-war human knowledge,
  maintained specifically against the possibility of a global catastrophe. Source:
  `reference/Worldspace/Characters/Dolls/Past_History_-_Known_to_Tepenians/Doris Morikawa/`.
- **Both of these are genuinely, deliberately open questions in existing canon, not gaps this file should
  close on its own initiative**: what happened to Doris and the Hall of Archives during the wars; whether any
  archive contents survived and reached Tepenia; whether Hawaii's neutrality protected it, made it a target,
  or turned out irrelevant to the violence. `Writers-Room-Hooks.md` flags the Hall of Archives by name as one
  of the strongest pieces of unclaimed dramatic material in the entire source — preserve that, don't resolve
  it here.

**What this means for this file specifically**: Hawaii's *reason for being neutral scientific ground* predates
the 2083 war and the Republic itself — it's a legacy of the pre-war U.S. territory hosting sensitive research
(U.R.U.K.) and a knowledge-preservation effort (the Hall of Archives) that anticipated catastrophe. The
post-war Sovereign Republic of Hawaii inheriting and formalizing that neutral-ground status, rather than
inventing it from scratch, is the most consistent read of the existing material — flagged as this file's own
reasoned interpretation, not asserted as settled.

## Founding & Settlement History

- **Founding/first-effective-settlement population:** TBD. Real-world Hawaii's actual population is layered
  — Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander (~10% of state population today), a large Asian-American plurality
  (largely Japanese, Filipino and Chinese descent, dating to 19th/20th-century plantation-era labor
  migration), and a substantial military/mainland-transplant population tied to Pearl Harbor. Any of these,
  or some combination, is a legitimate founding population for the post-2083 Republic — not yet decided here.
- **Timing of founding/re-founding:** Presumably concurrent with or shortly after the 2083 war, matching every
  other named Assembly-member successor state.
- **Route/manner of arrival:** N/A in the founding-migration sense (this is existing population, not a new
  arrival) — but worth asking whether the *research/archival* population (U.R.U.K. staff, Hall of Archives
  personnel) forms a distinct founding-elite layer on top of the general population, given how central both
  institutions are to the nation's declared identity.
- **Relationship to the 2083 war:** Open. Given Hawaii's real isolation (the most isolated populated
  archipelago on Earth, roughly 2,400 miles from the nearest continent) and the Oceania survey's own finding
  that direct attack on isolated Pacific territory is plausible to have been limited, Hawaii surviving largely
  intact is a reasonable extrapolation — but this cuts against the dramatic tension `Writers-Room-Hooks.md`
  wants preserved (whether neutrality protected it or made it a target). Left open deliberately.
- **Later demographic change:** TBD.

## Core Cultural Axes

- **Attitude toward authority/hierarchy:** TBD — a "neutral zone" identity plausibly implies either a
  technocratic/institutional government (legitimacy from competence and impartiality, not conquest or
  ideology) or a weak-central-government model that *can't* project the kind of authority that would
  compromise its neutrality. Both are live options.
- **Individualism vs. collective/communal orientation:** TBD. Real Native Hawaiian cultural values (ʻohana,
  mālama ʻāina — family, stewardship of the land) run communal; the research/archival institutions at the
  Republic's heart run more toward an ethic of individual expertise and stewardship-by-competence (echoing
  Doris Morikawa's own Social 2w3 profile). Worth deciding whether these reinforce or sit in tension.
- **Religious/ideological worldview:** TBD.
- **Equality/democratic participation vs. deference to an established elite:** TBD — see the authority axis
  above; a research/archival elite (however meritocratic) is still an elite, and a nation built around
  protecting two globally significant institutions has an obvious temptation to let expertise override
  ordinary democratic input on anything touching them.
- **Characteristic economic base:** See Material Position below — real Hawaii's actual post-plantation
  economy runs on tourism and the U.S. military presence, neither of which obviously survives a global
  collapse intact. This is a real open problem for the nation's post-war economic identity, not just a gap to
  fill in.

- **Surviving pre-catastrophe population present?** **Y** — Native Hawaiians are a real, present, surviving

  Hawaiian ancestry) with real, distinct, still-practiced cultural/land-stewardship traditions
  (ʻohana, mālama ʻāina, the real historical Kingdom of Hawaii's own governance traditions prior to the 1893
  overthrow and 1898 annexation).
- **If yes — own nation, or folded into this one?** TBD — per the method's own preference, a population with
  functional, surviving, place-specific traditions would normally get real weight in the nation's own
  identity rather than being background texture. Given the real-world Kingdom of Hawaii's own sovereignty
  movement is a live, present-day political fact (not just history), this is worth taking seriously when this
  file is developed further — whether the post-war Republic is a continuation/restoration of Native Hawaiian
  self-governance, a separate entity that formed alongside it, or something that deliberately folds the two
  together is a real creative decision, not yet made here.

## Material Position — Surplus, Deficit, and Dependency

**Real-world research, 2026-09-26. This is the sharpest, most consequential section in this file** — Hawaii's
declared identity (neutral scientific haven, keeper of civilization's knowledge) sits in direct tension with
its real-world material fragility, and per the template's own instruction, that tension is not a problem to
resolve, it's the most useful thing this file can say.

### Surplus — what it has more of than it needs

- **Food:** Narrow but real — fresh fruit (60.4% self-sufficiency) and seafood/protein (58.7%) are the two
  categories where Hawaii actually produces close to what it needs locally. Historically a major sugar and
  pineapple exporter (now much diminished as an industry, but the agricultural land and know-how still exist).
- **Energy:** Renewable potential is real and partly built already — ~31% of Hawaii's energy came from
  renewables as of 2023 (solar, wind, biomass, with geothermal and hydro at smaller scale), and the real-world
  state has a legal target of 100% renewable electricity by 2045. Geothermal specifically is a genuine
  domestic asset (the Big Island sits on live volcanic activity) that requires no import chain to keep
  running.
- **Metals and minerals:** None identified this pass.
- **Manufactured goods:** None identified this pass — see Deficit.
- **Other:** Strategic/military infrastructure (Pearl Harbor's naval shipyard and dry docks) is a real,
  substantial physical asset, though whether it functions as a "surplus" post-collapse depends entirely on
  who controls it and what it's used for — flagged here as an asset, not assumed to still serve its original
  purpose.

### Deficit — what it cannot produce at all

- **Food / calories:** **Severe.** 85-90% of food is imported in the real-world present day; refined
  self-sufficiency-ratio measurements put actual local-sourced consumption as low as 11.6%. Fresh vegetables
  sit at only 31.9% self-sufficiency and fresh milk at 12.4% — even the categories that aren't the worst case
  are still import-dependent majorities.
- **Energy / fuel:** **Severe.** Over 80% of all energy used for electricity, surface and air transport comes
  from imported fossil fuels (mostly oil, some coal) in the real-world present — the state currently uses
  roughly 16 times more energy than it produces, even accounting for the renewable buildout above.
- **Metals, cement, industrial inputs:** Not researched this pass — flagged as a gap.
- **Manufactured goods and technology:** Real Hawaii imports the overwhelming majority of manufactured goods
  and technology (not separately quantified this pass, but consistent with the food/fuel pattern and the
  general profile of a small, remote, service-economy island chain).
- **Other:** N/A.

### The Single Point of Failure

> **Shipping.** Every deficit above — food, fuel, manufactured goods — arrives by the same mechanism: ocean
> freight across thousands of miles of open Pacific. A nation whose entire declared purpose is safeguarding
> humanity's knowledge and the site of humanity's first sentient creation is, materially, one of the most
> import-fragile places in this entire project's worldbuilding so far — more so than Australia (food-secure,
> fuel/medicine-fragile — see the Oceania survey) and closer to the acute vulnerability the same survey found
> in the small Pacific Island states, not the continental resilience it found in Australia.

### Physical Dependencies

| Dependency | What it carries | Whose territory |
|---|---|---|
| Trans-Pacific shipping lanes | Food, fuel, manufactured goods — the bulk of Hawaii's material survival | International waters; controlled by whoever can project naval/merchant-marine power across the Pacific post-collapse, not yet determined |
| Pearl Harbor's port/shipyard infrastructure | Receiving capacity for the above; also the Republic's own naval/military asset | Hawaii's own territory |

*(This table is thin because the real research gap is upstream of the dependency itself — this file hasn't
yet established **who** Hawaii would actually be receiving shipments from post-collapse, which is the more
important unresolved question. See Trade Complementarity below.)*

### Trade Complementarity

- **Natural trading partners, and what specifically is exchanged:** TBD — this is the single most important
  open question this file has surfaced. Pre-collapse, Hawaii's supply chain ran overwhelmingly through the
  continental United States; post-2083, whichever North American successor state(s) control West Coast
  shipping capacity (Cascadia is the obvious real-world-geography candidate, per its own coastal Pacific
  position) become Hawaii's most plausible lifeline — but this hasn't been cross-checked against Cascadia's
  own material-base research yet.
- **Who needs this nation more than it needs them:** Plausibly no one, materially — Hawaii's value to others
  is almost entirely non-material (the Hall of Archives, U.R.U.K.'s legacy, Assembly-member neutral-ground
  status), while its material needs are severe and one-directional.
- **Who this nation needs more than they need it:** Whoever controls its shipping lifeline — see above.
- **What it would have to concede to obtain its deficits — territory, transit rights, sovereignty, labor:**
  TBD, but this is exactly the kind of leverage question that could compromise the "politically and
  economically neutral zone" identity the Republic is built around — a nation this dependent on one or two
  outside suppliers for survival calories and fuel has a very hard time also being neutral ground nobody can
  pressure. **This is the profile's own central tension, matching the template's advice to leave it
  unresolved**: the Republic's whole reason for being (neutrality, knowledge-keeping) may not be materially
  sustainable without a patron it can't actually stay neutral from.

### Post-Collapse Trajectory

- **Survives regardless:** Geothermal energy (Big Island volcanism); some fraction of fruit and seafood
  production; Native Hawaiian traditional land/ocean-stewardship knowledge, to whatever extent it survived
  colonization and could be revived at need.
- **Repairable with effort:** Renewable electricity infrastructure (already ~31% built pre-collapse);
  agricultural land currently under-used relative to historical plantation-era capacity.
- **Gone for good:** The tourism economy (dependent on exactly the kind of long-haul global travel a
  post-collapse world plausibly can't sustain at scale); most of the real-world fuel-import infrastructure,
  unless a successor state actively maintains the shipping relationship.
- **Carrying capacity vs. current population:** Not calculated this pass — flagged as a real gap, and an
  important one given the food self-sufficiency numbers above.

### What It Would Fight Over

> Control of, or guaranteed access to, whatever shipping lane and mainland port(s) still connect Hawaii to a
> functioning food/fuel supply — the same Single Point of Failure as above. A plausible flashpoint if more
> than one surviving Pacific-facing power wants to be Hawaii's patron (for the prestige/leverage of hosting a
> neutral, Assembly-recognized nation) rather than leaving it genuinely independent.
