## Why It Went This Way

Recorded because the maps previously carried the question, and anyone reading them later will want to know.

**The case for excluding the southern provinces rested on the author's language map, which shows Yunnan,
Guizhou and Guangxi as a shatter zone of roughly twenty-five distinct peoples. That reading is correct about
territory and wrong about population.**

| | Population | Han | Non-Han |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guangxi | 50,126,804 | **62.5%** | 18,807,577 |
| Yunnan | 47,209,277 | **67.0%** | 15,579,061 |
| Guizhou | 38,562,148 | **62.0%** | 14,653,616 |
| **Total** | **135,898,229** | **63.9%** | 49,040,255 |

**All three are Han-majority.** So the trade was never "a Han federation versus a non-Han one." It was:
**expel 86,857,974 Han in order to avoid absorbing 49,040,255 non-Han — 1.77 Han lost per non-Han avoided.**

**The Zhuang, specifically.** 15,721,956 people, over 90% of them in Guangxi, China's largest minority — a real
nation by any measure. But a Zhuang state in Guangxi would be **31% Zhuang and 62% Han.** Set against Tibet's
TAR at 86% Tibetan or the Tarim at ~95% non-Han, Guangxi is not an ethnic territory in the same sense.

**Gansu and Ningxia were settled earlier and separately**, by the decision that put them in The Northwest
alongside Shaanxi and Qinghai — a state that then unifies into the Federation. **Hainan sits inside Min.**

## Region 10 (Yungui) Is Closed

**It never forms. Do not draw it on any map, in any era.**

Yunnan and Guizhou are Bashu provinces in the early era and Federation provinces after unification.

The arithmetic that closed it: **a Yungui state would have been 64.8% Han** across 85,771,425 people — not a
non-Han polity at all. The survey's "Far West case" therefore has no East Asian instance, which is itself
worth knowing: **East Asia has frontier peoples beyond an ecological boundary, not a terrain-defined interior
that nobody could settle.**
