# China — Ethnolinguistic Map, Read From the Reference Image

2026-09-23. A reading of the language/dialect map the author acquired and had not yet used.

**Reference image:** `Doll-Fi/New World/maps/China - language map GRdUpfkXMAAxR4T.jpeg` — third-party material,
held **outside** the novel repo in the same folder as the original US county ancestry map. Not copied in; this
written extraction is the tracked artifact, matching the convention used for the Google Maps screenshots.

**Why it matters.** The author has stated that the "settling population" in this method is **ethnic identity,
grouped then subdivided**. For North America that role is played by the county ancestry map. **This is its East
Asian equivalent**, and it was sitting unreferenced by any text file in any repo. It supplies, directly and
with evidence, the Han-internal linguistic division the Sinian Federation is to be drawn with.

## The Finding That Settles the Southern-Border Question

**The map is decisive evidence, and it points one way.**

Yunnan, Guizhou and Guangxi — three of the four provinces whose membership in the Han core is currently an
open question — are a **shatter zone of non-Han peoples**, not Han territory with minorities in it. The map
labels, in that area alone: **Zhuang, Bouyei, Dong, Miao, Hmong, Nuosu, Bai, Naxi, Lisu, Lipo, Nasu, Lolopo,
Axi, Nisu, Lahu, Hani, Honi, Biyo, Lu, Bugan, Drung, Tai-Nua, Riang, Parauk, Vo-Wa** — and **Hlai** on Hainan.

**Zhuang is drawn as a large solid block in Guangxi**, comparable in map area to Wu or Gan. It is not a
minority enclave; it is a territory.

That does not decide the question — the author has asked for it to stay open — but it means the **Han-majority
line** option now has direct cartographic evidence behind it, and the **strict Qing Eighteen Provinces** option
requires knowingly placing a two-tier ethnic shatter zone inside a nation defined by Han identity. Both remain
choosable. Only one of them is now cheap.

## Five Consequences for the Map

1. **A Han federation is lopsided, and that is the interesting part.** Mandarin dwarfs the other seven. A
   federation where one member is three times the size of all others combined has a permanent internal
   politics — which is more useful than a tidy balance would be.
2. **Min should probably be one federal member, not five.** Splitting it five ways at this scale would give
   Fujian more representation than the entire north. The map's granularity exceeds what the nations layer can
   carry — the same tension Ribeiro's five Brasis created.
3. **Hakka cannot be drawn as a contiguous territory.** It is dispersed pockets across three provinces. Either
   it is a non-territorial member of the federation — which is genuinely unusual and worth considering — or it
   is absorbed into its neighbors and noted as a loss.
4. **Ethnic Tibet and ethnic Mongolia are both much larger than their administrative namesakes.** If frontier
   polities are drawn on ethnic lines rather than provincial ones, they take territory from Qinghai, Gansu and
   Sichuan. **But the whole-first-order-subdivision rule forbids splitting those provinces** — so this is a
   direct collision, and the provinces will have to go one way or the other entire.
5. **Manchuria is Han, not Manchu.** The map confirms it. Whatever polity forms there is a settler society in
   Woodard's original sense — the one clean case of the Doctrine anywhere in East Asia.

## Independent Convergence With the Frameworks Survey

**This reading and `../extractions/East Asia - Frontiers, Civilizational Spheres and the Han Core (survey).md`
were produced separately and did not see each other.** That survey worked from linguistic scholarship; this
worked from the map alone. They agree, which is worth recording, because independent convergence is the same
evidentiary test this project already applied to Ribeiro/Wagley and to Lattimore/Hu.

| | This map read | The frameworks survey |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of Sinitic groups** | "eight-plus" — Jin, Ping and Huizhou mapped separately from the traditional seven | **ten** — the seven, plus Jin, Huizhou and Pinghua on the *Language Atlas of China* (1987), Li Rong's classification |
| **The first cut inside Han** | Mandarin's mass against everything else | **Jerry Norman's** Northern / Central / Southern zones, with a mutual-intelligibility study independently finding the primary split between Mandarin-and-Jin and all others |
| **Min** | five separately-mapped languages, spanning the strait | an identifiable branch, with Taiwan as its island wing |

**The survey's middle tier is better than the one inferred here**, and supersedes it: Norman's three zones are
a named scholar's classification rather than an impression of relative map area. Use Norman for the tier-2
grouping and this map for the tier-3 territory.

## Caveats

- ⚠️ **This is a language map, not an ethnicity map.** They correlate strongly in China but are not identical —
  the Hui, for instance, are a distinct recognized ethnicity who speak Mandarin and would be invisible here.
  Manchu ethnicity vastly outnumbers Manchu speakers.
- ⚠️ **No date or source is visible on the image**, and the filename is a social-media hash. Treat the
  boundaries as indicative rather than authoritative, and confirm any figure against census data before it is
  treated as settled.
- ⚠️ **Sinitic classification is contested.** Whether Jin, Huizhou and Ping are separate top-level groups or
  subdivisions of Mandarin, Wu and Yue respectively is a live scholarly dispute. This map takes the
  more-divisions view.
- The map shows **language distribution, not population**. A large area may hold few people — which is exactly
  what the Hu Line finding says about the whole western half of the country.
