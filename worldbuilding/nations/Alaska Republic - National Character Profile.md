## Geography & Spread

- **Core territory:** Provisionally the Whitehorse–Yukon corridor and the Alaska railbelt
  (Anchorage–Fairbanks). Not formally settled.
- **Domain:** The Mackenzie valley and Great Slave Lake basin, reached seasonally.
- **Sphere:** Nunavut and the Arctic Archipelago — held in name, reached once a year by sealift.
- **Settlement/expansion mechanism:** TBD, though the real-world pattern is externally-directed: gold rushes,
  wartime highway and pipeline projects, and federally-subsidized resupply.
- **Environmentally-defined?** **Yes, emphatically.** This is the clearest Far-West-type case on the map — a
  territory whose character is set by cold, distance, and permafrost far more than by any settler population's
  values.

## Material Position — Surplus, Deficit, and Dependency

### Surplus — what it has more of than it needs

- **Food:** **Fish, enormously.** The North Pacific is ~62.5% of US commercial landings by volume. Commercial
  salmon alone is roughly **430,000 person-years of protein**; Bristol Bay sockeye is ~175,000 of that and is
  **entirely wild — 0% hatchery-dependent.**
- **Energy:** **Coal** — Usibelli at Healy, ~1 Mt/yr, reserve life rated *"2075+"*, and sitting on both the
  Parks Highway and the Alaska Railroad. **Crude oil** — North Slope via TAPS, currently ~458,570 bbl/d and
  declining from a 1988 peak of 2,032,928. **Hydro** at Yellowknife (~36 MW, Snare + Bluefish); Whitehorse
  ⚠️ unresearched.
- **Metals and minerals:** **Zinc (>80% of US production), lead (~42%), silver (>42%), and germanium — the
  only US producer.** **Placer gold** at Nome, Fairbanks and the Klondike. **Tungsten** at Cantung/Mactung,
  among the few significant non-Chinese sources. **Barite** — 1.1 billion short tons inferred at Anarraaq
  alone. **Mary River iron ore on Baffin Island, 65–70% Fe direct-shipping** — the best natural iron ore there
  is.
- **Manufactured goods:** **Essentially none.**
- **Other:** Enormous territory, and the Northwest Passage.

### Deficit — what it cannot produce at all

- **Food / calories:** **The defining shortfall.** Subsistence harvest provides **176% of protein requirement
  and only 25% of calories.** Agriculture ranks **50th of 50 states** — 745 acres of vegetables statewide,
  about **eleven acres** of protected growing, and a grain harvest equal to roughly **ten days of calories**
  for the territory. Terrestrial food farming is about **$35 per Alaskan per year.** ⚠️ The widely-cited "95%
  of food imported" figure could not be verified, but the arithmetic above makes the same point more strongly.
- **Energy / fuel:** **Gasoline specifically.** Of five refineries totaling 177,000 bbl/d, **only Kenai has
  catalytic reforming and hydrocracking** — the other four are topping plants. Diesel, jet fuel and heating oil
  are fine; gasoline is not.
- **Metals, cement, industrial inputs:** **No iron ore.** Alaska's own state resource table does not list iron
  at all, and none was found in the Yukon. **No verifiable cement plant** — no cement means no concrete. Mary
  River exists but sits at 71°19′N **above the tree line, with no fuel to smelt it on site.**
- **Manufactured goods and technology:** Nearly everything.

### The Single Point of Failure

> **Imported calories.** Protein is solved permanently and abundantly; carbohydrate is not solvable at any
> population this territory currently holds. Everything else — transit, fuel, manufactures — is a hardship.
> Food is an ending.

### Physical Dependencies

| Dependency | What it carries | Whose territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Alaska Highway** — ~630 of 1,387 miles | All overland traffic to the continent | **Cascadia** |
| **Watson Lake–Fort Nelson–Fort Liard corridor** | Whitehorse↔Yellowknife road traffic — ~650 km of a 1,919 km journey | **Cascadia** |
| **The Inside Passage**, Skagway, Haines, Prince Rupert | All protected coastal shipping | **Cascadia** |
| **White Pass & Yukon Route railway** | Whitehorse to tidewater | **Cascadia** (Skagway terminus) |
| **Mackenzie Highway + the Hay River railhead** | All NWT bulk resupply — Hay River is the head of the entire Mackenzie barge system | **Prairie Federation** (Alberta) |
| **Port of Churchill** | Hudson Bay's only port; natural outlet for Kivalliq | **Prairie Federation** (Manitoba) |
| Mackenzie River barge, Hay River→Tuktoyaktuk | Eastern resupply | Internal, but ~120 days a year |
| TAPS → Valdez marine terminal | Crude export | **Internal** — and the one major corridor that is |

**The pattern:** every overland route out of every region of this country crosses into a neighbor. Alaska and
the Yukon exit through Cascadia; the NWT and Nunavut exit through the Prairie Federation.

**The exception, and the opportunity:** the **Canol Trail Highway + Mackenzie River Highway** corridor
(Johnsons Crossing → Macmillan Pass → Norman Wells → Wrigley → Yellowknife) is entirely internal, ≈1,814 km —
**about 105 km shorter than the Cascadian route** — and **675 km of it is unbuilt**, of which 320 km is
already under construction. See `Alaska Republic - The Interior Corridors (Canol and Nahanni Range).md`.

### Trade Complementarity

- **Natural trading partners, and what specifically is exchanged:** **Cascadia, overwhelmingly.** The pairing
  is almost too neat: this Republic has **crude oil and one gasoline refinery**; Cascadia has **~2.0 million
  bbl/d of refining capacity and no domestic crude.** Add fish outbound and calories, refined product and
  manufactures inbound. The Valdez→Cherry Point/Anacortes/Ferndale tanker route already exists and survives
  partition, because Valdez faces the Gulf of Alaska rather than the Inside Passage.
- **Who needs this nation more than it needs them:** Cascadia needs Alaskan **crude** — its refineries are
  stranded without it.
- **Who this nation needs more than they need it:** Cascadia, for **calories, transit, ports and manufactured
  goods.** On balance the Republic is the junior partner, and knows it.
- **What it would have to concede:** transit fees, resource concessions, possibly port or basing rights.
  **TBD** — and this is the substance of Decision 5.

### Post-Collapse Trajectory

- **Survives regardless:** Placer gold at Nome, Fairbanks and the Klondike — recoverable with water, gravity
  and hand tools, and still worked that way today. Wild salmon runs with no hatchery component, above all
  Bristol Bay. Healy coal. Sand, gravel and building stone. The subsistence protein complex.
- **Repairable with effort:** **The Alaska Railroad** — tidewater → Anchorage → Healy coal → Fairbanks →
  Tanana barges, and its original locomotives were coal-fired. The best single reconstruction asset in the
  territory. Also Keno Hill silver (road-accessible, very high grade), Minto copper (idled but **mill intact**,
  under Selkirk First Nation ownership), and Yellowknife's hydro.
- **Gone for good:** **Red Dog** — not a mine so much as a logistics system, with a 52-mile private haul road
  running trucks every fifteen minutes to a shallow-water port that loads seasonally, shipping to smelters
  elsewhere and refining its germanium in Canada. **Fort Knox** — 36,000 t/day of 0.4 g/t ore by heap leach.
  The pollock factory fleet. **The hatchery system** — 34% of commercial salmon, 83% of chum, 84% of the entire
  Prince William Sound catch, on a 2–5 year delay. **TAPS, which fails closed** and becomes 800 miles of scrap.
  Imported seasonal processing labor. The NWT diamond industry, which is closing in reality already.
- **Carrying capacity vs. current population:** Alaska minus the Panhandle is **668,504**. NWT and Nunavut hold
  ~83,000 against an estimated **15,000–35,000** living fully off the land. **The binding constraint is
  distributional, not agricultural: 565,000 of 668,500 live in Anchorage, Mat-Su, Fairbanks and Kenai and
  produce almost none of the wild harvest. A collapse does not starve the Y-K delta. It starves Anchorage.**

### What It Would Fight Over

> **Transit and port access.** Not territory — the Republic has more land than it can use. It would fight over
> the right to *cross* land, and over a harbor it does not have. ⚠️ Provisional; depends on Decision 5 and on
> which of the three transit resolutions is adopted.
