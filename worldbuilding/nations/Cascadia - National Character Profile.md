## Geography & Spread

- **Core territory:** The Lower Mainland and Puget Sound. *(Provisional.)*
- **Domain:** The Willamette Valley, the Central Valley, and the coastal strip south to the Bay Area.
- **Sphere:** The Alaska Panhandle at one end and Baja California Sur at the other — held, but thinly.
- **Settlement/expansion mechanism:** Maritime and coastal. Expansion follows harbors, not frontiers.
- **Environmentally-defined?** **Partially.** Not in the Far West sense of hostile terrain, but the nation's
  *shape* is entirely environmental: it exists along a coast because the mountains behind it define where it
  stops.

**The defining structural fact — "a ladder, not a chain."** Roughly **4,000 km end to end** and rarely more
than a few hundred deep. Three of the four no-substitute chokepoints run **east–west**, not north–south, and
they fall along exactly the Woodard **Left Coast / Far West** fault line — the wet coast separating from the
dry interior. The country's physical fragility and its cultural fracture are the same line.

## Material Position — Surplus, Deficit, and Dependency

### Surplus — what it has more of than it needs

- **Food:** **The single greatest agricultural surplus on the map.** The Central Valley is among the most
  productive farmland on Earth; add Washington and Oregon, the Willamette, and the Okanagan. **This is the
  commodity every neighbor needs and few can match.**
- **Energy:** **Hydroelectricity** — the Columbia system, which is also what made the region's wind buildout
  absorbable. **Refining capacity ~2.0 million bbl/d** (California's 11 refineries at 1,338,171 b/d plus
  Washington's five) — but see the Deficit section: this is capacity, not supply.
- **Metals and minerals:** Modest. Not a mining power.
- **Manufactured goods:** **Aerospace** (Boeing), **shipbuilding and ship repair** (Puget Sound Naval Shipyard's
  six drydocks — Dock 1 cut in 1896 and still in the inventory — plus Vigor and Seaspan), **semiconductors**
  (Intel's Oregon operations, its largest global R&D site), **forestry, pulp and lumber**, and **food
  processing** at Central Valley scale.
- **Other:** **Ports, and this is decisive.** San Pedro Bay moves ~20 million TEU/yr; add Oakland,
  Seattle/Tacoma, Vancouver, and **Prince Rupert — 16 m of natural draft, the third-deepest natural harbor in
  the world.** Cascadia owns essentially every protected deep-water harbor on the west coast of the continent.

### Deficit — what it cannot produce at all

- **Food / calories:** **None.** This nation is food-secure in a way no neighbor is.
- **Energy / fuel:** **CRUDE OIL — the defining shortfall.** ~2.0 million bbl/d of refining capacity, and the
  crude comes from **Alberta and Prudhoe Bay. Both foreign** — Prairie Federation and Alaska Republic
  respectively. Enormous capacity to refine oil it has no way to obtain.
- **Metals, cement, industrial inputs:** Substantially import-dependent. ⚠️ Not quantified.
- **Manufactured goods and technology:** Largely self-sufficient, though semiconductor fabs depend on
  ultrapure water, uninterrupted power, and imported chemicals.
- **Other:** **Water, internally.** California and Baja are severely constrained; the Central Valley runs on
  the State Water Project and the Central Valley Project, and southern California on engineered delivery.

### The Single Point of Failure

> **Crude oil supply.** A refining economy with no domestic crude is one embargo from stopping. *(Internally,
> the parallel failure point is **engineered water** — but that is a collapse problem rather than a trade
> problem, and it cannot be solved by treaty.)*

### Physical Dependencies

| Dependency | What it carries | Whose territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Crude oil supply** | Feedstock for ~2.0M bbl/d of refining | **Alaska Republic** and **Prairie Federation** |
| **The Columbia Gorge** | The only sea-level route through the Cascades — I-84, US-30, SR-14, UP, BNSF and the barge channel in one 80-mile canyon | Internal |
| **Cascade Tunnel (1929)** | East–west rail; limited to **28 trains/day** by two 800-hp fans needing 20–30 min between trains | Internal |
| **Stampede Tunnel** | The alternate — but only **22 ft of clearance, too low for double-stack containers** | Internal |
| **Fraser Canyon / Coquihalla** | BC's east–west link | Internal |
| **State Water Project / CVP** | Central Valley agriculture | Internal, but wholly engineered |

**The measured resilience margin:** in **November 2021**, 200 mm of rain in two days severed Highways 1, 3, 5,
7 and 99 *and* both CN and CP simultaneously, cutting Metro Vancouver off from the rest of Canada for about a
week, with 40 ships at anchor. Full Coquihalla repair took until September 2022 — **with an intact economy and
a functioning government.**

### Trade Complementarity

- **Natural trading partners, and what specifically is exchanged:** **The Alaska Republic, and the fit is
  almost absurd.** Cascadia has the refineries and no crude; the Republic has the crude and only one refinery
  that can make gasoline. Cascadia has calories in surplus; the Republic covers 176% of its protein need and
  25% of its calories. Cascadia has every protected deep-water port; the Republic has none. **Northbound:
  refined product, food, manufactures, port and transit access. Southbound: crude, fish, zinc, lead, silver,
  tungsten.** The Valdez→Cherry Point/Anacortes/Ferndale tanker route already exists.
- **Who needs this nation more than it needs them:** **The Alaska Republic**, on almost every axis — calories,
  transit, ports, manufactures.
- **Who this nation needs more than they need it:** **Whoever holds the crude.** That is a genuine dependency
  and it is the one lever a poorer neighbor holds over a richer one.
- **What it would have to concede:** Transit rights across BC, port access, and refined product on terms.
  **TBD** — and, from the other side, this is the substance of Decision 5.

### Post-Collapse Trajectory

- **Survives regardless:** Central Valley **soil** and coastal climate. **Natural harbors — above all Prince
  Rupert**, which is worth as much with a rowboat as with a gantry crane. Forests. Fisheries. Hydro *sites*,
  as distinct from hydro plants.
- **Repairable with effort:** Hydroelectric dams. **Shipyards** — and the precedent is extraordinary: the
  Kaiser Shipyards built **1,490 vessels, 27% of all US Maritime Commission construction**, at Richmond,
  Portland/Swan Island and Vancouver WA, turning out Liberty ships in about two weeks and one in under five
  days. Those sites still exist, as do PSNS's six drydocks. Rail, with the tunnels as chokepoints.
- **Gone for good:** **Semiconductor fabrication** — needs ultrapure water, uninterrupted power and imported
  chemicals, and none of that survives. Container logistics and just-in-time supply chains. **Dredged
  channels** — the Columbia Bar entrance exists only because of three jetties (1884/1914/1939), continuous
  dredging to 55/48 ft, and 16 bar pilots who often board by helicopter. **Southern California's desert
  cities**, which are artifacts of engineered water. Most of Baja.
- **Carrying capacity vs. current population:** ⚠️ **Not computed.** No single reconciled population total
  across four jurisdictions and two countries was ever derived. **This is the largest quantitative gap on this
  sheet.** The qualitative answer is clear, though: unlike its northern neighbor, Cascadia can feed itself —
  the constraint is water delivery, not calories.

### What It Would Fight Over

> **Crude oil supply, and the Columbia Gorge.** The first because its refining economy dies without it; the
> second because it is the only sea-level route through its own mountains and severing it splits the country
> along its existing cultural fault line. ⚠️ Provisional.

## Scope Note

- **What's deliberately left out:** **The Left Coast / Far West merge.** Woodard treats the coastal ribbon and
  the dry interior as *culturally opposed*, and drawing Cascadia as one solid region merges two of his eleven
  nations. That merge is an authorial choice, and the east–west chokepoint finding suggests the fault line is
  physical as well as cultural. ⚠️ Worth deciding whether it stays merged.
