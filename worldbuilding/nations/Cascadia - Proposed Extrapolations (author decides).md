### (b) Founding, settlement, and the 2083 war

**Founding layers (reasoning from Woodard's method; all but the first three are from the extraction files):**

1. Indigenous nations throughout (Coast Salish, Haida, Tsimshian, Tlingit; Coast Miwok and Southern Pomo around Santa Rosa and Sonoma) (note: Profile; Maps extraction; Graton Rancheria, fetched).
2. Russian fur-trade presence on the Sonoma coast: Fort Ross, 1812 ⚠️M; Gravenstein apples reached Sonoma County in 1811 with Russian trappers (fetched).
3. Spanish and Mexican missions: San Rafael 1817 and San Francisco Solano (Sonoma) 1823 (note: Maps extraction). Under the Doctrine of First Effective Settlement the Santa Rosa area's first effective institutions are Hispanic, and the Yankee Left Coast wave arrives second.
4. British Hudson's Bay Company in BC, not the Yankee pattern (note: Profile and Vancouver file both flag this as an authorial extension).
5. Yankee missionaries and merchants plus Appalachian farmers (Left Coast), then rail and mining capital in the interior (Far West).

**Key finding (⚠️ file gap):** the Cross-Reference says Cascadia merges **two** Woodard nations. The Maps extraction puts El Norte
over Baja California and the San Diego coast, so Cascadia actually merges **three**: Left Coast (Bay Area north, Puget Sound,
BC and Panhandle coast), Far West (interior WA/OR, Central Valley and eastern California, BC interior), El Norte (Baja and
southern California). Recommend recording this as a scope note (Method step 9).

**The 2083 war: Cascadian places by target logic.** This is reasoning, not fact. Open-source Cold War target lists for the Bay
Area remain partly classified (search), so everything below is plausible inference.

| Place | Reasoning | Proposed state |
|---|---|---|
| Bangor/Kitsap, Seattle, Tacoma, Bremerton, Everett | Bangor is the largest concentration of deployed US warheads, about 720, roughly 20 miles from Seattle (search); Naval Station Everett, the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard, Boeing | Destroyed or gutted |
| Victoria/Esquimalt | Naval base ⚠️M | Damaged |
| Vancouver, Prince Rupert | No strategic military mass; harbor and geology survive regardless (note: Industry supplement C.4) | Survived, perhaps damaged |
| Portland and the Willamette | Fewer strategic targets than Puget Sound ⚠️M; port and Columbia Gorge access | Survived, damaged |
| Oakland, Alameda, Richmond/Martinez, Mare Island, Travis | Cold War naval, air and refinery complex (search) | Wounded; **author canon: north San Francisco, the Golden Gate Bridge and San Jose in ruins** |
| Santa Rosa | About 55 miles north of San Francisco ⚠️M | **Author canon: survives**, with pre-war buildings standing (Ch. 8, 10, 20) |
| Los Angeles, Long Beach, San Diego | Largest ports, Navy, aerospace; then the water system fails (note: Material Base 7.4) | Heavily damaged, then hollowed |
| Central Valley, Sierra, Panhandle, Baja | No principal targets ⚠️M | Spared the blast; water and fallout problems |

Fallout drifts east on prevailing westerlies ⚠️M, so the interior (Columbia Basin, Great Basin edge) carries the burden of coastal strikes.

**Options for the relationship to the war:**
- **W1 (PROPOSED):** mixed damage as in the table. Puget Sound's harbor survives (geology), its cities and yards do not.
- **W2:** Cascadia as a lightly struck refuge region. Less plausible given Bangor.
- **W3:** coast gutted, interior spared. Contradicts Santa Rosa and Vancouver.

**Recommendation: W1.** Forecloses a refuge-nation story; enables "rebuilder" as a generational vocation (Alan Benson, b. 2101) and
Gabriel Benson, 10 years old at the war (b. 2073), as living memory in 2149 (note: timeline.md).
⚠️ **Flagged tension:** the material layer treats Puget Sound as the post-war center of gravity and the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard's six
drydocks as "repairable with effort" (note: Profile; Industry supplement C). W1 says the harbor survives but Bangor and Seattle do not. The
author must decide whether the shipyard survived; if not, ship repair shifts to Vancouver/Seaspan, Portland's Swan Island and the
Kaiser sites, and the Bay.

**Later demographic change:** assimilation into founders' norms is the expected default. The rebuilding era plausibly drew
displaced people toward surviving harbors. PROPOSED, not decided.

### (c) The Republic to People's Democratic Republic transition

**Hard constraints (note):** the change happens after 2149 if Streetside's "Cascadia Republic" is canon; after Starlit Engines (date
unpinned, "pre-2318"); and before June 21, 2564. **PROPOSED window: 2318 to 2540.** The lower bound is the safest because it
guarantees both books sit in the Republic era whatever Starlit's final date is. The upper bound leaves a few decades for the
new institutions to be old news by the Treaty.

| Option | Trigger | What changes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 (recommended): "Works Settlement"** | A decades-long public-works crisis: Delta levees, Colorado River intakes, Columbia dams, the Gorge and the passes | Water, power and ports pass into public ownership with elected oversight; the franchise broadens; the state's self-description changes | Follows the material layer's "which old works to keep alive, and who pays." Fits a rebuilding identity |
| T2: "Personhood settlement" | The 2318 Jeju-do ruling forces a rewrite of civil law; "People's" means all legal persons | Property law and the registry system | Elegant, but makes the name robot-centric and sits awkwardly with the 2564 Treaty (which reverses personhood). Offer as a secondary reading only |
| T3: "Yard and harbor union state" | Maritime and shipyard labor consolidates (~2400s) | Labor-based representation | Fits the Kaiser precedent but narrows the nation to its ports |

**Recommendation: T1.** What it forecloses: a purely political-ideology origin. What it enables: a name that describes a
property regime (public works under elected oversight), parallel to New England's shared formula (k). No specific event
is asserted as settled; a later pass may name the arrangement. Consistency check: robot-as-property law survives the change
(robots stay property until 2318 at least), which makes "People's" a pointed irony the author can use or ignore.

### (d) Government type and system

| Option | Description | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **G1 (recommended for the Republic era)** | Thin federal compact among provinces/states; a "fed" with a local office in each major city; most daily life local | Matches Streetside: "the fed" exists but is a clerk, not a presence. Matches the ladder geography |
| G2 | Strong unitary planning state | Overweights the center; contradicts Streetside's distance |
| G3 | Harbor-valley confederation of near-sovereign regions | Fits geography, undercuts a single Assembly seat |

**Recommendation:** G1 in the Republic era, evolving after the Works Settlement into a **works-based republic**: elected
assemblies plus public authorities for water, power and ports, so that the "People's Democratic" label names a real change.
Forecloses a one-party-state reading, which would also be a stereotype; this is a design choice, not a verdict on other
nations that carry the same formula. Consistency check: Streetside's clerk and "fed" require a federal layer that is
present at a county desk but not in daily life.

### (f) Left Coast / Far West merge: holds or splits

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | Holds, bound by a standing east-west bargain: the coast has ports and people; the interior has hydro, grain, coal and the passes |
| B | Holds on paper with an autonomous interior region and its own legislature |
| C | Splits (a post-2564 story possibility only, since the People's Democratic Republic signs the Treaty in 2564) |

**How the chokepoints bear on it (note: Industry supplement B.3.9):** the Columbia Gorge, Fraser Canyon and Coquihalla, the Cascade
passes (Snoqualmie, Stevens, Stampede) and Tehachapi/Cajon are all east-west rungs that cut themselves in weather (November 2021)
and that the interior controls at the eastern end. Hydro flows west: Grand Coulee and the Columbia power the coast, which gives the
interior leverage. Hence a bargain, not a border: guaranteed transit, shared water and power allocations, dredging and bar-pilot
funding paid by the coast. Whole-state borders already keep the Gorge in one nation (note: Territory Definitions).
Forecloses: a secession plot within the book era. Enables: an internal political fault line the novels can leave unspoken.
Consistency check: the Colorado Republic's material base already treats the Columbia Plateau as Cascadia's internal fault (note).

### (i) Language, religion, money, law, and robots as property in 2149

*All PROPOSED, except where noted. This is the legal backdrop of Streetside Harmonies.*

**Language.** English and Spanish with equal-text statutes (California, Baja, Latino communities); Indigenous languages recognized
regionally; French and Chinese-, Punjabi- and other heritage-language communities as ordinary coast demography ⚠️M. Not recommended:
copying the Jeju-do rule that one language's text takes precedence (note: Jeju-do file); keep Cascadia plural.

**Religion.** No state religion; pluralist civil society; war-memorial culture. No community characterized.

**Money.** SETTLED: Cascadian dollar, fiat. PROPOSED origin options: (1) successor to the pre-war dollar via a regional bank; (2) a reconstruction
issue; (3) both in sequence. Recommend (3). Street currency exchanges (money changers) for Colorado and Midwest dollars and Sonoran pesos are SETTLED as a practice by Ch. 2 and Valerie.md; the Streetside notes use a word for them that is the author's personal shorthand, not a setting term, so the in-world name is **OPEN** (PROPOSED: plain "money changer" or "exchange stall" until the author picks one).

**Law.** Common-law base with a civil-law carve-out for Baja; a rebuilt land registry (pre-war deeds lost, claims cleared by filing). This
explains the "acquisition of titleship" and notice-of-acquisition process (Ch. 6, 8) and a government eager to see underused
parcels put to use. A developer files, the occupants are served, the occupants must appear in person.

**Robots as property.** Three options for the registry:

| Option | Description | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 (recommended)** | Bill of sale from the maker; maker-controlled serial registry (Valerie is EG7-KV42); public customs stamp at borders | The state touches robots only at borders and in title or liability disputes; matches "background texture" |
| R2 | Public vehicle-style title registry | Puts the state in every transaction |
| R3 | No registry; customs paper only | Weakest ownership proof; trouble for repair and theft |

- **Ownership:** a purchase is a sale of a serialized chattel (Craig bought Valerie from HyperHedral at 22, spending his whole
  inheritance; robots are rare and costly: a waiter double-takes, a clerk has never heard of siligel).
- **Liability:** the owner is strictly liable for what the robot does, as for a dangerous animal or vehicle. Harm to a robot is property damage
  to the owner: Craig can claim against the fair's operator for the Tesla Coil injury. The maker's repair monopoly (HH tech is "hard to come by") is the real cost.
- **Customs and travel:** humans need passports (Martin has one, Craig does not). Robots cannot hold one; they cross as **freight under a
  declared value and tariff**, travel inactive ("flight mode" as an air-cargo rule is a PROPOSED origin for the existing term), and require an
  owner bond. They cannot ride as accompanied passengers.
- **Resolves the Streetside tension:** "Valerie can't leave Cascadia" is true as a traveler (no passport, no seat). "Has enough to pay her own tariff" is
  true as freight (she can be shipped, for a price, powered down, separated from Craig, which he refuses). The author should confirm
  or replace.
- **Real analogues:** ATA carnets for touring instruments exist as temporary-admission documents (search); live animals cross with health
  certificates and a declaration (search); both are property-in-transit logic, not new inventions.
- **At sea:** aboard an owner's flagged vessel, a robot is ship's equipment rather than crew. This ties to Starlit Engines' ending (living at sea) and to
  Jennifer's fleet job. Marked PROPOSED.
- **Starlit Engines:** the same regime, applied at Skagway/White Pass and on the Alaska Highway; a transit agreement "necessarily includes rules about
  property in transit" (note: Human 1 file).
- **Public debate:** an anti-robot politician on the radio and a pro-rights defector mechanic (Ricardo Alarcón) are SETTLED in Streetside, so the robot question is live in 2149.

## 4. Cross-country consistency flags

### 4.1 What the other dossiers must match

**Alaska Republic:**
- The White Pass and Yukon Route from Skagway to Whitehorse is an international line; the Alaska Highway is about 45% Cascadian (about 630 of 1,387 miles); the Cassiar Highway and Haines Road also cross BC or the Panhandle (note: Transit Problem).
- Juneau, Alaska's real-world capital, is Cascadian here: a plausible grievance.
- The Valdez to Cherry Point/Anacortes/Ferndale tanker lane passes Dixon Entrance and the Strait of Juan de Fuca, both Cascadian. Cascadia holds the lane; Alaska holds the source (note).
- Whichever robot-transit regime (i) is chosen applies on both sides of the Yukon/BC line; Lucy Grayson works the Whitehorse railyard.

**Sonora:**
- The Gulf of California is shared: Baja is the west shore, Sonora the east (note: Sonora Material Base).
- The Colorado delta straddles the line; Morelos Dam is at the Baja California-Arizona line; the Mexicali Valley is Cascadian, San Luis Río Colorado is Sonoran (note).
- Cerro Prieto geothermal and La Rumorosa wind are in Cascadia: a grievance on the Sonoran side (note).
- Baja's grid is tied to the Western Interconnection rather than Mexico's separate system (note).
- Palo Verde is co-owned by entities in three successor nations, including Los Angeles utilities (note).

**Colorado Republic:** the California-Nevada line is the Vegas route's checkpoint (Mojave, I-15 ⚠️M); Lake Tahoe straddles the line ⚠️M; the Far West seam lies inside Washington and Oregon (note).

**Others:** Hawaii's shipping lifeline (Hawaii profile asks to be checked against this file); Prairie Federation tidewater and crude; New England's shared name formula.
