# Alaska Republic — Highway Network, Read From the Map Reference

2026-09-22. A reading of the author-supplied Google Maps reference showing four routes radiating from
**Whitehorse**, the Republic's capital. Purpose: settle, from a map the author chose, which overland
connections between the Republic's cities actually exist and which cross into foreign territory.

**Reference image:** `reference/web Screenshot - Google Maps highway maps - Whitehorse Yukon - north to Inuvik
- east to Yellowknife - west to Anchorage - south to Kitwanga 2026-09-22_20-15-54.png`
**Status:** official reference for the Alaska Republic. The image is third-party copyrighted material (Google

**Stated total for all four legs combined: 97 hours driving, 8,470 km.**

## The Headline Finding

**Of the four cardinal directions from the capital, only two stay inside the country.**

| Leg | Route | Foreign transit? |
|---|---|---|
| **North — Whitehorse → Inuvik** | Klondike Hwy → Dempster Hwy | **No. Fully internal** (Yukon → NWT) |
| **West — Whitehorse → Anchorage** | Alaska Hwy → Glenn Hwy | **No. Fully internal** (Yukon → Alaska) |
| **East — Whitehorse → Yellowknife** | Alaska Hwy → Liard Hwy → NWT 1 → NWT 3 | **YES — crosses into British Columbia and back** |
| **South — Whitehorse → Kitwanga** | Alaska Hwy → Cassiar Hwy 37 | **YES — almost entirely Cascadian** |

The Republic can reach its north and its west overland without leaving home. **It cannot reach its own eastern
city, and it cannot reach tidewater, without transiting Cascadia.**

---

## Leg 1 — North to Inuvik (internal)

Whitehorse → **Klondike Highway** north → **Dawson City** → the **Dempster Highway** branches east → Eagle
Plains → Fort McPherson → Tsiigehtchic → **Inuvik**, with the Inuvik–Tuktoyaktuk Highway continuing to the
Arctic coast.

Crosses the Yukon/NWT boundary, which is internal in this setting. This confirms the earlier research
finding: **the Dempster is the one highway that links two of the Republic's own territories directly.**

Caveat carried from `material-base/Alaska Republic - Northwest Territories and Nunavut (supplement).md`: the
Dempster uses **ferries in summer and ice bridges in winter** at the Peel and Mackenzie crossings, so it has
**shoulder seasons at freeze-up and break-up during which it does not operate at all.**

## Leg 2 — West to Anchorage (internal)

Whitehorse → **Alaska Highway** west → Haines Junction → Burwash Landing → Beaver Creek → **Alcan Border** →
Northway → **Tok** → then the **Glenn Highway** via Glennallen → Palmer → **Anchorage**.

Crosses the Yukon/Alaska boundary, internal here. A branch north from Tok toward Delta Junction and Fairbanks
is visible on the map though not part of the traced route.

## Leg 3 — East to Yellowknife (**crosses Cascadia twice**)

This is the finding that matters, and the map settles it unambiguously. Traced east from Whitehorse:

1. Alaska Highway southeast → Teslin → Rancheria → **Watson Lake** (Yukon).
2. The route then **crosses the 60th parallel south into British Columbia** and runs the Northern Rockies
   stretch of the Alaska Highway: **Coal River → Liard River → Muncho Lake → Toad River**, toward **Fort
   Nelson, BC**.
3. From near Fort Nelson it turns **north on the Liard Highway** (BC Highway 77 becoming NWT Highway 7),
   **re-crossing the 60th parallel** into the Northwest Territories at **Fort Liard**.
4. North past **Nahanni Butte** to the **Fort Simpson** area, then east on **NWT Highway 1** via **Jean Marie
   River → Fort Providence → Kakisa**.
5. Northeast on **NWT Highway 3** via **Behchokǫ̀** to **Yellowknife**, on the north arm of Great Slave Lake.

**In-world consequence:** the only all-season road between the Alaska Republic's capital and its principal
eastern city **runs through Cascadian territory for several hundred kilometers.** The two halves of the
country are joined, on the ground, by a neighbor's highway.

**This corrects an earlier hypothesis.** Before this map, the working theory was that an internal
Whitehorse–Yellowknife route might exist by going north — down the Mackenzie by barge to Inuvik, then back
south on the Dempster. The map shows the actual road network does not work that way: **the Mackenzie Valley
Highway between Wrigley and Inuvik does not exist**, so there is no road closing that loop. The northern
"internal" route would require the river in summer and the Mackenzie Valley winter road in winter — not a
highway at all.

Note also a nuance the earlier research left ambiguous: **NWT Highway 1 does run east–west between Fort
Simpson and Fort Providence.** The "Mackenzie Highway ends at Wrigley" statement refers to the *northward*
spur beyond Fort Simpson, not to the east–west link, which is intact.

## Leg 4 — South to Kitwanga (**Cascadian almost immediately**)

Whitehorse → Alaska Highway southeast to the **Cassiar Highway junction just west of Watson Lake** → **BC
Highway 37 south** → Dease Lake → Iskut → south toward **Kitwanga**, where it meets Highway 16 (the
Yellowhead).

**This is the route in *Alaska Rescue*.** The map confirms the junction geometry: the Cassiar turnoff is on
the Alaska Highway *west* of Watson Lake, so a traveler heading south from Whitehorse leaves the Yukon — and
the Alaska Republic — very early in the journey, and spends nearly the whole trip inside Cascadia.

## What This Settles, and What It Doesn't

**Settled by this map:**
- Whitehorse → Yellowknife requires foreign transit. There is no internal all-season alternative.
- Whitehorse → Inuvik and Whitehorse → Anchorage are internal.
- The Cassiar junction sits west of Watson Lake.
- NWT Highway 1 connects Fort Simpson to Fort Providence.
- No road exists up the Mackenzie Valley between Wrigley and Inuvik.

**Still open:**
- ⚠️ **Precise leg distances.** The map gives only a combined total (8,470 km / 97 hr) for all four legs.
  The individual figures — including the two unverified legs of the *Alaska Rescue* route (Whitehorse–Watson
  Lake and Terrace–Prince Rupert) — are still unresolved.
- ⚠️ **Seasonal closures and road surface** are not shown. Much of the Cassiar and the Dempster is gravel, and
  the Dempster's ferry/ice-bridge shoulder seasons are documented elsewhere, not here.
- ⚠️ Whether any **winter road** makes an internal Whitehorse–Yellowknife connection possible. The Mackenzie
  Valley winter road exists; whether it links westward to the Yukon is unconfirmed.

## Why This Matters to the Setting

The capital was settled at Whitehorse partly because it is the hinge between the Republic's halves. This map
sharpens what that means: **Whitehorse is the hinge, but the hinge is broken on the eastern side.** The
country's western two-thirds — Alaska, the Yukon, and the Mackenzie delta by way of the Dempster — hold
together on internal roads. Its eastern territory does not connect to any of that without a neighbor's
permission.

Which gives the Alaska Republic a permanent structural problem to build stories on: **it is a nation whose
capital can drive to the Arctic Ocean more easily than to its own second city.**
