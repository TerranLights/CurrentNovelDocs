## 2. Madhyama: the working structure 🟡

**One country, working name Madhyama, of nine units:** six core units and three Autonomous Zones. (Proposal B, revised; drawn in
`map-13-proposal-B-revised-three-autonomous-zones.png`.) The author strongly prefers it ("I really, really like the idea of the one single
country with two Autonomous Zones"; Arunachal then added as a third zone).

- **Totals:** six core units ≈ 48.1 M people on ≈ 153,155 km²; the three zones ≈ 6.22 M people, i.e. about 11.5% of the nine-unit total of ≈ 54.3 M.
- **Geometry (computed):** the six core units form one contiguous territory. Assam touches every other unit except Sikkim; Sikkim touches North Bengal
  only. Nagaland touches Assam, Arunachal and Manipur; Manipur touches Assam, Mizoram and Nagaland; Arunachal touches Assam, Nagaland, Bhutan and China.
  The Siliguri Corridor (26 km at Nepal-Bangladesh) and the Dooars ribbon (43 km at Bhutan-Bangladesh) both fall inside Madhyama.
- **Ruled out on the data:** the four-way union of Sikkim, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram (non-contiguous: only Tripura and Mizoram touch).
- **Not decided by this structure:** the internal arrangements of Assam (Bodoland, Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao, the Barak Valley, the citizenship register), the
  capital, and how the core units are governed among themselves. ⚠️

## 3. The name 🟡

**Madhyama** — the author's choice as "the cleanest and most fitting." Sanskrit for "middle"; the Buddhist Middle Way (Madhyama Pratipada, another name for the
Noble Eightfold Path, which also answers the author's sense of an eight-fold link). It does not lean hard into Buddhism at the expense of the region's
Christian and Muslim populations. It also fits the design (a middle way between independence and a unitary state) and the position (between India,
Tibet, Myanmar and Bangladesh). Cautions from the discussion: avoid "Madhyamika" (the West Bengal secondary-school exam name; from memory) and
"Madhyadesha" (the ancient Gangetic heartland; reads as a claim on the rest of India). The name is Sanskrit-derived, natural for Assamese, Bengali and Nepali
speakers; native-language names for the zones could balance it later. The final form (Madhyama, Madhyamaka, Madhyama-bhumi, Madhyama Rajya, …) is not fixed.

## 3b. The capital ✅ Guwahati (author, 2026-09-29)

**Settled: Guwahati (in Assam) is Madhyama's capital.** The author accepted the one caution below, that a capital in Assam reinforces Assam's weight. The reasoning and the
other candidates that were weighed are kept for the record.

The author asked for suggestions (2026-09-29). Geometry computed from the map data: the union's geometric center is about 93.1°E 26.35°N (near Nagaon, in Assam);
its population-weighted center is about 92.2°E 26.1°N (near Guwahati, about 43 km away). Candidates and distances (km): **Guwahati** (135 from the geometric center,
43 from the population center, 109 from Bangladesh, 75 from Bhutan) — the natural hub and leading candidate; **Shillong** (148 / 64 / 44 / 140) — the historic capital of
undivided Assam (background knowledge) and a hill city in Meghalaya rather than Assam, so a neutral-leaning option; **Nagaon** (40 / 58 / 144 / 82) — nearest to the exact
middle, a possible site for a planned capital; **Siliguri** (458 / 374 / 10 / 50) — the chokepoint hub, far too exposed and far west to be a capital; **Agartala** and
**Aizawl** — peripheral; **Kohima, Imphal, Itanagar** — natural seats of the three Autonomous Zones, not of the union. Options: (a) Guwahati; (b) a planned capital near the center
(compare the project's Nusantara); (c) a split capital, Guwahati for the executive and Shillong for the legislature. Cautions: a capital in Assam reinforces Assam's 65% weight in the
six core units; Guwahati's flood and earthquake exposure is background knowledge, unverified here.

## 4. The Autonomous Zones 🟡

- Nagaland, Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh are Autonomous Zones inside Madhyama (the project already uses Autonomous Zones for Araucanía-Patagonia, Aceh and Sarawak).
- **Each zone has a standing military** (author's direction). Size, equipment and structure are ⏳ undecided.
- **The zone bargain (author's direction):** the zone peoples pay **extended military service** (about twice the length of the rest of the nation's, plus extra
  responsibilities such as training the rest of the country) in place of taxes, and are otherwise free to run their own societies with minimal government
  interference. **The rest of the country's taxes pay for what the zone peoples would otherwise have paid taxes for.**
- **Disputed territory 🟡:** Manipur's five Naga-core districts (Senapati, Ukhrul, Kamjong, Tamenglong, Noney; 610,053 people in 2011) are proposed as "disputed"
  between the Manipur and Nagaland zones. The mixed Manipur districts (Kangpokpi, Chandel, Tengnoupal, Jiribam) and Arunachal's Naga-inhabited districts
  (Tirap, Changlang, Longding) are ⚠️ not yet assigned a status.

## 5. Deferred to a later session ⏳

The author has said they know little about military life, so **the whole internal military structure is deferred.** Questions to come back to:

1. **One union army, or three zone armies?** (The bargain reads like one union force drawing heavily on the zones; the author has also said each zone has its own military.)
2. **Universal service or quotas?** Rough arithmetic (⚠️ illustrative, assumed age structure): universal service with a doubled term would put on the order of
   150,000-200,000 zone residents in uniform at once, about 3% of the zones' population, against about 0.27% for Pakistan's active army. A quota or a
   larger zone share of the union army may be needed.
3. **Length and content of service:** the base term, the doubled term, the "extra responsibilities" (training, border duty, disaster response, anything else).
4. **Funding formula:** how the union's taxes pay for the zones' services, and whether the formula is constitutionally fixed (compare Article 371A's protections) so that neither side can use funding or arms as a bargaining chip.
5. **Civilian control:** how an army drawn heavily from the zones stays under civilian authority in a union whose civil core is Assam-centered (Assam is 65% of the core). The Pakistan survey's Frontier Corps (local soldiers under outside officers) is a counter-model.
6. **Folding existing armed factions into zone forces:** the NSCN groups (IM, K, Khango-Konyak, Unification), the Meitei groups (Arambai Tenggol and others), the Kuki-Zo groups.
7. **Zone powers beyond the military:** defense command, police, courts, land, customary law, the Myanmar and China borders, border trafficking (the surveys document arms and drug flows through the Myanmar border).
8. **Arunachal's terms:** whether it receives the same deal (it is the China frontier).
9. **Manipur inside one zone:** how a zone with a 57% Meitei valley and Naga and Kuki-Zo hills settles its own conflict, and whether the extended service applies to the Meitei valley.
10. **The disputed districts:** how the "disputed" status works in practice, and what happens to the mixed districts.
11. **Research to do before deciding:** precedents for a service-for-autonomy bargain (Habsburg Military Frontier, Cossack hosts, Gurkha recruitment, Swiss cantonal militias, Pakistan's Frontier Corps, Aceh's integration of GAM fighters, Israeli conscription); the Northeast's current dependence on central transfers (⚠️ background knowledge, unsourced); the current size and composition of the armed groups.

## 6. Other open items in South Asia, updated for Madhyama ⚠️

- **Teesta:** the river's whole Indian course now lies inside Madhyama, so Teesta water sharing becomes a Madhyama-Bangladesh question.
- **China's claims:** the "South Tibet" claim on Arunachal now falls on the Arunachal zone. Whether a separate Tibet inherits the PRC's claims on Bhutan and Arunachal is still open.
