# South Caucasus — Material and Economic Base

2026-09-28. **Survey only.** What **Georgia, Azerbaijan and Armenia** physically hold, and what survives a
collapse.

**Companion:** `../../extractions/South Caucasus - Three Churches, Two Pipelines and a Closed Border
(survey).md` — this file does not relitigate that survey's ethnic/political/territorial analysis (Nagorno-

## 0. The organizing fact — a land bridge one country wide

Every regional material survey in this project organizes around one physical fact: East Asia's Great Wall
line, the Mekong, the Strait of Malacca, Central Asia's closed Aral basin, the Middle East's two master
resources running in opposite directions. **The South Caucasus's is a land bridge exactly one country wide.**

Azerbaijan holds real hydrocarbon wealth (§1) but, like Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan in the companion Central
Asia survey, is functionally landlocked on the Caspian — its oil, gas and rail freight all have to cross
someone else's territory to reach open water or a rail network that connects to Europe. **That someone else
is always Georgia.** The Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan pipeline, the Baku-Tbilisi-Kars railway and the Southern Gas
Corridor all run the same route: Azerbaijan → Georgia → Turkey. Georgia holds almost none of the region's
hydrocarbon wealth itself, but **its entire strategic and economic weight in this region comes from sitting
in the one place Azerbaijan's exports have to cross.** Unlike Central Asia's still-nascent Middle Corridor
(companion survey §4), this corridor is fully built and has been operating at scale for two decades.

## 1. Azerbaijan — oil, gas, and one increasingly contested pipeline route

### 1.1 The Contract of the Century and the ACG field

The **September 1994 "Contract of the Century"** ($7.4 billion, 11 international companies from seven
countries) opened the Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli (ACG) offshore field complex to foreign development — the deal
that built modern Azerbaijan's oil economy from nothing. **ACG alone accounts for roughly 75% of Azerbaijan's
current oil production.** Combined, ACG and Shah Deniz (§1.2) have produced **~670.3 million tons of oil/
condensate and ~508.6 billion cubic meters of gas** since coming online through 1 January 2026; by 1 September
2026, ACG specifically had produced **over 628 million tons of oil and 253 billion cubic meters of gas.**

### 1.2 Shah Deniz and the Southern Gas Corridor

**Shah Deniz**, discovered 1999, is one of the world's largest gas/condensate fields. Its second-stage
expansion feeds the **Southern Gas Corridor**, a ~$40 billion pipeline system carrying Azerbaijani gas across
Georgia and Turkey to European markets — the gas equivalent of the BTC oil pipeline (§0), and the reason
Azerbaijan matters to European energy security specifically, not just regional politics.

### 1.3 The export chokepoint, and a new one being built to relieve it

**Everything above moves overland through exactly one corridor** (§0): BTC (oil), the Southern Gas Corridor
(gas), and BTK (rail). **A country's entire hydrocarbon export economy running through one neighboring
state's territory is the same structural shape as Kazakhstan's CPC-pipeline dependency on Russia** (companion
Central Asia material survey §2.1) — except Georgia is a willing, economically-aligned transit partner, not a
geopolitical rival, which materially changes the risk profile even though the physical dependency shape is
identical.

**The Zangezur Corridor/TRIPP** (frameworks survey §3) is a second, distinct transit project — not about
Azerbaijan's main exports, but about physically connecting mainland Azerbaijan to its own exclave, Nakhchivan,
via a planned corridor through Armenia. Construction of an associated 330 kV, ~1,000 MW energy transmission
line began January 2026. **This is infrastructure-in-progress, not yet operational**, and remains politically
contested (Iran and Russia both have live objections) — treat as a real, current construction project with an
unresolved final legal/political shape, the same caution the frameworks survey applies to it.

### 1.4 Population, GDP, first-order administrative structure

**Population**: ~10.15 million (2023-sourced figure, the most confident available this pass — treat as
approximate for 2026). **Nominal GDP (2026)**: $78.37 billion — the largest of the three South Caucasus
economies by a wide margin, driven almost entirely by hydrocarbons. **GDP per capita**: $7,467 — the lowest of
the three, despite the largest total economy, reflecting a population roughly 3x Georgia's or Armenia's own.

**Administrative structure, a genuine complexity flagged for Stage 3**: Azerbaijan's actual first-order
administrative units are **64 rayons (districts) plus 11 cities of republican significance** — a scale
comparable to Turkey's 81 provinces in the Middle East package. **This survey's own judgment call, flagged
rather than silently applied**: given the severe scale mismatch this would create against Georgia's ~12 units
and Armenia's ~11 (Azerbaijan alone would out-number both combined by a wide margin, the same shape of
imbalance Turkey's 81 provinces already created inside the Middle East package), Stage 3's `subdivisions.csv`
uses Azerbaijan's **14 economic regions** instead (Absheron-Khizi, Baku, Central Aran, East Zangezur,
Ganja-Dashkasan, Gazakh-Tovuz, Guba-Khachmaz, Karabakh, Lankaran-Astara, Mil-Mughan, Mountainous Shirvan,
Nakhchivan, Shaki-Zagatala, Shirvan-Salyan) — a real, current, presidentially-decreed (2021) administrative
categorization, but **not Azerbaijan's most granular administrative tier.** Flagged explicitly: if the author
wants rayon-level precision for a future border decision, that is a real, available, more time-consuming
research pass this one did not attempt. **East Zangezur and Karabakh are themselves the 2021 reorganization's
direct response to the post-2020-war recovered territories** — i.e., two of the fourteen economic regions
already encode the Nagorno-Karabakh resolution (frameworks survey §2.1) into Azerbaijan's own current
administrative map, not a name this survey invented.

## 2. Georgia — the corridor state, and a genuinely different economy from its neighbor

Georgia holds essentially none of the hydrocarbon wealth that defines Azerbaijan, and its material importance
to this region is almost entirely **positional** (§0) rather than resource-based. **Nominal GDP (2026):
$42.72 billion — GDP per capita $11,574, the highest of the three South Caucasus states** despite the smallest
total economy, reflecting both a smaller population (~3.94 million) and a more diversified, less
resource-concentrated economy than Azerbaijan's.

**First-order administrative structure**: nine ordinary regions (mkhare) — Guria, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo
Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Shida
Kartli — plus **two constitutionally-designated Autonomous Republics** (Abkhazia, formally retained on paper
despite being outside actual Tbilisi control since the 1990s war; Adjara, fully functioning and uncontested,
frameworks survey §2.3) plus the capital **Tbilisi**, held separately with special city status. **Twelve
first-order units in total**, one of the smallest counts of any country this project has mapped, a real
contrast with Azerbaijan's own administrative complexity next door.

**⚠️ No major mineral, agricultural-export, or manufacturing asset comparable to Azerbaijan's hydrocarbons or
Kazakhstan's uranium/oil was found for Georgia this pass** — its material position in this region really is
almost entirely the transit-corridor fact in §0. Flagged as a genuine research gap, not a settled "Georgia has
nothing" conclusion — Georgia's wine industry, hydropower potential, and Black Sea ports (Batumi, Poti) were
not independently researched this pass and are a natural next step.

## 3. Armenia — landlocked twice over, and materially isolated by its own closed borders

**Nominal GDP (2026): $31.873 billion. GDP per capita: $10,410. Population: ~3.06 million** — the smallest of
the three South Caucasus economies and the smallest population, though not dramatically smaller than
Georgia's on a per-capita basis.

**First-order administrative structure**: **ten marzer (provinces)** — Aragatsotn, Ararat, Armavir,
Gegharkunik, Kotayk, Lori, Shirak, Syunik, Tavush, Vayots Dzor — **plus Yerevan**, held separately with special
capital status, for **eleven units total.** Yerevan alone holds roughly a third of the entire country's
population (1,086,677 of ~3.06 million per the 2022 census, since grown to an estimated 1.14 million by early
2025) — a real primate-city concentration, though this pass did not compare it quantitatively against other
regions in this project's scope.

**⚠️ The frameworks survey's own §6 finding is the single most load-bearing material fact for Armenia**:
its fully open land borders run only to Georgia and Iran. **Its borders with Turkey (closed since 1993) and
Azerbaijan (closed by the unresolved broader conflict) are both non-functional.** This is not merely a
diplomatic inconvenience — it is a real, structural trade and connectivity constraint that shapes Armenia's
material position more than any single resource finding could. **No major mineral or hydrocarbon asset
comparable to Azerbaijan's was found for Armenia this pass either** — flagged as a genuine research gap, the
same as Georgia's.

**Syunik province** carries particular material weight beyond its ordinary administrative role: it is both
the physical route the proposed Zangezur Corridor would cross (§1.3) and Armenia's own land connection to
Iran, its one fully open southern border — **the same province is simultaneously Armenia's most valuable
external connection and the site of its most contested infrastructure question.**

## 4. Post-collapse assessment

Using the project's three-tier framework: **survives regardless / repairable with effort / gone for good.**

### Survives regardless

- **Azerbaijan's oil and gas reserves themselves** — the resource in the ground doesn't disappear; only the
  export infrastructure and customer relationships are fragile (below).
- **Georgia's positional value as a corridor** — geography doesn't collapse. Whatever political entities exist
  on either side of Georgia, a land bridge between the Caspian and the Black Sea/Turkey remains geographically
  necessary the same way the Strait of Malacca's chokepoint value survives any single political arrangement.
- **Armenia's and Georgia's ancient religious/cultural institutions** (frameworks survey §1) — the Armenian
  Apostolic and Georgian Orthodox Churches have survived Persian, Arab, Ottoman, and Russian/Soviet rule
  already; there is no reason a future collapse specifically threatens what centuries of prior conquest did
  not.

### Repairable with effort

- **The BTC/BTK/Southern Gas Corridor infrastructure itself** — physical pipelines and rail lines don't
  vanish in a collapse; restarting them requires functioning states at both ends and the customer relationship
  to reconstitute, the same "concrete doesn't disappear" logic the Central Asia survey applied to the Rogun
  Dam (companion material survey §7).
- **The Zangezur Corridor, at whatever stage of construction a collapse catches it** — currently early-stage
  (one transmission line under construction as of this survey), so less "repairable" than "barely started,"
  but the same category of physical-infrastructure resilience as the corridor's older siblings.

### Gone for good

- **Azerbaijan's single-corridor export economy as currently structured** — if Georgia's own political
  stability or the specific transit relationship fails, Azerbaijan's oil/gas wealth becomes stranded the same
  way Turkmenistan's gas trade depends entirely on an intact pipeline and an intact customer state (companion
  Central Asia survey §2.2).
- **The nascent Zangezur Corridor specifically** — the least-built, most politically contested piece of
  infrastructure in this survey; a collapse arriving before it matures likely ends the project outright rather
  than leaving something repairable.
- **Armenia's recent, fragile border-normalization progress with Turkey** (frameworks survey §6) — three
  decades of closure reversed only very recently and only partially; a collapse scenario plausibly reverts
  this to the closed-border default rather than the opening default.

## 5. What this means for the nations layer

Offered, not asserted, per this project's standing rule.

1. **Azerbaijan and Georgia's relationship is structurally the cleanest positive-sum case in this entire
   project's West Asia work** — a resource-wealthy landlocked state and a resource-poor transit state whose
   interests align rather than conflict, unlike Kazakhstan's fraught dependency on Russian pipeline territory.
   A post-collapse Azerbaijan without Georgian transit access is materially crippled in the same way a
   post-collapse Kazakhstan without CPC access is.
2. **Armenia's material position is defined by isolation, not resource absence** — its closed borders with
   two of its four neighbors are the load-bearing fact, not any mineral or export finding this pass could
   locate. A post-collapse Armenia's material fate depends heavily on whether those borders ever actually
   reopen, more than on anything Armenia itself holds.
3. **The Zangezur Corridor is this region's newest and least-settled infrastructure question** — genuinely
   comparable to the Central Asia survey's own Middle Corridor uncertainty (companion material survey §4),
   except here the stakes are sharper (it runs through the territory of one of the parties to the underlying
   conflict, not through neutral ground).

## 6. Explicit uncertainties and unverified items

1. **⚠️ Georgia's own resource base (wine, hydropower, Black Sea ports) was not researched this pass** — a
   real, named gap, not a "nothing to find" conclusion.
2. **⚠️ Armenia's own resource base beyond the border-isolation finding was not researched this pass** — same
   category of gap as Georgia's.
3. **⚠️ Azerbaijan's Shah Deniz/ACG dollar-revenue figures were not independently converted to a clean annual
   total this pass** — volume and production figures are sourced; a clean current-dollar export-revenue figure
   was not separately verified.
4. **⚠️ Azerbaijan's subdivision-tier judgment call (§1.4)** — economic regions rather than rayons — is this
   file's own tractability decision, not a research finding. Flagged for the author to override if
   rayon-level precision is wanted later.
5. **⚠️ The Zangezur Corridor's ultimate legal/political shape** is explicitly unresolved (frameworks survey
   §3) — this survey treats it as real, under construction, and contested, not as a settled future fact.
