## 2. What a Sonora story needs from the country

Questions the setting must be able to answer, whatever the story is. Written without assuming anything about
*Electric Dust*. **Author's note (2026-10-11): question 1 (water) is for other stories set in the Republic; for *Electric Dust* it is at most a background detail. For an origin story the questions that bear most are likely 2, 4, 6, 9, 10 and 12 (what a person is, language, the local city, archives and laboratories, citizenship, the war's presence); which of them matter is the author's call.**

1. **Who controls the water, and how do you find out?** (A ditch commission, an irrigation district, a federal
   authority, a foreign dam operator: each gives a different street.)
2. **What is a person, what is a thing, and who decides?** (Before 2318 robots are property; the country's
   property law is what a story touching them runs through. See 3(f).)
3. **How do you cross a line, and with what papers?** The four constituent units now share no internal border.
   Its external lines are with Cascadia, the Colorado Republic, the CSA and México.
4. **What language is a contract, a deed, a water title, a warrant in?** *(Answered 2026-10-12: English and Spanish, equally valid, all government documents in both; see 3(f).)*
5. **Where does power come from, and what happens when the grid is two grids?**
6. **What does the nearest city look like, and what is left of the old ones?** (Phoenix, Tucson, Albuquerque,
   Hermosillo, Juárez are the largest ruins-or-survivors in the territory (note: base §8.5).)
7. **Who owns the mines, the mill tailings, the smelters?**
8. **What is the working calendar?** (Monsoon, wheat harvest, winter vegetables, ditch-cleaning season.)
9. **Where is the nearest surviving library, laboratory or archive?** (Sandia and Los Alamos in the north; the
   CIMMYT station near Ciudad Obregón; Tucson's university and archives ⚠️M.)
10. **Who is a citizen, and how do the older nations living inside the borders relate to the state?** *(Franchise answered 2026-10-12: one vote per household, by the net breadwinner; see 3(d)(ii). Citizenship and the Indigenous nations' status remain open.)*
11. **How far is the next town, and how do you get there?** (Rail alignments, I-10/I-19/I-25, Highway 15, the
    ChePe, all of which cross somebody else's land at one end.)
12. **What is the era's relationship to the war?** (Ruins within sight, or a century of forgetting?)

### 3(b) Founding and settlement history

**The problem.** A four-state nation built from two US states and two Mexican states has no single founding moment.
Woodard's Doctrine of First Effective Settlement asks who first built stable, self-perpetuating institutions. The
notes support three candidate answers.

| Option | Founding template | What the notes say |
|---|---|---|
| **A. El Norte layered** | Spanish presidio, mission and ranch settlement: Oñate 1598, Santa Fe 1610, Presidio San Agustín del Tucsón authorized 20 Aug 1775, El Paso del Norte 1659, Hermosillo (Pitic) 1700, Albuquerque 1706 (search). Later Anglo corporate mining (Greene Consolidated at Cananea; Morenci; note: base §1.1) as a Far-West-type overlay. | Cross-Reference rates the fit "strong." Weakness: ignores the irrigators who preceded it. |
| **B. The ditch** | The *irrigation community* as the founding institution: Hohokam/O'odham floodwater and canal farming, Pueblo dryland and ditch farming, Yaqui valley farming "from at least the 6th century AD," Spanish-era acequias (note: base §§2.4, 3.3, 3.6; 600-700 acequias survive in NM, search). Spanish-Mexican and Anglo layers become successors on that base. | Fits the "water is the defining constraint" conclusion; ties the founding to the post-war floor. |
| **C. Two-founder compact** | Modeled on Woodard's New France (a partnership-and-intermarriage founding; note: Extraction): Indigenous and Hispano communities as co-founders, with the **1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (Arts. VIII-IX preserved property and offered citizenship) and the 1853 Gadsden Purchase** guarantees as the model for the union's founding document (search). | Gives the legal merger of 3(d) a historical template. |

**Recommendation (PROPOSED): B + C.** Treat the ditch as the first effective settlement and the
property-guarantee treaties as the founding-document precedent. Reasoning: (1) the method's Part Four, points 1
and 8, say the founder is the group with the first lasting institutions, and the notes show those were
irrigation communities; (2) the material base's whole post-collapse floor (§§3.6, 8.2) is these systems; (3) it
describes peoples by institutions, not by character.
*Forecloses:* a "pure" El Norte reading (option A alone), which would make the Indigenous nations background.
*Enables:* a founding story where the legitimacy of a claim is its water right.
*Later layers (documented):* Anglo mining and railroad capital from the 1850s on; Mennonite settlers near
Cuauhtémoc, Chihuahua from 1922 ("city of three cultures": Mennonite, mestizo and Rarámuri; Plautdietsch is
among the city's recognized languages; search); Mormon colonies in Chihuahua and Arizona ⚠️M. Other communities
(Chinese, Lebanese, Basque and others with documented regional history) are **not** developed here.
*Consistency checks:* METHOD rule 1 (whole units) holds; rule 3 (geographic coherence) is satisfied by the
shared Sonoran and Chihuahuan desert; rule 5 ("name comes from the culture") works because the nation's name is
also one of its four units, which should be explained in-world, not hidden. Cascadia's profile already flags the
same issue of merging two Woodard nations; Sonora merges El Norte with a Far-West-type corporate overlay.

### 3(d) Government type and system

A union of **two US states (common-law, state constitutions)** and **two Mexican states (civil-law, a federal
constitution, waters as national property under Article 27, concessions via CONAGUA; search)**. Water makes the
mismatch concrete: Mexican waters are national property by concession, whereas US western states use prior
appropriation and New Mexico also recognizes centuries-old acequia rights upheld after 1848 (note: base §3.3).
Community-property marriage law in Arizona and New Mexico descends from Spanish/Mexican law (search), so some
private law is already shared.

| Option | Shape |
|---|---|
| **A. Federal republic, bijural** | Four constituent states keep their constitutions and private-law traditions (a mixed-law federation; Louisiana and Quebec are the real precedents ⚠️M); a federal constitution holds uniform public law; a **water authority** sits above irrigation districts and acequia associations. |
| **B. Basin-based unitary republic** | Administrative units follow rivers (Colorado, Rio Grande, Yaqui-Mayo, Conchos). |
| **C. Confederal compact** | Four sovereign-style parties joined by a compact like the river compacts. |

**Recommendation (PROPOSED): A.** Reasoning: it matches "La República" and the four whole-unit map; it respects
Woodard's observation that this culture area repeatedly resisted central rule (note: Extraction), through
institutions rather than temperament; and it has a ready mechanism: an **accession compact** that reproduces the
property guarantees of Guadalupe Hidalgo and the Gadsden Purchase so that all prior titles (land grants, ejidos,
acequia rights, trust lands, Mexican concessions) continue. Union by compact rather than conquest keeps the
Indigenous question open (3(g)).
*Forecloses:* a strong center. *Enables:* a "district of districts" politics (irrigation districts, ditch
commissions, tribal councils, state governments).
*Consistency:* Cascadia carries a "People's Democratic Republic" label with a transformation implied; Sonora has
**no recorded earlier name or transformation** (OPEN). The unit "state" will mean different things on either side
of the old line; the constitution should say so.

### 3(d)(ii) The household vote (author, 2026-10-12)

**Settled (AUTHOR):** the Republic votes **one vote per household, made by the household's net breadwinner.**

**What follows directly (reasoning, not author statements):**
- The unit of political voice is the **household**, not the person. Seats, constituencies and turnout are counted in households.
- The state needs a way to know who a household's **net breadwinner** is (the member contributing the most net income). That implies a household registry or an income-based record; the same registry could carry the robot title registry proposed in 3(f).
- **A household with no earner has no vote at all (author, 2026-10-12).** Retirees without earnings, the unemployed, students and anyone else living in a household with no earner are outside the national franchise; the vote is tied to earning, not to residence.
- Adults in a household who are not its net breadwinner (spouses, adult children, elders, housemates) do **not** cast a national vote.
- **Robots are property**, so a robot is not a voter and not a household member for this purpose; whether income a robot earns counts toward its owner's net earnings is open (below).
- Ballots, like all government documents, are available in English and Spanish (3(f)).

**Still open (PROPOSED answers, author decides):**

| # | Gap | Recommended answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What counts as a household? | People who share a dwelling and pool resources, the same unit the census and tax record use. |
| 2 | How is the net breadwinner decided? | Highest net income over the last tax year; a tie goes to the household's eldest earner. |
| 3 | A household with no earner? | **Settled (author, 2026-10-12): it does not vote.** Open only at the margin: whether a household that earned nothing for one year but earned the year before keeps its vote (what counts as "earner", and over what period). Recommended: judge by the latest tax year, as with the breadwinner. |
| 4 | A communal household such as a tribe of robots and humans living together (the "hippies in the desert")? | Each independent economic unit within it counts as a household; whether a commune counts as one household or several is a **story-relevant question the author may want to decide on purpose**. |
| 5 | Does robot-earned income count toward the owner's net earnings? | Yes if robots can be hired out or employed (it is the owner's income), but this makes who the "breadwinner" is partly a question about the robots; the author decides how much this matters. |
| 6 | Do ditch associations and districts (acequia commissions, irrigation districts) vote by the same household rule, or by water-right holders? | Separate: water-right holders vote in water bodies, households vote nationally. |
| 7 | Is this a federal rule, or do the four constituent states set their own franchise? | Federal rule for national offices; states may keep their own for state offices (bijural, 3(d)). |

*Forecloses:* individual suffrage as a baseline. *Enables:* stories where a person's political voice depends on their household's finances and on who counts as one. No effect is asserted here for any character; the *Electric Dust* household questions (Carlos's household, Estefanía's place in it) are the author's.

### 3(f) Language, religion, money, law, and robots as property

**Language (author, 2026-10-12: settled).** As in Canada (English and French; ⚠️M), the Republic of Sonora is **officially and legally bilingual, in English and Spanish.** The two languages are **equally valid before the law**, and **all government documents must be available in both.**

*What follows (reasoning, not author statements):* statutes, court filings, permits, water titles and deeds exist in two versions; either language may be used in court and before officials (so interpreters are a right, not a favor); the oldest land and water documents are in Spanish, so a translation and archive bureaucracy is part of the state; and Canada's rule that both versions of a statute are equally authoritative (⚠️M) is the natural fit for "equally valid", which leaves a mismatch between versions as a question for the courts.

*Still open (PROPOSED, author decides):* (1) whether **private** contracts and deeds must also be bilingual, or only government documents (recommended: government documents only; private parties may use either language); (2) whether **Indigenous languages** (Diné bizaad, O'odham, Yoeme, Rarámuri, the Pueblo languages, the Apache languages ⚠️M; Plautdietsch locally, search) have any status beyond the two official ones (recommended: recognized regionally, with schooling and interpreter rights, without co-official status).

**Religion.** No state religion (see 3(e)). **Money.** PROPOSED: continuity of the **peso** as the unit, with
copper and silver coinage; the base's "foundry and mint" claim is weaker here because Met-Mex Torreón is out of
scope (Chihuahua silver and Sonora byproduct silver remain). **Law.** 3(d).

**Robots as property (pre-2318) — all PROPOSED, no assumption about any character.**

### 3(g) The Indigenous question
