2026-09-22. A state-by-state / province-by-province reading of
`reference/Reference/Images/Maps/North America with tentative labels.jpg`, done by examining the image at
high magnification. Purpose: give every downstream piece of work — worldbuilding research, nation profiles,
story logistics — one precise territorial reference instead of re-deriving borders from the image each time.

**This is a reading of the map, not a proposal.** Where the map is ambiguous at this resolution it says so.
Where the map diverges from Colin Woodard's *American Nations* (the analytical basis), that divergence is
noted, because several of them are substantial and deliberate-looking.

## Six Findings Worth Knowing

These are the places where the map does something non-obvious, and each has real downstream consequences.

### 1. Cascadia holds the Alaska Panhandle — so it controls the entire Inside Passage

The coastal strip and Alexander Archipelago (Juneau, Sitka, Skagway, Ketchikan) are Cascadian, not Alaskan.
Consequences: Cascadia controls the whole protected coastal shipping route from Puget Sound to the Gulf of
Alaska; and the Alaska Republic is cut off from the Inside Passage entirely, left with the Gulf of Alaska
(Anchorage, Valdez, Seward), the Bering Sea, and the Arctic coast.

### 2. Cascadia is enormous, and very long and thin

Running from the Alaska Panhandle to the tip of Baja California Sur, it is roughly 4,000 km end to end and
rarely more than a few hundred km deep. That gives it extraordinary assets — several world-class ports, the
Central Valley, Columbia River hydro — and one structural problem: north-south cohesion. Everything has to
move along a single coastal axis, and the mountain ranges run the wrong way.

### 3. Midwestland is the plains, not the Ohio Valley

This is the easiest region to get wrong, because the real-world "Midwest" label suggests otherwise. It is the
**trans-Mississippi** block: the Dakotas, Minnesota, Nebraska, Iowa, Kansas, Missouri. **Chicago is not in
it** — Illinois is Appalachian on this map. Its hubs are Kansas City, Omaha, Minneapolis-St. Paul, and
St. Louis, and its character is far more agricultural and far less industrial than the name implies.

### 4. Appalachia is much larger than the mountains it is named for

It takes in the entire industrial Great Lakes belt — Wisconsin, Michigan, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio — alongside
the Appalachian core. That means **Appalachia, not Midwestland, holds most of the continent's historical
manufacturing capacity**: Detroit, Chicago, Cleveland, Pittsburgh-adjacent industry, and the Great Lakes
shipping system.

### 5. Texas is entirely CSA

Despite Woodard's El Norte reaching well into south and west Texas, the map gives the whole state to the CSA.
Sonora stops at the New Mexico line. A consequence: the **Rio Grande becomes an international border between
Sonora and the CSA**, which turns real-world US–Mexico water allocation into an in-world Sonora/CSA dispute.

### 6. The Alaska Republic is the largest region by area and among the smallest by population

Alaska plus the entire Canadian North. Its additions beyond Alaska and the Yukon are substantial: the
Mackenzie River system, Great Bear and Great Slave Lakes, the NWT diamond fields, Baffin Island iron, and the
Northwest Passage.

## Direct Consequence for *Alaska Rescue*

Whitehorse is in the **Yukon**, inside the **Alaska Republic**. Prince Rupert and Vancouver are in **British
Columbia**, inside **Cascadia**. The route the story already follows — Whitehorse south to Prince Rupert —
therefore **crosses an international frontier between two sovereign nations.**

That is not a detail the existing notes account for, and it is load-bearing: both dolls travel this route
during an era in which neither holds legal personhood. Two pieces of property crossing a national border,
accompanied by men who do not own them, is a materially different proposition from an internal journey. It
also gives the Alaska Panhandle a role — the White Pass and Yukon Route railway from Skagway to Whitehorse
would likewise be an international line.

Flagged for the author rather than resolved here.

## Ambiguities at This Resolution

- The **Maryland/Delaware/DC** area reads as Appalachian but sits near a three-way boundary; worth a closer
  look before relying on it.
- The exact **Ontario** split between the Prairie Federation, the DMZ, and Quebec is approximate here.
- Whether Cascadia's **California** claim extends over the whole state or stops at the Sierra crest cannot be
  resolved from this image — the map appears to use whole-state fills, which suggests the whole state.
- The **Alaska Panhandle boundary** is clear in color but its exact inland extent is not.
