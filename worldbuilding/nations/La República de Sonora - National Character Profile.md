  (Cananea, Morenci; note: material base §1.1).
- **Environmentally-defined?** **Likely yes, TBD.** Two deserts set the terms: the Sonoran (west, bi-seasonal
  rain) and the Chihuahuan (east, higher, summer-dominated rain) (note: material base §2.6). Rainfall runs from
  under 100 mm/yr at Yuma to roughly 300-500 mm/yr in the Chihuahuan highlands. Aridity and water allocation
  shape the nation more than any one settler population's values, the Far-West-type pattern in the template.

**Territory (settled):** Arizona and New Mexico (both entire), and the Mexican states of **Sonora** and
**Chihuahua**; nothing else (note: Territory Definitions, correction 2026-09-23; México (post-war) §1). Contiguous.
**Baja California is Cascadian**, so the Gulf of California has Sonora on its eastern shore and Cascadia on its
western; **Texas is the CSA's**, so the upper Rio Grande (El Paso-Juárez to roughly Presidio-Ojinaga) is the
Sonora/CSA frontier. **Colorado River delta** straddles the Sonora/Cascadia line; San Luis Río Colorado is in
Sonora, the Mexicali Valley is Cascadian (note: material base §2.1).

**Neighbors by land and sea (from the notes):** Cascadia (California and Baja California, across the Colorado
River and the Gulf); the Colorado Republic (Nevada, Utah, Colorado, to the north); the CSA (Texas and Oklahoma, to
the east); post-war México (Sinaloa, Durango, Coahuila, to the south and east). ⚠️ See Scope Note on Coahuila.

## Indigenous / Pre-Catastrophe Population

- **Surviving pre-catastrophe population present?** **Yes.** The notes document the Tohono O'odham (ak-chin
  floodwater farming), the Hopi, Zuni and Pueblo dryland farmers, the Navajo (uranium-era contamination, Grants
  belt), Apache historical use of Santa Rita copper, and the Yaqui (Yaqui Valley, water-rights dispute over the
  Independencia aqueduct) (note: material base §§1.1, 1.7, 2.4, 3.6). Federally recognized nations in real
  Arizona number 22 (search); the Navajo Nation covers more than 27,325 square miles across Arizona, Utah and New
  Mexico (search); the Tohono O'odham Nation holds about 2.8 million acres (search).
- **If yes: own nation, or folded into this one?** **TBD, and a significant open decision** (same unmade decision
  as the Alaska Republic's and Cascadia's). Per the method (Extraction, Part Four, point 8), a population never

  own nation. The material base's pre-industrial floor (§3.6) is exactly that kind of knowledge. ⚠️ The Navajo
  Nation also extends into Utah, which is the Colorado Republic's.

## Material Position — Surplus, Deficit, and Dependency

*(Filled from the material base and re-cut for the four-state territory. Where a base conclusion depended on
Coahuila, Nuevo León or Tamaulipas, it is marked OUT OF SCOPE and not counted.)*

### Surplus — what it has more of than it needs

- **Food:** **The Yaqui and Mayo valleys**, about 450,000 ha of alluvium under gravity irrigation (DR 041, DR 038),
  wheat first, with the world's most important arid-irrigated wheat germplasm and breeding station (CIMMYT's
  CENEB near Ciudad Obregón). **Yuma County** supplies about 90% of US leafy vegetables November-March (Colorado
  River water). New Mexico chile and pecans; Chihuahua pecans and apples; extensive cattle rangeland, though at
  tens to well over a hundred acres per animal unit. All (note: base §3). The pre-industrial crop package
  (tepary beans, desert maize, squash, mesquite, agave, cholla) is the floor beneath all of it.
- **Energy:** **Solar** (among the best on Earth; Arizona technical potential 5,147 GW PV per NREL 2012, note:
  base §4.1). **New Mexico oil**: about 805 million barrels in 2025, roughly 2.2 million bbl/d (computed from the
  EIA figure in base §4.2), second to Texas, but see the post-collapse caveat. San Juan Basin gas. **Palo Verde**,
  about 3.3 GW average and 32 TWh/yr, three PWRs. Small gravity hydro: El Novillo 135 MW, La Boquilla 25 MW.
  Coal remains in the ground at San Juan/Four Corners (NM) even though the plants are closing (note: §4.3).
- **Metals and minerals:** **Copper**, world-class on both sides of the line: Arizona is about 70% of US output
  (Morenci, Bagdad, Ray, Sierrita, Safford), New Mexico has Chino and Tyrone, Sonora has Buenavista/Cananea
  (about 4.52 Bt at 0.42% Cu) and La Caridad. **Molybdenum** (La Caridad; four of seven US byproduct operations
  are in Arizona). **Silver-lead-zinc** from the Chihuahua districts (Santa Bárbara, Parral, Naica). **Gold**
  (La Herradura, Mulatos; Pinos Altos, Palmarejo). **Potash** at Carlsbad, NM (the majority of US output, one of
  the three irreplaceable macronutrients). **Uranium** in the Grants belt (second-largest identified reserves of
  any US state). Hayden (AZ) primary copper smelting (note: base §1; smelter count reconciliation flagged there).
- **Manufactured goods:** Legacy only (see Post-Collapse). Sandia and Los Alamos knowledge is the most plausible
  surviving technical institutional memory in the territory (note: base §8.5).
- **Other:** The Borlaug/CIMMYT germplasm collection. The sunniest city on Earth (Yuma, per Guinness; note: §2.6).

### Deficit — what it cannot produce at all

- **Food / calories:** Not a structural deficit at reduced population, but **entirely a water problem**: far more
  of the territory's current agriculture runs on pumped groundwater than on gravity surface water (note: §8.4).
  ⚠️ No four-state calorie balance has been computed.
- **Energy / fuel:** ⚠️ **In-territory refining capacity was never researched** (Arizona is believed to have no
  refineries, ⚠️M). Oil exists; the oil *industry* does not survive (note: §8.4, "having oil, but not having the
  oil industry").
- **Metals, cement, industrial inputs:** **Aluminum, tin, nickel, chromium**: no bauxite, weak to absent others;
  no large-scale rare-earth separation (note: §1.10). **Integrated steel is NOT established for this nation.** The
  base's "hidden keystone" (AHMSA at Monclova, Sabinas coking coal, Coahuila iron) is **OUT OF SCOPE**. What the
  four states hold instead: small Sonora iron deposits (under 5 Mt, under 50% Fe, skarn type; search), La Perla in
  Chihuahua (listed as a northern iron mine; search), anthracite near Tonichi, Sonora (Santa Clara district,
  abandoned about 1911, found unsuitable for locomotives; search), and scrap. Coking-coal quality of the San Juan
  Basin and Black Mesa coals is unassessed ⚠️. **Primary lead/silver refining at Met-Mex Torreón is also OUT OF
  SCOPE**; lead-acid battery capacity therefore rests on Chihuahua concentrates and scrap, ⚠️ with no in-territory
  primary smelter established.
- **Manufactured goods and technology:** Semiconductors (TSMC Arizona, Intel Chandler) and the maquiladora belt are
  pure artifacts of global trade and vanish (note: §8.4).
- **Other:** **Water**, the defining constraint: the Colorado is over-allocated by design (Mead structural deficit
  about 1.2 maf/yr) and the Rio Grande delivers about 20% of its flow to the sea (note: §§2.1, 2.3).
  **Losing Baja costs the Cerro Prieto geothermal field and the La Rumorosa wind resource** (note: §4.6).

### The Single Point of Failure

> **Water.** Externally, the allocations on the two shared rivers (the Colorado and the Rio Grande) and who can
> close the tap upstream; internally, the **Oviáchic dam and the Yaqui/Mayo canal network**, which the base calls
> the single most valuable physical possession of the nation (note: §8.6). ⚠️ Provisional; the template asks for
> one, and the two are the same fact at different distances.

### Trade Complementarity

- **Natural trading partners, and what specifically is exchanged:** **Cascadia.** Cascadia is "substantially
  import-dependent" for metals (note: Cascadia profile) and owns every protected deep-water port on the west
  coast; Sonora has the metals and needs Pacific access. Northbound-westbound from Sonora: copper, molybdenum,
  silver, potash, winter vegetables, grain, (some) power. Eastbound-southbound into Sonora: port and transit
  access, manufactures, aluminum and tin by re-export. **Friction:** the Colorado River (Cascadia's Imperial
  Valley, Metropolitan Water District aqueduct and Tijuana all run on it; note: Cascadia material base).
- **Who needs this nation more than it needs them:** Cascadia (river water, Palo Verde power, metals), the CSA
  (Rio Grande deliveries; El Paso is downstream), ⚠️ possibly Cascadia for **crude** (see "What it would fight
  over").
- **Who this nation needs more than they need it:** The **Colorado Republic** (upstream on both rivers and holds
  the Compact's delivery obligation), **Cascadia** (ports), **México** (the southbound land corridor through
  Sinaloa).
- **What it would have to concede to obtain its deficits:** TBD. Likely transit rights, water assurances, and
  (as the Alaska Republic does with Cascadia) a standing dependency on a neighbor's ports.

### Post-Collapse Trajectory

*(Re-cut from base §8 for the four states. The base's assumed horizon is about 2283; the novel's era is unknown.)*

- **Survives regardless:** Every ore body listed above; the solar resource; the alluvial soils (Yaqui/Mayo,
  Mesilla, the Salt River valley, Yuma); the pre-industrial crop package and the knowledge of it; the acequia
  systems (600-700 in New Mexico, search), which are gravity-fed and community-governed.
- **Repairable with effort:** The gravity dam-and-canal systems (Oviáchic, El Novillo, La Angostura, Mocúzarit, La
  Boquilla, Elephant Butte, Caballo, the Salt River Project dams); small hydro; the San Juan-Chama gravity tunnel;
  Arizona copper smelting (multi-generational); rail alignments (Sunset Route, BNSF, the Chihuahua leg of the ChePe);
  cement from local limestone; lead-acid batteries (⚠️ with the lead-refining caveat above).
- **Gone for good:** The CAP (no low-technology version; "the single most consequential loss"); deep groundwater
  pumping (most of Chihuahua's irrigated farming, Costa de Hermosillo, eastern New Mexico's Ogallala circles,
  Tucson's historic supply); Palo Verde (needs 50+ million gallons/day of municipal effluent); URENCO Eunice;
  the fabs; the maquiladora belt; the Permian at anything like 2.2 million bbl/d; modern low-grade copper
  metallurgy; aluminum (note: base §8.4).
- **Carrying capacity vs. current population:** Current: about **15,955,733** (computed, 2020 censuses: Arizona
  7,151,502 [⚠️M exact; search confirms "7.1 million"], New Mexico 2,117,522, Sonora 2,944,840, Chihuahua
  3,741,869; search). The base's "35-40 million" for the old seven-state scope does not reconcile with the
  census sum (28.4 million, computed); ⚠️ it is also built on mixed census years. The base's survivors-by-region
  list puts people in the Yaqui/Mayo valleys, a chain of Rio Grande and lower Colorado river towns, the Salt River
  valley (low hundreds of thousands), and a shrunken Albuquerque; **two of its four named destinations (the
  Monterrey-Saltillo corridor and the Tamaulipas coast) are out of scope.** ⚠️ **No four-state carrying capacity
  has been computed. This is the largest quantitative gap on this sheet.**

## Scope Note

- **What's deliberately left out:**
  - **Coahuila, Nuevo León and Tamaulipas.** ⚠️ **The files disagree.** `Territory Definitions` (09-22/23), the
    material base and the Latin America survey call them unclaimed; the later `México (post-war)` file (2026-09-25)
    says they stay with post-war México. This profile follows the later file. Consequence: the CSA's lower Rio
    Grande frontier faces México, not unclaimed ground, and the old material on Monterrey, Monclova, Torreón
    (Met-Mex), Saltillo, Tampico and the Gulf ports is dossier material on **another nation's** territory.
  - **El Norte's reach into Texas.** Woodard's El Norte includes south and west Texas; the author's map gives all
    of Texas to the CSA (note: Territory Definitions, Finding 5).
  - **Internal distinctions not subdivided:** the Indigenous nations (see above), Mennonite settlements near
    Cuauhtémoc, Chihuahua (arrived 1922; search), and other documented communities. Listed so they are not
    silently omitted.
  - **Woodard's El Norte is an analytic frame about founding institutions.** It is not a description of the
    temperament of present-day people, and this profile does not use it as one (the author's No National
    Stereotypes law).
