# World Nations & Regions Roster — Post-2083 World

Running log, started 2026-09-18, building toward the "30+ countries" scope for the saga's convergence cast.
Logged as the author works through each region — not a finished list, expect ongoing additions. Distinct from
`Falkland-Treaty/`'s Assembly-of-Nations signatory list (a narrower, later-era subset of whichever of these
nations actually joined the United Assembly of Nations) — this file is the broader world-geography roster this
saga's cast gets drawn from, whether or not a given nation ends up an Assembly member.

**Source key:** **[MAP]** — `InnerTepeniaGDD/Reference/Images/Maps/North America with tentative labels.jpg`.
**[ASSEMBLY]** — the drafted Falkland Treaty's confirmed signatory list. **[CHAR]** — inferred from an existing
character's own file. **[AUTHOR 2026-09-18]** — stated directly by the author this session, not yet documented
anywhere else in the shared universe.

### East Asia — surveyed 2026-09-23, no new nations named yet

Two research surveys now cover China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia and Taiwan, in the same two layers used for Latin
America. **They name no nations** — they are the groundwork for deriving them.

- **`../extractions/East Asia - Frontiers, Civilizational Spheres and the Han Core (survey).md`** — the
  analytical basis. There is no Woodard equivalent and there cannot be: his Doctrine of First Effective
  Settlement describes settler colonies, and East Asia has continuous civilizations with no founding-settler
  moment. **Owen Lattimore's steppe/sown frontier (1940), the Hu Line (1935) and G. William Skinner's nine
  macroregions** do the equivalent work.
- **`material-base/East Asia - Material and Economic Base (survey).md`** — what the territory physically holds,
  with economic-complexity data and a post-collapse assessment.
- **`China - Ethnolinguistic Map (read from reference).md`** — the tier-2 evidence layer, read from a
  third-party language map. The East Asian counterpart of the US county ancestry map.
- **`east-asia-map-data/`** — the surveys converted to CSV for map generation: **12 candidate regions**, the
  **10 Sinitic internal divisions** of the Federation, and **7 excluded candidates**, with a README of prompts
  and constraints. FOR CONSIDERATION ONLY — three of the twelve are canon, the rest are arguable.

**Settled by the author this session:** the **Sinian Federation is the Han core, not modern PRC borders** —
Xinjiang, Tibet, Inner Mongolia and Manchuria become separate polities — and the Han core is drawn as a
**federation of linguistic nations** (Mandarin, Wu, Yue, Min, Hakka, Xiang, Gan, Jin) under one Sinian
government. This is what the post-2083 fragmentation fragmented *into*.

**⚠️ Deliberately left open — the southern border.** Whether **Yunnan, Guizhou, Guangxi and Gansu** fall inside
the Han core has evidence cutting both ways, and the author's instruction is to mark it rather than decide it.
*Against:* the language map shows those provinces as a non-Han shatter zone, with Zhuang a solid territorial
block. *For:* they carry over 90% of China's nickel, its largest aluminum/lead/zinc/tin reserves, >40% of its
phosphate, and 160.9 million people. The Qing Eighteen Provinces definition already includes all four.

**This is four decisions, not one — the provinces are separable.** **Gansu** has the weakest ethnolinguistic
case for exclusion and the strongest material case for inclusion, so it is the strongest to keep. **Guangxi**
is the hardest to keep and the cheapest to lose. The survey sets out three named options at §3.3 and does not
choose between them.

**⚠️ A material caveat that weakens the strongest argument for inclusion:** Chinese **chromium** was never
verified, and **without chromium there is no stainless steel regardless of nickel.** Gansu's nickel therefore
may not buy what it appears to. Settle chromium before weighing the trade — see the recheck list.

**Open:** Russia not yet worked out **[AUTHOR 2026-09-18]**, and deliberately deferred in the East Asia pass —
Siberian territory is to be marked unclaimed where it abuts, as the Latin America work handled the Falklands.
South Asia, Southeast Asia, Central Asia and the Middle East remain entirely undocumented.

## South America

**Open: no nations named yet** — but no longer undocumented. Two research surveys were completed 2026-09-23
covering everything south of Sonora (all Mexico below the northern tier, Central America, the Caribbean, and
all of South America including the Guianas):

- **`../extractions/Latin America - Regional Frameworks and Colonial Origins (survey).md`** — the analytical
  basis. There is no direct Woodard equivalent, but **Darcy Ribeiro's *As Américas e a Civilização* (1970)**
  makes the same founding-population move 41 years earlier, and his *O Povo Brasileiro* (1995) is effectively
  *American Nations* for Brazil. Includes a first-pass regional division offered for argument, not adoption.
- **`material-base/Latin America - Material and Economic Base (survey).md`** — what the territory physically
  holds, with economic-complexity data and a post-collapse assessment.

**Still structurally significant**, since the saga's own environ notes (`../../background notes/environ notes.md`)
place a setting shift through South America en route to the Falkland Islands — so any eventual regional division
needs to leave that corridor coherent.

**Next phase, per the author (2026-09-23): research proposes, author disposes.** Candidate nations will be
derived from these surveys with reasoning; the author accepts, rejects or redraws, then maps it. That inverts
the North American workflow, where the map came first.
