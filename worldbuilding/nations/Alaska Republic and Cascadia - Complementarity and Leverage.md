# Alaska Republic ↔ Cascadia — Complementarity and Leverage

2026-09-23. A side-by-side material comparison of the two nations, drawn from their filled National Character
Profiles and the underlying material-base research. **Nothing here is invented** — every row traces to
researched real-world fact about the territory in question.

**Why this file exists:** the two countries' surpluses and deficits interlock almost exactly. That interlock
is the foundation under Decision 5 (what Human #1 went to Whitehorse to negotiate), and under any treaty,
rivalry or war between them.

## Directional Summary

**Northbound into the Alaska Republic:** refined product (especially gasoline), grain and calories, cement and
structural steel, manufactured goods, port and transit access.

**Southbound into Cascadia:** crude oil, fish, zinc, lead, silver, germanium, tungsten, coal.

**The physical route already exists and survives partition.** Valdez → Cherry Point / Anacortes / Ferndale by
tanker, because **Valdez faces the Gulf of Alaska rather than the Inside Passage.** Nothing has to be built for
this trade to function.

---

## The Leverage Asymmetry

**Cascadia holds more cards, and by a wide margin.** It can feed itself, ship for itself, build for itself, and
reach its own territory without permission. The Alaska Republic can do none of those things.

**But the asymmetry is not total, and the exception is the whole story.** Cascadia's single point of failure is
**crude oil**, and its northern neighbor sits on the crude. A refining economy with two million barrels a day
of capacity and no domestic supply is one embargo from stopping.

So the relationship is structurally this:

> **Cascadia can hurt the Alaska Republic slowly and in a dozen ways. The Alaska Republic can hurt Cascadia
> quickly, in exactly one.**

That is not a stable friendship or a clean rivalry. It is **mutual hostage-taking between unequal partners**,
which is a far more interesting basis for a treaty — and for a negotiator's journey — than either alliance or
enmity would be.

**Note where this collides with the cultural layer.** Per the profile template's own instruction, a conflict
between material dependency and cultural affinity should not be resolved. The material layer points these two
hard at each other regardless of whether they like each other — and neither nation's cultural axes have been
designed yet, so the affinity question is entirely open.

## The Next Question — Why Hasn't This Already Been Solved?

**This is the load-bearing question, and it is not yet answered.**

A complementarity this clean, between two adjacent states, with an existing tanker route and no technical
obstacle, should resolve itself. Two countries that need exactly what the other has, and have needed it for
generations, would normally have built the institutions to trade — or one would have taken what it needed by
force.

**And the timeframe makes this sharper, not softer.** The war was **2083**. *Alaska Rescue* sits in the early
First Interwar Period, somewhere before the 2318 Jeju-do ruling. Even at the early end, that is **many decades
of reconstruction — plausibly a century or more.** "They haven't rebuilt yet" is not an answer over that span.
Several generations have lived and died inside this problem.

So the setting owes an explanation for each of the following:

1. **Why the Alaska Republic still cannot feed itself**, when Cascadia has surplus grain a tanker-voyage away.
2. **Why Cascadia still has no secure crude supply**, when its neighbor has more than it can refine.
3. **Why the Canol corridor is still unbuilt** — 355 km of a road that already existed in 1944, on a route
   105 km *shorter* than the foreign alternative.
4. **Why the Alaska Republic still has no port of its own**, when it has a coastline.
5. **Why neither has taken what it needs by force**, if the trade is not working.

**Good answers are structural, not circumstantial.** "There was a war and things are hard" explains a decade,
not a century. Candidate shapes worth testing:

- **The trade does work, and the problem is the terms.** Not absence but *price* — the relationship exists and
  is resented, which is the likeliest reading and the one that best fits a negotiator traveling north.
- **Something prevents it that nobody can fix**, and it has a name. A treaty, a debt, a prior war, a legal
  regime.
- **The capacity is fake.** Perhaps Cascadia's ~2.0M bbl/d of *pre-war* refining capacity does not survive in
  anything like that form, and the real figure is a fraction — in which case the shortfall is smaller and less
  urgent than the raw numbers suggest.
- **Population is far lower than assumed**, making both problems smaller and the urgency less.
- **Someone benefits from it staying broken.** A third party, an internal faction, or an industry that profits
  from scarcity.

⚠️ **A blocking dependency:** answering any of this requires **settling roughly when *Alaska Rescue* takes
place.** Forty years after the war and two hundred years after it are different civilizations, and the
plausibility of every explanation above depends on which one this is. That date is currently a range, not a
fact.
