## The India slice: exact scope, author-settled 2026-09-29

**The dividing line** (author's own words, drawn from direct Google Maps inspection): starting at the
**Nepal–Bihar–West Bengal tripoint** (the river-border with Nepal), the line follows the real **Bihar/West
Bengal state border** east/south until it meets the **Mahananda River**, then follows the Mahananda River
onward until the river reaches the **India–Bangladesh border**. The exact crossing point at the Bangladesh
border is between two named local landmarks the author pulled directly from Google Maps: **"East
Bandarjhuli"** and **"জিরো পয়েন্ট আমগাছ তলা"** ("Zero Point Amgachh Tola") — both real, small tourist-attraction
place names at the Darjeeling/Uttar Dinajpur/Tentulia stretch of the border, right in the area that also happens to be
the real Siliguri Corridor's actual narrowest chokepoint (see below).

**Everything south/west of that line** (the rest of West Bengal, all of Bihar, and the whole rest of India) —
**zero research, not in scope, by explicit author instruction.**

**Everything north/east of that line** — the "breakaway landmass" — includes, confirmed explicitly by the
author ("the entire area, and not just that province" re: Arunachal Pradesh):
- The slice of West Bengal north/east of the line: Darjeeling, Kalimpong, Jalpaiguri, Alipurduar, and Cooch
  Behar. **No part of Uttar Dinajpur** is on this side — the Mahananda is itself the Darjeeling/Uttar
  Dinajpur district boundary along the whole stretch the line follows, so Uttar Dinajpur stays entirely in
  India. Kishanganj (Bihar) likewise stays entirely on India's side, including its northern lobe.
- Sikkim
- Assam
- Meghalaya
- Nagaland
- Manipur
- Tripura
- Mizoram
- **All of Arunachal Pradesh** (explicitly confirmed in scope, not just the "Seven Sisters" states)

is at `y-files/Map Files/Asia (South-Central)/01 initial generations/siliguri_line_check.png`.

- **1 to 2**: the real Kishanganj/Darjeeling district boundary, ~45 km of winding line. Kishanganj's northern
  lobe bulges up to ~26.557°N and stays in Bihar, on India's side.
- **2 to 3**: ~13 km along the Mahananda, which here is the Darjeeling/Uttar Dinajpur district boundary.
- **Point 3 is author-verified against Google Maps**: the two landmarks (author-supplied coordinates
  26.49732, 88.33190 and 26.49505, 88.33347) are ~300 m apart and fall on either side of the
  Darjeeling/Uttar Dinajpur boundary; the crossing is between them, and point 3 is their midpoint.
- The corridor's narrowest span, Nepal (point 1) to Bangladesh (point 3), is ~23 km, consistent with the

**Stage 3/4 consequence**: no Mahananda-precision tradeoff is needed. The line is made entirely of whole
district boundaries (Kishanganj/Darjeeling, then Darjeeling/Uttar Dinajpur), which the dataset already
carries. The river runs ~1 km off the trijunction in the OpenStreetMap data, which does not matter at map
scale.

### Real geography of the corridor itself

The real "Siliguri Corridor" (nicknamed the "Chicken's Neck") narrows to **20–22 km** at its tightest point,
sitting within West Bengal and Sikkim, flanked by Nepal (west), Bhutan (north), Bangladesh (south), and
China's Chumbi Valley (northeast). It is genuinely the most strategically vulnerable chokepoint in real India —
a Chinese military advance of under 130 km could historically cut off ~40–50 million people in the
northeast; this nearly happened in the 1962 Sino-Indian War and resurfaced as a live concern during the 2017
Doklam standoff. The Indian Army treats it as its own strongest defensive line. This is real-world grounding
for why a "slicing disconnect" here is physically plausible, not just narratively convenient.

**Darjeeling and Kalimpong** specifically carry a genuinely distinct, century-old separatist history: the
**Gorkhaland movement**, rooted in Nepali-speaking Gorkha ethnic identity (distinct from Bengali West
Bengal), dates to a 1909 petition, was named in the 1980s, and won a semi-autonomous council (the Gorkhaland
Territorial Administration, 2012) short of full statehood. This is real-world grounding for treating these
two districts as culturally aligned with the breakaway side (Sikkim, Nepali/Gorkha-adjacent) rather than with
Bengali-majority mainland India. ⚠️ This holds only for the Gorkha hill core (~0.88M of the ~8.54M people in
the five northern West Bengal districts the line takes in, about 10%, 2011 census); the rest is Bengali-plurality
Siliguri, Rajbanshi Scheduled Caste plains, and Adivasi tea labor — see the Assam-Tripura-Sikkim-Gorkha hills
survey.

## Headline findings: how many countries does the breakaway landmass become?

**The author's own question — "one single country or possibly multiple countries" — has a clear
real-world-grounded answer: multiple.** Every real separatist/insurgent movement in this region pursues its
*own* ethnic group's independence, never a unified "Seven Sisters" nationalism; that label itself is a 1972
journalist's coinage, not an organic identity. Assam has already fractured repeatedly along ethnic lines in
real history — Nagaland split off in 1963, Meghalaya/Mizoram/Arunachal Pradesh all split off in 1972 — so
further fragmentation in a post-collapse setting is extrapolation from an established real pattern, not
invention.

### Tier 1 — real, active, well-documented fracture lines (strong candidates for their own country)

1. **Sikkim.** A real independent kingdom from 1642–1975 (the Namgyal dynasty, Buddhist priest-kings),
   annexed by India via a 1975 referendum among a population of only ~200,000 (⚠️ the often-cited
   "20,000–40,000 Indian troops present" figure is unverified). The 1975 abolition was carried by the
   Nepali-origin majority; the Bhutia-Lepcha groups (~16.5%) would be the restoration constituency, so the
   "restoration" framing is weaker than it first looks. Ethnic base: Lepcha (earliest
   inhabitants), Bhutia, and a numerically dominant Nepali/Gorkha population.
2. **"Nagalim" / Greater Nagaland.** Nagaland state, plus the Naga-inhabited hill districts of **Manipur**
   (~20% of Manipur's population, concentrated in the northern hills), plus the Naga-inhabited eastern
   districts of **Arunachal Pradesh** (the NSCN's organizing there is contested; the eastern Naga districts' own
   current vehicle is the Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority, signed February 2026). This is a real, current,
   cross-border unification claim, not an invented composite — the NSCN insurgency (1980–present) already
   treats these three states' Naga-inhabited areas as one people.
3. **Meitei Manipur.** The Imphal Valley core (~53% of Manipur's population), once the Naga hills (above) and
   Kuki-Zo hills (below) are carved out.
4. **Kuki-Zo bloc.** The southern Manipur hill districts (~16% of Manipur's population). Not a hypothetical
   fracture — the real 2023 Manipur ethnic war (175 dead by September 2023, 258 by November 2024, over 260 reported in 2026;
   28,899 officially displaced as of 3 September 2026) is exactly this fault line, and a separate Naga-Kuki
   front opened in 2026. See the Naga-Manipur-Mizoram hills survey for the district-level picture, including
   the five demographically mixed districts.
5. **Mizoram.** A single, comparatively unified Mizo identity; the real Mizo National Front insurgency
   (1954–1986) ended in a genuine, holding peace accord — a real resolution to build a stable post-collapse
   state from, rather than an open wound.
6. **Meghalaya.** Formed in 1972 by welding together three formerly separate tribal kingdoms — Khasi (central
   plateau, 33.8% of speakers), Garo (west, 31.6%), Jaintia/Pnar (east, 10.7%) — but ⚠️ **live internal movements exist**: a Garoland demand
   (August 2025), Inner Line Permit agitation, and the HNLC (banned to 2029). Whether it stays one entity is
   an open call — see the Assam-Tripura-Sikkim-Gorkha hills survey.

### Tier 2 — real evidence exists, but how far the fracture actually goes is a genuinely open call

7. **Assam's Barak Valley.** Constitutionally already distinct from the Assamese-majority Brahmaputra
   Valley — Bengali, not Assamese, is the Barak Valley's own official language. Geographically adjacent to
   both Bangladesh and Tripura. Could split off as its own entity or stay attached to a rump Assam.
8. **Bodoland.** A real, named, still-active Bodo tribal autonomy demand inside Assam. Karbi and Dimasa
   autonomous-state demands are more than historical flavor (see the Assam-Tripura-Sikkim-Gorkha hills
   survey); the Bodoland Territorial Region is a plurality-run area (ST share 33.5%, Bodo speakers ~30%).
9. **Tripura.** The demographic mirror image of Assam's Barak Valley problem: **tribal-majority (Tripuri)
   before 1947**, flipped to **Bengali-majority** by two waves of refugee influx (Partition 1947, then the
   1971 Bangladesh Liberation War), with a real decades-long insurgency (NLFT, All Tripura Tiger Force,
   resolved by a 2024 peace accord) fought along exactly that fault line. Could stay unified (as today) or
   split into a Bengali-majority core plus the area covered by the real Tripura Tribal Areas Autonomous
   District Council (TTAADC, 587 village committees, ~68% of the state's land and ~87% of its tribal population).
10. **Rest of Arunachal Pradesh** (after the Naga-claimed eastern districts leave for Nagalim, see Tier 1 #2).
    23 major tribes, no single alternative grouping with strong internal-fracture evidence — Monpa/Buddhist in
    the west (Tawang), Tani-speaking groups (Nyishi, Adi, Galo, Apatani, Tagin) in the center, Mishmi and Tai
    peoples in the east. Default: stays one entity. **Real external complication, not internal**: China
    claims the *entire* state as "South Tibet" under a territorial dispute dating to the 1913–14 McMahon
    Line, fought over in the real 1962 war, and Tawang Monastery specifically is the actual crux of that
    claim — worth a deliberate decision on how a post-collapse China treats this claim, separate from the
    internal-fracture question.
11. **Darjeeling/Kalimpong** (the Gorkha hill districts pulled into the breakaway landmass by the author's own
    corridor line — see above). Real open question: merge into Sikkim (shared Nepali/Gorkha identity, direct
    geographic neighbor) or stand alone as their own small entity.

**Not yet decided**: how granular the author wants to go — Tier 1 alone would be six countries; adding every
Tier 2 candidate could reach ten or more. This is exactly the kind of call this project's own "research
proposes, author disposes" rule exists for. **Explicitly parked, not defaulted, pending the author's own
read of this menu.**
