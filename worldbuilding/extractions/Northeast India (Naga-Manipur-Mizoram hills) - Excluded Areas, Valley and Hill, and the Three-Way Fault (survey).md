# Northeast India (Naga-Manipur-Mizoram hills) — Excluded Areas, Valley and Hill, and the Three-Way Fault (survey)

**Stage 1 frameworks survey. Started 2026-09-29, deepening pass on the first-pass Northeast India material in
`South Asia - The Siliguri Line and the Nagalim Question (survey).md`. Not a finished analysis.**
Covers the **Naga / Manipur / Mizoram hill bloc** of the breakaway landmass northeast of the Siliguri line:
**Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram**, plus the Naga-inhabited districts of **Arunachal Pradesh** (Tirap, Changlang,
Longding) and the hill districts of **Assam** that touch the bloc (Dima Hasao, Karbi Anglong). Companion
material-base survey not yet started (Stage 2); no map-data package yet (Stage 3). **The rest of India is out of scope
and untouched.** Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim, the Gorkha hills and the rest of Arunachal are other researchers'
lanes; this file adds one-line seam mentions only. The Myanmar seam (Chin, Naga, Kachin, Sagaing) cross-references
`Mainland Southeast Asia - Mandalas, the Massif and the Tai Continuum (survey).md`.

**Status of this file: research proposes, the author disposes.** Nothing here names a nation, draws a border or
picks a winner. Every candidate entity is laid out with its costs; the granularity decision is the author's.
Conflict figures are given as ranges with source attribution; partisan sources (NSCN, Kuki, Meitei, Indian
government or Army-affiliated) are labeled. All names are romanized; no non-Latin script appears in this file.

**Two corrections to the working brief, found in the data.** (1) **Nagaland now has 17 districts, not 16** —
Meluri was upgraded from a sub-division to a district in 2024, after the 2021-22 creations of Chumoukedima,
Niuland, Tseminyu, Noklak and Shamator. (2) Manipur has 16 districts (since December 2016) and Mizoram has 11
(since 2019), as expected. All 2011 census figures below predate these reorganizations, so district populations
must be read with the parent-district caveats in §3. ⚠️ **The census is 15 years old**; no post-2011 ethnic count was found, so every
"current population base" here is 2011 data, and every hill-district ethnic share is politically contested.

## Headline findings

1. **First effective settlement fails here for a fifth distinct reason: the hills were sorted, frozen and
   confessionalized by a colonial administrative category, and that category is what the peoples now claim
   as their nation.** The British governed the Naga, Lushai and North Cachar
   Hills as separate "excluded" frontier areas, left Manipur a princely valley state with its hills attached, and
   admitted Baptist and Presbyterian missions that turned tribal clusters into confessional nations. "Naga" as a
   pan-tribal identity was itself consolidated as late as 1956-57 (Manipur Naga Council merging into the Naga
   National Council, per an Indian Army think-tank study). See §1.
2. **Manipur is the load-bearing fact and it is a valley-and-hill dyad in its purest form.** The Imphal Valley
   holds **57.2% of the population (1,633,672 of 2,855,794, 2011)** on roughly **8-10% of the land**, while the
   hill districts hold **42.8% (1,222,122)**. The hills split almost exactly in half: the five Naga-core
   districts (Senapati, Ukhrul, Kamjong, Tamenglong, Noney) total **610,053**; the three pre-2016 districts behind
   the Kuki-Zo claim (Churachandpur, Kangpokpi, Chandel) total **612,069** (my sums from §3). Every trunk road
   into the valley (NH-2 to Dimapur, NH-37 to Silchar, Imphal-Moreh) crosses hill territory.
3. **The first-pass survey's "Naga north, Kuki south" picture is too tidy.** Kuki-Zo populations hold the west
   (Kangpokpi, the old Sadar Hills), the south (Churachandpur, Pherzawl) and the southeast (Chandel,
   Tengnoupal), a horseshoe around the valley with Naga districts interposed to the north and east. Kangpokpi,
   Chandel, Tengnoupal, Noney and Jiribam are demographically mixed and contested (§13).
4. **A third front opened in 2026 that the first-pass survey does not have: Naga versus Kuki-Zo.** Starting with a
   7 February 2026 assault in Litan (Ukhrul district), it produced at least 25 deaths by July 2026, spread to
   Kamjong, Kangpokpi, Senapati, Chandel and Tengnoupal, and reached four reported Tangkhul deaths near the
   Myanmar border on 24 September 2026. The United Naga Council urged suspending "all kinds of social and economic
   ties" with Kukis in April. The Nagas were a neutral third party in 2023; they no longer are. See §7.4.
5. **The 2023-26 Meitei-Kuki war has produced a de facto partition on the ground.** Official deaths: **175
   (September 2023) rising to 258 (November 2024), reported as "over 260" in 2026**, unofficial counts higher;
   displacement peaked near **60,000**; as of **3 September 2026** the state reported **28,899** still displaced
   (24,530 in 105 camps, 4,369 in temporary accommodation) after **36,000** resettled. Central forces hold
   buffer zones between valley and hills; the Kuki-Zo side treats them as permanent, the government denies they
   exist. President's Rule ran 13 February 2025 to 4 February 2026. See §7.
6. **The Center has rejected the Kuki-Zo "Union Territory with legislature" demand** (MHA, 11 March 2025, and again
   reported November 2025), and **the Naga settlement has stalled on flag, constitution and territory**, so
   nothing on the ground currently delivers either Kukiland or Nagalim. Meanwhile **a partial Naga-side
   devolution actually happened: the Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority (FNTA)**, a tripartite agreement
   signed in early February 2026 covering six eastern Nagaland districts with 46 subjects devolved — the
   ENPO dropped its separate-state demand for it. The first-pass survey does not mention it. See §8.
7. **Mizoram is stable but not simple.** The 1986 accord holds and the ruling Zoram People's Movement (27 of 40
   seats, December 2023) is sympathetic to Zo reunification; but the state hosts **34,350 Myanmar Chin refugees
   (the largest share of ~78,731 in India)**, at least **12,300 Manipur Kuki-Zo displaced (2023)**, and three Sixth
   Schedule minority councils (Chakma, Lai, Mara).
8. **The Nagalim map as published is mostly not Naga.** The maximal NSCN-IM claim (~120,000 sq km) adds to Nagaland
   (16,527-16,579 sq km, sources differ) districts in Assam whose census data show Naga tribes as small minorities (Dima Hasao is
   67% Hindu; Karbi Anglong 80% Hindu, 44% Karbi-speaking) and Arunachal districts whose own tribal bodies
   asked for Union Territory status rather than either Arunachal or Nagalim (CLAWS 2018). A
   demographically defensible Nagalim is a much smaller object: **Nagaland plus five Manipur districts is roughly
   2.59 million (2011)** — 1,978,502 + 610,053.
9. **The Myanmar border is where all three peoples continue, and it is closing.** The Free Movement Regime was
   scrapped (announced February 2024; border passes from December 2024); a Rs 31,000 crore fencing plan was approved
   (September 2024) but only ~30 km of 1,643 km was fenced by 2025. Mizoram and Nagaland assemblies opposed fencing;
   Manipur and Arunachal supported it. UNODC's 2025 survey found poppy in northern Sagaing for the first time.
10. **This region cannot reach the sea without crossing someone else.** Cutting the Siliguri Corridor removes the
    Kolkata route. Every candidate depends on Assam's rail and road hubs (Guwahati, Lumding, Dimapur, the Barak
    Valley), then on Bangladesh (Chittagong) or Myanmar's Sittwe via the unfinished Kaladan road. Aizawl got its
    first railhead on 13 September 2025; Imphal has none (Jiribam-Imphal line: 2028 per Manipur CM, June 2026).

## Section 1 — Method: what replaces first effective settlement here

Per `METHOD - How Nations Get Drawn.md`, Woodard's doctrine needs an arriving population that founds a
self-perpetuating society in territory the colonizer treats as open. The Naga-Manipur-Mizoram hills fail this
test in a way distinct from every region surveyed so far.

**What fails.**

- **There is no single first settlement to point to, and the attempt to point to one is the conflict.** Which people
  arrived first in Manipur's hills, and when, is exactly what the 2023-26 violence litigates. Meitei and Naga
  narratives call the Kuki-Zo "unwanted and non-indigenous" migrants of roughly the 1830s from Myanmar; Kuki
  scholars counter with genetic studies they read as showing "roots in the Indian subcontinent stretching to
  more than a millennia" (Scroll, 2026; ⚠️ both are advocacy positions, not settled scholarship). Political
  demands follow: **a National Register of Citizens with a 1951 or 1961 cutoff** on one side, **"census before
  NRC"** and "rejection of 1951 as the sole baseline" on the other (Kuki-Zo groups, 10 September 2026). Applying
  Woodard here means adjudicating a live indigeneity war. This survey records the claims; it does not adjudicate.
- **The hills are the classic Zomia case, and the term was coined for them.** Willem van Schendel coined "Zomia"
  from *Zomi*, the highlander word in the Tibeto-Burman languages of the India-Bangladesh-Myanmar borderlands
  (see the Mainland Southeast Asia survey, Part 2). Manipur's hill-valley structure is Scott's model almost
  literally: a wet-rice valley court state (the Meitei polity, with a written constitution said to date to the
  early 12th century in one source, and a forced Hinduization under King Pamheiba, 1709-1748) surrounded by
  swidden hill peoples who were never fully governed from it. The Lieberman and Brass criticisms (MSEA survey
  §2.2) apply here too; which hill groups were "pushed, not chosen" is precisely what the indigeneity dispute above
  contests, and this survey does not adjudicate it.

**What replaces it. Proposed replacement framework (working name: "Excluded-Area Settlement"; the author may
rename).** Three linked tests, in descending order of confidence:

1. **The colonial administrative category is the effective settlement.** The British sorted the hills into
   Excluded/Partially Excluded areas (Naga Hills, Lushai Hills, North Cachar Hills; Tuensang was declared an
   "Excluded Area" in 1935 per an Indian Government-affiliated IDSA backgrounder), left Manipur as a princely
   state (from 1891) whose hills were ruled loosely from the valley, and admitted missionaries. That sorting fixed
   each hill people's boundary, and it remains the *unit of political self-understanding*: "Nagaland" is a
   British-era district scaled up; "Mizoram" is the Lushai Hills; "Kangleipak" is the valley principality.
   ⚠️ The wider 1935 Excluded Area sorting is general knowledge, corroborated here only for Tuensang.
2. **Confession is the ethnogenesis mechanism.** Nagaland is 87.9% Christian, about 80% Baptist; Mizoram 87%
   Christian; Manipur's hills 79.9% to 97% Christian district by district (§3). Baptist and Presbyterian
   conversion made "Naga" and "Mizo" and "Kuki-Zo" thinkable as nations. An Indian Army study notes that the NSCN-IM's
   Baptist association "attracted the Baptist converts" among Assam's Zeme Nagas, while the non-Christian Heraka
   resist a "Nagaland for Christ" identity. **Christianity is the state-formation tool, not a demographic detail**,
   as Puritanism was for Woodard's New England.
3. **Land-law regime is the operative border.** Article 371A (Nagaland; no Act of Parliament on Naga religious or
   social practice, customary law, or land ownership without a state Assembly resolution), Article 371G (Mizoram,
   same structure for Mizo customary law and land), Article 371C (Manipur; a Hill Areas Committee of the
   Assembly), the 1971 Manipur (Hill Areas) District Councils Act, the Sixth Schedule councils in Mizoram and
   Assam's hills, and the Inner Line Permit (Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation of 1873; in force in Arunachal,
   Nagaland, Mizoram and, since **11 December 2019**, Manipur). ⚠️ The 371A/371C/371G provisions are from general
   knowledge of the Constitution and were not re-fetched this pass. Manipur and Tripura, as princely states,
   never received the Sixth Schedule (a source consulted for this file), which is why Manipur's hill councils are weaker
   and why "Sixth Schedule for the Naga districts" is a live Naga demand (CLAWS 2018).

**Application to the project's two-tier scheme.** Tier 1 (draws borders) = three confessional-ethnic blocs: the
**Naga family** (Baptist, Tibeto-Burman, ~2.5 million in India per one estimate), the **Zo / Kuki-Chin-Mizo
family** (Presbyterian/Baptist), and the **Meitei valley** (Vaishnavite Hindu with Sanamahi and a large Muslim
minority). Tier 2 (draws what is inside) = tribes: Angami, Ao, Lotha, Sumi, Konyak, Tangkhul, Kuki, Thadou, Paite,
Hmar, Lushai, Mara, Lai, Chakma. **METHOD's caution 2 applies with force: Tier 1 is itself contestable and the
contest is the story.** "Naga" as a pan-tribal label is 20th-century and Tangkhul-led; "Chin" as a pan-ethnic
identity "is a modern construction, shaped by British rule and post-independence ethnic politics" (Wikipedia,
Chin people); "Kuki" is an exonym that several tribes reject in favor of "Zo", which is why the 2024 umbrella body
chose the compromise "Kuki-Zo".

**Where the whole-first-order-subdivision rule collides.** In India the first-order unit is the state. Whole-state
drawing gives exactly three units here (Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram) and cannot express Nagalim, Kukiland or a
Meitei-only state. Any of those needs a district-level (second-order) exception, which the author has already accepted
elsewhere (Iraq's Kirkuk and Diyala marked "Disputed Territory"; Lebanon's confessional mosaic disclosed but not drawn,
per the tracker). The project also has named designations for unresolved ground: Autonomous Zone (Aceh, Sarawak,
the Araucania-Patagonia zone) and "Uncertain/Unstable/Disputed Territory" (Fergana, Khuzestan, Dhale). Those
tools exist for a Manipur buffer zone; whether to use them is the author's call (§13).

## Section 2 — Geography: the Patkai, the valley and the three drainages

- **The ridge system.** The Patkai range straddles the Indo-Myanmar border; its highest peak is **Mount Saramati,
  3,826 m**, on the Nagaland-Myanmar border; **Pangsau Pass** (Changlang) carried the Ledo Road and is still the most
  important passage (Wikipedia, Patkai). The Naga Hills continue south into Manipur; the Lushai Hills (Mizoram) form
  the southern system.
- **The Imphal Valley.** Roughly **700 sq mi (~1,813 sq km)** of alluvial floor at about 790 m elevation, holding the
  capital and the majority of Manipur's population. ⚠️ The valley is commonly cited as ~8-10% of the state's
  22,327 sq km, but the Army study cited here says hills are "70 per cent" of the territory; treat 70-92% hill as
  source-dependent.
- **Loktak Lake**, the largest freshwater lake in Northeast India, is a "pulsating" lake of 250-500 sq km by
  season, with floating phumdis and Keibul Lamjao National Park; the Ithai Barrage produces **105 MW** and irrigates about
  23,000 ha (Wikipedia). It is the valley's water and energy anchor.
- **Three drainages.** Manipur's valley rivers feed Loktak and drain south toward Myanmar's Chindwin system; the
  west and southwest hills (Pherzawl, Churachandpur, Noney) drain to the **Barak River** and on to Bangladesh (the
  Barak, Tuipi and Tuivai rivers flow through Pherzawl); Nagaland's and Eastern Nagaland's slopes drain to the
  Brahmaputra and to the Chindwin. ⚠️ Chindwin routing is general knowledge. **Consequence for a partition: the water divide runs through the hills, not along the ethnic line.**
- **The Kabaw Valley**, between the Yomadong range and the Chindwin, was conquered by Manipur around 1470 by its own
  chronicles and **handed back to Burma by the British in 1834** with Manipur compensated by a Rs 6,000 annual
  subsidy; the Moreh-Tamu twin border towns sit in it (Wikipedia, Kabaw Valley). Manipur's border with Myanmar is
  reported at about 398 km (India Today NE calls it unfenced in 2026; IMPRI reports about 10 km fenced near Moreh
  in 2025; the two do not fully reconcile).

## Section 4 — The peoples

### 4.1 The Naga (Tier 1), and the tribes (Tier 2)

**Population.** Wikipedia's estimate: **India ~2.5 million+ (Nagaland 1.6 million+; Manipur 650,000+; Arunachal 150,000+;
Assam 40,000+; Meghalaya 2,556; Mizoram 760); Myanmar ~300,000.** Over 87% Christian, mostly Baptist. Naga languages,
"over 89 languages and dialects, mostly unintelligible with each other" — Naga unity is a political-religious
achievement, not a linguistic given. The 1929 Naga Club memorandum to the Simon Commission asked for self-determination
after British withdrawal.

**Internal Naga fault lines (CLAWS 2018 and IDSA; partisan where noted).** (a) **Tangkhul dominance** of NSCN-IM
leadership worries other tribes; (b) the Poumai and Thangal resent NSCN-IM's administrative partition of their
territory; (c) the Zeliangrong split between Christians and Heraka and between two militant factions; (d) most Manipur
Naga tribes reject centralized codification of customary law (only the Tangkhul want it); (e) **Eastern vs. western
Nagas**: the ENPO claims a population share near 50% but about 3% of public employment despite a 25% reservation
(ENPO-side claim); (f) the NNPG (seven groups; "Agreed Position," 2017) called the FNT demand "antithetical" to broader
Naga aspirations, while NSCN-K factions backed ENPO. **The one point every Manipur Naga tribe agrees on is territorial
integration of Naga areas under one administration** (CLAWS 2018).

### 4.2 The Meitei (Tier 1: valley)

About **1.8 million** worldwide (Wikipedia); **1,522,132 in Manipur (86%)**, 168,127 in Assam (9.5%, mostly the
Cachar/Barak side), ~25,000 in Myanmar, ~15,000 in Bangladesh. Religion (2011, Meitei only): Hindu 85.3%
(predominantly Vaishnavite), Sanamahi (traditional) 14.6%, a few thousand Christians. The Meitei Pangal (Muslims,
8.4% of the state) are a separate confessional community: 25.42% of Thoubal, 15.8% of Imphal East, 7.87% of
Bishnupur, 34.07% of Jiribam. Meitei identity now includes a script revival (Meitei Mayek, replaced by a Bengali-based alphabet in the 18th
century), a Sanamahi religious revival, and the Arambai Tenggol movement. ⚠️ One Wikipedia summary says the "entire
Meitei ethnicity" was forcibly converted under Pamheiba (scriptures burned in 1729); the extent of coercion is a
scholarly and political question.

### 4.3 The Kuki-Zo (Tier 1: Zo family) and their Chin and Mizo kin

**The naming problem is the fault line.** "Kuki" was an exonym (Bengali usage; British records from 1777). Non-Thadou
tribes rejected it and accepted the broader "Zo"; the **Kuki-Zo Council** (founded **11 October 2024**; chairman
Henlianthang Thanglet, a Vaiphei) uses the compromise "Kuki-Zo". It merged the Indigenous Tribal Leaders' Forum
(Churachandpur) and the Committee on Tribal Unity (Kangpokpi) with Kuki Inpi Manipur, the Paite Tribe Council and
Zou representatives. Manipur Kuki-Zo ST population: **448,197 (15.7%, 2011)**; the Thadou form the largest single
language group (9.16% of the state).

**Tribes**: Thadou (largest), Paite, Hmar, Zou, Vaiphei, Gangte, Simte, Kom, Aimol, Chothe, Anal (Old Kuki, also
counted with the Nagas), and others. The 1997-98 **Kuki-Paite conflict** displaced about 22,000 people (Wikipedia).
Kin: **the Chin** (about **1.2 million** in Myanmar, "90% Christian, predominantly Baptist" — from 35% in 1966), the
**Mizo/Lushai** (Mizoram, 83.4%), and further Zo populations in Assam, Tripura and Bangladesh's Chittagong Hill Tracts.
⚠️ My own arithmetic, not a sourced figure: Mizoram 1,097,206 + Kuki-Zo Manipur 448,197 + ~1.2 million Chin is
roughly 2.7 million, before Tripura, Assam and Bangladesh.

**Historical position in Manipur.** The 1917-1919 Kuki Rebellion against the British is the founding grievance-event; the
**1992-97 Kuki-Naga conflict** killed roughly **1,000+ Kuki (Kuki bodies' count; 350+ villages uprooted)** or
**about 2,000 in total with ~100,000 displaced** (one 1992-99 summary); another source gives **114,300 displaced and
~600 villages burned**. **Joupi massacre, 13 September 1993: about 115 Kuki killed.** ⚠️ These are irreconcilable
ranges from partisan-tinged summaries; use "1,000-2,000 dead, 100,000-114,000 displaced, 350-600 villages" as the
honest range. Kuki demands still standing from 1992-97: an NSCN-IM apology, customary corpse-price payment and
compensation (CLAWS 2018). Kuki claims to the hill areas include the **Sadar Hills district demand** (satisfied in
2016 with Kangpokpi) and now the Union Territory demand (§7.5).

### 4.4 The Mizo (Tier 1: Zo family, Mizoram core)

The Mizo (Lushai and assimilated Zo groups) are Presbyterian-Baptist, 83.4% of Mizoram by one count, with Mara, Lai and
Chakma communities on the southern and southwestern edge. Historically: the **Mautam** bamboo-flowering famine of
1958-61 gave birth to the Mizo National Famine Front, which became the **Mizo National Front on 22 October 1961**;
the armed uprising began in **1966**; the **Mizoram Peace Accord of 30 June 1986** ended it; statehood followed in
February 1987 (Wikipedia). The MNF won the 1987, 1998 and 2003 elections under Zoramthanga; **the Zoram People's
Movement (ZPM) won 27 of 40 seats (37.87% of votes) on 7 November 2023 (result 4 December)**, MNF 10, Congress 1, BJP 2.
The 2023 campaign was dominated by the Manipur violence and Assam border disputes; the MNF's manifesto emphasized
"unification of Zo people." **ZPM Chief Minister Lalduhoma** has sheltered Kuki-Zo refugees and, per one opinion
piece, supported the reunification movement (⚠️ an India Today NE opinion piece, not a government statement).

## Section 5 — Special legal regimes, in one place

| Regime | Where | Effect that matters for partition |
|---|---|---|
| **Article 371A** | Nagaland (1962-63) | Parliament cannot legislate on Naga religious or social practice, customary law, or land ownership without Assembly consent; the FNTA agreement explicitly preserves it |
| **Article 371G** | Mizoram | Same design for Mizo customary law and land |
| **Article 371C / 1971 Hill Areas Act** | Manipur | Hill Areas Committee of the Assembly; district councils weaker than Sixth Schedule ones (⚠️ general knowledge) |
| **Sixth Schedule** | Mizoram (Chakma, Lai, Mara ADCs); Assam (Bodoland Territorial Council, North Cachar Hills, Karbi Anglong); not Manipur or Nagaland | Ten Sixth Schedule districts exist in NE India (source consulted) |
| **Inner Line Permit** | Arunachal, Nagaland, Mizoram, Manipur (from 11 December 2019) | Entry control; originated in the Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation of 1873 |
| **AFSPA** | Manipur (extended six months from 1 October 2026, "except 13 police stations in five districts"); Nagaland; Arunachal's three Naga districts | ⚠️ Nagaland/Arunachal status from general knowledge |

The **land-law asymmetry in Manipur is the operating grievance**: the hill districts (roughly 90% of the land) are
protected tribal land that valley people cannot buy, while the valley (~10%) is open to all; Meitei nationalist
politics answers with an ST-status demand, an ILP, and a 1951-61 cutoff NRC, and Kuki-Zo politics reads all three as a
threat. The **Meitei ST demand** was the proximate trigger of 3 May 2023: on **14 April 2023** the Manipur High Court
recommended ST status for the Meitei, and the All Tribal Student Union Manipur held a "Tribal Solidarity March" on 3 May.
⚠️ The High Court's directive was later withdrawn by a division bench in early 2024; that is from general knowledge and
not re-verified.

## Section 6 — History in dated compression

**Naga.** 1866 British consolidation of the Naga Hills; **1918** Naga Club; **1929** memorandum to the Simon
Commission; **1935** Tuensang an Excluded Area; 1945-46 Naga National Council (NNC) under **A. Z. Phizo** (Angami);
**14 August 1947** declaration of independence (⚠️ general knowledge); **1951** plebiscite (NNC claims near-unanimity;
**no Mao village in Manipur participated**, per CLAWS); 1948 Tuensang under NEFA; **1957** merger with the Naga Hills
District; **1960** 16-Point Agreement; **1 December 1963** statehood; **1975 Shillong Accord** (rejected by dissidents) →
**1980 NSCN** (Isak Chishi Swu, T. Muivah, S. S. Khaplang); **30 April 1988** split into **NSCN-IM** (about 5,000
members) and **NSCN-K** (under 500; Khaplang died 2017, Yung Aung now leads it from Sagaing); splinters NSCN-U (2007),
NSCN-KK (Khango Konyak, 2011), NSCN-R (2015), NSCN-KN (2018), NSCN-K-NS (2020). **1997** ceasefire with IM; **3 August
2015 Framework Agreement** (R. N. Ravi, Isak Chishi Swu and Muivah, in Modi's presence) after "over 80 rounds of
talks"; the Center "recognized the unique history, culture and position of the Nagas"; the text is undisclosed.

**Manipur.** Kangleipak polity (Ningthouja dynasty); **1709-1748** Pamheiba; **1819-1826** Seven Years Devastation under
Burma; **1834** Kabaw returned to Burma; **1891** Anglo-Manipur War, princely state; **1917-19** Kuki Rebellion; **1947**
Manipur State Constitution Act, elections in 1948 (a constitutional monarchy with an elected Assembly; accession to
India 11 August 1947 per Wikipedia); **21 September 1949 Merger Agreement** signed at Shillong. **Legitimacy dispute
(partisan on both sides):** Meitei nationalist and armed-group sources (e.g., the Revolutionary Peoples' Front's UN
memorandum) say Maharaja Bodhachandra was restricted in Shillong from 17 to 21 September 1949 and signed without
consulting the elected Assembly; an academic paper finds "procedural ambiguities, absence of ratification, and lack of
popular consent," while the Indian position treats the agreement as valid. ⚠️ I did not retrieve the Indian
government's own account or the primary text. **1 November 1956** Union Territory;
**21 January 1972** statehood. The dispute matters because it is the "Kangleipak was never legitimately merged"
claim behind Meitei valley separatism (UNLF, PLA, KYKL, PREPAK) and it sits behind any "Meitei Manipur" option.

**Mizoram.** See §4.4: 1958-61 Mautam; 1961 MNF; 1966 uprising; 30 June 1986 accord; 1987 statehood. ⚠️ The 5 March 1966
aerial bombing of Aizawl and the 1967 village regrouping are from general knowledge and not re-verified.

## Section 7 — The Manipur conflict, 2023-26, in depth

### 7.1 Chronology and casualties

**Outbreak (3 May 2023).** After the High Court's 14 April ST recommendation, the All Tribal Student Union Manipur held
its 3 May march (about 100,000 by one count at Churachandpur); a counter-blockade at Torbung-Kangvai (Bishnupur/
Churachandpur boundary) turned to arson, and by evening violence reached Imphal. **4 May**: curfews in eight districts;
126 Army columns and 62 paramilitary companies deployed by 14 May. **12-14 June 2023: Khamenlok clash
(Kangpokpi)**: about 3,000 Meitei assailants (Arambai Tenggol and UNLF cadres per Wikipedia) attacked Kuki villages;
official count 9 Meitei and 1 Kuki dead; unofficial claims of 200-300 Meitei dead are not corroborated (⚠️ I flag
them as an outlier). **18 May 2023**: all 10 Kuki-Zo legislators, including BJP members, demanded a "separate
administration." The Supreme Court described an "absolute breakdown of constitutional machinery" (1 August 2023) after
a 19 July video of two Kuki women paraded naked surfaced.

Property (Wikipedia, citing official and press tallies): about **4,786 houses** burned; about **400 churches** damaged or

contain the conflict; a Kuki civil-society body's audio recordings, attributed by a private lab to the Chief Minister
at "93% probability," went to the Supreme Court (⚠️ partisan and unadjudicated). **N. Biren Singh resigned 9 February 2025;
President's Rule 13 February 2025 to 4 February 2026.** In the 2024 general election, the BJP lost both Manipur seats
to Congress.

### 7.2 Displacement

Peak about **60,000** (Wikipedia; the figure is repeated across 2023-26 sources); **26,358 in 178 camps by 14 May 2023**;
at least **12,300 Kuki-Zo went to Mizoram** (Mizoram government records, 2023), notably Kolasib (2,744), Aizawl (2,610) and
Saitual (2,229). Hmar and Thadou residents of Jiribam town were displaced to Assam's Cachar in May-June 2024. **Latest
official figure (Chief Minister to the Assembly, ~3-4 September 2026): 36,000 resettled, 28,899 still displaced —
24,530 in 105 relief camps plus 4,369 in temporary accommodation**; Bishnupur has the largest camp population (7,892
in 41 camps), then Churachandpur (5,195 in 19), Imphal West (4,395 in 13) and Kangpokpi (3,389 in 12, plus 2,506
in temporary shelters). ⚠️ "36,000 resettled" is a government claim; the Kuki-Zo Council disputes free movement. The Prime Minister visited
Imphal and Churachandpur in September 2025 and announced a Rs 500 crore package for 7,000 permanent homes for the
displaced, among others (India Today NE).

### 7.3 The geography of separation (the buffer-zone reality)

By June 2023 the state was "divided along a line of armed checkpoints and buffer zones." As of 2025-26: **central
security forces hold strips along the valley-hill foothills** (Army, Assam Rifles, BSF and CRPF each man one of four
barricades on the Imphal-Churachandpur road). Named zones and pressure points (Imphal Reviews, a Meitei-region outlet,
so its framing is Meitei): **Kangpokpi (northern edge, NH-2)**; **Bishnupur's Phougakchao Ikhai (southern gateway)**;
**Tengnoupal/Chandel (Moreh corridor; Pallel)**; Yaingangpokpi (Imphal-Ukhrul road); and Jiribam's boundary with Pherzawl.
Result: Meitei residents "confined to five central districts" and cut off from hill land, and from the road to Moreh
(AH-1); a Meitei farmer in Leitanpokpi (Imphal East) cannot reach two acres in a hillside buffer zone. **The Kuki-Zo
Council/KNO position: "The Buffer Zone is to be respected at all costs," treating it as a permanent line
until a political settlement**; six or seven Kuki organizations warned Meiteis not to cross. **The Union Home Ministry
announced a reopening of NH-2 on 4 September 2025; the Kuki-Zo side said "no free movement has been permitted."**
The government denies the zones exist formally but acknowledges "special security measures"; a Manipur MP called
the zones an "imaginary, unconstitutional line" (India Today NE, 30 June 2025). ⚠️ I did not find a published map of
the zones; Stage 3 would need one drawn from press accounts.

### 7.4 The 2026 Naga-Kuki front

**7 February 2026**: a Tangkhul teacher was beaten in Litan (Ukhrul) after a brawl; **8-11 February**: retaliatory
arson around Litan (Sikibung, Sereikhong, Litan Sareikhong), **74 homes destroyed (police records)**; **March-April**:
killings on both sides, including two Naga civilians (one an Army veteran) on 18 April; **at least 25 deaths by July**
(Scroll); 51 Kuki students evacuated from a school at Ramva, Ukhrul (17 February); the **United Naga Council called a
three-day shutdown and urged suspending "all kinds of social and economic ties with Kukis"** (April). Kamjong (Tangkhul
89.1%) became a main theater: cross-border attacks blamed on Myanmar-based **KNA-B**, drones by May, clashes among Naga
factions (28 March), and **four Tangkhul civilians killed near Pilong, Border Pillars 91-93, on 24 September 2026**.
A Kamjong analysis counts 19 insurgency-related deaths there in 2026 through 27 September (SL Guardian; the statistics
read like South Asia Terrorism Portal data, ⚠️ original source unconfirmed).

**The stated root: land jurisdiction, not a brawl.** Sikibung village claims Litan Sareikhong under a 1967 district-court
judgment; a 1973 accord allowed Kuki settlement for Rs 20,000 (the Naga side says payment was incomplete); the Tangkhul
Naga Long declared Litan under Sikibung in September 2024 and NSCN-IM called it "ancestral land of Sikibung" in
October 2024. The Kuki side counts about **14 gazetted Kuki villages in Ukhrul district**. A Naga MLA: "The Kuki people
want to establish a separate administration, which will include many Naga areas especially in Kangpokpi, Chandel and
Tengnoupal." Security officials describe the conflict as "jurisdictional control of land." **The Kuki UT claim and the
Nagalim claim overlap on the same hill pockets** (Kangpokpi/Senapati, Chandel/Tengnoupal, and Kuki pockets in Ukhrul,
Kamjong, Senapati, Tamenglong, Noney); this overlap, not the brawl, is why the Nagas broke neutrality. Thoubal River
bridge has become a de facto restricted crossing (Scroll).

### 7.5 Politics after President's Rule

**4 February 2026: Yumnam Khemchand Singh (BJP) sworn in as Chief Minister.** He held the first direct talks with
the Kuki-Zo Council in Guwahati on **21 March 2026** ("forgive and forget"); Naga bodies accused him of "appeasement."
A **7 April bomb in Tronglaobi (Bishnupur) killed two small children**; **a May ambush killed three church leaders**
(India Today NE). The Kuki-Zo groups' position (KNO and UPF, **11 and 8
Suspension of Operations groups respectively per a 2023 report**; press conference **10 September 2026**): **Union
Territory with legislature under Article 239A** (a Puducherry model, five districts: Churachandpur, Kangpokpi,
Chandel, Tengnoupal, Pherzawl, with "pockets" in Ukhrul, Kamjong, Senapati, Tamenglong, Noney), "census before NRC,"
"no reintegration." **The Center's answer: MHA interlocutor A. K. Mishra told the groups on 11 March 2025 that "current policy
does not support the creation of new Union Territories,"** and the Kuki side then discussed a referendum. The Meitei side
(COCOMI; also Arambai Tenggol, Meitei Leepun) insists on territorial integrity and has called for a "national war" against
"Chin-Kuki narco-terrorists" (COCOMI, June 2023; ⚠️ partisan). **Manipur's next Assembly election is due in early 2027**
(⚠️ from the constitutional cycle, not retrieved). The SoO agreement with Kuki groups was renewed during President's Rule;
⚠️ its status beyond September 2026 is unverified.

## Section 8 — The Naga process and the Eastern Nagaland exit

**The Framework (2015) and the wall.** NSCN-IM says the 2015 Framework Agreement must be "the basis of signing any final
agreement." The stall points are a **separate Naga flag and constitution** (refused by New Delhi) and **"Greater
Nagalim" territorial integration** (rejected by Assam, Manipur and Arunachal). The Framework reportedly provides for
shared sovereign powers via a renegotiated division of competencies, but **no change to state boundaries**; instead
"cultural integration through special measures" and autonomy for Naga areas outside Nagaland (CLAWS 2018, citing
speculation, not a text). **AK Mishra** (former Intelligence Bureau special director) has replaced R. N. Ravi as interlocutor.
The latest item found: an NSCN-IM and Peace Advisory Committee meeting in mid-September 2026 about delay and an "apex
committee" (ANI, 16 September 2026, headline only). ⚠️ **The content of any draft settlement is not public.** The 2001
extension of the ceasefire to Manipur's Naga hill districts triggered Meitei-wide protests (CLAWS; ⚠️ the 18 deaths in
June 2001 are general knowledge).

**The 2016-17 blockade.** The seven new Manipur districts fragmented Naga majorities (Noney out of Tamenglong raised Kuki parity;
Kamjong out of Ukhrul diluted Tangkhul dominance; Tengnoupal out of Chandel; Sadar Hills/Kangpokpi out of Senapati,
per CLAWS's account), and the UNC's protest produced "the longest ever economic blockade in the state in 2016-17."

**Eastern Nagaland / Frontier Nagaland Territory (FNT).** Tuensang, Mon, Longleng, Kiphire (and later Noklak and Shamator)
were the old **Tuensang Frontier Division**, run as an Excluded Area under NEFA, merged into the Naga Hills District in 1957,
and its special regional council was abolished in 1974. The Eastern Naga People's Organization (ENPO; formed 1994
as the Tuensang Mon People's Organization; formal separate-state demand 2007; other sources say 2010) demanded a separate
state (originally with two Arunachal districts, then six Nagaland districts only), 40 of 60 assembly seats (currently 20),
and boycotted the 2023 election until Amit Shah's assurances. **Tripartite talks (Center, Nagaland, ENPO): December 2024
proposal; July 2025 third meeting; December 2025 fifth meeting; agreement signed on or about 5-7 February 2026 (sources
disagree on the date)** for a **Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority**: six districts, **46 subjects** with executive
and legislative powers, a mini-secretariat under an Additional Chief Secretary, development funds shared by population and area,
initial costs borne by the MHA, Article 371A preserved, ENPO's separate-state demand dropped, a **10-year review** (IDSA).
⚠️ Implementation status is unknown. This makes Eastern Nagaland an *autonomy-within-Nagaland* case for now, not a
secession case, and moves the Nagaland-only option from one unit to "one unit with a formally autonomous eastern third."

## Section 9 — Myanmar spillover, borders, drugs and arms

**Border scale.** The India-Myanmar border is **1,643 km** across Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur (about 398 km) and
Mizoram. The **Free Movement Regime** (16 km either side, 14-day stays under the 2018 agreement) was scrapped:
announced February 2024, replaced by biometric QR-coded border passes in December 2024. A **Rs 31,000 crore 10-year
fencing plan** was approved in September 2024; about **30 km (under 2%)** was fenced by 2025, about 10 km at Moreh. **Mizoram and Nagaland assemblies passed resolutions against fencing; Manipur and Arunachal
supported the Center.** The border was drawn across Naga, Kuki-Zo and Chin lands by the 1826 Yandabo treaty and the 1953
Nehru-U Nu demarcation, which NNC and NSCN reject (CLAWS).

**Refugees.** About **78,731 Myanmar refugees in India since the 2021 coup, of whom 34,350 are in Mizoram (the largest
share)**; over 4,000 Chin entered in July 2025 after clashes; 34 more in April 2026 fleeing Chin National Army
conscription. The Mizoram government refused the Center's biometric collection order and provides food, shelter, health
and education (via search summaries of The Diplomat and others; the Diplomat page itself returned 403). Manipur took a restrictive
line. The **Zo Reunification Organization (ZoRO)** published, on 20 May 2024, a warning that it might take up
arms if FMR was suspended and the border fenced.

**Cross-border armed geography.** NSCN-K (Yung Aung faction) and, reportedly, Meitei valley groups have bases in Sagaing; **KNA-B**
(Kuki National Army-Burma) attacks Tangkhul settlements in Kamjong; in April 2026 **four suspected KNA-B cadres were
killed by NSCN-IM forces (13 April) and five by an NSCN faction (24 April)**; Myanmar's PDF, an NSCN-IM "Eastern Flank" and
the Shanni National Army are also present. About 100 cadres from valley insurgent groups were assessed (by Army
intelligence, per Wikipedia) to have crossed from Myanmar to fight on the Meitei side in 2023. On 11 April 2026 a cache
near Border Pillar 115 held AK-47s, 16 mortar bombs, 6 RPG rounds, 5 grenades and 461 rounds. Chin State's
resistance forces (Chin National Army, Chin Brotherhood) took Falam and the last junta base in the state in 2025 (MSEA
survey §3.1); the Naga SAZ and Sagaing are the resistance heartland. **Consequence: any post-collapse Zo, Naga or
Meitei entity has a civil-war neighbor across its whole eastern flank.**

**Drugs and arms (fact, not moralizing).** UNODC's Myanmar Opium Survey 2025 (December 2025): **53,100 hectares of poppy,
up from 45,200; eastern Shan +32%, Chin State +26%; 552 ha in northern Sagaing near India, a first.** UNODC identifies
**Mizoram, and the Champhai corridor bordering Chin State, as an important entry point for methamphetamine**, moved on to
Silchar (Barak Valley). **Manipur's reported drug seizures rose from 284 kg (2024) to 2,556 kg (2025)** (⚠️ enforcement,
not necessarily volume). Typical seizures: 2.285 kg of methamphetamine tablets (about Rs 1.83 crore) in Saitual. The "war
on drugs" also fed the conflict: the Meitei-side "Chin-Kuki narco-terrorist" frame and 2023 forest-eviction drives
against Kuki villages (⚠️ partisan framing on both sides).

## Section 11 — Each candidate entity: claim, base, contiguity, viability, fault lines

Population bases are **2011 census** sums computed from §3; nothing here is a sourced projection.

### 11.1 Nagalim, maximal (the NSCN-IM map)

- **Real claim.** About **120,000 sq km** versus Nagaland's 16,527-16,579 (SATP, NSCN-IM's own published map, 2015 Framework
  Agreement context). Includes Nagaland; Manipur's **Tamenglong, Senapati, Ukhrul, Chandel** ("significant parts" of four
  districts); Arunachal's **Tirap, Changlang, Dibang Valley and Lohit** (Longding was created later from Tirap; ⚠️ Dibang
  Valley and Lohit are in Arunachal outside this pass's Naga-district set and not analyzed); Assam's **Karbi Anglong and North
  Cachar Hills**, and parts of Golaghat, Sibsagar, Dibrugarh, Tinsukia, Jorhat; and Myanmar territory divided between Kachin
  State and Sagaing. ULFA, though it once trained with NSCN-IM, called the Assam claim "neither credibility nor any historical
  basis" (CLAWS, partisan Assamese source).
- **Real population base.** Nagaland 1,978,502; Manipur five Naga-core districts 610,053; Arunachal three districts about
  260,000-317,000; Assam Dima Hasao 214,102 and Karbi Anglong ⚠️ 660,955+; Myanmar NSAZ 116,828 plus unquantified areas. A
  "whole-district maximal" sum is therefore about **3.8-4.2 million** (⚠️ my arithmetic; the upper end assumes undivided Karbi Anglong of roughly 965,000, a figure I recall but did not retrieve), of which Naga are certainly a
  minority in Assam's two hill districts and in Changlang.
- **Contiguity.** Nagaland-Senapati/Ukhrul/Tamenglong is contiguous. Arunachal's three districts touch Mon and Tuensang/Noklak
  (contiguous). Assam's Dima Hasao borders Peren and Noney/Tamenglong; Karbi Anglong borders Wokha/Dimapur (⚠️ adjacencies from general knowledge).
  ⚠️ Contiguity for a Myanmar extension depends on Sagaing's control. Physical contiguity is plausible (a belt along the
  Patkai), but it would cut Assam's rail (Lumding-Badarpur, which runs through Dima Hasao) and the Kohima-Dimapur road.
- **Viability.** Landlocked; needs Assam's hubs and, in effect, a settlement with whoever holds Guwahati and Dimapur.
  Nagaland alone generates 88 MW against a 243 MW demand. The economy is transfer-dependent.
- **Internal fault lines.** Tangkhul vs. others; eastern vs. western Nagas; Heraka vs. Christian Zeme; Poumai/Thangal
  territorial splits; opposition from three states; Dimasa, Karbi, Kuki, Chin and Kachin counter-claims.
- **Would hold:** every Manipur Naga tribe agrees on territorial integration; a shared Baptist church network; a
  40-year movement with an organization and a 2015 recognition of "unique history." **Would not:** the maximal map is
  non-Naga-majority in Assam and partly in Arunachal; the Naga are split on process; the FNTA shows Eastern Nagas prefer
  autonomy inside Nagaland; and the 2026 Kuki-Zo front has armed the contested hill pockets.

### 11.2 Nagaland-only (with or without the FNTA)

- **Claim.** The existing state (17 districts). **1,978,502 (2011); ~16,527-16,579 sq km.** Contiguous. 87.9% Christian; Dimapur
  (378,811; 61.8% Christian, 28.8% Hindu, 8.24% Muslim) is the non-Naga economic hub and gateway to Assam's rail.
- **Viability.** Rice is 80% of cropped area at low productivity; 88 MW versus 243 MW demand.
- **Fault lines.** Eastern (28.9%) vs. Western; the Kuki and Kachari minority (Kuki 18,391 by language; Kachari claims to
  Dimapur, "the capital of the medieval Kachari kingdom," per CLAWS); 371A protects Naga land, which leaves Dimapur's
  non-Nagas legally second-class in land ownership (⚠️ inference from 371A).
- **Would hold:** the most settled of the Naga options; the state already exists. **Would not:** it excludes the Tangkhul
  heartland, and the Tangkhul lead NSCN-IM.

### 11.3 Eastern Nagaland / Frontier Nagaland as its own unit

- **Claim.** Six districts: Tuensang, Mon, Longleng, Kiphire, Noklak, Shamator. **571,344 (2011, 28.9%)**; contiguous; borders
  Myanmar on the east and Arunachal's Tirap/Changlang/Longding to the north and east. Konyak (244,135 speakers), Chang, Phom,
  Sangtam, Yimkhiung, Khiamniungan, Tikhir.
- **Viability.** Weakest infrastructure in the region ("the terrain ranges from hundreds to nearly 4,000 m"; 56.99% literacy in
  Mon vs. 91.6% in Mokokchung); no rail; the Konyak cross-border ties and NSCN-K legacy give it a Myanmar orientation.
- **Fault lines.** Konyak vs. the smaller tribes; the FNTA (2026) already answers most of the grievance short of secession.
- **Would hold:** a genuine, recent, formalized autonomy claim with an apex body (ENPO). **Would not:** 46-subject autonomy
  was accepted in place of statehood in 2026; a separate country from it requires reversing that.

### 11.4 Zeliangrong (a Naga sub-option)

- **Claim.** A homeland of about **12,000 sq km** across Peren (Nagaland), Tamenglong, Noney, Senapati and Kangpokpi parts
  (Manipur) and Dima Hasao (Assam); the ZUF (2011) as the militant vehicle; the Heraka (Jadonang, Gaidinliu) as the
  religious core.
- **Population.** **No total retrieved.** A floor from the 2011 language figures I have: Zeme speakers in Nagaland 71,954, Rongmei
  3.83% of Manipur (about 109,000), Zeme in Dima Hasao about 20,700 (9.65% of 214,102) sum to ~200,000; the true Zeliangrong
  total, with Liangmai and Inpui, is higher (⚠️ gap).
- **Contiguity.** The Tamenglong-Noney-Peren-Dima Hasao belt is contiguous; Peren (95,219) is the only Nagaland district.
- **Viability.** Small, upland, no rail, and it sits on NH-37's Noney-Jiribam stretch and the Jiribam-Imphal rail line (the
  141 m Noney bridge) — a chokepoint that matters to any Meitei valley option.
- **Would hold:** a real named identity and homeland movement; the tri-junction is contiguous. **Would not:** a Christian /
  Heraka split, two competing militant groups, and dependence on the Nagalim project.

### 11.5 Meitei Manipur

Two sub-options.

**11.5a Valley-only Meitei state.** Imphal West, Imphal East, Thoubal, Kakching, Bishnupur (and Jiribam). **1,589,834
(excluding Jiribam) or 1,633,672 (including)**; about 1,813 sq km (⚠️ ~700 sq mi, the valley floor only); density about 900 per
sq km. Loktak and 105 MW. **Contiguity** is fine (a single valley) and **every arterial road passes through Naga or Kuki-Zo
hills**: NH-2 via Kangpokpi/Senapati, NH-37 via Noney/Jiribam, AH-1 via Tengnoupal. **Fault lines**: Meitei Hindu vs. Sanamahi
revivalism (Hindu 61.5-74.8% by valley district, Sanamahi 10-17%), Meitei vs. Pangal (Thoubal 25.42% Muslim, Jiribam
34.07%), the historic question whether Meitei valley is the "core" of Manipur or of an older Kangleipak that included the
hill kingdoms. **Hold:** a single culturally cohesive core with an established capital and lake economy. **Not hold:**
landlocked *and* hill-encircled; the buffer-zone experience of 2023-26 is exactly what a valley-only state would face as
a permanent condition; the 1949 legitimacy dispute is a Meitei nationalist claim, but the mainstream Meitei civil-society
position (COCOMI) is "territorial integrity of Manipur," not a valley-only state.

**11.5b Manipur as one (current territory).** **2,855,794 (2011); 22,327 sq km; 16 districts.** Meitei ~53%, Naga ~21.8% and
Kuki-Zo ~15.7% of the population. **It is the rule-compliant option** (whole state, zero exceptions) and the one the Center and the
Meitei side both defend. **Costs:** it keeps the three-way fault inside one border, which as of 2026 is a two-front partition on the
ground (Meitei/Kuki-Zo; Naga/Kuki-Zo), with the Meitei calling Naga integration a "dangerous game" leading to the
balkanization of Manipur and the Assembly resolved to protect territorial integrity (CLAWS 2018). A Manipur government would be a
valley government ruling hills (42.8% of the people, most of the land) it cannot enter without central forces.

### 11.6 The Kuki-Zo bloc (Manipur hills)

- **Claim.** UT with legislature over five districts: **Churachandpur, Kangpokpi, Chandel, Tengnoupal, Pherzawl**, plus
  pockets in Ukhrul, Kamjong, Senapati, Tamenglong, Noney (KNO/UPF and Kuki-Zo Council; MHA rejected it in 2025).
- **Population base.** The three pre-2016 districts (Churachandpur, Kangpokpi via old Senapati, Chandel) = **612,069**
  people in 2011 (21.4% of Manipur), against a **Kuki-Zo statewide total of only 448,197**, so **at least about 164,000 (⚠️ my
  arithmetic; the Kuki-Zo total also includes a small number in Naga districts, so the non-Kuki-Zo share of the five
  districts is likely somewhat higher) residents of the claimed area are not Kuki-Zo**: Nepali (16.0% of Kangpokpi, about
  31,000), Naga and Old Kuki/Naga (Anal 17.9% and Maring 15.3% of pre-split Chandel; Maring 34.0% of Tengnoupal), Meitei and Tamil
  traders at Moreh, and Hindus (Kangpokpi 17.68%, Tengnoupal 10.01%).
- **Contiguity.** ⚠️ **Open.** Churachandpur, Pherzawl and Chandel/Tengnoupal form a southern and eastern block that is
  contiguous, and adjacency lists show Noney bordering both Kangpokpi and Churachandpur/Pherzawl; whether Kangpokpi joins the
  southern block by a real land boundary or only via Noney (a Zeliangrong district) needs GIS (Stage 3). The bloc is a
  **horseshoe around the valley**, which is why the "pockets" in Naga districts are in the demand.
- **Viability.** Border with Mizoram (Pherzawl/Churachandpur), Myanmar's Chin State (Tedim/Falam), Moreh trade corridor
  through Tengnoupal; **no sea access**; hill agriculture; poppy and methamphetamine transit issues; Tipaimukh dam on the
  Barak (Pherzawl; ⚠️ project history from general knowledge).
- **Fault lines.** Kuki vs. Zomi vs. Paite vs. Hmar naming (the 2024 Kuki-Zo Council was an attempt to unify these); the
  1997-98 Kuki-Paite conflict (22,000 displaced); Zou/Paite/Hmar preference for "Zo" leans toward Mizoram; Kuki-Naga
  hostility (1992-97, 2026); the non-Kuki-Zo minorities in the claim area.
- **Would hold:** de facto separation is already the reality in Churachandpur and Kangpokpi; a coordinated umbrella body and
  armed groups under SoO; cross-border kin. **Would not:** the internal identity split; overlap with Nagalim; and the Center's
  refusal.

### 11.7 Mizoram

- **Claim.** The current state: **1,097,206 (2011); ~21,081 sq km; 11 districts**; contiguous; borders Bangladesh (Mamit,
  Lawngtlai), Myanmar (Champhai, Saiha, Lawngtlai), Assam (Kolasib, Mamit), Tripura (Mamit) and Manipur (Pherzawl).
- **Viability.** Best infrastructure of the three: Sairang railhead (2025), highest literacy (Serchhip 97.91%, Aizawl
  98.5%), 3.77% below the poverty line (2023, Wikipedia), HDI 0.810 (Wikipedia); hydro 72 MW; the Kaladan route to Sittwe
  and the Bangladesh posts at Marpara and Tuipuibari. Weakest food base (jhum agriculture; 65% of people on the land).
- **Fault lines.** Mizo vs. Chakma (Buddhist; Lawngtlai 43.72% Buddhist), Lai, Mara, Bru; refugees (34,350 Chin, ~12,300 Kuki-Zo
  IDPs) and the ZoRO reunification movement pulling in both directions; the July 2021 Assam-Mizoram border clash (⚠️ general
  knowledge, six Assam policemen killed).
- **Would hold:** the 1986 accord, a 40-year democratic tradition, a ZPM government with a 27-of-40 mandate, 87% Christian
  homogeneity. **Would not:** its ADC minorities in the south and its proximity to two civil wars.

### 11.8 Zo union (Mizoram plus Kuki-Zo Manipur, and perhaps Chin)

- **Claim.** Zo reunification, first voiced at the **1892 Chin-Lushai Conference in Calcutta** (Scroll); the 1986 accord
  recognized but did not grant it. Organizations: ZoRO/ZRO, KNO, Kuki-Zo Council, Zomi Council, Kuki Inpi Manipur, and in
  Mizoram the **Mizo Integration Council (launched 15 October 2025)** and **MNF-Integration Movement (September 2025)**.
- **Base.** Mizoram 1,097,206 + Churachandpur/Pherzawl (274,143) = about 1.37 million; adding all five Kuki-claim districts
  612,069 gives 1.71 million (⚠️ my sums); with Chin State the total is about 2.7 million (⚠️ see §4.3).
- **Contiguity.** Pherzawl and Churachandpur meet Mizoram's Champhai/Kolasib border; the Chin State side is contiguous via
  Champhai-Zokhawthar.
- **Fault lines.** Kuki-Zo groups' current demand is a UT *within* India, not merger with Mizoram (Scroll); Mizoram's ADC
  minorities would be diluted; the Naga overlap; the Myanmar civil war; "Zo" is not accepted by every Kuki group.
- **Would hold:** shared Christianity, kinship and language family; the ZPM government's sympathy; refugee and IDP flows
  already linking the populations. **Would not:** no single organization; Chin State sits in another survey's scope.

## Section 13 — Where real ethnic geography breaks the whole-first-order rule

In India the first-order unit is the state, so **every non-A option needs a district-level exception**; the geoBoundaries
ADM2 layer the first-pass survey already used carries districts, so the geometry exists (unlike the Iraq case, which had no
admin-2 layer). The rule is compatible with district exceptions only where a district is demographically clean. From the §3.2
table:

| Category | Districts | Evidence (2011) |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean Meitei valley** | Imphal West, Imphal East, Thoubal, Kakching, Bishnupur | Manipuri 90.9-99.35%; ST 0.2-4.7%; Muslim (Pangal) 4.7-25.4% |
| **Clean Naga** | Ukhrul, Kamjong, Senapati, Tamenglong | Tangkhul 87-89%; Poumai/Mao/Maram ~89% of Senapati; Rongmei majority; ST 92.7-96.6%. Caveats: Kamjong is 6.6% Thadou and Ukhrul has ~14 gazetted Kuki villages |
| **Clean Kuki-Zo** | Churachandpur, Pherzawl | Kuki tribes 87% of Churachandpur; ST 92.9%; Pherzawl official language Hmar |
| **Mixed / contested (the flagged decision)** | **Kangpokpi** (Thadou 52.9%, Nepali 16.0%, ST 79.76%, Hindu 17.68%), **Chandel** (pre-split Thadou 37.7, Anal 17.9, Maring 15.3), **Tengnoupal** (Maring 34.0, Thadou 33.6, Manipuri 8.03, urban 28.5%), **Noney** (Rongmei majority with Inpui, Thadou, Gangte), **Jiribam** (Bengali 50.8, Manipuri 30.5, Hmar 8.04; Muslim 34%) | The Naga-claim and Kuki-claim maps both contain the first four; Jiribam is a Meitei/Pangal/Bengali/Hmar mixture on the Assam border |

**Flagged decision (author).** If any Manipur split is chosen, the project needs an explicit rule for the five mixed
districts: (1) assign whole to the plurality group and accept a misfit (Kangpokpi to Kuki-Zo, Chandel/Tengnoupal to
Kuki-Zo or Naga by Maring's classification, Noney to Naga); (2) mark them Disputed/Uncertain Territory (the Iraq-Diyala and
Fergana precedents); or (3) leave Manipur whole. **Maring and Anal are "Old Kuki/Naga"** and are claimed by both sides
in Chandel and Tengnoupal; 74,978 Old Kuki/Naga statewide (2.6%) is the size of this classification problem. Also note
that the Kuki UT claim is drafted around district names precisely to avoid this problem ("without redefining districts"),
which the Naga side reads as annexing Naga pockets.

**The Mizoram side needs no exception** as a whole state. Inside it, Lawngtlai (Chakma 39.3%, Buddhist 43.72%) and Saiha (Mara
72.1%) are the minority-majority districts; neither is a separate-unit candidate on any evidence retrieved.

  scope for Myanmar as an open author call) is a direct seam decision. The **Kabaw Valley** (Tamu, Moreh's twin) is Manipur's
  historic claim on Sagaing.
