## Headline findings

1. **Bangladesh is the one country in this project where the real-world border and the real-world nation
   already nearly coincide, and that is the finding that makes it structurally unlike Central Asia.** About
   **99% of the population is Bengali** and speaks one language; the state is a 1971 language-nation carved
   out of a 1947 religion-nation. Woodard's First Effective Settlement fails here for a fifth distinct reason
   (§1): there was no arriving founding population; the closest thing to a "founding settlement" is an
   *internal agrarian frontier*, the clearing of the deltaic forests under Muslim pioneer-saints between the
   13th and 17th centuries (Richard Eaton's thesis, corroborated on Wikipedia's Islam in Bangladesh page).
2. **Bangladesh's ethnic fault line runs mostly on the far side of its border, not inside it.** The Bengali
   identity straddles the frontier with West Bengal (out of scope), Tripura and Assam's Barak Valley
   (both in the slice). Inside Bangladesh the live fracture is **ideological rather than territorial**:
   secular-linguistic "Bangalee" nationalism (Awami League, 1971) against Islamic "Bangladeshi" nationalism
   (BNP and Jamaat), with only a soft regional coloring (§9.1).
3. **The single real territorial fracture inside Bangladesh is the Chittagong Hill Tracts (CHT).** Three
   districts (Rangamati, Bandarban, Khagrachhari), **1,842,815 people, ~13,344 km2**, and at the 2022 census
   **49.94% indigenous** against **50.06% Bengali**, in a region that was overwhelmingly Buddhist and
   Tibeto-Burman-speaking at partition. The 1997 Peace Accord is, by the signatory party's account, **nearly
   two-thirds unimplemented after 28 years**, and September 2025 saw fresh deadly violence (§6).
4. **Bangladesh breaks the project's "whole first-order subdivisions" rule in exactly one important place.**
   The CHT is not a division; it is three districts inside **Chattogram Division (33.2 million)**, which also
   holds the country's port, the Rohingya camps and the Comilla/Noakhali Bengali plain. Drawing the CHT
   without splitting a division is impossible (§3.3).
5. **The state's whole economy hangs on three concentrated dependencies:** ready-made garments (**$39.34
   billion, 81.49% of exports, FY2024-25**), one port (**Chattogram handles ~92% of trade and ~98% of
   containerized cargo**), and imported energy (LNG bill ~$3.88 billion in 2025 as domestic gas declines).
   This is Bangladesh's version of Oceania's import lifeline and Central Asia's remittance crux (§9).
6. **The Ganges Water Treaty expires in December 2026, and the geography of the water disputes splits across
   the project's scope line.** The **Ganges/Farakka** barrage sits in Murshidabad, in the out-of-scope rest of
   West Bengal. But the **Teesta's entire Indian course (Sikkim, Kalimpong, Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri, Cooch
   Behar) lies inside the breakaway slice**. In the project's fiction the Teesta dispute would become
   Bangladesh against a slice polity, while the Farakka dispute stays Bangladesh against rump India (§8).
7. **The 2015 India–Bangladesh enclave exchange is the ready-made precedent for sub-district exclave
   handling**, and it happened almost wholly on the slice-side border (Cooch Behar, Tripura, Assam,
   Meghalaya). It is the direct counterpart to the Fergana enclave problem in the Central Asia survey (§10.5).
8. **The 2024 uprising and 2026 election reset the country, but this pass found only thin sourcing for the
   post-February-2026 period** (§5.6). Hasina was ousted 5 August 2024, sentenced to death in absentia
   17 November 2025; the BNP won a two-thirds-scale landslide on 12 February 2026 (209 of 297 counted
   seats). The Awami League has been banned since 10 April 2025. ⚠️ Anything after February 2026 rests on
   one Wikipedia page and search summaries.

## Section 1 — Method: why first effective settlement fails here, and what replaces it

Per `METHOD - How Nations Get Drawn.md`, Woodard's doctrine needs an arriving population founding a
self-perpetuating society in territory that was open from the colonizer's point of view. Every prior region
failed it for a different reason:

- **East Asia / Russia:** continuous imperial cores absorbing neighbors; no founding moment.
- **Mainland Southeast Asia:** the defining layer (Indianization) was adopted, not imposed.
- **Central Asia:** the borders were engineered by an outside power specifically to prevent a unified nation.
- **Bangladesh fails it a fifth way: the founding event is an *internal* frontier of agrarianization and
  conversion, and the borders are the product of two successive partitions that each split a pre-existing
  Bengali whole along a different axis (religion in 1947, then geography-plus-language in 1971).**

### 1.1 The nearest thing to a "first effective settlement": the deltaic rice-and-pir frontier

The best-known scholarly account (Richard Eaton, *The Rise of Islam and the Bengal Frontier*, cited via
Wikipedia's **Islam in Bangladesh** page) holds that during the Mughal period **eastern Bengal was still
heavily forested but highly fertile**, and that peasants were incentivized to bring land under cultivation
under the leadership of Muslim pioneer-saints (pirs), so that "Islam became the main religion in the delta."
Sufi missionaries are described there as "the single most important factor" in conversion in what is now
Bangladesh, and conversions reached tribal peoples in interior Chittagong. Syncretism is documented: local
deities of smallpox, cholera and snakes (Shitala, Olabibi, Manasa) were folded into the pir tradition.

**What this means for the method.** This is Woodard's mechanism in miniature, minus the arriving
population: a *pioneer institution* (the shrine-and-clearing network) set the lasting character of the
territory, regardless of who the later demographic majority was. It is also why the **east of the delta is
Muslim-majority and the west of the delta (Bengal proper, mostly India today) was more Hindu** in 1947, and
why Bangladesh's Hindu share tracks the old *older-settled* and zamindar districts (§4.4). ⚠️ This is a
scholarly thesis, not a settled fact; it is contested in detail and should be used as a framing device, not
a claim. Confidence: medium.

### 1.2 The double break

| Break | What it split | Axis | What it left |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1905** (annulled 1911) | Bengal into East Bengal and Assam / West Bengal | Administrative; Muslim-majority east | A template: Dhaka as a capital of the east |
| **1947** (Radcliffe, published 17 Aug 1947) | Bengal into East Bengal (Pakistan) and West Bengal (India); Sylhet separately by referendum | **Religion** | A Bengali-speaking Muslim-majority "East Pakistan" |
| **1971** | East Pakistan from West Pakistan | **Language and regional grievance** | A Bengali-language nation-state |

The 1947 break made a state out of religion; the 1971 break unmade it on language. Bangladeshi politics
still carries both layers (§9.1). **This is the country's real "tier" structure, and it is the mirror image
of Central Asia's:** there, one people was partitioned into five states; here, one language-people was
partitioned into two states (Bangladesh and West Bengal) by religion, and then a *second* state (Pakistan)
was dissolved off it by language.

### 1.3 The tier table (following the Central Asia and Southeast Asia format)

| Tier 1 | Example Tier 2 identities | Where | Historical mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bengali (Indo-Aryan) — the deltaic agrarian core** | Regional Bengali speech communities: Sylheti, Chittagonian, Noakhali, Bakerganj/Barisal, Rajshahi-Varendra, Rangpur; plus Bengali Hindu (7.95%) | All eight divisions; ~99% of the population | Wet-rice agrarian frontier, Muslim pioneer settlement, Sultanate and Mughal province |
| **Tibeto-Burman highland (Zomia's western edge)** | Chakma (Indo-Aryan-derived speech but Buddhist hill people), Marma, Tripura, Mro, Bawm, Tanchangya, Khumi, Khyang; Garo; Khasi (Austroasiatic) | CHT, Mymensingh Division, Sylhet Division | Shifting cultivation, hill polities, British-era "Excluded Area" status |
| **Munda / Dravidian plateau migrants** | Santal, Oraon, Munda | Rajshahi and Rangpur Divisions (Naogaon, Dinajpur, Rangpur, Gaibandha) | 19th-century plantation and tenancy migration |
| **Arakanese Buddhist** | Rakhine | Barguna, Patuakhali (Kuakata), Cox's Bazar, CHT | 1784 Burmese conquest of Arakan; refuge migration |
| **Urdu-speaking (Bihari) camp population** | — | ~66 camps, Dhaka, Saidpur, Mymensingh, Khulna | 1947 and 1971 |
| **Stateless Rohingya** | — | Cox's Bazar (33 camps), Bhasan Char | Arakan displacement, 1978, 1991-92, 2017-present |

**⚠️ The Tier 1 split is again a simplification.** "Chakma" is a tier-2 identity within the highland tier 1
but the Chakma language descends from Indo-Aryan rather than Tibeto-Burman, a paradox that the Chakma
Wikipedia page notes only obliquely; treat the tier-1 label as a *culture-and-habitat* category (Buddhist
hill peoples) rather than a strict language-family one. The highland tier is the **Zomia** frame of the
Mainland Southeast Asia survey; the term itself (Willem van Schendel, 2002) was coined from the Tibeto-Burman
word for highlander in exactly the India–Bangladesh–Myanmar borderlands.

### 1.4 What replaces the doctrine

**Proposed replacement (research proposes, author disposes): the "frontier-of-cultivation" reading, with
two overlays.**

1. **Core = the deltaic agrarian Bengali civilization.** The identity that persists is Bengali language and
   wet-rice culture on the Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna delta; Tier 2 is the regional speech and land-history
   layer, not a religious layer.
2. **Overlay A (Zomia edge):** the hill peoples of the CHT and the Garo/Khasi belts are *not* part of the
   delta's frontier story; they sit on the massif's western edge, exactly as the Mainland Southeast Asia
   survey treats Shan State. Their lasting character comes from the *hills*, not the delta.
3. **Overlay B (the 1947/1971 religious-and-linguistic scar):** the Bihari camps, the Hindu minority's
   long decline (33% in 1901 to 7.95% in 2022) and the Rohingya are the residue of the two partitions.

**Consequence for the whole-first-order-subdivision rule:** because the delta is ethnically near-uniform, the
*only* place tier 2 outruns the division map is the highland overlay in Chattogram Division (§3.3).
Bangladesh is otherwise the easiest country in the region to draw as a unit.

## Section 2 — Physical geography: a delta first, with two rims

**Area and shape.** Total area **148,460 km2** (land 130,170 km2, water 18,290 km2), a 580 km coastline, and
land borders of about **4,096 km with India** (Wikipedia's border page; the Geography page gives 4,156 km, a
discrepancy of 60 km that this pass did not resolve ⚠️) and **271 km with Myanmar**. About **80% is alluvial
plain** at mostly under 10 m above sea level; the highest point is **Mowdok Taung, 1,052 m**, in the
Chittagong Hills. The Ganges (Padma), Brahmaputra (Jamuna) and Meghna systems "occupy 79 percent of the
country" by Wikipedia's account. The divisional areas sum to about 147,570 km2 (my addition of the
Wikipedia divisions table).

**The four sub-landscapes that matter for a map:**

- **The active delta and the char lands.** Chars are the shifting river islands and bars of the Jamuna and
  Padma. Estimates of how many people live on them vary wildly, from **~2 million (an ADB citation) to ~12
  million (another estimate), covering 5-6% of national territory** ⚠️ (the two figures are irreconcilable
  in the sources found; treat "millions, unsettled" as the honest answer). The Jamuna and Padma together
  eroded about **50,955 ha in 22 years**, making over half a million people homeless; roughly one million
  people are affected by riverbank erosion in a typical year (Daily Star, ResearchGate summaries).
  Kurigram, Gaibandha, Sirajganj (northwest-central) and the Padma reach in Rajshahi and Chandpur are the
  hot spots.
- **The Sundarbans (southwest).** The world's largest mangrove forest at **10,277 km2 overall, about 60%
  (~6,017 km2) in Bangladesh** across Khulna, Satkhira and Bagerhat districts, home to ~106 tigers on the
  Bangladesh side (camera-trap count) and a cyclone buffer for a population of over four million who depend
  on it. Cyclone Sidr (2007) damaged about 40% of the forest. The coal-fired **Rampal plant (1,320 MW)** sits
  14 km from it.
- **The Sylhet haor basin (northeast).** A bowl of large seasonal wetlands (Hakaluki is the largest) fed by
  **eight transboundary rivers and 100+ hill streams from Meghalaya**; the 2022 floods submerged about **94%
  of Sunamganj district and over 84% of Sylhet district** (TBS, Dhaka Tribune search summaries). Flash floods
  arrive with little warning because the Meghalaya slope is steep and it rains on the upstream side of the
  border (§10.3).
- **The Chittagong Hill Tracts and the eastern rim.** The country's only real relief, about 13,344 km2 of
  forested ridges (Mowdok Taung 1,052 m), drained by the **Karnaphuli**, which the **Kaptai Dam (1962)** ponded
  into a **777 km2 lake**, inundating 655 km2 including **40% of the region's cultivable land** and
  displacing about **100,000 people, roughly 70% Chakma** (Wikipedia, Kaptai Dam).

**Climate exposure.** The World Bank's Groundswell projection puts Bangladesh at **up to 13.3 million
internal climate migrants by 2050** (Daily Star, Dhaka Tribune); Wikipedia's Bangladesh climate page says
**62% of coastal land is under 3 m elevation**, the **1991 cyclone killed about 140,000 people**, and the
**Bangladesh Delta Plan 2100** (2018) commits about $3 billion per year. ⚠️ The Wikipedia page also lists a
much lower sea-level-rise displacement figure (0.9 million by 2050 for 0.30 m of rise); the two numbers
measure different scenarios and should not be merged. Confidence: medium.

**Density.** The 2022 enumerated census (165,158,616) over the summed divisional areas gives about **1,119
people per km2** (my arithmetic ⚠️). Wikipedia's demographics page quotes **1,333 per km2** as a "2024
estimate"; by back-calculation that figure appears to divide a ~173.5 million population estimate by *land*
area (130,170 km2), not total area (my inference ⚠️). Either way Bangladesh is among the most densely
populated large countries in the world.

### 3.3 Where real ethnic geography breaks the "whole first-order subdivisions only" rule

The rule (`METHOD`, rule 1; STATUS §0 item 3) is absolute, but Bangladesh strains it in five places, in
descending order of seriousness:

1. **The Chittagong Hill Tracts are three districts inside Chattogram Division.** The CHT is **1.84 million
   of the division's 33.2 million (5.5%)** but roughly **40% of its land area** (13,344 of about 33,909 km2).
   Any treatment of the CHT as its own entity requires either a sphere/overlay, a hatched pocket (the
   Wa/Hakka precedent from the Mainland Southeast Asia and East Asia surveys), or a bounded exception to
   the rule. This is the one place the rule cannot simply be applied. **Decision ⚠️ open (see §6.5).**
2. **The Rohingya camps are in Cox's Bazar District** (Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas), also inside Chattogram
   Division, and mix with a host population of 2.82 million. It is not an ethnic territory in any drawable
   sense but a stateless enclave (§7).
3. **Enclaves and exclaves.** Before 2015 the border carried 162 enclaves; after the exchange the
   **Dahagram-Angarpota** composite enclave (Lalmonirhat) remained connected to the mainland only by the
   leased Tin Bigha Corridor through Cooch Behar (§10.5). Under the project's "no enclaves, no exclaves"
   contiguity rule (`METHOD` rule 3) this is a flagged exception, small enough to ignore at map scale.
4. **The Sundarbans straddle the border** (about 60% Bangladesh, 40% West Bengal, the latter out of scope).
   It sits across three Khulna Division districts, so the division rule handles the Bangladesh half cleanly.
5. **Dacope Upazila (Khulna) is the country's only Hindu-majority upazila (54.44%)**, and several tribal
   pockets (Santal in Naogaon and Dinajpur, Garo in Mymensingh Division, Khasi in Moulvibazar) are
   sub-district. Wherever the CHT rule is settled, these pockets are just tier-2 evidence to compress.

## Section 4 — Ethnolinguistic and religious layers

### 4.1 The Bengali majority and its regional speech communities

Bangladesh is about **99% Bengali** by Wikipedia's demographics page. The official language is Bengali, but
**regional Bengali speech communities** are strong: **Sylheti** (about 11 million speakers across Sylhet
Division, India's Barak Valley and northern Tripura, and about 400,000 in the UK; linguists divide on whether
it is a separate language; not officially recognized in either country), **Chittagonian** and **Noakhali**
(both listed as distinctive regional languages on the Chattogram Division page; speaker counts not sourced
this pass ⚠️), plus the Rajshahi-Varendra, Rangpur and Barisal speech areas. **Sylheti is the clearest
tier-2 case**: it has its own historic script (Sylheti Nagri, obsolete by mid-20th century), a diaspora that
is unusually large (**95% of ethnic British Bangladeshis in 1986 came from the Sylhet region**), and a
status as a "low-status dialect" in a diglossic relationship with standard Bengali.

### 4.2 Indigenous minorities: the census, and why it is contested

(Wikipedia, Ethnic minorities in Bangladesh). Largest groups:

Others named in the sources: **Tanchangya (42,943 in the CHT), Mro (51,724), Bawm, Khumi, Khyang, Chak,

on land-grabbing); plus tea-garden communities (Kurmi, Musahar and others) recruited under British rule.
Bangladesh's constitution recognizes them as "tribes, minor races, ethnic sects and communities" rather than
as indigenous peoples (general knowledge, 2011 amendment).

**⚠️ The census figures are widely disputed and almost certainly low.** Wikipedia's Garo page cites about
**120,000 Garo in Bangladesh** against the ~77,000 the census share implies, and the Rakhine and Khasi
figures show similar spreads. Indigenous advocacy bodies have long claimed several times the census total;
this pass did not find a sourced advocacy figure, so none is used. The safe framing is "**1.65 million
counted, materially undercounted by an unquantified margin**."

### 4.3 The stateless populations

- **Urdu-speaking Biharis ("Stranded Pakistanis").** About **300,000-600,000** in **~66 camps** (Wikipedia's
  demographics page says ~300,000; the dedicated page says 300,000-600,000; advocacy committees claim
  240,000-500,000 ⚠️), concentrated in Dhaka (Geneva Camp, Mohammadpur), with others in Saidpur (Rangpur
  Division, Nilphamari), Mymensingh and Khulna. They are descendants of Bihari Muslims who moved to East
  Pakistan in 1947 and mostly sided with West Pakistan in 1971. A **2008 High Court ruling** extended
  citizenship to those who were minors in 1971 or born afterward; Pakistan says more than 170,000 have been
  repatriated and denies further responsibility (Musharraf, 2002: Pakistan "could not allow them to
  emigrate"). ⚠️ Whether camp residents have full political and property rights in practice was not verified.
- **Rohingya.** Covered in §7.

missions). **Muslims are 99% Sunni** (Hanafi and Maturidi), with about 100,000 Ahmadis and a small Shia
community "particularly among Bihari communities."

**Consequence for the frame:** the religious map does **not** produce a territorial cleavage, because no
division is below 86% Muslim and no non-Muslim majority exists outside Rangamati and one Khulna upazila. The
only religion-plus-ethnicity block that could sustain a border is the hill Buddhist belt in the CHT, and it
is 49.94% indigenous, not a majority.

## Section 5 — History: only what still runs through the map

### 5.1 Sultanate and Mughal Bengal

The **Bengal Sultanate (1352-1576)** established "Bangalah" as a named independent polity; the Ilyas Shahi
dynasty lasted to 1487 and the Hussain Shahi (1494-1538) is regarded as a cultural golden age. **Akbar
conquered Bengal in 1576** after four decades of struggle against the Baro-Bhuyans (twelve landlords, led by
Isa Khan from Sonargaon). Bengal became **"the Mughal Empire's wealthiest province"** (rice, silk, textiles).
**Dhaka became the provincial capital in 1610** and peaked under Shaista Khan (1644-1677). This is the
historical basis for Bengal-as-one-entity that all later Bengali nationalisms invoke.

### 5.2 Colonial Bengal and the seeds of 1947

The **Battle of Plassey (1757)** and the **Permanent Settlement (1793)** established a land-revenue system
(general knowledge) that in East Bengal tended to put Hindu zamindars over Muslim peasants, which fed the
communal politics of the 20th century. **Partition of Bengal, 1905**: East Bengal and Assam, capital Dhaka,
Muslim-majority; **annulled 1911** after nationalist opposition (Wikipedia, History of Bangladesh).

### 5.3 1947: Radcliffe, Sylhet and the Hill Tracts

- **The Radcliffe Line was published 17 August 1947.** East Bengal (East Pakistan) went to Pakistan.
- **Sylhet** had been transferred from Bengal to Assam in 1874; the **6 July 1947 referendum** went **56.56%
  (239,619) for East Bengal/Pakistan against 43.44% (184,041) for Assam/India**, on 423,660 votes cast.
  **Karimganj subdivision, despite a 50.44% vote for Pakistan, was awarded to India** in the Radcliffe Line,
  which is why today's Barak Valley is a Bengali-speaking enclave inside Assam (§10.1).
- **The CHT went to Pakistan despite an overwhelming non-Muslim population** (general knowledge; the
  Buddhist and tribal majority in 1947 is widely cited at 97%+; ⚠️ the exact 1947 Bengali share was not
  verified this pass, though the dated 1974 figure is in §6.1). This is the root of §6.

### 5.4 1952 and 1971

The **Language Movement of 21 February 1952** (general knowledge) made Bengali-language recognition the
founding grievance. After the **1970 election and the devastating 1970 cyclone**, the Pakistani junta under
Yahya Khan launched **Operation Searchlight on 25 March 1971**; the war ran from 26 March to **16 December
1971**, ending in the "largest surrender of armed personnel since the Second World War" (Wikipedia). About
**10 million refugees fled to India** and 30 million were internally displaced. **The death toll is
contested: the Bangladesh government says 3 million; scholarly estimates run from 300,000 to 500,000.** ⚠️
This gap is a live political fact and should not be silently resolved.

### 5.5 1975-2024: coups, military rule, and two dynasties

**15 August 1975:** Sheikh Mujibur Rahman assassinated. **30 May 1981:** Ziaur Rahman assassinated.
**1982-1990:** General Ershad's military rule. **1991:** parliamentary democracy restored. **1996-2001 and
2009-2024:** Sheikh Hasina in power. (Wikipedia, History of Bangladesh; caretaker-government details from
general knowledge.) The constitutional identity was reversed twice: Zia replaced "Bengali" nationalism with
"Bangladeshi" nationalism and removed secularism; Ershad made Islam the state religion (1988); a 2011
amendment restored secularism while keeping Islam as state religion (all general knowledge, not
re-verified). **This flip-flop is the internal fault line** (§9.1).

### 5.6 2024-2026: the uprising, the interim government, the election

⚠️ **This is the least well-sourced part of the file; every claim is dated and attributed.**

- **Quota-reform movement, June-August 2024.** It began after the **5 June 2024** High Court reinstatement of
  the 30% freedom-fighter-descendant quota and escalated into an anti-government uprising whose "sole demand"
  was Hasina's resignation. She **resigned and fled to India on 5 August 2024**; **Muhammad Yunus** was
  sworn in as head of an interim government **on 8 August**. (Wikipedia, Non-cooperation Movement (2024)
  page.)
- **Death toll: three figures, not one.** Wikipedia's page cites "at least 600" (an early UN OHCHR
  preliminary estimate) plus 232+ more in three days after her resignation; the **OHCHR fact-finding report
  (February 2025) states about 1,400 killed**, roughly 12% of them children; another Wikipedia page cites a
  Health Ministry estimate of **1,650**. The honest range is roughly **600 to 1,650**, depending on date,
  source and definition.
- **Hasina's conviction.** On **17 November 2025** the International Crimes Tribunal-1 convicted her (and
  ex-home minister Asaduzzaman Khan Kamal) of crimes against humanity and **sentenced both to death in
  absentia**; Amnesty International condemned the sentences (Al Jazeera, CNN, Newsonair). She lives in
  exile in India; Bangladesh demanded her extradition.
- **Awami League banned** on **10 April 2025** under the Anti-Terrorism Act, "until the International
  Crimes Tribunal completed trials of party leaders."
- **Attacks on Hindus.** The BHBCUC (Hindu-Buddhist-Christian Unity Council) reported **2,010 incidents from
  4 to 20 August 2024** (69 temples) and **2,244 incidents from 4 August 2024 to 30 June 2025**; the interim
  government registered **88 cases of communal violence against Hindus between 5 August and 22 October
  2024** with 70 arrests. Advocacy groups cite over 2,500 incidents through December 2025. ⚠️ The gap
  between the minority council's counts and the government's is large and is itself a political dispute;
  this pass did not adjudicate it. (Wikipedia; NPR, 25 March 2026, covered a "surge in attacks.")
- **12 February 2026: election and constitutional referendum.** **BNP 209 seats, Jamaat-e-Islami 68, National
  Citizen Party 6**; the BNP alliance 212 and the Jamaat-led 11-party alliance 77, of 297 constituencies
  counted; **turnout 59.44%**. The **July Charter referendum passed with 68% "yes" on ~60% turnout**, which
  turns the new parliament into a constituent assembly (Al Jazeera, Wikipedia). **Tarique Rahman**, returned
  after 17 years of exile in the UK, was sworn in as **prime minister on 17 February 2026** with a
  30-member cabinet. The Awami League did not contest under its own name. ⚠️ Discrepancy: the Wikipedia
  election page says 300 directly elected seats; the results tallies quoted above are out of 297 (the
  difference is presumably postponed or withheld constituencies; not verified).
- **After February 2026 (thin).** The Rahman government is described as seeking to "normalize" India
  relations; the PM visited China 21-26 June 2026 (17 MoUs, a "Comprehensive Strategic Cooperative
  Partnership"); it is reviewing the US trade deal after a US Supreme Court ruling; fuel prices rose in
  mid-2026 amid an energy crisis; July 2026 inflation is reported at 9.16%. ⚠️ **No sourced coverage was
  found for constitutional-reform progress, CHT policy, Rohingya policy, or civil unrest after February 2026.**

district figures sum to 1,842,811 by my addition):

**Three traditional "circles"** (British-era chieftaincies): Chakma, Mong and Bohmong. The CHT was a single
district until **1984**, when it became three. Bengali settlers made up **11.6% of the population in 1974 and
48.5% by 1991**, driven by state-sponsored settlement, per Wikipedia's conflict page (⚠️ 1947 baseline not
verified; general knowledge says a low single-digit share). At the 2022 census, **Bengalis were 50.06%**, so
**the region crossed from indigenous-plurality to bare Bengali-plurality within living memory**.

### 6.2 The grievance chain

1. **The 1900 CHT Regulation** gave the hills special autonomous status under the British.
2. **1947:** the CHT was awarded to Pakistan despite a non-Muslim majority (§5.3).
3. **Kaptai Dam, 1962:** displaced ~100,000 (about 70% Chakma), flooded 40% of cultivable land, with
   reportedly no compensation; **more than 40,000 Chakmas emigrated to Arunachal Pradesh** in the 1960s
   (Wikipedia; note that Arunachal is in the breakaway slice).
4. **1972-73:** Manabendra Narayan Larma, objecting to a constitution that "recognised only Bengali culture
   and the Bengali language," founded the **PCJSS** (Parbatya Chattagram Jana Samhati Samiti) in 1973;
   its armed wing, the **Shanti Bahini**, began the insurgency in 1977.
5. **Settlement program and militarization** (§6.1's 11.6% to 48.5%).
6. **Refugee flight to India:** roughly **50,000 Chakmas took refuge in Tripura and Meghalaya (1986-1997)**.

### 6.3 The 1997 Accord and its implementation gap

**Signed 2 December 1997** between the government and the PCJSS (signatories: Abul Hasnat Abdullah for the
government, Jyotirindra Bodhipriya Larma for the PCJSS). Provisions: a **Regional Council** for the three
hill districts with authority over "law and order, social justice and tribal laws," a **Ministry of Tribal
Affairs** headed by someone of tribal ethnicity, land survey and repatriation programs, and consultation
rights. **Delivered:** Shanti Bahini disarmed and compensated; the PCJSS became a mainstream party; **more than
50,000 displaced people returned** (Wikipedia). **Not delivered** (per the Peace Accords Matrix summary and
ICG-style commentary): a functioning **Land Commission**, full **rehabilitation of returnees**, **withdrawal
of non-permanent army camps**, devolution of real powers to the Regional Council, and **election of Hill
District Councils**. **The PCJSS said on 1 January 2026 that "nearly two-thirds of the accord, including its
core provisions, remain unimplemented even after 28 years,"** and that the interim government "made no
meaningful progress" (Daily Star). ⚠️ The government's own count of implemented clauses was not found; the
"two-thirds" figure is the signatory party's, not a neutral audit.

### 6.4 2024-2025: violence and armed groups

- **PCJSS 2025 report (Daily Star, 1 January 2026):** 268 rights violations affecting 606 people, 8
  extrajudicial killings, 117 arbitrary arrests, 193 army search operations, and 300+ acres illegally
  occupied. ⚠️ Advocacy-source data; no independent count found.
- **Khagrachhari, 23-30 September 2025:** after the reported gang rape of an indigenous schoolgirl
  (24 September), a blockade turned violent: **at least 3 killed (three Marma youths shot, per the PCJSS),
  13 army personnel and dozens of civilians injured, ~50 homes and ~40 shops burned at Ramesu Bazar** (Daily
  Star, TBS, Wikipedia); the violence spread to Rangamati (a 19-year-old, Anik Chakma, beaten to death) and
  the blockade extended to all three hill districts. **Claims conflict:** the government's home adviser blamed
  India and the Awami League; the army said foreign terrorists were involved and that UPDF militants used
  villagers as human shields; local media and rights groups reported communal Bengali-indigenous violence and
  police inaction. ⚠️ No single account is reliable; the event is best held as "a live flashpoint whose
  causes are disputed."
- **Kuki-Chin National Front (KNF).** Founded in 2017 by Nathan Bom, a Bawm; seeks a sovereign state for
  the Bawm around nine upazilas of Rangamati and Bandarban; attacked bank branches in Bandarban on
  2-4 April 2024 and clashed with army patrols on 3 April and 6 August 2024 (Diplomat, IWGIA, Wikipedia).
  It sits on the Myanmar border, adjacent to the Chin-Kuki-Zo world of Mizoram, Manipur and Chin State,
  and is a rare *new* armed separatist claim, distinct from the PCJSS mainstream.
- **The Arakan Army's advance** (§7) put a Myanmar rebel force on the entire Bangladeshi border from
  9 December 2024, which changes the security geometry of the southern CHT.

## Section 8 — Water: the Ganges, the Teesta and the rest of the rivers

**The context.** Bangladesh is a lower riparian on the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna systems, and the
water dispute is the country's oldest live foreign-policy issue.

### 8.1 The Ganges and Farakka (the rest of West Bengal, out of scope)

The **Farakka Barrage** (Murshidabad district, West Bengal, about 18 km from the Bangladesh border, 2,304 m
long, **operational 21 April 1975**) diverts up to 1,800 cubic meters per second toward the Hooghly to flush
sediment from Kolkata's port. Its dry-season effects on Bangladesh are documented: **saltwater intrusion in
the southwest, damage to the Sundarbans, disrupted fisheries and blocked hilsa migration**. The **1996
Ganges Water Sharing Treaty (signed December 1996, 30 years, reviewable every five)** shares dry-season flow
at Farakka; the tiered formula (general knowledge, ⚠️ only partly re-verified) gives each side 50% when flow
is 70,000 cusecs or lower, with a **35,000-cusec guarantee** to Bangladesh in the middle band. **It expires at
the end of 2026.** As of 13 February 2026 India told its parliament renewal discussions "had not yet
commenced"; more recent reports mention technical-level meetings; Bangladesh wants a **new** treaty with
"guarantee clauses" rather than a simple renewal (Water Diplomat, ISAS, TBS). ⚠️ **No sourced outcome as of
29 September 2026;** the treaty's expiry is roughly 90 days away and the negotiation is unresolved so far as
this pass found.

### 8.2 The Teesta (a slice river)

The Teesta is **414 km long, drains 12,540 km2**, and flows from Sikkim through **Mangan, Gangtok, Pakyong,
Kalimpong, Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri and Cooch Behar** in India, then into Bangladesh's Rangpur Division
(**Lalmonirhat, Rangpur, Kurigram, Gaibandha**) to join the Jamuna (Wikipedia). **A 1983 interim arrangement
allocated 36% to Bangladesh and 39% to India with the balance unallocated; the dispute has run about 35
years** and a 2011 draft was blocked on the Indian side (general knowledge, ⚠️ not re-verified this pass).
The Teesta III (1,200 MW) and Teesta V (510 MW) dams are in Sikkim, and the barrage-and-irrigation network
sits in Jalpaiguri (general knowledge: the Gajoldoba barrage, ⚠️).

**The finding for the project.** Every Indian district the Teesta touches is inside the breakaway slice
(Sikkim, Kalimpong, Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri, Cooch Behar). **The Teesta dispute therefore moves from
"Bangladesh against India" to "Bangladesh against whichever slice polity holds Jalpaiguri and Cooch Behar,
with Sikkim upstream."** The Ganges/Farakka dispute stays with the rest of India. This is one of the few
places where the pinned Siliguri Line reshapes a real-world Bangladeshi treaty relationship. ⚠️ Not a
decision; it is a structural consequence to be aware of.

### 8.3 The other rivers

Meghalaya's rivers deliver flash floods into the Sylhet haors (§2 and §10.3). ⚠️ The **Tipaimukh Dam** on the
Barak (Manipur) has long been a Bangladeshi concern for the Surma-Kushiyara system (general knowledge, not
verified this pass; Manipur is in the slice).

## Section 9 — The internal fault line, and the economy

### 9.1 Is the real fault line inside Bangladesh at all?

**Short answer, on this pass's evidence: barely, and not territorially.** Three separate cleavages exist:

1. **Ideological (dominant):** secular-linguistic "Bangalee" nationalism (the 1971 founding, Awami League)
   against Islamic "Bangladeshi" nationalism (BNP, Jamaat). The constitution has been flipped between them
   (§5.5). The **Awami League ban (April 2025)** and **Jamaat's 68 seats (February 2026)** are the two most
   recent data points.
2. **Regional coloring (soft):** in February 2026 **Jamaat beat the BNP in Khulna Division** (48.26% to
   43.55% of the vote; **25 of 36 seats**, more than a third of Jamaat's national total), polled **about 40%
   in Rajshahi and Rangpur**, and was weakest in **Sylhet (22.62%)**, Dhaka (22.38%), Barisal (23.46%),
   Mymensingh (21.85%) and Chattogram (28%). **The BNP's strongest region was Sylhet (~60%)** and its weakest
   Rangpur (just under 42%) (TBS analysis). That is a **southwest and northwest Islamist lean against a
   northeast and center BNP lean**, a real map but not an ethnic one.
3. **Ethnic (territorial, small):** the CHT, and more marginally the Garo, Khasi, Santal and Rakhine
   pockets (§4.2, §6).

**The Bengal identity question.** The identity that cuts across the border (Bengali language and
culture) does not fracture Bangladesh; it links it to West Bengal (out of scope), Tripura and Barak Valley
(in the slice). **The fracture that matters for the project is therefore across the border, not inside it**
(§10).

  deferral to 2029; the UN Committee for Development Policy recommended it; final General Assembly approval
  was pending as of late September 2026** (Daily Star, Prothom Alo). Loss of EU "Everything but Arms" access
  would mean ~11.5% duty on exports. **US trade:** a 9 February 2026 agreement set most tariffs at 19% with a
  capped zero-tariff pathway for apparel using US cotton; the US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on
  20 February 2026, leaving the deal "legally precarious"; the Rahman government is reviewing it.
- **India transit.** After Yunus called the Northeast "landlocked" in March-April 2025, **India withdrew its
  transshipment facility for Bangladeshi exports on 8 April 2025** (MEA statement, via Outlook and others).
  Bangladesh's leverage is the slice's need for Chattogram and Mongla, the mirror of the Siliguri chokepoint
  (§10.4).

**Structural takeaway.** RMG monoculture, single-port concentration and imported energy make Bangladesh the
region's clearest **import-and-export lifeline crux**, the counterpart of Oceania's import dependency and
Central Asia's remittance dependency.

## Section 10 — The border with the breakaway slice, from Bangladesh's side

**The slice-side frontier is about 1,879 km by my addition of the lengths Wikipedia gives** (Tripura 856,
Meghalaya 443, Mizoram 318, Assam 262), plus the northern West Bengal stretch (Cooch Behar and Jalpaiguri
districts) whose length was not separately sourced ⚠️; the whole India border is 4,096 km, of which West
Bengal is 2,217 km (mostly the out-of-scope portion). Fencing covers about 3,180 km, with 916 km left to
physical and non-physical barriers (Wikipedia, 2024). Border killings are a chronic grievance: "over 900
Bangladeshi citizens were killed during the first decade of the 21st century" (HRW via Wikipedia).

### 10.1 Sylhet and the Barak Valley

The 1947 decision (§5.3) split one Sylheti-speaking region: **Sylhet went to East Pakistan, Karimganj (now
Sribhumi) stayed in Assam.** The Barak Valley (Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi) has **3.6 million people (2011),
80.8% Bengali-speaking**, is **50% Hindu and 48.1% Muslim** (Cachar 59.83% Hindu; Karimganj 56.36% and
Hailakandi 60.31% Muslim), and has **Bengali as its official language**; the **19 May 1961 Silchar language
firing killed about eleven demonstrators** (Wikipedia). The Sylheti-speaking population straddles the border
(~11 million total). **Bangladesh's side of this seam is the Sylhet Division (11.03 million; Hindu 13.51%),
which has Meghalaya to the north and the Barak Valley to the east.** The Northeast survey's Tier 2 item 7
(Barak Valley possibly splitting off) is directly relevant: a separated Barak Valley would be a
Sylheti-and-Bengali polity bordering a Sylheti-and-Bengali division. ⚠️ **Whether the two are drawn together
is a real choice.** Cost of merging: crosses an international border the project treats as settled and
opens a Bangladesh-to-slice irredentism question with no sourced current movement.

### 10.2 Tripura

Tripura wraps Bangladesh on three sides (**856 km, the longest slice frontier**). **Tripura's Tripuri (tribal)
majority before 1947 became a Bengali majority after the 1947 and 1971 refugee waves** (Northeast survey).
The Bangladesh side of the border is the Cumilla, Brahmanbaria, Feni, Khagrachhari and Chattogram area, so
**both sides hold Bengali plains and Tripuri hills**: Bangladesh's **156,578 Tripuris** (§4.2) are the
mirror of Tripura's tribal population. The physical links are: the **Agartala-Akhaura rail link
(15.064 km, Agartala to Akhaura in Brahmanbaria)** and the **Maitri Setu over the Feni River linking Sabroom
to Chittagong port, about 80 km away**. ⚠️ Both were "to become operational soon" as of May 2024 (Indian
sources); whether they operate normally in 2026 was not verified. **Tripura is the slice state most
dependent on Bangladeshi territory for outside access**, which is why the port question (§9.3) matters on
both sides.

### 10.3 Mizoram, Meghalaya, and the cross-border peoples

- **Mizoram (318 km) and the CHT.** The border runs along the eastern edge of Rangamati and Bandarban. The
  **Chakma Autonomous District Council in Mizoram** is India's Chakma administrative home. The Chakma in
  India (2011 census, via Wikipedia) total about **228,000: Mizoram ~92,850, Tripura ~84,269, Arunachal
  ~47,073** (all in slice states), against **483,299** in Bangladesh (2022) and **~43,100 "Daingnet" in
  Rakhine**. Mizo and Bawm/Pankhu/Lushai belong to the Kuki-Chin (Zo) linguistic cluster that also
  includes Chin State and Manipur's Kuki-Zo bloc, which the Northeast survey lists as its own Tier 1
  candidate; the KNF (§6.4) is the Bangladeshi-side expression of the same cluster. ⚠️ **The seam is a
  cross-border ethnic cluster, not a border dispute;** Mizoram also has an unresolved boundary dispute
  with Assam over 1,318 km2 (Barak Valley page, ⚠️ off Bangladesh's scope).
- **Meghalaya (443 km).** The Garo Hills adjoin the Garo areas of **Mymensingh Division (Sherpur,
  Netrokona)**, and the Khasi-Jaintia Hills adjoin **Sylhet Division (Sylhet, Moulvibazar)**. **Garo:
  ~997,716 in Meghalaya versus ~77,000-120,000 in Bangladesh; Khasi: ~30,000-85,000 in Bangladesh.** The
  economic ties are **coal, limestone, boulders and horticulture** (about **200 trucks of limestone daily**
  across **Dawki-Tamabil**; the 1932 Dawki bridge over the Umngot) and, in the reverse direction, **water**:
  **eight transboundary rivers and 100+ hill streams** carry Meghalaya's rain into the haors (§2). **Khasi
  and Garo Christianity, matriliny, and betel-leaf and orange economies** make these the least-Bengali
  populations on the Bangladeshi side. ⚠️ The Northeast survey concludes Meghalaya stays whole, so on the
  Bangladesh side the seam is Meghalaya-as-one against three divisions (Sylhet, Mymensingh, and Rangpur).

### 10.4 Tetulia, Panchagarh and the Siliguri end of the line

**The pinned dividing line's point 3 lies at ≈ 88.3327°E, 26.4962°N, at the Mahananda's entry to Bangladesh
in Tentulia (Tetulia) Upazila, Panchagarh District, Rangpur Division.** No Bangladeshi territory is split;
the line ends at the border. From Bangladesh's side, **the Siliguri Corridor is the northern edge of Rangpur
Division**: Panchagarh, Nilphamari and Lalmonirhat face the slice's Jalpaiguri and Cooch Behar
districts, and **Lalmonirhat is about 12-15 km from the Indian border and about 135 km from the "Chicken's
Neck."** A **March 2025 announcement** of Chinese help to revive the **dormant World War II-era Lalmonirhat
airfield**, with a Pakistani company as a reported subcontractor and work "expected to commence in October
2025" (Business Today, Assam Tribune, SPS Aviation), has made this stretch a live strategic story in India.
⚠️ **Whether the airbase was built, is under construction, or was cancelled was not established this pass**
(the Rahman government's stance is unsourced); treat it as a proposal, not a fact on the ground. **Banglabandha
land port** (Tetulia) is the traditional overland gateway for Nepali and Bhutanese trade using the
corridor (general knowledge; the Nepal and Bhutan surveys should confirm).

### 10.5 The 2015 enclave exchange: the precedent

**162 enclaves were exchanged on the night of 31 July 2015 (effective 1 August): India received 51
Bangladeshi enclaves (7,110 acres), Bangladesh 111 Indian enclaves (17,160 acres)**; about **51,549 people**
lived in them (**37,334 in Indian enclaves inside Bangladesh; 14,215 in Bangladeshi enclaves inside India**),
with residents choosing their nationality (transfers completed by November 2015). The enclaves lay in
**West Bengal (Cooch Behar), Tripura, Assam and Meghalaya**, so **most of the exchange happened on the
slice-side border**. **Dahala Khagrabari** was "the world's only third-order enclave" and **Dahagram-Angarpota
(Lalmonirhat) remained a Bangladeshi composite exclave** reached through the **Tin Bigha Corridor**
(perpetual lease). **Why it matters:** it is (a) a real, negotiated, *humane* exclave resolution that
contrasts with the Fergana tangle (Central Asia survey §2); (b) a precedent that **sub-district exclave
problems can be dissolved by exchange at a treaty moment**, which a project map could mimic; and (c) a
reminder that the slice-Bangladesh boundary already has a treaty history (the 2015 Land Boundary Agreement,
which would need re-endorsement by any successor polity). ⚠️ Not a design decision; a precedent.

**India's 2025 "push-in" practice.** Between May 2025 and January 2026 **2,303 (BGB count) to 2,479 (another
count) people were pushed across into Bangladesh**, of whom at least 120-126 were later confirmed Indian
nationals; those pushed were held in makeshift camps in **Tripura, Meghalaya and Assam** before being sent
across (Diplomat, Daily Star). ⚠️ The rest of India is out of scope; the point is only that **the slice
states are where the practice operates from**, so a slice polity would inherit a live cross-border
population-flow and citizenship problem.

### 10.6 One flagged note on the rest of West Bengal

⚠️ **Seam only, no research.** Bengali is one language and one cultural identity on both sides of the
Radcliffe Line, which drew a religion-based border through a Bengali-speaking whole in 1947; Bangladesh's
2,217 km border with West Bengal (mostly the out-of-scope portion) is the largest single seam in the
country's political geography. **Nothing about West Bengal as a state was researched.** Any "Bengal
reunification" theme belongs to the author's decision, not this survey.
