# American Nations ↔ New North America — Cross-Reference

Maps the project's own 12 New North America regions (per
`worldbuilding/nations/World-Nations-Roster.md`, sourced from
`InnerTepeniaGDD/Reference/Images/Maps/North America with tentative labels.jpg`) against Colin Woodard's 11
county-level regional cultures, as described in `American_Nations_Maps_Extraction.md` and
`American_Nations_Extraction.md` (same folder). Purpose: surface where a clean state-line map and a
county-level cultural map actually diverge, since that's the concrete, checkable value a source like this adds
over redrawing borders freehand — not to resolve any of these unilaterally, just to put the discrepancies in
front of the author for a decision.

## The Real Discrepancies — What a State-Line Map Can't Capture

These are the actual point of consulting a county-level source rather than eyeballing state borders.

### Cascadia is two cultures wearing one name

Woodard's Left Coast is a **narrow coastal ribbon**, never more than one or two counties deep, running the
length of the BC/WA/OR/CA coast — it does not claim the interior of any state it touches. The Far West is the
large interior block behind it (eastern WA/OR, ID, MT, WY, most of NV, UT, western CO). If Cascadia is drawn as
a single solid region covering the whole Pacific Northwest quadrant, it's actually merging two of Woodard's
regions that he treats as culturally opposed — coastal, internationalist, and politically progressive (Left
Coast) versus interior, individualist, and historically populated by disappointed prospectors and ranchers
(Far West). Worth deciding whether that merge is intentional or whether Cascadia should split the way Colorado
already implicitly does along the Far West line.

### The CSA is at least three cultures, plus a genuine enclave

If the CSA is drawn as one solid block from Texas through Florida, county-level reality inside that block is:
**Deep South** as the actual core (LA/MS/AL/GA/SC/FL panhandle+peninsula to roughly Orlando/Tampa), **El Norte**
claiming the southern/western Texas borderlands along the Rio Grande, and **The Far West** claiming the Texas
panhandle specifically. On top of that, Woodard's New France has a small, fully-enclosed enclave around New
Orleans — a single nation's territory sitting entirely inside another's with no shared outside border. If
Quebec and the CSA are meant to honor this, there's a real design option here: a small non-contiguous "New
France" pocket around New Orleans that belongs to Quebec's culture but sits geographically inside CSA
territory — exactly the kind of enclave a state-line map can never represent, but which a saga spanning New
Orleans-adjacent territory could actually use.

**One point of agreement, not discrepancy:** Woodard's Deep South does *not* claim the southern tip of Florida
either — the Miami/Everglades area is left unshaded on his own map, outside all eleven regions. If the CSA's
own map similarly excludes that area, the two sources already agree without anyone having coordinated it.

### New England is smaller than its Woodard counterpart

Yankeedom, per the map description, covers all of New England **plus** upstate/western New York (excluding
NYC) and a strip of southern Ontario — and has a second, disconnected western lobe in Minnesota and northern
Wisconsin. If New England is drawn as just the historical six states, it's a meaningfully smaller claim than
Woodard's own Yankeedom. Not necessarily wrong — just worth knowing the county-level source treats "Yankee"
culture as extending well past New England's own borders in two separate directions.

### The Prairie Federation is where Woodard's own coverage runs out

This is the one region where the gap isn't a discrepancy so much as an absence: Woodard's map details the
US side of the northern Plains (the Far West's eastern reach into the Dakotas/Nebraska/Kansas) but the Canadian
prairie provinces (Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba) fall under his broad, admittedly vague "First Nation" band
— which itself is described in the extraction as the one boundary on the whole map that doesn't track any
political line, "a rough settlement/climatic line" rather than a real cultural-analysis result. **This is
exactly the kind of region the closing synthesis in `American_Nations_Extraction.md` flags as needing the
*method*, not the findings** — Woodard's own book doesn't have a real answer for the Canadian prairies either.

### Texas, split three ways, deserves its own note regardless of which region it lands in

Called out separately because it's the single most-divided US state on Woodard's own map, regardless of
whether the CSA claims all of it: El Norte (south/west borderlands along the Rio Grande), Deep South (east),
and The Far West (the panhandle). Whatever region(s) end up claiming Texas territory, no single clean claim on
the whole state matches the county-level source.

### The DMZ sits on Woodard's own most tangled boundary knot — independently

Worth flagging with real emphasis, the same way the Maggie archetype work flagged undesigned convergences as
validation rather than coincidence: the DMZ was placed around the Great Lakes/Ontario border area with **no
stated reasoning on record** (per `World-Nations-Roster.md`: "Genuinely new, unexplained find... What conflict
or settlement produced it is completely open"). That is *exactly* the area where Woodard's map is most
contested — Michigan splits three ways (Midlands' two separate lobes plus First Nation-adjacent territory near
the UP), Ohio splits three ways (Midlands' Lake Erie sliver, Yankeedom's northeast corner, Appalachia's south),
and Yankeedom's own two lobes are separated specifically by the Midlands' Great Lakes wedge running through
this same zone. A DMZ placed there independently of this source lines up with the real-world region Woodard's
own analysis treats as the least culturally coherent, most fought-over ground on the whole continent. This is
worth treating as a strong, usable justification for *why* a demilitarized zone would end up there — the
underlying cultural fragmentation was already established before anyone drew the DMZ on top of it.

## Still Open

- Whether the Cascadia/Far West and CSA three-way splits above are deliberate simplifications the author wants
  to keep, or gaps worth redrawing at the sub-state level.
- Mexico and Central America remain unclaimed on **both** sources — the New North America map leaves them
  blank, and Woodard's own map shows only a lighter, unlabeled tone south of El Norte "for geographic context,
  not claimed as one of the eleven regions." Neither source solves this; it's open territory either way.
- The Canadian prairie provinces (Prairie Federation's likely northern/western extent) have no real Woodard
  analysis to check against — see above.
- Whether a small, non-contiguous New France/Louisiana enclave inside CSA territory is worth adopting, per the
  New Orleans note above.
