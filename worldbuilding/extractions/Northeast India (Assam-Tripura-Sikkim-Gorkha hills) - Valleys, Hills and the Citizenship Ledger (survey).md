# Northeast India (Assam-Tripura-Sikkim-Gorkha hills) - Valleys, Hills and the Citizenship Ledger

**Stage 1 frameworks survey. Started 2026-09-29, deepening pass - not a finished analysis.**
Covers the **part of the breakaway landmass outside the Naga/Manipur/Mizoram hill bloc**: **Assam** (Brahmaputra
Valley, Barak Valley, Bodoland Territorial Region, Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao), **Meghalaya**, **Tripura**,
**Sikkim**, **Arunachal Pradesh except its Naga-inhabited districts** (Tirap, Changlang, Longding - the other
researcher's), and the **northern West Bengal districts that fall on the breakaway side of the pinned line**:
**Darjeeling, Kalimpong, Jalpaiguri, Alipurduar, Cooch Behar** (Gorkhaland / Kamtapur / Dooars / Siliguri
Corridor). This file replaces, for that sub-area, the Wikipedia-only first pass in
`South Asia - The Siliguri Line and the Nagalim Question (survey).md`; it does **not** edit that file (the
reconciliation is a separate step). **The rest of India is permanently out of scope - zero research on it.**
Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram and Arunachal's Naga districts are the other researcher's; Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan
are other researchers' countries - this file gives **seam notes only** for them. Latin script only, by
standing instruction; every place and group name below is romanized/English.

**Status:** district-level Census of India 2011 numbers pulled where they could be (Assam fully, from the state's
own reproduction of the census tables; northern Bengal by sub-district; Meghalaya, Tripura, Sikkim, Arunachal
partly - see the gap list in the Open Questions). **2011 is still the latest census** (the next reference date is
1 March 2027, with caste enumeration for the first time since 1931 - so every ethnic figure here is 15 years
old, and the ones with 2021+ dates are estimates, not counts). **Research proposes, the author disposes: no
nation is named, no border drawn, no winner picked.** Where a source is partisan, that is said in place.

## Headline findings

1. **The pinned line drags a large, mostly non-Gorkha, non-Northeast population into the breakaway landmass.**
   The first pass treated Darjeeling/Kalimpong's Gorkha identity as "independently confirming" the line. The
   numbers say the Gorkha hills are a small part of what the line takes: **the five northern-Bengal districts
   held 8.54 million people in 2011; the Gorkha hill core (Darjeeling Sadar + Kurseong + Mirik + Kalimpong)
   held ~0.88 million - about 10%.** The other ~7.7 million are **Siliguri's Bengali-speaking urban and
   Terai population (971,120), Rajbanshi (Scheduled Caste) plains cultivators, Adivasi tea-garden labor,
   Nepali tea workers, and a 25.5% Muslim Cooch Behar.** No shared "Northeast" identity binds them; see §9.
   **This is the single biggest correction to the first pass.**
2. **Assam is 64% of everything in this survey and cannot be treated as one thing or as a rounding error.**
   31.2 million people (2011) of the ~48.4 million in this file's scope (§4). Its own internal ledger is:
   61.5% Hindu, 34.2% Muslim (11 Muslim-majority districts; **~60% of all Assam Muslims live in seven
   central/western districts** - computed, §4.1), 12.4% Scheduled Tribe, 48.4% Assamese-speaking, 28.9%
   Bengali-speaking. **No other candidate here is as demographically balanced against itself.**
3. **Citizenship, not just ethnicity, is the load-bearing fault line in Assam - and every figure on it is
   contested.** NRC final list (31 Aug 2019): 3.11 crore included, **1,906,657 excluded**, of whom roughly a
   third each were Bengali Hindus, Bengali Muslims and other Hindus (⚠️ the "roughly equal" split is a
   press/Wikipedia summary, not an audited figure). The Supreme Court **upheld Section 6A (the Assam Accord's
   25 March 1971 cutoff) 4:1 on 17 Oct 2024.** Since 2025 the state has run evictions and "pushbacks" at scale
   (tens of thousands claimed pushed back; ~166,000 ever declared "foreigners" by tribunals - ⚠️ all
   advocacy- or government-sourced, both sides partisan). §4.3.
4. **The Bodoland arrangement is the working template for "hills-within-a-state" - and it is a minority-rule
   arrangement run by a plurality over a mixed area.** In the four original BTR districts the **ST share is ~33.5% (ST
   1,055,732 of 3,151,067, computed from the census tables; Bodo speakers ~30%)**; Muslims ~19%, Christians ~9%, Hindus ~71%.
   The Bodo People's Front swept the September 2025 council election (28 of 40). §4.4.
5. **Tripura is the demographic inverse of Assam's Barak Valley and the cleanest "tribal core inside a
   Bengali majority" case.** Tribal share fell **63.77% (1881) -> 37.23% (1951) -> 28.95% (1971) -> 31.80%
   (2011)**; the tribal-council area (TTAADC) is **68% of the land, 1,216,465 people, 83.4% ST, and holds
   ~87% of all Tripura's Scheduled Tribes (computed).** TIPRA Motha won **24 of 28 elected TTAADC seats on 17 April 2026 (57.62%)**. §6.
6. **Sikkim is a "restoration" case whose restoring constituency is the minority.** The Nepali-origin
   majority *voted for* the 1975 abolition (97.55%, 59,637 yes; 31 of 32 seats to the pro-merger party in
   1974), while the Bhutia-Lepcha groups (~16.5% by community shares) were the Chogyal's base. Restoring
   the Namgyal state is therefore not an obvious majority project (§7) - **inference, flagged.**
7. **The breakaway landmass would not inherit a chokepoint problem - it would inherit the chokepoint.**
   Siliguri, New Jalpaiguri junction, Bagdogra, the Panitanki and Jaigaon border crossings (the routes for
   Nepal's and Bhutan's trade to Kolkata - ⚠️ general knowledge, not fetched) all sit on the *breakaway* side of the pinned line. Its *own*
   internal neck is different: the **Cooch Behar - Dooars - Dhubri/Kokrajhar ribbon** squeezed between
   Bangladesh and Bhutan, which links Sikkim/Gorkha hills/Siliguri to Assam (§10.1). ⚠️ Not measured this pass.
8. **Meghalaya's "stays whole" default in the first pass is weaker than stated.** There *is* a live Garoland
   movement (memorandum to the Union tribal affairs minister, Aug 2025; 24 of 60 assembly seats; nearly
   9 of Meghalaya's ~12 lakh Garos live in the five Garo Hills districts), a live Khasi-led Inner Line Permit
   agitation (Jan 2026), and an HNLC insurgent group banned until 2029. Low-intensity, but not absent (§5).
9. **Two seams with China are real and unresolved: Tawang and the Yarlung Tsangpo.** China's fifth batch of
   "standardized" Arunachal names (27 places, May 2025); **construction of the 60 GW Medog station began
   19 July 2025, ~30 km from the border**, upstream of the Siang-Brahmaputra system that runs through the whole of Assam; India's countering 11,000 MW Siang project has drawn Adi protests (§8, §10.4).
10. **Woodard's First Effective Settlement fails here for a further distinct reason, and the replacement is a
    "citizenship ledger" layered over a valley-vs-hill frontier** - colonial tea-labor migration, the
    1947/1971 refugee overlays, and a 1971 legal cutoff date are the *settlement events*, and they are
    documented to the year. §1.
11. **Where real geography breaks the "whole first-order subdivision" rule** - the BTR's four districts,
    Karbi Anglong/Dima Hasao, the Barak Valley, Darjeeling district (hills vs Siliguri plains), Tripura's
    TTAADC, Assam's own new districts - the specific units and their mixed-population evidence are in §11.
    Each needs a flagged decision like Iraq and Lebanon received.

## Section 1 - Method: does First Effective Settlement hold, and what replaces it

`METHOD - How Nations Get Drawn.md` says Woodard's doctrine works only on settler colonies and that ethnic
identity, in two tiers, is what survives the translation: **Tier 1 draws the border; Tier 2 draws what is
inside it.** Northeast India tests that harder than any earlier region because it stacks **five different
settlement logics on one map**, and each produces a different "first" population.

### 1.1 The five overlays

| Layer | Settlement event | Approx. date | What it sets | Where it fails as "first" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Valley state** | Tai-Ahom kingdom founded by Sukaphaa | 1228-1826 | Upper Assam's Ahom/Sutiya/Moran/Chutia culture; Assamese as valley lingua franca | The Ahom themselves were <10% of Assam's population by the 1870s census - a small founding elite absorbed into a plural society (this is Woodard's "small founding population" in the raw) |
| **2. Hill polities** | Khasi syiemships, Jaintia, Garo, Dimasa (Kachari), Tripura's Manikya, Sikkim's Namgyal | 16th c.-1949 | Hill-tribal domains with their own law | Never fully inside the valley states; the British later froze them as "excluded areas" |
| **3. Colonial tea-labor** | Assam Company (1839); indenture from Chotanagpur/Odisha/Andhra 1860s-1890s | 1839-1920s | The **tea tribes** (~7 million, ~4.5 million in Assam estates) and Dooars/Darjeeling Adivasi and Nepali labor | Arrived *after* the valley states but before the modern nation; they hold the labor, not the land, and no first-settlement claim |
| **4. Peasant migration** | Mymensingh/Sylhet Bengali Muslims settled the Brahmaputra chars, 1901-1941, encouraged by the colonial state (Line System 1920) | 1901-1947 | The **Miya / char-chapori** population, now the largest single bloc in central/western Assam (⚠️ inference from the district Muslim majorities) | Their settlement was *invited*; their citizenship is what is now contested |
| **5. Partition and war refugees** | 1947 (Bengali Hindus into Tripura, Assam, Cooch Behar); 1971 | 1947-1971 | Tripura's flip to Bengali majority; Assam's Bengali Hindu belt; the Assam Accord's 1971 cutoff | Legally fixed at midnight 24-25 March 1971 - a **statute defines who counts as settled** |

**Reading:** Woodard would say the earliest effective settlement wins (Ahom, or the pre-Ahom Kamarupa-Koch
layer). But the region's *actual* politics is fought over layers 3-5, and the legal system has **frozen
layer 5's cutoff into constitutional law** (Section 6A). **First Effective Settlement therefore fails here
because the "first" question is not the operative one; the operative one is "who was counted, and as what,
on which date."** Compare Central Asia's "borders as outside engineering" and Mainland SE Asia's "chosen
culture": this is another distinct mode - **borders and belonging set by a citizenship ledger.**

### 1.2 Frameworks that do hold

- **The Zomia/hill frontier holds strongly for the hills.** James C. Scott's *The Art of Not Being Governed*
  (2009) follows Willem van Schendel's 2002 "Zomia," which explicitly includes Northeast India's hills. The
  Naga, Khasi-Jaintia, Garo, Mishmi and Tani groups are the textbook case: upland groups whose identity
  is partly *defined against* valley states. The Southeast Asia survey already carries Zomia as a "domain
  above the states"; the same treatment fits Meghalaya, Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao and Arunachal.
  ⚠️ Scott's thesis is contested (Lieberman, Brass - see the Southeast Asia survey §2.2); it is used here
  for structure, not as settled fact. **Books cited from memory in this section were not re-read this pass.**
- **The Ahom kingdom is a mandala in Wolters' sense** (a core with tributary hill chiefs at the edge; the
  Ahom "paik" corvee system and the treaties with Dimasa, Jaintia, Naga and Bhutia frontier peoples).
  The core/domain/sphere vocabulary maps directly: **core = Upper Assam valley; domain = Lower Assam and the
  Koch/Kachari tributaries; sphere = hill chiefs paid off with "posa" and Duar rights.** ⚠️ The "posa" (hill
  tribute) system is recalled from general knowledge, not fetched this pass.
- **Colonial "excluded areas" and the Inner Line** (Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation 1873; Excluded and
  Partially Excluded Areas under the 1935 Act; Sixth Schedule 1949-50) **are the best single existing proxy for
  the hill-tribal domain.** They are, unusually, a *pre-existing legal map* of the hill/valley split. Today's
  Sixth Schedule councils - **KAAC/Karbi Anglong, the Dima Hasao council, the BTC, Khasi/Jaintia/Garo
  councils, and the TTAADC** - are that map's living form.
- **"Sons of the soil" politics** (Myron Weiner, 1978) describes the Assam Movement: an indigenous majority in
  its own state feeling demographically threatened by internal and cross-border migrants. It explains why
  citizenship dominates.

### 1.3 Proposed replacement framework - the "Frontier Ledger"

Not a decision - a proposal for how the author might organize the evidence, parallel to Central Asia's
"delimitation" and Southeast Asia's "mandala":

1. **A valley-versus-hill frontier as the structural axis.** Valleys (Brahmaputra, Barak, Tripura's west,
   Terai/Dooars plains) carry the state tradition and the majorities; hills (Meghalaya, Karbi/Dima Hasao,
   Tripura's ridges, Sikkim, Darjeeling, Arunachal) carry the tribal domains.
2. **A citizenship ledger for the valleys**: layered dates (1826, 1839-1920s, 1901-1941, 1947, 1971) with
   the 24-25 March 1971 line as a legal fact. Whoever draws the map has to decide whose ledger governs.
3. **A sphere layer for the cross-border cultural claims**: Greater Tipraland (extending into Bangladesh and
   Mizoram), Greater Cooch Behar/Kamtapur (Rajbanshi across four countries), Tai-Ahom heritage (Myanmar
   Shan), Nepali/Gorkha (Nepal, Bhutan's Lhotshampa diaspora, Sikkim), Monpa (Bhutan/Tibet). Overlapping
   spheres, as the mandala carve-out already permits.

## Section 3 - History that produced the map (sourced dates)

**3.1 Before the British.** The **Ahom kingdom (1228-1826)**, founded by the Tai prince Sukaphaa, expanded by
annexing the Chutia kingdom in 1523 and taking Kachari (Dimasa) territory; it **defeated the Mughals at
Saraighat (1671) and fixed its western border at the Manas River**; the **Moamoria rebellion (1769-1805)**
and then Burmese invasions broke it. "The Ahom" were a ruling elite: **Tai-Ahom were under 10% of Assam's
population by the 1870s.** West of the Manas lay the **Koch kingdom (Kamata / Cooch Behar)**, whose
Rajbanshi population is the ancestral base of northern Bengal's plains today. **Tripura** was ruled by the
**Manikya dynasty** for "roughly 184 kings" per Tripura's own tradition (⚠️ figure from a partisan
Tipraland-advocacy summary). **Sikkim** was founded as a Buddhist priest-kingdom in **1642** by Phuntsog
Namgyal at Yuksom - the first of 12 Chogyals of one dynasty, ending 1975. The **Jaintia** kingdom's last
ruler (Rajendra Singh of Jaintiapur) ceded the hills to the British in **1835**. **Khasi** identity sat in ~25
syiemships.

**3.2 The British layer.** **Treaty of Yandabo (24 Feb 1826)** ended the First Anglo-Burmese War: Burma
renounced claims to Assam and Manipur, stopped interfering in Cachar and the Jaintia Hills, ceded Arakan
and Tenasserim and paid £1 million. **1839: the Assam Company begins commercial tea** (tea indenture follows,
§4.8). **Darjeeling** was leased/ceded by Sikkim in 1835 and formally annexed in 1853; **Kalimpong and the
Dooars** were taken from **Bhutan after the Duar War (1864-65)** under the **Treaty of Sinchula, 11 Nov
1865** - Bhutan ceded the Assam Duars, Bengal Duars and 83 km2 of Dewangiri, losing about a fifth of its
territory, and received a subsidy of Rs 50,000/year. **The Inner Line (1873)** and later excluded areas froze
the hill/valley divide. ⚠️ Darjeeling's 1835 cession date and the 1873 regulation are general knowledge,
consistent with but not proven by the fetched sources.

**3.3 1947 and Sylhet.** Sylhet, a Muslim-majority district in Hindu-majority Assam, voted on **6 July 1947 to
join East Bengal (56.56% to 43.44%)**; Assam's chief minister Gopinath Bordoloi had himself said he preferred
to hand Sylhet over for provincial homogeneity. **Karimganj subdivision voted 50.44% for Pakistan but was
transferred to India by the Radcliffe award** - which is why the Barak Valley (Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi)
exists as a Bengali-speaking, half-Hindu, half-Muslim appendage of Assam. **Cooch Behar** merged into India
on 12 Sept 1949 under a Chief Commissioner and into West Bengal on 1 Jan 1950 - a Rajbanshi-nationalist
grievance to this day (§9.6). **Tripura** merged on **15 Oct 1949** (merger agreement signed 9 Sept 1949).
Meanwhile **the 1947-1958 flow** of Bengali Hindus into Assam totaled ~486,000 by two cited counts (274,455 in
1946-51; 212,545 in 1952-58) and Tripura took ~610,000 in 1947-51 - nearly the state's whole 1941
population. ⚠️ Refugee counts are official-archive citations via Wikipedia, and 1971 numbers in particular
mix transient and permanent arrivals.

**3.4 The reorganizations.** Nagaland (1963), **Meghalaya (autonomous 1970; full state 21 Jan 1972)**,
Tripura, Manipur and Meghalaya statehood in 1972, **Arunachal as a Union Territory in 1972 and a state on 20
Feb 1987**, Mizoram 1987, **Sikkim's merger on 16 May 1975**. Correction to the first pass: **Arunachal (the
NEFA) was administered under the Assam governor's agency, never part of Assam state proper**; the
"Assam fractured repeatedly" story is strictly true of Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram - Arunachal and Tripura
came from other channels.

**3.5 Assam's own great fault: the Assam Movement.** **1979-85**, led by the All Assam Students' Union (AASU),
demanded detection and deportation of "foreigners." **Over 855 deaths** are officially counted as movement
martyrs; the **Nellie massacre of 18 Feb 1983** killed an officially cited **1,800+** people (unofficial
estimates ~2,000-3,000; mostly Bengali-origin Muslim women and children) - and the **Tiwari Commission report,
long suppressed, was released by the Assam government in Nov 2025** ahead of the 2026 election. The
**Assam Accord (15 Aug 1985)** set **1 Jan 1966** (accepted) and **25 March 1971** (cutoff) as the dates for
identification. **ULFA** was founded on **7 April 1979** at Rang Ghar, Sivasagar, to establish "an independent
sovereign Assam." It split in 2011; the pro-talks faction (about 8,200 cadres cited) signed a peace accord on
**29 Dec 2023**; **ULFA-I under Paresh Baruah retains ~250 cadres in Myanmar's Sagaing region** and alleged
Indian drone strikes on its camps in July 2025 (denied). ⚠️ Cadre numbers are a single Wikipedia summary.

### 4.3 Citizenship, the NRC, and the ledger

- **NRC:** update mandated by the Supreme Court; cutoff **midnight 24-25 March 1971**. **Final list 31 Aug 2019:
  33,027,661 applicants; 31,121,004 included; 1,906,657 excluded.** The excluded "divided roughly equally"
  among Bengali Hindus, Bengali Muslims and other Hindus (⚠️ summary claim). A Comptroller and Auditor General
  audit (Dec 2022) found irregularities including exclusion of indigenous Assamese and a cost rise from Rs 288
  crore (2014) to Rs 1,602 crore (Mar 2022). **No government - including the state's - has accepted the list.**
  Excluded persons appeal to **Foreigners Tribunals**; press reports say **~166,000 have ever been declared
  "foreigners" and ~85,000 cases are pending (2025), with 36,358 declared 2021 to May 2026** (⚠️ figures via
  press; totals vary by outlet).
- **Section 6A:** the Supreme Court **upheld it 4:1 on 17 Oct 2024** (Chandrachud CJ, Surya Kant, Sundresh,
  Misra for; Pardiwala dissenting), keeping **25 March 1971** as the cutoff. This is the *legal spine* of every
  Assam settlement scenario.
- **Since 2025:** the state ran mass evictions (Goalpara wetland, 16-18 June 2025: 660 families, 4,000+
  people, ~495 acres; Golaghat/Uriamghat: 2,000+ Bengali-origin Muslim families). Advocacy sources say **50,000
  acres cleared and 17,000 homes razed since 2016** (⚠️ partisan, unverified). The chief minister has said he
  wants "pressure" so that Bengali-speaking Muslims "leave by themselves" and predicted that a voter-roll
  revision will delete "4-5 lakh Miya votes" (⚠️ reported quotes from opposing-press outlets; the 2026
  election already occurred). Government-and-press figures on **pushbacks** to Bangladesh range from 303 (a
  CM assembly statement) to **29,663 "pushed back," 470 formally deported (Al Jazeera, Maktoob citing state
  data)** - the numbers do not agree. **Flag: any figure on who is or is not a citizen is politically
  produced.**
- **2026 election (4 May):** BJP 82 of 126, INC 19, AGP 10, BPF 10, RD 2, AIUDF 2, AITC 1 - the lowest Congress
  tally ever; a state government with a mandate to press the citizenship line.

### 4.4 The Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR)

- **The arrangement:** 1993 Bodo Accord (BAC, 8,795 km2) -> **10 Feb 2003 Bodoland Territorial Council** (first
  sitting 7 Dec 2003) -> **27 Jan 2020 accord** with NDFB factions, ABSU and UBPO: BTAD renamed BTR, council seats
  46->60 (⚠️ but only 40 elected seats were contested in 2025, so the increase had evidently not yet taken effect), Bodo made an associate official language, **Rs 1,500 crore** package,
  and a commitment to **redraw the boundary "to make Boro speakers majority."** ⚠️ The exact village-exchange
  mechanism was not verified this pass.
- **Composition (2011, four original districts, computed):** 3,151,047 people; **ST 33.5% (1,055,732)**, Hindu
  71.2%, Muslim 19.1%, Christian 9.1%. A Wikipedia summary gives Bodo speakers at 30.4% and Assamese 25.5%,
  Bengali 20.4% - i.e. **the Bodo are a plurality, not a majority, in the area that carries their name.**
  (Other sources say "Bodos ~1.2 million (31.5%)" which is inconsistent with the ST total; ⚠️ flagged.)
  Tamulpur district (2022) was added to the BTR.
- **Violence:** 2012 Kokrajhar violence ~100 dead and **400,000-500,000 displaced**; 1996 and 1998 Bodo-Adivasi
  clashes and the Dec 2014 killings of Adivasis are recorded but were not numerically pulled here (⚠️ open item).
- **Politics:** BTC election **22 Sept 2025, turnout 78.42%: BPF 28, UPPL 7, BJP 5** of 40 (registered
  electorate 2,658,477); **Hagrama Mohilary** is CEM again. The stated demand ("a Bodoland state from seven
  districts") persists in ABSU rhetoric.

### 4.5 Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao

- **KAAC** was created 17 Nov 1951 (10,434 km2 across Karbi Anglong and West Karbi Anglong); population 961,275
  in 2011 per one source (956,313 in the state table - the two differ by ~5,000; ⚠️). ST 56.3%; Christian 16.5%;
  Karbi with Bengali, Nepali, Adivasi, Kuki, Dimasa, Hmar, Tiwa, Garo, Man Tai, Rengma Naga minorities.
- **Karbi Anglong Agreement, 4 Sept 2021:** five insurgent groups, ~1,000 cadres, Rs 1,000 crore over five
  years, KAAC upgraded to KAATC (seats 30->50), a Karbi Welfare Council for Karbis outside the council area.
  Separatist Karbi groups **rejected Sixth Schedule and demand an autonomous state under Article 244(A)**.
- **Dimasa:** 23 April 2023 MoS with DNLA/DPSC (181 cadres; NCHAC renamed DHATC; seats 28->40; Rs 1,000
  crore). The Dimasa historic claim (a Dimaraji) is a cultural-nationalist one; the council area holds only
  214,102 people.
- **Combined:** demands for **one autonomous state of Karbi Anglong, West Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao**
  (1,170,415 people; 12,256 km2) exist but the two peoples' own claims are not one movement. **IDSA's own
  assessment: agreements have not settled statehood demands and have not protected Kukis, Hmars and
  Adivasis inside the council areas.**

### 4.6 The Barak Valley

- 3,624,599 people in 2011 (Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi); **80.8% Bengali speakers; 50.0% Hindu / 48.1%
  Muslim** (the two halves roughly equal - an internal fault line not present in the Brahmaputra Valley);
  Hindi 10%, Manipuri 3.5%, Bishnupriya Manipuri 1.4%, Dimasa 0.6%, plus tea tribes and Khasi and Kuki
  pockets. **The valley exists because Karimganj was transferred in 1947 despite a 50.44% Pakistan vote.**
- **1961 language movement:** on **19 May 1961, eleven Bengali protesters were killed at Silchar** during
  protests over Assamese as sole medium; the settlement kept Bengali official in the valley (⚠️ that the
  valley's official language is Bengali is recalled from general knowledge and consistent with the 80.8% share;
  not directly fetched).
- **Separate-status movement:** the Union Territory Demand Committee has sought UT status since the 1960s;
  the **Barak Democratic Front** seeks a state ("Purbachal," ~42 lakh people) and appealed to the Prime
  Minister in **Nov 2023**; **on 7 Sept 2023 Assam's chief minister said he would not oppose separation "if the
  people of Barak Valley want it."** UTDC alumni warn a new hierarchy could inflame the Hindu-Muslim parity.
  **A demand exists; it is not a mass movement (⚠️ no poll found).**
- **Geography:** it borders Bangladesh (Sylhet) and is the only Assamese-state land route to Tripura and
  Mizoram. **Any Barak Valley detachment isolates Tripura and Mizoram from the Brahmaputra Valley** - this is the
  single most important geographic fact about the Barak Valley for a multi-country design (§10.1).

### 4.7 Upper Assam, the Ahom, and the "six communities"

Upper Assam (7.69 million) is the Ahom-Chutia-Moran-Motok core: 87.9% Hindu, 7.3% Muslim, 13.8% ST (Mising
and Deori). **Tai-Ahom, Chutia, Moran, Motok, Koch-Rajbongshi and the Tea Tribes are the "six communities"
seeking ST status.** The Group of Ministers' report (Assam cabinet, late 2025) proposes ST (Plains) for Moran,
Matak and undivided-Goalpara Koch-Rajbongshi and a **new category "ST (Valley)"** for Ahom, Chutia, tea tribes
and other Koch-Rajbongshi; the **Coordination Committee of Tribal Organisations of Assam rejected it on 2 Jan
2026** as "unconstitutional" and a threat to existing ST reservations. **This is the newest live fault line
inside the Assamese-speaking indigenous camp itself** - if all six were ST, the ST share of Assam would
approach a third to half (⚠️ inference; the six communities' populations are not enumerated because they are
OBC, and Koch-Rajbongshi estimates of "7 million" appear in one Wikipedia figure of doubtful provenance).

### 4.8 Inter-state borders (seams)

- **Assam-Meghalaya:** 12 disputed areas; **29 March 2022 MoU resolved 6** (36.79 km2 split 18.51 Assam /
  18.28 Meghalaya; ~70% of the border now dispute-free); the other six (Langpih, Borduar, Nongwah-Mawtamur, Desh
  Doomreah, Blocks I and II, Psiar-Khanduli - reported list) remain. **Mukroh firing, 22 Nov 2022: six dead.**
  Phase II committees re-constituted in 2025; pillar erection in five areas due by 15 Aug 2025.
- **Assam-Arunachal:** **20 April 2023 MoU: 123 villages, 71 resolved.** **Assam-Nagaland:** Original Suit
  2/1988 in the Supreme Court; Assam alleges ~60,000 hectares encroached; no settlement (seam only).
- **Assam-Mizoram:** a July 2021 border clash (six Assam police killed) is Mizoram's lane - one-line seam.

### 4.9 Economy and material base

- **GSDP ~Rs 871,451 crore (~US$90 billion)**, per capita ~Rs 203,760 (~US$2,100), 17th among Indian states;
  agriculture ~25%, industry ~30%, services ~45% (2021-22); 14.47% below the poverty line (2022-23).
- **Tea:** Assam ~652.95 million kg (about half of India's crop); Dooars + Terai add 226 million kg (over a
  quarter). ⚠️ One source says Assam is "60%" of national output - it depends on the year and source; treat
  as **50-60%**.
- **Oil and gas:** Assam ~25% of India's *onshore* crude (~15% of total); **1,875,460 tonnes crude in Apr-Aug
  2024 (ONGC 439,840; Oil India 1,435,620)**, i.e. roughly 4.5 million tonnes/year annualized; Oil India's FY
  2024-25 record 6.718 MMTOE oil-equivalent and 3.429 bcm gas (Oil India also produces outside Assam).
  **Four refineries totaling ~7 million tonnes/year (Guwahati, Digboi, Numaligarh, Bongaigaon)**; Digboi
  is Asia's first mechanically drilled well (1867). **Numaligarh is tripling from 3 to 9 MMTPA, fed by a
  ~1,635 km import-crude pipeline from Paradip in Odisha** - which would run through what the pinned line
  makes the *rest of India*. ⚠️ **Inference: the expansion's crude supply is not domestic.**
- **Brahmaputra:** flood-driven agriculture and the upstream Medog/Siang dam problem (§10.4).

holds as one is decided less by an independence movement than by whether the state's citizenship politics
leaves room for the ~10 million people it targets.**

### 9.2 What identity binds the Dooars, Jalpaiguri, Alipurduar and Cooch Behar?

**None of them alone; five sub-identities sit on three axes, and the question has to be posed as a choice.**

1. **Rajbanshi / Koch (Kamtapuri) - the only pre-colonial indigenous layer with a state tradition.** The Koch
   kingdom (Kamata/Cooch Behar) is the ancestor; Rajbanshi organized as a "Kshatriya" movement around 1910 under
   Panchanan Barma (Kshatriya Samiti, Rangpur), recognized in the 1911 census; SC in West Bengal, OBC in Assam.
   The **Greater Cooch Behar People's Association** and the **Kamtapur Liberation Organization (KLO)** demand
   a state or Union Territory. **KLO's stated territory is six districts - Cooch Behar, Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri, and
   North and South Dinajpur - and therefore straddles the pinned line: the two Dinajpurs stay in India.**
   **Anant Maharaj (Nagen Roy)**, the movement's leader, sits in the Rajya Sabha for the BJP since 19 Aug 2023.
   The 1949 merger deal promised Cooch Behar special status as a centrally administered area; it merged into
   West Bengal on 1 Jan 1950. Kamtapuri was later notified as an official language (⚠️ by the previous state
   government, not re-verified). ⚠️ Rajbanshi population claims ("Assam 7,021,254; West Bengal 3,983,316, 2011
   census") on Wikipedia are **not credible as census counts** (caste/OBC groups are not enumerated in Assam);
   the 1991 census counted **2,982,280 Rajbongshi speakers**.
2. **Adivasi tea-garden labor** (Oraon/Kurukh, Munda, Santal; lingua franca Sadri) - the tea belt blocks
   above; ST in West Bengal (unlike Assam where they are OBC); brought in from Chotanagpur from the 1870s
   after the Dooars passed to Britain. **No first-settler claim, but the largest labor bloc and the one with
   the least land.**
3. **Nepali / Gorkha laborers and settlers** in Jalpaiguri and Alipurduar (4.9%-9.7% Nepali by language) -
   connect the Dooars to the hills via labor migration.
4. **Bengali (Hindu refugees of 1947 and 1971, plus long-settled Bengali Hindus and Muslims)** - the state's
   official language; Cooch Behar's 25.5% Muslims are largely Bengali-speaking; Siliguri is a Bengali city in a
   Gorkha district.
5. **Small indigenous peoples** - Toto, Mech, Rabha, Garo, Bhutia; most pressed by tea and by border and
   conservation land use.

**The bonds are economic, not ethnic:** (a) tea (Dooars + Terai ~226 million kg, over a quarter of India's crop;
Darjeeling's high-value crop besides) and (b) **the trade corridor to Bhutan (Jaigaon) and Nepal
(Kakarbhitta-Panitanki)**. A "Bengali-cultural remnant" reading applies only to Siliguri and perhaps Cooch Behar
Sadar; it does not describe the tea belt or the Rajbanshi countryside.

- **The Gorkhaland *territorial* claim** is wider than the hills: the movement claims **Darjeeling, Kalimpong

## Section 10 - Cross-cutting: the shape of the breakaway landmass

### 10.1 The corridor from the other side, and the internal neck

- **The Siliguri Corridor** is ~200 km long, 17-60 km wide, 20-22 km at the narrowest, ~12,200 km2. **Under
  the pinned line (points 1-3 in the first pass), the breakaway landmass would hold Siliguri, New Jalpaiguri
  junction, Bagdogra, Hasimara and the Panitanki/Jaigaon crossings; India's remainder would hold only the strip
  south and west of the Mahananda (Kishanganj, Uttar Dinajpur).** ⚠️ The exact location of Indian bases (XXXIII
  Corps HQ at Sukna, Hasimara) relative to the line is inferred from general knowledge and not
  re-verified; the 2025 announcement of new garrisons at Kishanganj and Chopra places two of them on the *Indian* side, while the third (Bamunigaon, Assam) is on the breakaway side. That NJP, Bagdogra, Panitanki and Jaigaon lie in Darjeeling and Alipurduar districts is general knowledge, not fetched.
- **Then the internal neck moves east.** The breakaway landmass is an east-west ribbon: Sikkim/Gorkha hills/
  Siliguri (west) - Jalpaiguri/Alipurduar/Cooch Behar (center) - Assam (east). **Between Bhutan (north) and
  Bangladesh (south), the Cooch Behar-to-Dhubri/Kokrajhar strip is the new chicken's neck for this territory.**
  ⚠️ **Not measured; a Stage 3 geometric measurement is needed.**
- **Adjacency (who touches whom):** Meghalaya touches only Assam and Bangladesh; Tripura touches only Assam
  (Barak Valley), Mizoram and Bangladesh; Sikkim touches only the Gorkha hills and Nepal/Bhutan/China; Arunachal
  touches Assam, Nagaland, Bhutan, China, Myanmar; **every unit except Sikkim/Gorkha hills/Siliguri and the
  Dooars reaches every other only through Assam.** **Assam is structurally the hub: any design that splits the
  Barak Valley, the Karbi/Dima Hasao spine or the Dooars-Assam junction cuts a link that no alternative land
  route replaces.**

### 10.2 Sea access and Bangladesh

The breakaway landmass has **no sea coast** and India's own ports (Haldia, Kolkata) lie behind the line. It
the Northeast states share ~1,880 km with Bangladesh (Assam ~263 km, Meghalaya 443, Tripura 856, Mizoram 318 -
computed; Yunus's own figure was "nearly 1,600 km"), plus the northern-Bengal segments (not separately pulled; of
the India-Bangladesh total 4,096.7 km, West Bengal has 2,216.7 in all). **Chittagong and Mongla have been
usable for NE transit since a 2023 agreement**, but **on 8 April 2025 India withdrew Bangladesh's trans-shipment
facility after Muhammad Yunus described the Northeast as "landlocked" with Bangladesh as "the guardian of the
ocean"** - showing the leverage runs both ways. **Kaladan** (Kolkata-Sittwe sea leg, Sittwe-Paletwa river leg,
109 km Paletwa-Zorinpui road to Mizoram) is targeted for 2027 but depends on the Arakan Army, which by
early 2025 held nearly all of Rakhine (a Mizoram/Myanmar seam). **Tripura-Chittagong** is the shortest sea
outlet for the Northeast; Agartala-Akhaura rail (inaugurated Nov 2023, not yet running) is its road-map.

### 10.3 China, Bhutan and the Chumbi Valley

**Doklam standoff 16 June-28 Aug 2017** (India moved ~270 troops on 18 June); China cites the 1890 convention;
Bhutan and India place the border at the Jampheri Ridge; it ended in "mutual disengagement." **China-Bhutan
boundary talks are still running: 25 rounds and the 15th Expert Group Meeting (30 March-1 April 2026, Beijing)
on a "Three-Step Roadmap"**; the Chumbi Valley is the wedge next to Sikkim. **A Bhutan-China settlement that
concedes Doklam would put Chinese territory closer to the corridor** (⚠️ analysts' inference, not a sourced
fact). **Tawang and the 27-place renaming** are §8.

### 10.4 Water and the Brahmaputra

**Medog (Yarlung Tsangpo lower reaches): construction officially began 19 July 2025, cost 1.2 trillion yuan
(~US$165 billion), 60 GW installed capacity, ~300 billion kWh a year (over three times Three Gorges),
five cascade stations over ~50 km with a ~2,000 m drop, ~30 km from the border.** India's answer is
the **Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (11,000+ MW, 2028-2032 timeline as reported)**, blocked by Adi protest.
**Assam's floodplain agriculture, fisheries and Guwahati's water depend on the river; there is no
sourced water-sharing treaty with China** (⚠️ general knowledge). **Sikkim's Teesta dams** (Teesta III failed in
Oct 2023) and the un-signed India-Bangladesh Teesta water-sharing question (⚠️ not researched here) sit
downstream of the Gorkha hills.
