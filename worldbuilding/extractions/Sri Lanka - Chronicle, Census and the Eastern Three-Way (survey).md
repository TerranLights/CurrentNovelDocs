## Headline findings

1. **First effective settlement fails on deep history but reappears in two later layers.** The island has two
   rival chronicle claims (the Sinhala Buddhist Mahavamsa tradition from about 543 BCE; the Tamil Jaffna-kingdom
   tradition) that no Woodard-style test can adjudicate. But two real settlement events behave like Woodard's
   doctrine: the **British plantation labor system** (South Indian laborers from 1827) and the **state-sponsored
   Sinhalese colonization of the dry-zone east** (Gal Oya from 1949, Mahaweli from the 1960s-80s), the direct
   cause of the Eastern Province's mix. See §1 and §1.2.
2. **The Eastern Province is the load-bearing seam and it breaks the "whole first-order subdivisions" rule.**
   2024 census, province level: **Moor/Muslim 39.5%, Sri Lankan Tamil 38.1%, Sinhalese 22.1%**, no majority; no
   district resolves it (Batticaloa is 71.3% Tamil, but Ampara is 45.6% Muslim / 37.1% Sinhalese / 16.9% Tamil
   and Trincomalee 46.1% Muslim / 28.9% Tamil / 24.5% Sinhalese). See §3 and §11.
3. **The Northern Province is the cleanest whole-province fit**: 1,150,148 people, **91.5% Sri Lankan Tamil**.
   Any Tamil-area claim beyond it is a decision about the East (§10).
4. **The "ethnic groups" are partly census artifacts.** The colonial census split Sinhalese into Low Country and
   Kandyan through 1971, then dropped it; "Moors" are a separate ethnicity on religious grounds though most speak
   Tamil; "Indian Tamil" was renamed "Indian Tamil/Malaiyaga Thamilar" for 2024. The proposed replacement
   framework (chronicle polity + administrative category) treats these categories as evidence of how identities
   were administratively frozen (§1.3).
5. **Up-country (Malaiyaha) Tamils are shrinking, scattered and a majority of no province**: 600,360 (2.76%) in
   2024, down from about 1.17 million in 1971 after the 1948 Act made about 700,000 stateless. **Nuwara Eliya is
   the only district where they are the largest group (50.0%)**; 92% live in seven hill districts (§4.4).
6. **The 2021-22 crisis is a real stress test of an import-dependent island**: default on 12 April 2022, power
   cuts up to 15 hours, and a reported 20-32% rice fall after the April 2021 fertilizer ban. Recovery is real
   (5% growth in 2024 and 2025, IMF program on track) but Cyclone Ditwah (Nov-Dec 2025, 647 dead, worst in the
   tea highlands) shows how thin the buffer is (§7, §9).
7. **The ports are a three-power arrangement**: Colombo (about 70% transshipment, about 60% of cargo
   India-linked) with Chinese and Indian-led terminals; Hambantota (99-year Chinese lease, 2017); Trincomalee (a
   natural harbor in the most contested province, with an April 2025 India-UAE-Sri Lanka energy hub MoU). The
   port map and the ethnic map overlap only at Trincomalee (§8).
8. **2024 politics**: the JVP-led NPP holds 159 of 225 seats and won Jaffna district (a first for a non-Tamil
   party), but the North-East voted for Sajith Premadasa and the common Tamil candidate in the presidential
   round, the NPP's devolution stance is ambiguous, and **provincial councils have been inactive since
   2017-2019** (§7).

## Section 1 — Method: does first effective settlement hold, fail or need replacing?

Per this project's method (`METHOD - How Nations Get Drawn.md`), Woodard's doctrine requires an arriving
population founding a self-perpetuating society in territory that was, from the arriving side's point of view,
open — and it is corroborated, not replaced, by present-day ancestry. Sri Lanka gives a **split verdict**.

### 1.1 Where it fails: the deep chronicle layer

- **Two rival first-settlement claims exist as living political ideologies.** The Sinhala Buddhist claim rests on
  the **Mahavamsa** (Pali chronicle compiled by the monk Mahanama in the 5th-6th century CE at the Mahavihara
  in Anuradhapura), which narrates Prince Vijaya's arrival (traditional date 543 BCE), the arrival of Buddhism
  under Mahinda (3rd century BCE), and the Dutugemunu-Elara story. Modern scholarship treats the sections from
  roughly Devanampiyatissa onward as corroborated by archaeology and inscriptions, and the Vijaya and
  Buddha-visit sections as disputed. In 2023 UNESCO listed the Mahavamsa as documentary heritage. It has also
  become central to Sinhala Buddhist nationalism, a use contested by Tamil scholars (Wikipedia, "Mahavamsa").
  The Tamil counter-claim rests on the Jaffna kingdom (1215-1619 in the fetched summary) and the Vanni
  chieftaincies, and on archaeological evidence of Tamil presence from about the 2nd century BCE that the
  scholarly consensus treats as debated (Wikipedia, "Sri Lankan Tamils").
- **Neither claim can be adjudicated by "who was first and effective."** Both are chronicle identities: a
  people defined by a founding text and a court tradition, not by a documented settlement event. This is a
  fifth failure mode alongside the four already recorded in the Central Asia survey (no founding moment; chosen
  Indianization; old imperial core; engineered borders). **The Sri Lanka failure is "two founding myths, each
  with a state tradition behind it."**
- **Genetics is not a tiebreaker either.** The fetched sources note strong gene flow between Sinhalese and
  Sri Lankan Tamils and South Indian affinities for the Sinhalese (Wikipedia, "Sinhalese people"); the small
  Vedda population is genetically closer to some Indian tribal groups in a 2024 study (Wikipedia, "Vedda").
  ⚠️ I treat these as color, not as a basis for any border claim.

### 1.2 Where it holds, in a different form: two real settlement layers

- **The plantation layer (1827 onward).** Indian Tamils arrived from 1827, recruited from South Indian
  districts to work coffee and then tea estates (tea was introduced in 1867 by James Taylor after coffee leaf
  rust). The British recruited roughly 780,000 South Indian laborers over the colonial period (Wikipedia,
  "Ceylon Citizenship Act"). This is a **captive-labor frontier**: the population was placed in the highlands
  by outside capital and kept there by the estate system, the same structural shape as Woodard's plantation
  regions, but with no landowning settler class of its own. The 2024 estate-rural sector is still **80.5%
  Hindu** (865,679 people, 4.0% of the island).
- **The state-colonization layer (1949 onward).** The Gal Oya scheme (from 1949; 120,000 acres, 40
  settlements) is the documented case: in the summary source only six settlements went to Tamils (900
  families, mostly local), while more than 7,000 Sinhalese families from outside were settled in the rest.
  Ampara district was created in 1961 by bifurcating the colonized areas from Batticaloa. In Trincomalee
  district, the Sinhalese population rose from 15,706 (21%) in 1946 to 85,503 (33%) in 1981 (search-result
  summary of Wikipedia's "State-sponsored Sinhalese colonisation" and district pages). **This is the closest
  thing in the project's world set to an actual Woodard-style founding event, and it happened in living memory
  by government decision.** ⚠️ Its interpretation is itself the central political dispute (Tamil nationalists
  call it "colonization" of a traditional homeland; supporters call it dry-zone development and resettlement of
  the landless). I present the fact and the dispute, not a verdict.

### 1.3 The replacement framework proposed here: "chronicle polity + administrative category"

As the Central Asia and Southeast Asia surveys did, this survey proposes a replacement that answers the
doctrine's real question (*who are these people and why do they stay distinct?*) without needing a founding
event.

| Layer | What it supplies | Sri Lanka instance |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronicle polity** | The founding text or court tradition a community anchors on | Sinhala Buddhist: Mahavamsa / Anuradhapura-Polonnaruwa-Kandy. Tamil Hindu-Christian: Jaffna kingdom / Vanni / Batticaloa coast. Tamil-speaking Muslim: trade-port and coastal-settlement tradition (traders traced by some to the 9th century; ⚠️ genetic studies suggest mainly Indian origin per Wikipedia) |
| **Administrative category** | The census or legal box that froze the identity | Low Country vs Kandyan Sinhalese (census 1901-1971); Sri Lanka Moor vs Indian Moor (to 1971); Ceylon Tamil vs Indian Tamil; Burgher and Malay as colonial-military categories |
| **Settlement overlay** | The real migration events that changed the map | Plantation labor 1827-; dry-zone colonization 1949-; wartime displacement 1983-2009 |

**Tier 1 (which polity a territory would belong to):** Sinhala Buddhist / Sri Lankan Tamil / Tamil-speaking
Muslim / Malaiyaha Tamil (four), with the caveat below.
**Tier 2 (internal divisions):** within Sinhalese, **Low Country vs Kandyan**; within Sri Lankan Tamils, **Jaffna
vs Vanni vs Batticaloa** (⚠️ regional differentiation among Tamils is widely reported but this pass did not
source it; treat as unverified); within Muslims, **Eastern Muslims vs Colombo/Western trader Muslims vs
Malays**; within Christians, a cross-cutting layer (§4.5) that does **not** align with ethnic tier 1 at all.

**⚠️ Caveats stated up front.**
1. **Religion is not a clean proxy for ethnicity here.** Catholics are 29.4% of Puttalam district and 18.2% of
   Gampaha but only 0.3-1.3% of most Tamil districts, while Mannar is 46.6% Catholic (2024 census). "Christian"
   is a cross-cutting identity, not a tier-1 group (§4.5).
2. **The census is self-reported.** The 2024 census says ethnicity is "the ethnicity reported by individuals
   themselves." Category changes (Veddas reintroduced; "Malaiyaga Thamilar" added) and shifts between categories
   (§4.4) mean tier-2 evidence is soft at the margins.
3. **Tier 1 for Muslims is contestable.** Officially a separate ethnicity, distinct from Tamils, "primarily
   under Islamic religious identity rather than linguistic markers," though most speak Tamil (Wikipedia, "Sri
   Lankan Moors"). Whether they are a tier-1 grouping or a tier-2 division inside a "Tamil-speaking" tier 1 is
   the exact question the Eastern Province forces (§10).

## Section 2 — Physical geography

- **Size and shape.** 65,610 sq km, the world's 25th largest island, comparable to Tasmania (Wikipedia,
  "Geography of Sri Lanka"). The nine provincial areas sum to the same figure.
- **Central Highlands.** A southern-central massif rising to Pidurutalagala (2,524 m). The tea belt sits here
  (Nuwara Eliya, Uva, Dimbula; mid-country around Kandy; low-country tea in Ruhuna and Sabaragamuwa).
- **Wet zone / dry zone.** The wet zone (southwest and mountains) receives more than 2,500 mm a year; the dry zone
  (east, north, southeast and much of the north-central plains) roughly 1,200-1,900 mm, with drier fringes
  (600-1,200 mm) on parts of the northwest and southeast coasts (same source). **The demographic map follows the
  rain**: 83.6% Sinhalese Western Province (wet, densest, 1,661 people per sq km) versus 129 per sq km in the
  Northern and 134 in the North Central (my calculations from 2024 census populations and the areas in §3.1).
- **Monsoons.** The southwest monsoon (May-October) waters the wet zone; the northeast monsoon (December-March)
  waters the north and east. Two paddy seasons (Maha and Yala) follow the same split. ⚠️ The season names are
  from background knowledge, not the fetched summary.
- **The Mahaweli.** The longest river (335 km; Wikipedia). The Mahaweli Development Programme (concept 1961; a
  master plan prepared with UN help; accelerated after 1977 by the Jayewardene government, compressing a 30-year
  timeline into about six years) built Victoria (210 MW), Kotmale (200 MW) and Randenigala (135 MW) dams and
  aimed at about 365,000 hectares of irrigation and 470 MW of hydropower; Moragahakanda (2007-2018) was the last
  major reservoir. **The Mahaweli is both the island's water-and-power backbone and the delivery mechanism of
  the dry-zone settlement policy** that shifted the ethnic map of the north-central and eastern dry zone. It also
  displaced the Vedda (below).
- **The Palk Strait.** The northern Jaffna peninsula lies about 22 miles from India (Wikipedia, "Northern
  Province"). Adam's Bridge is a chain of limestone shoals, mostly submerged after a storm around 1480.
- **The Jaffna peninsula vs the Vanni.** The Northern Province has two distinct regions: the peninsula (relying
  on underground aquifers) and the Vanni (irrigated by rivers and tanks).

### 3.3 Comparison to 1981, and what has moved

- **Eastern Province, 1981** (search summary): Tamils 410,156 (42.06%), Muslims 315,436 (32.34%), Sinhalese
  243,701 (24.99%). **2024**: Moor/Muslim 39.5%, Tamil 38.1%, Sinhalese 22.1%. **Muslims have moved from a
  clear second to the plurality**, largely through a higher growth rate plus wartime Tamil emigration; the
  Sinhalese share has slipped slightly since 1981. ⚠️ The mechanism (growth versus emigration) is my inference
  from the numbers, not a sourced finding.
- **Trincomalee district:** 1981 Tamil 36.4%, Sinhalese 33.4%, Muslim 29.3% (Wikipedia). By 2012 Moors were the
  largest (40.4%), and by 2024 they are 46.1%. The census itself flags "the most significant increases" in the
  Moor/Muslim population in the Ampara, Trincomalee and Colombo districts.
- **Batticaloa district:** Tamil 71.98% (1981), 72.61% (2012), 71.3% (2024): stable. Sinhalese fell from 3.4% to

  the Weli Oya area (⚠️ the Weli Oya link is background knowledge, not verified this pass). Mannar's Sinhalese
  fell from 2,305 to 678, and its Muslim population doubled (16,436 to 32,752), consistent with returns after
  the 1990 expulsion (§6.4).

### 4.1 Sinhalese Buddhists, and the Low Country / Kandyan divide

- **Size and base.** 74.1% nationally; 94.8% of the Southern, 90.5% of the North Central, 86.5% of Sabaragamuwa,
  84.8% of the North Western and 83.6% of the Western provinces. About 93% of Sinhalese are Theravada Buddhist
  (Wikipedia, "Sinhalese people"); the rest are largely Christian (§4.5).
- **The split.** "Since 1815 the Sinhalese divided into two subgroups: upland Kandyan communities and coastal
  lowland populations" (same source). **The colonial census kept the split from 1901 through 1971 and then
  dropped it** (census text). In 1911 Low Country Sinhalese were 1,716,900 and Kandyan 998,600 (63% and 37% of
  Sinhalese); in 1971, the last census with the split, they were 5,425,800 and 3,705,500 (59% and 41%). Since
  1981 there is **no census-level evidence** for the divide at all, so any district or province mapping of it
  is inference, not data.
- **The geography (⚠️ inference, not sourced this pass).** The Kandyan heartland corresponds to Kandy, Matale and
  the highlands of Uva and Sabaragamuwa; the Low Country to the Western and Southern coastal belt. The
  Portuguese arrived in 1505, the Dutch displaced them by 1658, and both held only the coast; **the Kandyan
  kingdom (1594-1815 in the fetched summary) remained independent** until the British Kandyan Convention of 1815,
  and Colebrooke-Cameron (1833) unified the island's administration. That history is why the coastal population
  had three colonial layers (Catholic conversion under the Portuguese, Dutch Reformed and Roman-Dutch law, then
  British) and the highland population had one. The census notes that Kandyan Sinhalese retain the option of
  marrying under Kandyan law (census text, marriage chapter).
- **Political weight.** The JVP, now governing, drew historically on rural and southern Sinhalese support (§5.3).
  ⚠️ The 1950s "Kandyan federation" proposals are background knowledge I could not verify in this pass; flagged
  for the next pass.

### 4.2 Sri Lankan Tamils (north and east)

- **2.68 million (12.3%)**; the census's own Table 6.1.11 implies **64.6% live in the Northern and Eastern
  provinces** in 2024 (my calculation; the Wikipedia summary's older "70%" figure appears out of date). The rest
  are in Colombo (243,856), Kandy (122,772), Ratnapura (91,300), Badulla (81,854), Gampaha and elsewhere.
- **Where they are the majority.** Jaffna (98.6%), Kilinochchi (97.3%), Mullaitivu (88.1%), Vavuniya (78.4%),
  Mannar (72.7%), Batticaloa (71.3%). Not the majority in Trincomalee (28.9%) or Ampara (16.9%).
- **Religion.** The Northern Province is 72.1% Hindu and 19.7% Christian (Wikipedia). Jaffna is 82.3% Hindu,
  13.0% Catholic; Mannar is 46.6% Catholic (my calculation from census Table 6.1.15). Batticaloa is 62.9% Hindu,
  27.1% Muslim, 9.0% Christian.
- **Diaspora.** Estimates of Sri Lankan Tamils abroad range from **450,000 to one million** (Wikipedia): Canada
  about 102,000-222,500, UK about 120,000, France about 100,000, Germany about 60,000. ⚠️ A wide range; this
  matters because the diaspora funded the LTTE and continues to fund political activity (§6).
- **Historical anchors.** Jaffna kingdom (1215-1619); a caste society "comparable to South Indian structures";
  Shaivism dominant; maritime commerce; less feudalized than the Sinhalese kingdoms, with salaried soldiers and
  officials paid in cash (Wikipedia, "Jaffna kingdom"). It was conquered by Kotte around 1450, regained
  independence in 1467, and ended when the Portuguese defeated Cankili II in 1619.

### 4.3 Moors and Malays

- **Sri Lanka Moors: 2,283,246 (10.5%)**, up from 5.7% in 1911. Concentrated in the Western Province (709,992) and
  Eastern Province (575,936) per the fetched summary; district numbers in Table 6.1.11 show Ampara (339,290),
  Colombo (285,346), Kandy (219,905), Trincomalee (203,987), Puttalam (174,497), Batticaloa (160,284). **The Moor/Muslim
  population rose in every district between 2012 and 2024** (census text).
- **Language and identity.** Most speak Tamil; many also speak Sinhala (58.7%) and English (30.4%). Some claim
  Arab-trader descent from the 9th century; genetic studies point to mainly Indian origins (Wikipedia). Officially
  a separate ethnicity, categorized "primarily under Islamic religious identity rather than linguistic
  markers."
- **The Eastern pattern.** A Muslim-majority coastal belt in Ampara district (Kalmunai is the province's largest
  city) and Trincomalee's Muslim-majority district. Kattankudy (Batticaloa district) is the site of the 3 August
  1990 mosque massacre by the LTTE, over 147 killed per the search summary (§6.4).
- **Malays: 26,650 (0.12%)**, down from 44,130 in 2012 and about 54,800 in 2001 (18 districts only), with 24,718 in the Western
  Province. Descended from political exiles, soldiers and convicts brought by the Dutch East India Company from
  the mid-1600s (Java, Madura, Ambon, the Banda Islands); almost all Sunni Muslim; a Malay variety heavily
  restructured by Sinhala and Tamil contact. Genetically 55-61% Sri Lankan and 39-45% Malay in a 2015 study
  (Wikipedia, "Sri Lankan Malays"). This ties into the Indian Ocean trade-port framing in the Maritime Southeast
  Asia survey (§8 there): Sri Lanka's Malays are the same Dutch-colonial diaspora seen from the other end.

### 4.4 Indian-origin (Malaiyaha / Up-country) Tamils

- **Size.** 1,174,900 (1971 peak) to 818,700 (1981) to 839,504 (2012) to **600,360 in 2024** (2.76%). The fall
  reflects repatriation to India and, after 2003, absorbed citizenship. The 2024 decline occurred in every
  district except Hambantota.
- **Where.** Of the 600,360, **550,496 (92%) are in seven hill districts**: Nuwara Eliya 362,299, Kandy 38,311,
  Badulla 95,686, Ratnapura 23,991, Kegalle 21,244, Matale 7,716, Moneragala 1,249 (my sums from Table 6.1.11).
  **Nuwara Eliya alone holds 60.3% of all Indian Tamils in the country**, up from 45.0% in 2012 (the census's own
  statement; this is the district's share of the national group, not the group's share of the district, which is
  50.0%). Province-level: **Central 15.0%, Uva 6.9%, Sabaragamuwa 2.2%**.
- **Religion and sector.** About 90% Hindu (Wikipedia); the estate-rural sector is 80.5% Hindu and 865,679
  strong (4.0% of the population). ⚠️ The estate sector's own ethnic composition is not in the pages I read;
  the census contains that table and I did not extract it.
- **Citizenship.** The **1948 Ceylon Citizenship Act** required proof that the applicant's father was born in
  Ceylon; **only about 5,000 Indian Tamils qualified, and more than 700,000 (about 11% of the population) were
  made stateless**; the **1949 Act** also removed their vote. Repatriation and citizenship agreements followed:
  the 1954 Nehru-Kotelawala pact (India refused automatic citizenship), the **1964 Sirima-Shastri Pact** (525,000
  repatriations and 300,000 grants), the **1974 Sirimavo-Gandhi Pact** (the remaining ~150,000 stateless), a
  **1988** Act for the rest, and a final **2003 Act granting citizenship to 168,141** (Wikipedia). ⚠️ Different
  summaries cite about 600,000 as the number repatriated versus the 525,000 planned; the pacts' planned and
  actual numbers differ.
- **A possible self-identification shift (⚠️ my inference).** Between 2012 and 2024 the Indian Tamil count fell
  by about 239,000, while Sri Lanka Tamil counts in the hill districts rose sharply: **Badulla 21,880 to 81,854;
  Kandy 69,210 to 122,772; Nuwara Eliya 32,563 to 60,288; Ratnapura 54,437 to 91,300** (Table 6.1.11). Part of
  the shift plausibly reflects people reporting as "Sri Lanka Tamil" rather than the (renamed) Indian Tamil
  category. The census does not say this; it only reports the numbers and notes the Badulla, Kandy and
  Batticaloa increases. Treat the hill-district Tamil totals as one combined Tamil-speaking hill population until
  a later pass checks.
- **Conditions.** Persistent poverty, wages far below national norms; the fetched source gives a nominal wage of
  Rs 101 per day in 1998 (a dated figure, no longer useful). ⚠️ Current wage and living-standard data were not
  gathered this pass.

### 4.5 Burghers, Veddas, Chettys, Bharathas, and Christians

- **Burghers**: 31,721 (0.15%), down from a peak of about 46,000 (1953) through emigration; Colombo (10,643),
  Gampaha (7,030), Batticaloa (2,899). Descended from Portuguese and Dutch colonial populations (background; the
  Wikipedia page I tried returned 404).
- **Veddas**: **1,373 in the 2024 census** (reintroduced as a category "at the request of data users"; folded
  into "Other" from 1963). Wikipedia cites 2,500-6,600 for 2001 on other counting. The Vedda language, a
  Sinhala-based creole, is nearing extinction, and **the Mahaweli project (1977-1983) and Maduru Oya National
  Park displaced them**: a "first people" with no territorial recognition.
- **Chettys (2,443) and Bharathas (1,183)**: small colonial-era trading and fishing communities.
- **Christians: 1,506,533 (6.92%)**; Roman Catholic 1,224,348 (5.6%), other Christian 282,185 (1.3%).
  Catholics cluster on the northwest coastal arc: Gampaha 442,291, Puttalam 240,975, Colombo 139,882. In Puttalam
  Catholics are 29.4% while Buddhists are 44.1% and Sinhalese 72.3%, so roughly a third or more of the
  district's Sinhalese must be Catholic (⚠️ the Catholic fishing-community belt is background, not verified).
  Tamil Christians: Jaffna 77,197 Catholic + 20,857 other; Mannar 57,713 + 5,560; Batticaloa 25,803 + 27,728.
  **Christianity crosses both major linguistic groups** and weakens any simple Sinhalese-Buddhist versus
  Tamil-Hindu map; it is not a territorial marker.

## Section 5 — Deep history: kingdoms, Ceylon and independence

### 5.1 Kingdoms and colonial layers
- **Anuradhapura period (377 BCE-1017)** with a Chola conquest (993 in the fetched summary; 1017 elsewhere; a
  flagged discrepancy); **Polonnaruwa (1056-1232)**; a transitional era (1232-1505) of capitals at Dambadeniya,
  Gampola and Kotte. ⚠️ The "collapse of the dry-zone irrigation civilization" narrative is widely told but not
  sourced here.
- **Jaffna kingdom (1215-1619)**: arose after Kalinga Magha's 1215 invasion; tribute to the Pandyan Empire, then
  independent in the early 14th century; conquered by Kotte around 1450; independent again 1467; ended by the
  Portuguese in 1619. Its reach included the **Vanni and parts of the east coast**, which matters for the
  Eastern Province argument (§10).
- **Colonial Ceylon**: Portuguese from 1505; Dutch displaced them by 1658; British 1796-1948; the **Kandyan
  kingdom (1594-1815 in the fetched summary)** survived until the **Kandyan Convention of 1815**;
  **Colebrooke-Cameron (1833)** unified the administration; **universal suffrage 1931** (Donoughmore). Coffee
  gave way to tea from 1867. Trincomalee changed hands repeatedly (Danish 1620, Dutch 1639-1795, French 1782,
  British 1795-1948); the Portuguese destroyed the Koneswaram temple there in 1622.

### 5.2 Independence, language and riots
- **4 February 1948** independence; the **1948 Citizenship Act** followed within months (§4.4).
- **1956 Sinhala Only Act**, despite nearly a quarter of the population using Tamil as primary language. The
  **1957 Bandaranaike-Chelvanayakam pact** (Tamil administrative status in the north and east) was cancelled under
  pressure from Sinhala nationalists and some monks.
- **1958 riots (22 May-2 June)**: deaths estimated from **158 (official) to about 1,500**, a state of emergency
  only on 27 May. ⚠️ Range from irreconcilable sources.
- **1970s**: 1970 "double standards" in university admissions and civil service; 1972 republican constitution;
  1973 Federal Party demand for a separate state; **1976 Vaddukoddai Resolution**; 1977 TULF won 18 seats on
  that platform; **1983 Sixth Amendment** expelled TULF members from parliament.
- **Black July (24-30 July 1983)**, triggered by the 23 July LTTE ambush that killed 13 soldiers: **government
  claim about 300; independent estimates 400-3,000; Tamil Centre for Human Rights 5,638**; about 18,000 homes
  and 5,000 shops destroyed; **150,000-250,000 displaced**; 53 Tamil prisoners killed at Welikada on 25 July;
  mobs held voter lists; no prosecutions or compensation (Wikipedia). ⚠️ Range, not a point estimate; the
  5,638 figure is an upper bound from an interested source.

### 5.3 The IPKF and the JVP
- **Indo-Sri Lanka Accord, 29 July 1987** (Rajiv Gandhi and J. R. Jayewardene): created Provincial Councils (13th
  Amendment, 14 November 1987), made Tamil an official language, and brought in the **Indian Peace Keeping Force
  (July 1987-March 1990)**, peaking near **100,000 troops**, with **1,165 killed and 3,009 wounded** by Indian
  official disclosure; estimates of over **10,000** Tamil civilians killed or disappeared during the operation
  are low-confidence. The IPKF left in March 1990 at both governments' order. ⚠️ Sinhalese-south hostility to
  the Accord, which fed the JVP's second insurrection, is background knowledge, not sourced this pass.
- **JVP (Marxist-Leninist)**: **1971 uprising**, deaths estimated **5,000-30,000**; **1987-1989 insurgency**,
  **40,000-60,000**; founder Rohana Wijeweera killed in 1989. ⚠️ Sinhalese-on-Sinhalese figures, wide ranges.
  The party that led the NPP coalition to power in 2024 (§7.3).

## Section 6 — The Northern-Eastern merger, the war and the de facto state

### 6.1 The merger (1988-2006) and demerger
- **September 1988**: President Jayewardene issued proclamations merging the Northern and Eastern provinces into
  a single **North Eastern Province**, presented as a **temporary measure** pending a referendum in the East that
  **was never held** (Wikipedia, "Provinces of Sri Lanka"). A Provincial Council election was held in 1988.
- **March 1990**: the Chief Minister of the North Eastern Province, A. Varadaraja Perumal, "moved a motion
  declaring independent Eelam"; President Premadasa dissolved the council and imposed direct rule (Wikipedia,
  "Provincial councils of Sri Lanka"). The merged province was then run by centrally appointed governors for
  most of the period until 2006.
- **16 October 2006**: the Supreme Court ruled the 1988 proclamations "null and void."
- **1 January 2007**: the North Eastern Province was formally demerged into the Northern and Eastern provinces.
- **The merged unit's demographics in 2024 terms**: 2,933,362 people, **59.0% Sri Lankan Tamil, 26.0% Moor/
  Muslim, 14.6% Sinhalese**, 0.2% Indian Tamil (my aggregation of the eight districts; ⚠️ the merger did not
  happen in 2024, this is the equivalent boundary). **Tamil-speaking (Tamil plus Moor/Muslim) share: 85.0%.**
- ⚠️ **The reasons for the 2006 demerger** (a JVP-supported petition, and Muslim and Sinhalese objections in the
  East) are background knowledge I did not verify. I include the ruling and the fact that the 1988 measure's
  own referendum promise went unfulfilled.

### 6.2 The war and the de facto Tamil Eelam
- **Dates**: 23 July 1983 to **18 May 2009**, about 26 years; four "Eelam Wars" plus the 1987-1990 Indian
  intervention (Wikipedia). The **2002 ceasefire** (a unilateral LTTE declaration, dropping the separate-state
  demand for regional autonomy in the fetched summary) preceded the 2008-2009 final offensive. ⚠️ The 2004
  Karuna-faction split in the East and the government's 2007 capture of the East are background knowledge, not
  verified here.
- **Territory**: at its **2000 peak the LTTE controlled about 76% of the Northern and Eastern provinces'
  landmass** (Wikipedia); a share of land area, not of population.
- **Institutions**: courts, a police force, a bank, media outlets, humanitarian-coordination offices; a de facto
  state period of 1984-2009 in that source (a Kilinochchi capital is background knowledge). The armed wing had
  "at least 11 separate divisions" including a naval and an air wing.
- **After 2009**: the Tamil National Alliance abandoned independence for federalism; the diaspora Transnational
  Government of Tamil Eelam continues non-violent advocacy; diaspora referendums in ten countries in 2009-2010.
  The LTTE was destroyed in May 2009 and Prabhakaran killed on 18-19 May.

accusations of war crimes (Wikipedia).

### 6.4 Muslims and the war
- **October 1990**: the LTTE forcibly expelled Muslims from the Northern Province ("Black October," 15-30 October
  starting with Chavakachcheri and ending with Jaffna town), estimated at **72,000-75,000** people, "about
  20,000 Muslim families" from Jaffna alone (search summary). In 2002 Prabhakaran formally apologized
  (Wikipedia, "Sri Lankan Moors"). Only a small fraction of the families had returned by the late 2010s per the
  same source. Mannar's Muslim population doubling since 2012 (§3.3) fits a partial return, an inference.
- **3 August 1990**, **Kattankudy mosque massacre** in Batticaloa district: **over 147** worshippers killed (search
  summary; ⚠️ the count "over 147" is from press, numbers vary by source).
- **Structural consequence:** the Eastern Muslims and the Northern Muslims have a memory of being victims of the
  Tamil nationalist movement, which is why **a Tamil-majority North-East unit is not automatically a Muslim-
  supported one** (⚠️ this is inference from the events plus background on the merger debate; verify in the next
  pass).

## Section 7 — Post-war politics, the 2022 collapse and 2024 election

### 7.1 The provincial-council machinery
- **13th Amendment (14 November 1987).** Provincial Councils exist under it; the Wikipedia summary lists devolved
  subjects (agriculture, education, health, housing, local government, planning, road transport, social services)
  but flags "practical problems in devolving land, the police and financial powers."
- **Current status:** "All provincial councils are inactive"; the Western Province council's term ended in April
  2019, the most recent dissolution, and "no elections have been held since 2017-2019 dissolutions." The stated
  reason is "lack of consensus on the delimitation of provincial councils." The Northern Province last voted in
  **September 2013**; the fetched summary lists the Eastern Province's last vote as **May 2008** (⚠️ I recall an
  Eastern election in 2012; not verified). **The NPP government's position is ambiguous** in 2026 search
  summaries: some figures support Provincial Councils, others smaller administrative units; an election in 2026
  was discussed but reportedly deferred by disaster relief spending to possibly 2027 (⚠️ press summaries, not a
  primary statement). **Pending decisions**: a new constitution, election timing, and whether land and police
  powers are devolved.

### 7.2 The 2019-2022 crisis
- **Causes** (Wikipedia): 2019 tax cuts, central bank money printing, the **April 2021 abrupt ban on chemical
  fertilizers** (reversed after about seven months), the **2019 Easter bombings**, and COVID's collapse of
  tourism.
- **Facts**: reserves fell to about **$2.31 billion in February 2022**; **sovereign default on 12 April 2022**,
  the first in Asia-Pacific this century; fuel and medicine shortages; power cuts up to 15 hours a day;
  **inflation peak 17.5% in the fetched summary** (⚠️ I recall headline CPI peaks far higher in late 2022; do not
  use 17.5% without a primary CBSL check); the Aragalaya protests forced President Gotabaya Rajapaksa to flee in
  July 2022 and Ranil Wickremesinghe became president.
- **The fertilizer ban's effect** (secondary sources): rice output down about 20% within six months (some
  cite 32%), tea down 18%, about **$425 million in lost tea exports** and **$450 million of rice imports**. ⚠️ The
  20-32% spread is real disagreement.
- **IMF program**: Extended Fund Facility (Board approval March 2023, about $2.9 billion, background knowledge,
  not verified). The **combined fifth and sixth reviews were completed on 27 May 2026**; a Rapid Financing
  Instrument followed the cyclone in December 2025; an IMF staff visit concluded on 23 September 2026 reporting
  strong first-half 2026 fiscal outturn and debt restructuring "largely completed" (IMF pages returned 403; I
  rely on search-summary paraphrase). **As of February 2026, agreement with just under 99% of external
  creditors and over 92% of public external debt restructuring fully implemented.** Public debt fell from 125.8%
  of GDP (2022) to 105.7% (2024) in one IMF citation, while the Wikipedia summary gives 91.6% for "government
  debt." ⚠️ Different definitions or years; verify.

### 7.3 The 2024 elections
- **Presidential (21 September 2024).** Anura Kumara Dissanayake (NPP/JVP) won **42.31%** on the first count
  (5,634,915), Sajith Premadasa (SJB) 32.76%, Ranil Wickremesinghe 17.27%. This was the first presidential
  election in which no one won a majority on first preferences, so second preferences were counted: **Dissanayake
  55.89% to Premadasa 44.11%**. Turnout 79.46% of 17,140,354 registered voters. Dissanayake is the first
  president from outside the UNP-SLFP duopoly and was inaugurated on 23 September 2024.
- **The North-East voted differently** (first counts, search summary of the official results): **Jaffna**:
  Premadasa 32.60%, the common Tamil candidate Ariyanethiran 31.39%, Wickremesinghe 22.75%, **Dissanayake 7.29%**.
  **Batticaloa**: Premadasa 43.66%, Wickremesinghe 28.60%, Dissanayake 12.19%, Ariyanethiran 11.58%.
  **Trincomalee**: Premadasa 50.36%, Dissanayake 20.83%. **Vanni**: Dissanayake 9.86%. **The Sinhalese-majority
  south gave Dissanayake his victory; the North and East did not.**
- **Parliamentary (14 November 2024).** The NPP won **159 of 225 seats**, "more than any Sri Lankan political
  party in history," on a turnout of about 68.93%; it **won Jaffna district**, the first time a non-Tamil party
  did, and per the fetched summary "won every district except Batticaloa." The ITAK won 8 seats, another Tamil
  alliance 4, and a record 21 women were elected. ⚠️ This reflects a protest vote over the economy and
  corruption; I do not infer a Tamil endorsement of the NPP's constitutional policy.
- **Cyclone Ditwah (late November-early December 2025).** **647 dead (644 in Sri Lanka), 183 missing**, about
  1-1.5 million displaced, $4.1-4.14 billion damage; the worst-hit districts were the central-highland tea
  districts (**Kandy 234 dead, Badulla 90, Nuwara Eliya 89, Matale 28**); the state of emergency was declared on
  28 November 2025; India's Operation Sagar Bandhu delivered over 1,030 tonnes of relief (Wikipedia). The IMF
  revised its 2026 growth projection from 3.1% to 2.9%. **The disaster fell disproportionately on the Malaiyaha
  Tamil estate belt and the Kandyan highlands, the two identities §4 marks as territorially awkward.** ⚠️ Death
  counts by ethnicity are not in the sources; that the estate workforce was disproportionately affected is my
  inference from geography.

## Section 9 — Economy and the import lifeline (the Oceania-style crux)

The crux, as for Oceania and Central Asia, is **what an import-dependent island loses when dollars run out**.

### 9.1 Export and inflow base
- **Economy**: growth 5.0% in both 2024 and 2025; nominal GDP about **$108.8 billion** (2025); services 54.6%,
  industry 25.4%, agriculture 8.4%. **Apparel about 40% of merchandise exports** (350,000+ employed); **tea
  exports about $1.435 billion (2024)**, 2% of GDP, 1 million+ people; IT services $1.644 billion (2025). Export
  markets: US 22.8%, UK 9.0%, India 7.2%. ⚠️ Rubber and coconut shares not gathered.
- **Remittances**: a record **$8.1 billion in 2025** in one summary (about $6.6 billion in 2024); a Newsfirst
  headline says **$7.8 billion**. Use **$7.8-8.1 billion** as the range; **$3.06 billion in the first four months
  of 2026**.
- **Tourism (2025)**: **2.25-2.36 million arrivals** (headlines differ), a record, about **$3.2 billion**
  revenue, the second-largest foreign-exchange earner.
- **External debt** $37.6 billion (34.6% of GDP), Wikipedia summary, loosely dated.

### 9.2 Import lifeline
- **Fuel** was **21.52% of merchandise imports in 2024**; **no wheat production** (imports forecast at 1.2
  million tonnes for 2024/2025); rice production has "consistently failed to meet domestic consumption needs"
  over five years, with imports mainly from India; lentils 97% from Australia, India and Canada; raw sugar and
  milk powder are major imports; **agriculture about 15% of the 2024 import bill**.
- **Suppliers (2024)**: China 23.3% and India 20.3% (one summary); another gives India 23%, China 17%, UAE 10% for
  a different year. ⚠️ Both agree the top two are India and China.
- **Fertilizer**: the 2021 ban cut imports (about $400 million a year) and caused larger losses in rice and tea;
  India delivered **65,000 tonnes of urea** as relief.

### 9.3 What the 2021-22 stress test shows
1. A single policy shock on an import-dependent food system transmits within one or two harvest seasons (rice
   down 20-32%, tea 18%).
2. A foreign-exchange collapse hits fuel, medicine and power first, food second.
3. Remittances and tourism are the buffer and recovered fast after 2022; both are external dependencies, like
   Central Asia's dependence on Russia (⚠️ remittance source countries not researched).
4. Highland tea and estate labor are the export core and the demographic weak point (Ditwah, §7.3).
5. India is the principal food, fuel-adjacent and transit-cargo partner; in a collapse scenario the dependence on
   one large neighbor is unavoidable. ⚠️ A fact about trade flows only; no research on India itself.

| **A** | **Unitary island, no split** | Yes | Sinhalese are 74.1% nationally; no province other than the North is majority Tamil | The 1976-2009 record shows a Tamil territorial demand persisted 33 years; a post-collapse weakened center makes it likelier, not less |
| **B** | **Northern Province alone** as a Tamil unit | **Yes, clean fit** | 1,150,148 people; **91.5% Sri Lankan Tamil**; 72.1% Hindu; no majority-status ambiguity | Excludes the East's 678,549 Tamils (about 25% of all Sri Lankan Tamils); Jaffna peninsula depends on aquifers; unit only 5.3% of population; would sit on the closest point to India (~22 miles); the East becomes a leftover |
| **C** | **The merged North-East (the 1988-2006 unit)** | **Yes: two whole provinces, with real precedent** | 2,933,362 people; **59.0% Tamil, 26.0% Muslim, 14.6% Sinhalese; 85% Tamil-speaking**; the LTTE controlled about 76% of its land at the 2000 peak | Muslims (26%) become a minority in a Tamil-majority unit, after the 1990 expulsions and massacres; Sinhalese settler districts (Ampara 37.1%) sit inside it; the 1988 referendum promise was never fulfilled and the Supreme Court annulled the merger in 2006; 9,996 + 8,884 = 18,880 sq km, nearly 29% of the island's land on 13.5% of its population |
| **D** | **North plus Batticaloa only** | **No: needs a district split of the Eastern Province** | Adds 595,918 people, 71.3% Tamil | Breaks the whole-province rule; strands Trincomalee (46% Muslim, 24.5% Sinhalese) and Ampara; Trincomalee's harbor and tank farm stay with the center |
| **E** | **Eastern Province as its own tri-ethnic unit** | Yes | 1,783,214 people; **Muslim 39.5%, Tamil 38.1%, Sinhalese 22.1%**; no majority; religion 39.65% Muslim, 33.51% Hindu, 21.82% Buddhist, 5.02% Christian | The only whole-province option that reflects the three-way mix, but a unit with no majority group is a consociational or partitioned-government problem; the 2024 numbers are a moving target (Muslim growth) |
| **F** | **Three-way East divided by district** | No | Batticaloa Tamil; Ampara Muslim-plurality; Trincomalee Muslim-plurality | Three districts, no clean group; splits the province, fits none of the 25 districts as pure, and Ampara and Trincomalee are the two districts most exposed to the Sinhalese-settlement dispute |
| **G** | **Up-country as a cultural or overlay unit** | **No** | 600,360 people; Nuwara Eliya district 50.0% Indian Tamil (the only plurality); Central Province 15.0%, Uva 6.9% | No province and only one district has a Malaiyaha plurality; a territorial unit needs sub-district drawing (breaks the rule), and the community is 2.76% of the island. Evidence supports an overlay or personal autonomy, not a territory |
| **H** | **Central Province as Kandyan highland unit (⚠️ recalled, unsourced)** | Yes | Central: Sinhalese 65.8%, Indian Tamil 15.0%, Moor/Muslim 10.7%; holds the tea belt | Would carry the plantation economy and the Malaiyaha population inside a Sinhalese-majority unit: a reason for it (economic base), a cost (a minority inside it holds the export core) |
| **I** | **Unitary with devolution (13th Amendment implemented in full, or replaced)** | Yes | Provincial Councils exist in law; none has met since 2017-2019 | The political path most consistent with the real record; nothing here decides what happens after a collapse |

**Costs common to every option.**
- **Import lifeline (§9):** the island's fuel, wheat and much rice come through Colombo and the south; any
  northern or eastern unit inherits ports (Trincomalee, Jaffna area) with far less cargo and a coast facing
  India. ⚠️ Not researched: Jaffna and Kankesanthurai port capacity.
- **Water and power:** the Mahaweli dams are in the Central highlands and feed the north-central and eastern dry
  zone (§2); a split that separates the Mahaweli headworks from the eastern irrigated land is a dependency
  question the author must weigh. ⚠️ Which specific dams and irrigation districts fall in each unit is a map task.
- **Settlement politics:** Ampara, Trincomalee and Mullaitivu (Weli Oya) all contain the results of state
  colonization; each option has to say what happens to those populations (my note; no source proposes a remedy).

## Section 11 — Where real geography breaks "whole first-order subdivisions only"

1. **The Eastern Province (the main break).** No whole-province rule can classify it: it has no majority group,
   and none of its three districts is the same group as another. **The rule would force an all-or-nothing
   assignment of 1.78 million people.** Even splitting at the district level fails for Ampara (no group above 44% in 2012) and Trincomalee. Subdistrict (divisional secretariat) data exist in the
   census but were not extracted. ⚠️ **Rule bend or a designed exception is a decision for the author** (a
   hatched pocket, an overlay, or accepting Option C or E as a whole-province choice).
2. **Up-country Tamils.** Scattered across Central (15.0%), Uva (6.9%) and Sabaragamuwa (2.2%); 92% in seven
   districts; a plurality in only one (Nuwara Eliya). No province boundary isolates them, so under the rule they
   can exist only as a **sphere overlay** (the pattern used for the Tai Continuum and the Mongolic World) rather
   than a border.
3. **The Northern Province's small minorities.** Vavuniya (10.8% Sinhalese, 9.7% Muslim), Mannar (26.5%
   Muslim) and Mullaitivu (9.1% Sinhalese) sit inside a whole-province unit and are absorbed by the rule.
4. **Muslim pockets outside the East.** Puttalam (21.3%), Kandy (15.0%), Colombo (12.0%) and Kalutara (10.4%)
   are dispersed; they can be handled as a tier-2 identity but not as a territory.
5. **The Low Country / Kandyan line** is not a province boundary and is not in any census after 1971.
6. **Precedent worth recording.** The **North Eastern Province (1988-2006)** was itself a whole first-order unit
   made by joining two provinces, so the rule does not forbid a merged NE unit; it only forbids splitting the
   Eastern Province.

## Section 12 — The India seam (flagged, from Sri Lanka's side only)

⚠️ **A flag, not research on India.** Sri Lanka's only land-adjacent neighbor is India, across the Palk Strait
(about 22 miles at the Jaffna peninsula). I did no research on Tamil Nadu or India as a country.

- **The Tamil link.** Sri Lankan Tamil dialects are "more conservative than Indian varieties"; about 100,000
  Sri Lankan Tamils live in India (Wikipedia); the Jaffna kingdom paid tribute to the Pandyan Empire early on.
  The 1987 Accord and the 1987-1990 IPKF (§5.3) are the episode where India acted as a party.
- **Katchatheevu.** An uninhabited **285-acre** island in Jaffna district's waters. India recognized Sri Lanka's
  ownership in the **1974** maritime agreement; a **1976** agreement restricted fishing access in each
  country's exclusive economic zone. A Catholic shrine (St. Anthony's) holds a joint annual three-day festival,
  visa-free for Indian pilgrims. Sri Lanka's position, in an earlier foreign minister's phrase, is that it was
  "resolved 50 years ago"; a Tamil Nadu resolution of **March 2025** sought retrieval (Wikipedia). ⚠️ The
  current foreign ministry's 2026 stance was not confirmed.
- **Fishing.** The Sri Lankan Navy arrested **346 Indian fishermen and seized 44 trawlers in 2025** (one press
  count; 128 and 18 as of 15 April). Bottom trawling in the narrow shared Palk Strait ground is the Sri Lankan
  objection, with northern Sri Lankan fishers the injured party from Sri Lanka's side (⚠️ background, not
  verified).
- **Economic partner.** India-linked cargo is about 60% of Colombo's throughput; India supplied urea in 2021,
  Ditwah relief in 2025 (Operation Sagar Bandhu), and signed the April 2025 defence MoU and Trincomalee hub (§8).
  **A project set in the post-collapse world must decide what the neighbor across the strait is**: an
  author-level decision about a country this survey does not cover.
